Editorial standards

How the information on this site and in the Astor editions is checked, and what to do if you find a mistake.

Quotations

Quotations in the study guides are compared with a named public-domain text of each book before they are published. For most of Shakespeare this is the Globe text published online by the MIT Shakespeare site. For prose it is usually a Project Gutenberg or Standard Ebooks transcription, and for translations it is the translation the Astor edition prints.

Each guide names its text. References give act, scene and line for plays, line numbers for poems, and the chapter or section for prose. Line numbers vary by a line or two between modern editions, and a few Shakespeare guides follow the numbering of the Astor edition.

Dates and history

Dates of writing, publication and performance come from library catalogues, facsimiles of early editions, theatre records and standard reference works. Each book page lists the sources it used.

Many dates are uncertain, particularly before 1700. Where they are, the page gives the date most scholars accept and marks it as approximate, for example “c. 1606” for Macbeth.

Criticism

When a guide describes what a critic has argued, it names the critic and the book or essay, with its date. Short quotations are taken from the published work. The guides do not credit arguments to unnamed groups of “critics” or “readers”.

The sections on themes and language give readings of the text and support them with quotations. They are a starting point for your own reading and are not the only possible interpretation.

Summaries

Plot summaries follow the order of events in the book. Interpretation is kept to the sections on characters, themes, language and context.

Images

The covers are those of the Astor editions. Historical images on book pages are in the public domain or openly licensed, and each caption says where the image comes from.

Exams

The study guides and Study Editions are not written for a particular exam board. If you are studying a book for an exam, check the set edition and the assessment requirements against your own course.

Corrections

If you find a mistake, please write to support@astorlibrary.com with the page and the error. Corrections to the site are made once they have been checked against a source.
