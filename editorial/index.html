Editorial standards

How Astor Library researches and edits its publications.

Astor Library publishes complete texts, explanatory notes, summaries, contextual material, study editions and free guides.

Editorial work begins with the literary text. Factual claims are checked against named sources. Interpretation is supported with quotations or precise references and is distinguished from publication history and other verifiable information.

01 · Literary text

Summaries and analysis refer to specific wording and structure.

Plot, character and theme are explained through quotations, scene or chapter structure and formal features. Summaries record the sequence of events without presenting one interpretation as the only possible reading.

02 · Factual record

Dates and historical claims require sources.

Publication dates, textual history, performances and historical context are checked against dependable records. Book pages link to the libraries, archives, museums, theatres and scholarly collections used.

03 · Interpretation

Critical claims require evidence.

Astor pages distinguish verifiable facts from interpretation. Where readers or editors disagree, the page identifies the disagreement and the textual evidence involved.

04 · Revision

Published pages are corrected and updated.

Pages may be revised when better sources, clearer explanations, new performance records or factual corrections become available.

Sources

Sources are named and linked.

Where a page uses external historical or visual material, its sources are linked at the end. Astor Library favours primary texts and established collections such as the Folger Shakespeare Library, the British Library, the Library of Congress, university archives and author societies.

The free guides remain separate from the main editions, but they follow the same principle. They support reading and teaching; they do not replace the original work.

Browse the book pages or open the free resource library.

Editorial test

Every note must provide identifiable information.

A note should define a word, identify a reference, explain relevant context, summarise an event or support an interpretation. Material that does none of these should be removed.
