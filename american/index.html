Period

American.

American books from Hawthorne and Poe to Fitzgerald: slavery and freedom in Douglass, Stowe and Twain, the sea in Moby-Dick, the Civil War in The Red Badge of Courage, the Klondike in Jack London, and ghost stories and Christmas stories from American magazines.

Paperback · Mark Twain

A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur’s Court

Hank Morgan, head superintendent at the Colt arms factory in Hartford, is knocked out in a fight and wakes up near Camelot in the year 528. He saves himself from the stake by predicting an eclipse, then starts building telegraph lines, newspapers and factories. Twain’s novel appeared in 1889, and this edition follows the first-edition text.

Paperback · Various authors

American Christmas Stories

‘A Visit from St. Nicholas’, usually credited to Clement Clarke Moore, appeared without a name in the Troy Sentinel in December 1823 and gave Santa Claus eight named reindeer. It is collected here with eight complete American Christmas stories written between 1872 and 1905, from Bret Harte to O. Henry, and six chapters of history.

Paperback · F. Marion Crawford, Ambrose Bierce, Ellen Glasgow and others

American Ghost Stories

Ten American ghost stories from 1878 to 1925, seven of them by women, among them Mary E. Wilkins Freeman, Ellen Glasgow and Edith Wharton. Nearly all first appeared in magazines, and the essays go back to the magazines, editors and publishers who printed them.

Paperback · Mark Twain

The Tragedy of Pudd’nhead Wilson

Roxy, enslaved and one-sixteenth Black, swaps her baby son with her master’s baby in the cradle, so that her child will grow up free and white. Years later, in the Missouri river town of Dawson’s Landing, a murder trial is settled by the fingerprints David Wilson has been collecting as a hobby. This edition follows the 1894 American text and lists its corrections.

Paperback · Edgar Allan Poe

Edgar Allan Poe: Selected Tales

Poe revised his stories again and again, so every edition has to choose a version. This one prints ten tales, among them ‘The Fall of the House of Usher’, ‘The Tell-Tale Heart’, ‘The Black Cat’ and two of the Dupin detective stories, and says for each which printing it follows.

Paperback · Kate Chopin

The Awakening

Edna Pontellier, twenty-eight, married to a New Orleans businessman and the mother of two boys, spends a summer at Grand Isle and learns to swim. By the autumn she has moved out of her husband’s house. Reviewers in 1899 disliked the book so much that Kate Chopin published very little afterwards. It is printed here in the first-edition text, without footnotes.

Paperback · Stephen Crane

The Open Boat and Other Stories

In January 1897 Stephen Crane spent thirty hours in a dinghy off the Florida coast after the Commodore sank, and ‘The Open Boat’ is the story he made of it. The London edition of 1898 collected it with sixteen other stories in two parts, and Astor follows that order.

Paperback · Mark Twain

The Adventures of Tom Sawyer

Tom Sawyer tricks the boys of St. Petersburg, Missouri, into whitewashing his aunt’s fence for him, runs off to be a pirate on Jackson’s Island, and with Huck Finn sees a murder in the graveyard at midnight. Twain drew the town from Hannibal, where he grew up in the 1840s. The book appeared in 1876, in London before New York.

Paperback · Jack London

White Fang

White Fang, three-quarters wolf, is born in the Yukon wild and passes from a Native owner, Gray Beaver, to a dog-fighter called Beauty Smith before a mining engineer, Weedon Scott, takes him home to California. Jack London wrote it in 1906 as a companion to The Call of the Wild, with the journey running the other way.

1851

Moby-Dick; or, The Whale, Herman Melville

Two Astor editions trace the novel’s British and American publication in 1851 and the maritime stories behind it, including the Essex disaster and Mocha Dick. From nineteenth-century whaling to Ishmael’s restless narration and Ahab’s authority, the book rewards close attention before opening into a long life on stage and screen.

1895

The Red Badge of Courage, Stephen Crane

Crane places Henry Fleming’s fear, flight, shame and accidental wound against the Civil War and the context of Chancellorsville. The 1895 D. Appleton edition leads into his psychological realism and colour imagery, then to John Huston’s 1951 film.

1903

The Call of the Wild, Jack London

Buck is taken from a California estate to the Klondike, where the sled team and the wolf pack force a new kind of life upon him. His journey opens questions about naturalism, labour, hunger and violence, while the book’s publication and screen history show how often that story has been reshaped.

1851–52

Uncle Tom’s Cabin, Harriet Beecher Stowe

Mr Shelby, a Kentucky farmer in debt, sells two of the people he enslaves: Tom and a small boy, Harry. Harry’s mother, Eliza, runs with him that night and crosses the half-frozen Ohio River, jumping from one ice floe to the next. Tom is taken south, to Simon Legree’s plantation. Harriet Beecher Stowe’s novel sold 300,000 copies in America in its first year, 1852.

1884–85

Adventures of Huckleberry Finn, Mark Twain

Huck fakes his own death to get away from his father and hides out on Jackson’s Island. There he finds Jim, who has run away because Miss Watson means to sell him down the river. They plan to float to Cairo and go up the Ohio into the free states, and in a fog one night they drift past it.

1845

Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass, Frederick Douglass

Frederick Douglass was born into slavery on Maryland’s Eastern Shore, never knew his birthday, and saw his mother only a few times, at night. He learned to read in Baltimore after his mistress’s lessons were stopped, fought back against the slave-breaker Edward Covey, and escaped north in 1838. He published this account in 1845, naming the men who had owned him.

American romance

The Scarlet Letter

In Puritan Boston in the 1640s, Hester Prynne is made to stand on the scaffold with her baby and to wear a scarlet A on her dress for the rest of her life. She will not say who the father is. The minister who urges her to name him is Arthur Dimmesdale, and her husband, who has arrived in the town that day under a false name, is watching from the crowd.

Two stories · 1891–92

The Yellow Wall-Paper and The Giant Wistaria

Charlotte Perkins Gilman’s two stories appeared in the New England Magazine seven months apart, ‘The Giant Wistaria’ in June 1891 and ‘The Yellow Wall-Paper’ in January 1892. One is a ghost story about an old colonial house; the other is about a woman shut in a nursery on her doctor-husband’s orders. The notes record where the surviving texts differ.

Novel · 1911

Ethan Frome

A young engineer spending a winter in the Massachusetts village of Starkfield notices Ethan Frome, lame and bent at fifty-two, and wants to know what happened to him. The story he pieces together goes back twenty-four years, to Ethan’s sickly wife, Zeena, and her young cousin, Mattie Silver. Edith Wharton published it in 1911.

Novel · 1925

The Great Gatsby

Nick Carraway rents a small house in West Egg, Long Island, in the summer of 1922, next door to the mansion where Jay Gatsby gives parties for hundreds of people he does not know. Across the bay lives Nick’s cousin Daisy, whom Gatsby has loved for five years. Fitzgerald published the novel in April 1925; this edition follows the first-edition text.

Annotated collection · twelve stories

Sleepy Hollow and Other American Halloween Stories

None of these twelve stories was written for Halloween. Irving’s headless horseman, Hawthorne’s Goodman Brown, Poe’s black cat and Gilman’s yellow wallpaper were reprinted in so many October anthologies that they now belong to the season, and the introduction traces how that happened.
