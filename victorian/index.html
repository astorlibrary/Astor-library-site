Period

Victorian.

Dickens and Brontë join Hardy, Stevenson, Stoker, Wilde, Wells, Conan Doyle, Henry James and Ibsen in a collection moving between Christmas rooms, city streets, country houses, moors, laboratories and consulting rooms. Each edition places the work beside its publication and social history.

Paperback · Henry James

The Turn of the Screw

A young governess takes charge of two orphaned children, Miles and Flora, at a country house called Bly, on condition that she never troubles their uncle in London. She begins to see a man and a woman about the grounds, and learns that the last valet and the last governess are both dead. Henry James published the story in 1898, and readers still disagree about whether the ghosts are there.

Paperback · Henrik Ibsen

A Doll’s House

Years ago Nora Helmer forged her dead father’s signature to borrow the money that saved her husband’s life. Now Krogstad, the man who lent it, is about to lose his job at Torvald’s bank, and he still has the forged paper. Ibsen’s play was first performed in Copenhagen in 1879; Astor uses R. Farquharson Sharp’s translation of 1910.

Paperback · Oscar Wilde

The Importance of Being Earnest

Jack Worthing is Ernest in town and Jack in the country; his friend Algernon has invented an invalid called Bunbury so that he can get out of dinners. Both are in love with women who say they could only marry a man called Ernest. Wilde’s play opened at the St James’s Theatre on 14 February 1895, and this edition follows the first book edition of 1899.

Paperback · George and Weedon Grossmith

The Diary of a Nobody

‘Why should I not publish my diary?’ asks Charles Pooter, a City clerk who has just moved into The Laurels, Brickfield Terrace, Holloway. For fifteen months he records trouble with tradesmen, a humiliating ball at the Mansion House and his son Lupin’s schemes. It began as a series in Punch in 1888–89.

Paperback · Jerome K. Jerome

Three Men in a Boat

J., George and Harris decide that what they need is a rest, and take a boat up the Thames from Kingston to Oxford with a fox terrier called Montmorency. Jerome K. Jerome meant the book to be a guide to the river with jokes in it; the jokes took over. This edition reproduces A. Frederics’s illustrations of 1889.

1843

A Christmas Carol, Charles Dickens

Dickens’s little Christmas book appeared in 1843 with illustrations by John Leech. Scrooge’s night with Marley and the three spirits leads back to Tiny Tim and the poverty beyond his own door. Dickens’s public readings helped carry the story towards its long life on stage and screen.

1891

Tess of the d’Urbervilles, Thomas Hardy

Hardy’s novel moved from its serial appearance in The Graphic to a three-volume book edition. Tess’s journey through Marlott, Talbothays, Flintcomb-Ash and Stonehenge makes Wessex a place of rural labour, social judgement and hypocrisy, all bearing upon her relationships with Alec and Angel.

1860–61

Great Expectations, Charles Dickens

Published in serial parts in 1860–61, the novel follows Pip from Kent to London as ambition draws him towards crime, Miss Havisham and Magwitch. Its two published endings leave the question of his reward open in different ways; screen versions have kept returning to both his rise and his reckoning.

1847

Wuthering Heights, Emily Brontë

Published as the work of Ellis Bell in 1847, the novel reaches us through the layered accounts of Lockwood and Nelly Dean. Haworth and the moors ground Heathcliff and Catherine’s story of inheritance, revenge, Gothic violence and dialect, which later versions have carried to stage and screen.

1886

Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde, Robert Louis Stevenson

Victorian London, scientific ambition and secrecy close around Stevenson’s story of divided identity. Its publication history and carefully managed narrative explain how the novella creates its mystery, before the story moves into its stage and screen afterlives.

1890/1891

The Picture of Dorian Gray, Oscar Wilde

The novel first appeared in a magazine in 1890, then in a revised book text in 1891. Those differences place Dorian’s portrait amid aestheticism, decadence and moral controversy, before Wilde’s trials and later versions gave the story further lives on stage and screen.

1890

The Sign of Four, Arthur Conan Doyle

Conan Doyle’s second Sherlock Holmes novel appeared in Lippincott’s in 1890. Mary Morstan’s case brings together the Agra treasure and a pursuit along the Thames, joining Holmes’s detective method to the story’s 1857 colonial frame. Later versions took that mystery to stage and screen.

1897

Dracula, Bram Stoker

Stoker tells his vampire story through documents, bringing Whitby, Victorian technology, medicine and gender into an older tradition. The link between the Count’s name and the historical Vlad Dracula remains debated, while stage and screen adaptations reveal how freely later artists have remade him.

1897 / 1898

The War of the Worlds, H. G. Wells

Serialised in 1897 and published as a book in 1898, Wells’s invasion begins at Horsell Common and brings terror to Woking. The Heat-Ray and fighting-machines turn Victorian science and imperial power inside out. Radio, screen and other adaptations have kept finding new fears in the story.

1891–92

The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes, Arthur Conan Doyle

The first twelve Holmes short stories, printed in the Strand Magazine between July 1891 and June 1892 and collected that October. They include ‘A Scandal in Bohemia’, in which Holmes is outwitted by Irene Adler, ‘The Red-Headed League’ and ‘The Speckled Band’.

1845

The Cricket on the Hearth, Charles Dickens

John Peerybingle, a carrier, is married to Dot, who is much younger than him, and a cricket sings on their hearth. Then an old stranger who seems to be deaf comes to lodge with them, and one evening John sees Dot with the man, who is no longer old. Dickens’s third Christmas book came out in December 1845.

Cultural history

A Victorian Christmas

Henry Cole had the first Christmas card printed in 1843, the year of A Christmas Carol, and in 1848 the Illustrated London News showed the royal family round a decorated tree. This is a history of how the Victorians made the Christmas that is still kept, with three pieces by Dickens printed complete.

Novel · 1847

Jane Eyre

Jane Eyre is an orphan at Gateshead, shut in the red room by her aunt, and then a pupil at Lowood, a charity school where the girls go hungry. At eighteen she becomes governess at Thornfield Hall, where Mr Rochester lives and where she hears laughter from the third storey. Charlotte Brontë published the novel in 1847 as Currer Bell.

Christmas book · 1848

The Haunted Man and the Ghost’s Bargain

Redlaw, a chemist and lecturer, is visited by a phantom that looks exactly like him and offers to take away his painful memories. He accepts, and finds that he now passes the same forgetting to everyone he meets. It was the last of Dickens’s five Christmas books, published in December 1848.

Annotated collection · 1844–1901

Victorian Ghost Stories

Elizabeth Gaskell was paid £10 for ‘The Old Nurse’s Story’, written for the Christmas number of Household Words in 1852. It opens this collection of eight Victorian ghost stories and four poems, which ends with Bram Stoker’s ‘The Judge’s House’, alongside documents from the séances and the Society for Psychical Research.

Four Christmas books · 1843–48

Dickens at Christmas

Dickens wrote five Christmas books between 1843 and 1848. Four are here: A Christmas Carol, The Chimes, The Cricket on the Hearth and The Haunted Man. The fifth, The Battle of Life (1846), is the only one without a supernatural visitor, and it is not included.

Novel · 1865

Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland

Charles Dodgson first told the story to the three Liddell sisters on a boat trip up the Thames on 4 July 1862, and later wrote it out for Alice as Alice’s Adventures Under Ground. This edition prints his final revised text, with notes on the Victorian poems he parodies and the Oxford people behind the characters.

Annotated cultural history

A Victorian Bonfire Night

Until 1859 the Fifth of November was a holiday fixed by Act of Parliament, with its own service in the Prayer Book. This collection gathers Victorian stories, essays, folklore and newspaper reports from the years when it became something else: children with guys, bonfire societies, fireworks and bonfire toffee.

Novel · 1859–60 / 1861 text

The Woman in White

Walking back to London late at night, the drawing master Walter Hartright meets a young woman dressed entirely in white who has escaped from an asylum. She looks very like his new pupil, Laura Fairlie, who is engaged to Sir Percival Glyde. Wilkie Collins’s novel ran in All the Year Round in 1859–60.

Novel · 1868

The Moonstone

On her eighteenth birthday Rachel Verinder is given a great yellow diamond, taken from India when Seringapatam was stormed in 1799. By the next morning it has gone from her room. The story is told by a series of witnesses, starting with the family’s old steward, Gabriel Betteredge, who consults Robinson Crusoe in every crisis.

Christmas book · 1844

The Chimes

Trotty Veck, a London ticket-porter, reads in the newspaper that the poor have no right to a New Year, and half believes it. On New Year’s Eve the goblins of the church bells show him what will happen to his daughter and her friends. Dickens wrote it in Genoa in 1844, the year after A Christmas Carol.
