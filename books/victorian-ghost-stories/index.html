About the work

Eight stories, four poems and the documents beside them.

The account below names the complete contents, from The Old Nurse’s Story to The Judge’s House, and the historical material that surrounds them.

The work

Story, argument and structure.

Victorian Ghost Stories follows the supernatural across the second half of the nineteenth century. Haunted rooms, uncanny journeys and disturbed domestic spaces are placed beside the historical conditions in which Victorian readers encountered them.

The eight complete stories are The Old Nurse’s Story, The Haunted and the Haunters, The Cold Embrace, The Phantom Coach, The Signal-Man, The Open Door, Man-Size in Marble and The Judge’s House.

Reading the work

What the complete text brings into view.

Four complete poems extend the collection beyond short fiction. Together, the poetry and prose show how apparitions, warnings, haunted houses, railway danger and the return of the dead moved between literary forms.

A chronology and documentary material on spiritualism and psychical research place the stories beside changing efforts to investigate, report and explain supernatural experience.

Text and context

Complete texts, with fiction and document kept apart.

Notes accompany unabridged stories and poems, and the documents on séances and psychical research stay distinct from the fiction beside them.

The writers represented

The prose selection brings together Elizabeth Gaskell, Edward Bulwer-Lytton, Mary Elizabeth Braddon, Amelia B. Edwards, Charles Dickens, Margaret Oliphant, E. Nesbit and Bram Stoker. Their careers extended across novels, journalism, magazines and other forms of writing; the ghost stories are individual contributions to a larger publishing culture rather than the work of a single organised school.

The collection’s literary contributors also include Thomas Hood, Dante Gabriel Rossetti and Christina Rossetti. Its four complete poems give verse a separate place within the volume. The subtitle’s date range, 1844–1901, applies to the anthology of stories, poems and documents as a whole. It should not be read as a publication date for every story, or as a claim that the eight prose texts alone cover all those years.

Christmas issues and the periodical record

Gaskell’s ‘The Old Nurse’s Story’ appeared in the 1852 Christmas number of Household Words. The journal’s surviving office records, indexed by Dickens Journals Online, identify Gaskell as its author and record a payment of £10. Such evidence makes it possible to place the story within a particular commission and publication, rather than treating it simply as an anonymous piece of seasonal folklore.

Dickens’s ‘The Signal-Man’ appeared in the 1866 Christmas number of All the Year Round, Mugby Junction, under the heading ‘No. 1 Branch Line’. The railway setting therefore belonged to the organising subject of the issue as well as to the individual tale. Reading the stories in a later anthology changes their immediate surroundings: notes on first publication restore information about the magazines and collective projects in which readers originally encountered them.

Literary ghosts and organised inquiry

The documentary material places literary apparitions beside historical discussion of spiritualism and psychical research. The Society for Psychical Research was founded in London in February 1882 after a January conference considering how such claims might be investigated. Its first president was the Cambridge philosopher Henry Sidgwick; early associates included Frederic Myers and Edmund Gurney.

The society developed journals, records of testimony and investigative procedures. Its work included examining apparent manifestations for possible deception as well as collecting accounts of unusual experiences. These activities provide a context for the anthology’s documents, but a report of an apparition and a fictional ghost story have different purposes and evidential status. The collection’s separate literary and documentary sections allow those differences to remain visible while readers compare vocabulary, settings, witnesses and the methods used to describe or question supernatural events.

Organised psychical inquiry thus developed during the period covered by the anthology, decades after its earliest literary material had first appeared.
