About this edition

The selection follows the 1921 reprint, which expanded the twenty-three poems of the 1920 collection to twenty-four. Notes explain vocabulary, references and the conditions of military service.

The editorial material covers trench warfare, gas, shell shock, Owen’s relationship with Siegfried Sassoon, pararhyme and critical reception. A glossary, critical perspectives and teaching pathways accompany the poems.

Poems, 1920

Wilfred Owen was born near Oswestry on 18 March 1893 and was killed on 4 November 1918, during the crossing of the Sambre–Oise Canal, a week before the Armistice. His mother received the telegram on Armistice Day, while the church bells in Shrewsbury were ringing.

Only five of his poems were published in his lifetime. He had met Siegfried Sassoon at Craiglockhart War Hospital in Edinburgh, where both were patients, and Sassoon, with help from Edith Sitwell, edited Poems and wrote its introduction. Chatto and Windus published it in 1920. The book had twenty-three poems and the unfinished Preface found among Owen’s papers: “My subject is War, and the pity of War. The Poetry is in the pity.”

Edmund Blunden’s edition of 1931 added nineteen more.
