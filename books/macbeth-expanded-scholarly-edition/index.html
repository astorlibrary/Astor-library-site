About the work

From the witches’ greeting to Birnam Wood.

The account below follows the murder of Duncan into its consequences, and how equivocation works because a listener chooses the interpretation that protects desire.

The work

What happens.

Three witches greet Macbeth as future king. When the first prediction comes true, Lady Macbeth and Macbeth plan to murder Duncan while he is a guest in their castle.

The crown cannot contain the crime. Macbeth orders Banquo’s death, attacks Macduff’s family and returns to the witches for assurances he interprets as guarantees. Lady Macbeth, once resolute, walks in her sleep and relives the violence she believed could be washed away.

Reading the work

Reading it whole.

Birnam Wood and Macduff fulfil the prophecies through meanings Macbeth refused to hear. The tragedy makes equivocation effective because a listener chooses the interpretation that protects desire.

A closer look

Publication, text and historical context.

An eyewitness at the Globe

Simon Forman’s Book of Plays preserves an account of seeing Macbeth at the Globe on 20 April, normally dated 1611. His manuscript gives 1610, but the date is corrected in the Shakespeare Documented catalogue. It is an eyewitness record of a performance, not a transcript of the play. Forman records the principal actions and the lessons he took from them.

The distinction is useful when setting his account beside the Folio printed twelve years later. An audience member may omit a scene, misunderstand a detail or concentrate on a particular moral. The record establishes a performance and preserves what one spectator noticed; it cannot by itself recover every speech or prove that a passage absent from his summary was absent from the stage.

Holinshed and the treatment of Banquo

Shakespeare drew on the Scottish history in the 1587 edition of Holinshed’s Chronicles. One substantial alteration concerns Banquo: in the chronicle narrative he assists Macbeth’s conspiracy against Duncan, whereas Shakespeare’s Banquo does not take part in the murder. His knowledge of the witches’ predictions nevertheless makes him dangerous to the new king.

James VI of Scotland, who became James I of England in 1603, traced his ancestry to Banquo. That claim forms part of the play’s early Stuart context and helps explain why scholars attend closely to the procession of future kings. The historical connection can be documented while particular claims about Shakespeare’s intentions require a separate argument from the play and its circumstances.

What survives in the Folio

The 1623 Folio is the only early printed authority for Macbeth, so an editor cannot compare an earlier quarto against it as in Hamlet or Othello. The Hecate passages and song references have attracted particular attention because of their relationship to material in Thomas Middleton’s The Witch. The extent and timing of any adaptation remain questions within the textual history.

Other performance decisions arise directly from the dramatic action. A bell signals Macbeth to go to Duncan’s chamber; knocking interrupts the aftermath; the banquet requires Macbeth to respond to Banquo’s ghost while the guests respond to Macbeth. Later, a doctor and gentlewoman watch Lady Macbeth walking in her sleep. Reading these scenes through their entrances, sounds, objects and witnesses distinguishes the words preserved by the text from choices a production must make about visibility, timing and movement.
