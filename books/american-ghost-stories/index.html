American Ghost Stories

Ten American ghost stories from 1878 to 1925, seven of them by women, among them Mary E. Wilkins Freeman, Ellen Glasgow and Edith Wharton. Nearly all first appeared in magazines, and the essays go back to the magazines, editors and publishers who printed them.

The ten complete stories

The contents are The Upper Berth by F. Marion Crawford; The Middle Toe of the Right Foot by Ambrose Bierce; The Shadowy Third by Ellen Glasgow; The House That Was Not by Elia Wilkinson Peattie; An Itinerant House by Emma Frances Dawson; The Shadows on the Wall by Mary E. Wilkins Freeman; The House of the Nightmare by Edward Lucas White; Bewitched by Edith Wharton; The Little Room by Madeline Yale Wynne; and The Shell of Sense by Olivia Howard Dunbar.

The earliest first appearance represented is Dawson’s An Itinerant House in 1878; the latest is Wharton’s Bewitched in 1925. The settings include a ship’s cabin, a hospital and private sickroom, farms, roads and houses whose occupants disagree about what is present. Each of the ten stories is reproduced in full.

The texts used

Each story is headed by information identifying its source edition. Eight are taken from collections assembled by their authors or publishers. Bierce’s The Middle Toe of the Right Foot and Dunbar’s The Shell of Sense are taken from Dorothy Scarborough’s Famous Modern Ghost Stories, published in 1921.

Spelling and punctuation follow the named copy-texts. Where the historical evidence disagrees about a date or a detail of someone’s life, the notes say so. The stated first-publication range describes the histories of the stories; it does not mean that every text has been transcribed from its first magazine appearance.

Six parts of editorial material

The Inheritance introduces the American ghost story’s literary and historical background. The Magazine Ghost examines payment, periodical publication, Christmas annuals and illustration. Houses That Do Not Stay Put discusses stories in which the house or room itself is in question.

Away From Home considers encounters at sea and on the road. The People Who Were Paid To Be There examines characters present because of employment, including a hired nurse and people accommodating paying visitors. Afterwards follows criticism, anthology selection and reprinting. These six parts precede the complete story texts and give the volume a researched subject beyond the selection alone.

Ghost stories as magazine publications

The essays place the stories in the commercial circumstances of their publication. Glasgow’s correspondence with her agent supplies evidence about the market for The Shadowy Third. Freeman’s supernatural stories appeared in Everybody’s Magazine before being collected, while Wharton’s Bewitched reached readers through Pictorial Review.

The volume also examines the relation between an annual’s cover date and the time it actually went on sale, using the publication of Crawford’s The Upper Berth in The Broken Shaft, a Christmas annual dated 1886. Such details help distinguish a printed date from evidence about circulation. Illustration is discussed as part of original publication history.

Dunbar, Scarborough and the later record

In 1905 Olivia Howard Dunbar argued in The Dial that the ghost had declined in American magazine fiction. Her own The Shell of Sense appeared in Harper’s Magazine in December 1908. Both the argument and the later story are discussed, giving the collection a documented example of a critic who also wrote supernatural fiction.

Dorothy Scarborough’s research and anthology work provide another part of the later record. The volume examines how stories were selected, grouped and made available to new readers. It also follows later critical work on Wynne and Peattie, distinguishing a published comparison between their stories from an unsupported assumption about influence.

Context boxes and reference material

The editorial chapters close with context boxes containing checkable details tied to the discussion. Where a chapter discusses a work not reproduced in this volume, a Story So Far section supplies its plot. For works reproduced in full, that summary section is omitted. Some boxes include an Anachronism watch note where a date affects what could have been present.

A chronology records publication and historical events, while the glossary defines words that occur in the ten stories and identifies the relevant story. Sources and further reading document the research on Spiritualism, psychical inquiry, magazine culture, domestic spaces and the later circulation of American ghost fiction.
