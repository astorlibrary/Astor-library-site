About this edition

This edition includes all fifteen stories, from The Sisters to The Dead. Explanatory footnotes accompany the text, while the supporting material traces the book’s difficult route to publication and its later reception.

The historical material covers Dublin’s streets and institutions, Catholicism, politics, work, emigration, music and language, alongside the stories’ adaptations.

Nine years to publication

Joyce’s first published fiction was an early version of “The Sisters”, printed in The Irish Homestead on 13 August 1904 under the name Stephen Daedalus. Versions of “Eveline” and “After the Race” followed in the same paper before the end of the year.

Grant Richards agreed to publish the collection in 1905. His printer refused to set “Two Gallants”, Richards asked for further cuts, and when Joyce agreed to only some of them Richards withdrew. Maunsel and Company of Dublin signed a contract in 1909 and had the book set in type, but its managing director, George Roberts, objected to the real people and places named in it and would not publish. In 1912 the printer destroyed the sheets. Joyce kept a set of proofs, and on the journey back from Dublin wrote a broadside in verse against Roberts, “Gas from a Burner”.

Grant Richards finally published Dubliners on 15 June 1914, setting it from the Maunsel proofs. Joyce had submitted the book eighteen times to fifteen publishers.
