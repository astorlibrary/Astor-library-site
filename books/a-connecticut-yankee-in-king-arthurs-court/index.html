The novel and its narrative

After a blow to the head in a Connecticut factory, Hank Morgan finds himself in sixth-century Britain. His knowledge of an approaching eclipse gives him authority at Arthur’s court, where he becomes known as the Boss. He introduces schools, newspapers, telephones, factories and new weapons, while travelling among the people whose society he proposes to reorganise.

Twain presents Hank’s account through a framing encounter and a manuscript. This edition includes the Preface, ‘A Word of Explanation’, ‘The Stranger’s History’, ‘The Tale of the Lost Land’, all forty-four numbered chapters, and the concluding manuscript material and final postscript. The frame is part of the novel’s structure, rather than an editorial addition.

The 1889 publication

Charles L. Webster & Company published the American edition in December 1889. Chatto & Windus issued the British edition that month under the different title A Yankee at the Court of King Arthur. The American title page used A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur’s Court, although the cover used a shorter form.

The original American volume included illustrations by Daniel Carter Beard. The Astor editorial material discusses those illustrations and their place in the book’s publication history. This edition does not reproduce Beard’s original illustrations.

The text in this edition

The novel follows the complete 1889 first-edition text. Twain’s American spellings, inconsistent hyphenation and deliberate misspellings remain, including the typographical mistakes in the Camelot Weekly Hosannah. The text is not rewritten into modern English.

Changes concern presentation: the scene break in Chapter XV becomes a printed ornament, and the borrowed Malory chapter, newspaper passages and proclamations are displayed separately. Twain’s own footnotes in Chapters XIX and XXII appear with asterisks. A single editorial footnote in Chapter XVI uses a dagger. The textual note explains these distinctions so readers can identify what belongs to Twain and what the edition supplies.

Twain, printing and machinery

Samuel Langhorne Clemens, who wrote as Mark Twain, was born in Missouri in 1835. He worked as a printer, Mississippi river pilot and newspaper writer before establishing his career as an author. His earlier books included The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Life on the Mississippi and Adventures of Huckleberry Finn.

Printing and invention also occupied him outside literature. He was involved in the publishing firm Charles L. Webster & Company and invested heavily in the Paige typesetting machine. The edition places this practical involvement with industrial technology alongside Hank’s career as a factory foreman, without treating the fictional narrator as a straightforward statement of Twain’s own opinions.

Arthurian sources and historical context

The supporting material identifies the Arthurian literature on which Twain drew, including Thomas Malory, and distinguishes borrowed passages from Hank’s narration. It also examines the nineteenth-century revival of interest in Camelot, which provides a different context from the remote period in which Hank’s adventures take place.

Historical sections address monarchy, inherited rank, slavery, work and political economy. These subjects occur in concrete episodes: Hank and the king travel in disguise, encounter poverty and disease, and are themselves sold as slaves. The later chapters bring industrial weapons into conflict with the institutions Hank has tried to replace.

Commentary and reference material

Chapter summaries are accompanied by researched context, allowing a reader to review events and then investigate particular historical references. The introductory material includes a chronology, an account of the narrative framework, language and style, character studies, and a separate discussion of the book’s argument.

Critical perspectives and reception history record how the novel has been read, while the glossary and bibliography provide reference points for further study. The edition’s purpose is to keep the complete novel available alongside information about its sources, publication and historical vocabulary, with authorial and editorial material clearly identified.
