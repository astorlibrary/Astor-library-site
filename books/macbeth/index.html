William Shakespeare

Macbeth

Three witches greet Macbeth on a heath as the future king of Scotland. That night Duncan stays at his castle, and Macbeth, urged on by his wife, stabs him in his sleep. It is the shortest of Shakespeare’s tragedies and it moves fast: Banquo, and then Macduff’s wife and children, are murdered before the English army reaches Dunsinane.

Macbeth, William Shakespeare

After three witches predict that Macbeth will become king, he and Lady Macbeth plan Duncan’s murder. Macbeth takes the crown, orders the deaths of Banquo and Macduff’s family, and is defeated when Malcolm and Macduff lead an army against him.

The edition contains the complete play with scene summaries, line numbers and explanatory notes on language and imagery. Its longer sections cover Holinshed’s Chronicles, Jacobean politics, succession, the supernatural and selected stage, opera and film adaptations.

The text and its sources

1611 / 1623

Forman’s account and the First Folio

The earliest record of a performance is in the notebook of the astrologer Simon Forman, who described seeing Macbeth at the Globe in April 1611. The play was not printed in Shakespeare’s lifetime. The only early text is the one in the First Folio of 1623, and it is the shortest of his tragedies.

Most editors think the Folio text was revised for performance after Shakespeare’s death. Two songs cued in the witches’ scenes appear in full in Thomas Middleton’s play The Witch, and the scenes with Hecate are often attributed to Middleton.

1040–1057

The historical Macbeth

Macbeth ruled the Scottish kingdom of Alba from 1040 to 1057. He became king after Duncan I was killed fighting against him, and he was himself killed in battle at Lumphanan in 1057 by the forces of Duncan’s son Malcolm. Duncan was not the old man of the play.

Shakespeare took the story from the 1587 edition of Raphael Holinshed’s Chronicles, which also gave him the witches, there called the “weird sisters”. In Holinshed, Banquo helps Macbeth to kill Duncan. In the play he has no part in the murder.

1597 / 1606

James I, Banquo and the Porter

James VI of Scotland, who became James I of England in 1603, was held to be descended from Banquo, and in the fourth act the witches show Macbeth eight kings descended from Banquo. James had published Daemonologie, a treatise on witchcraft, in 1597.

The Porter’s joke about an “equivocator” who “could not equivocate to heaven” is usually connected with the trial in 1606 of the Jesuit Henry Garnet, who was executed for treason after the Gunpowder Plot. His defence of equivocation was cited against him at the trial.

On stage, 1660–1900

1664

Davenant’s Restoration Macbeth and Pepys

  • Sir William Davenant’s adaptation was performed by the Duke’s Company after the Restoration.
  • The version expanded the witches and made music, song and spectacle central to the performance.
  • Samuel Pepys saw it several times in the 1660s.
  • On 5 November 1664 Pepys wrote that the play was “admirably acted”.
  • Versions of Davenant’s witches’ songs and dances stayed in performances of the play until the nineteenth century.

1744 / 1785

Garrick, Pritchard and Siddons

  • David Garrick revived Macbeth in 1744 and wrote a new death speech for Macbeth.
  • Hannah Pritchard played Lady Macbeth opposite Garrick until she retired from the stage in 1768.
  • Sarah Siddons first played Lady Macbeth at Drury Lane on 2 February 1785.

1820–1851

Macready, Forrest and the Astor Place Riot

  • William Charles Macready played Macbeth across more than thirty years, beginning at Covent Garden in 1820.
  • On 10 May 1849, rival Macbeth performances by Macready and Edwin Forrest formed part of the background to the Astor Place Riot in New York.
  • Macready performed at the Astor Opera House; Forrest performed nearby at the Broadway Theatre.
  • More than twenty people were killed.

1847 / 1865

Verdi’s opera Macbeth

  • Verdi’s Macbeth premiered at the Teatro della Pergola, Florence, on 14 March 1847.
  • The libretto was by Francesco Maria Piave, with later contribution from Andrea Maffei.
  • Verdi revised the opera for Paris in 1865.
  • The Paris version added a ballet, as French opera houses expected.

1888 / 1889

Henry Irving, Ellen Terry and Sargent

  • Henry Irving’s Macbeth opened at the Lyceum Theatre, London, on 29 December 1888.
  • Irving played Macbeth opposite Ellen Terry as Lady Macbeth.
  • Arthur Sullivan composed incidental music for the production.
  • John Singer Sargent painted Ellen Terry as Lady Macbeth in 1889 after seeing the production.
  • Terry’s green dress was decorated with iridescent beetle wings; the National Trust holds the costume at Smallhythe Place.

On stage since 1900

1928 / 1936

Barry Jackson and Orson Welles

  • Barry Jackson’s 1928 production for the Birmingham Repertory company, staged at the Royal Court Theatre in London, was played in modern dress.
  • Orson Welles directed the Federal Theatre Project’s Black-cast Macbeth at the Lafayette Theatre, Harlem, opening on 14 April 1936.
  • The setting was moved from Scotland to a Caribbean island associated with Haiti.
  • The production became widely known as the “Voodoo Macbeth”.
  • Surviving footage appears in the WPA film We Work Again.

1976–1978

Trevor Nunn: Ian McKellen and Judi Dench

  • Trevor Nunn’s production opened at The Other Place on 9 September 1976.
  • It later transferred to Newcastle, the Royal Shakespeare Theatre, the Donmar Warehouse and the Young Vic.
  • Thames Television filmed the production in March 1978.
  • Ian McKellen won a Plays and Players London Theatre Critics’ Best Actor award.
  • Judi Dench won the 1977 SWET Best Actress in a Revival award.

1999 / 2011 / 2018 / 2023 / 2025

Later RSC records

  • Gregory Doran’s 1999 RSC production starred Antony Sher and Harriet Walter.
  • Michael Boyd’s 2011 RSC production starred Jonathan Slinger and Aislín McGuckin.
  • Polly Findlay’s 2018 RSC production starred Christopher Eccleston and Niamh Cusack.
  • Wils Wilson’s 2023 RSC production starred Reuben Joseph and Valene Kane.
  • Daniel Raggett’s 2025 production returned Macbeth to The Other Place with Sam Heughan and Lia Williams.

2021

Yaël Farber: Almeida Theatre

  • The Almeida production ran from 2 October to 27 November 2021.
  • Yaël Farber directed.
  • James McArdle played Macbeth.
  • Saoirse Ronan made her UK stage debut as Lady Macbeth.

2023–2025

Max Webster: Donmar Warehouse

  • The production opened at the Donmar in December 2023 and transferred to the Harold Pinter Theatre in 2024.
  • Max Webster directed.
  • David Tennant played Macbeth and Cush Jumbo played Lady Macbeth.
  • The production used binaural sound, with audience members wearing headphones.
  • A recording of the production was released in cinemas on 5 February 2025.

2010 / 2013 / 2023–24

Other modern records

  • David Greig’s Dunsinane continues the story after Macbeth’s death and was first staged by the RSC in 2010.
  • Kenneth Branagh and Rob Ashford co-directed Macbeth for Manchester International Festival in 2013; Branagh played Macbeth opposite Alex Kingston as Lady Macbeth.
  • Simon Godwin directed a site-specific Macbeth starring Ralph Fiennes and Indira Varma in 2023–24.

On screen

1948 / 1957 / 1971 / 2015 / 2021

Major screen versions

  • Orson Welles directed and starred in Macbeth in 1948 for Republic Pictures / Mercury Productions.
  • Akira Kurosawa’s Throne of Blood relocated the play to feudal Japan and was released in 1957.
  • Roman Polanski’s 1971 film starred Jon Finch and Francesca Annis, with a screenplay by Polanski and Kenneth Tynan.
  • Justin Kurzel’s 2015 film starred Michael Fassbender and Marion Cotillard.
  • Joel Coen’s The Tragedy of Macbeth (2021) starred Denzel Washington and Frances McDormand.
