About the work

One June day in post-war London.

The account below follows Clarissa and Septimus through the city, and the clocks, crowds and party that join their separate stories.

The work

Story, argument and structure.

Clarissa walks through Westminster and St James’s to buy flowers, remembering Peter Walsh, Sally Seton and the choices that produced her present life with Richard Dalloway. The party gathers private memory, social performance and public London into one evening.

Elsewhere in the same city, Septimus Warren Smith suffers hallucinations after returning from the First World War unable to grieve the death of his officer and friend Evans. Doctors interpret his distress as a failure of proportion and prescribe isolation rather than sympathy.

Reading the work

What the complete text brings into view.

Motor cars, aeroplanes, bells, crowds and the repeated striking of clocks move the narrative from one consciousness to another. When news of Septimus reaches Clarissa’s party, the separate stories become different responses to life, death, isolation and survival.

The edition explains historical and literary references while preserving the punctuation that carries Woolf’s rhythms and transitions between minds.

A closer look

Publication, text and historical context.

A day shared by several different Londons

The novel repeatedly allows a public incident to connect people who do not know one another. A motor car draws speculation from a crowd; an aeroplane writes an advertisement in the sky; clocks and passing traffic are heard from different positions. Woolf can move between observers while keeping the shared incident in view. The resulting account includes private memories that the other people present cannot hear.

The city is also geographically and socially divided. Clarissa’s flower-buying, Peter Walsh’s walk, Septimus and Rezia’s time in Regent’s Park, and Elizabeth Dalloway’s excursion towards the Strand take place in different circumstances. Elizabeth’s thoughts about a possible profession differ from Miss Kilman’s dependence and resentment. Following these journeys helps distinguish a character’s recollections from events taking place elsewhere during the same day.

The people who remember Bourton

Bourton, the country house of Clarissa’s youth, enters the novel through memories revisited by people who have since led separate lives. Clarissa recalls Sally Seton and her rejection of Peter’s marriage proposal. Peter returns from India with his own interpretation of those events. When Sally arrives at the party as Lady Rosseter, her present circumstances complicate the image retained from youth.

The novel does not appoint one of these recollections as a complete account of the past. Small gestures and remembered conversations acquire different importance for different people. Richard’s attempt to express his affection by bringing Clarissa flowers is another example of the distance between intention and speech: the reader knows more of his preparation than Clarissa is told. Attending to such differences makes the transitions between viewpoints easier to follow.

Institutions, advice and public influence

Lady Bruton’s luncheon provides a concrete instance of political influence conducted through private sociability. Richard Dalloway and Hugh Whitbread help her prepare a letter to The Times concerning emigration to Canada. The scene shows the labour behind a public intervention, including the value assigned to Hugh’s command of the appropriate style.

Septimus encounters institutional authority in a different form. Dr Holmes repeatedly minimises his condition; Sir William Bradshaw prescribes separation and rest, exercising power through the language of professional judgement. Rezia’s observations and her attempts to protect time alone with her husband provide another account of his needs. The hat-making scene briefly records their practical cooperation before medical intervention returns. When the Bradshaws carry news of his death into the party, a private crisis enters Clarissa’s social world through the very people associated with his treatment.
