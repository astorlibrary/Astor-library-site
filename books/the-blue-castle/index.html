L. M. Montgomery

The Blue Castle

Valancy Stirling is twenty-nine, unmarried and ordered about by her family in the Ontario town of Deerwood. Then a doctor’s letter tells her she has a year to live, and she stops doing what she is told. Unusually for L. M. Montgomery, the novel is set in Ontario, not Prince Edward Island. It came out in 1926.

Valancy Stirling and Deerwood

At twenty-nine, Valancy Stirling lives under the close supervision of her family in the fictional Ontario town of Deerwood. Her private refuge is the Blue Castle of her imagination. A medical consultation and the diagnosis that follows lead her to abandon habits of deference that her relatives have treated as permanent.

She goes to nurse Cissy Gay, whose circumstances have made her an object of local disapproval, and forms a relationship with Barney Snaith. Her choices take her beyond the familiar routines of the Stirling family. The narrative connects this change with work, companionship, illness and a different experience of the surrounding country.

A novel set in Ontario

The Blue Castle was published in 1926 by McClelland & Stewart in Toronto and the Frederick A. Stokes Company in New York. It is set in the Muskoka region of Ontario, where Montgomery and her family had holidayed in 1922. The local landscape and its recreational use provide a specific setting for the novel’s lake, island and woodland scenes.

The book has a different geographical setting from the Prince Edward Island world associated with Anne of Green Gables. Reading it within Montgomery’s career therefore brings attention to her adult life in Ontario as well as to the island of her childhood. The historical material in this edition supplies that biographical and regional background.

The text and manuscript history

The Astor edition retains Montgomery’s Canadian spelling and punctuation and adds explanatory material around the novel. The chapter summaries provide a reference to events, while footnotes clarify particular language and references. Historical context appears alongside broader discussion of the work’s composition, publication and reception.

The editorial material considers the manuscript as evidence of revision. Montgomery’s developing names and details can be compared with those in the published novel. The later publication of a manuscript edition, edited by Carolyn Strom Collins in 2024, has made that process more accessible. The Astor novel remains the published work described here, with manuscript history supplied as context.

Medicine, marriage and social expectation

Valancy’s diagnosis changes what she believes she can risk. So the doctor’s advice is part of the plot. The edition provides period information to help readers distinguish the fictional consultation from present-day assumptions about diagnosis and treatment.

The family’s response to Valancy’s conduct involves expectations about marriage, respectability and women’s behaviour. Her move into a household that her relatives disapprove of has practical social consequences. The notes explain relevant customs and circumstances without replacing the characters’ particular disagreements with a general account of every Canadian family in the period.

Objects, travel and the natural world

Motoring, patent medicines, household products and outdoor recreation belong to the material world of the novel. Identifying them can clarify a joke, an expense or a character’s habits. The historical sections also address prohibition and the treatment of alcohol, a subject connected with the reputations of people Valancy meets.

Descriptions of nature appear both directly and through the books of John Foster, the author Valancy admires. The text distinguishes her reading from her later experience of the island and woods. The supporting material on Muskoka gives geographical context for those passages, while leaving readers able to compare the fictional places with the documented region.

Montgomery and this edition

Lucy Maud Montgomery was born in 1874 on Prince Edward Island. The success of Anne of Green Gables in 1908 established a career that continued through novels, short stories and poetry. Her journals and correspondence, alongside surviving manuscripts, provide substantial evidence for her working life.

This edition brings together biography, composition and publication history with chapter-level assistance. Its scope includes the people, places, laws and objects mentioned in the novel, as well as critical perspectives on its reception. Readers can use the summaries to relocate an episode, consult a footnote for a particular reference, or read the longer essays alongside the complete text.
