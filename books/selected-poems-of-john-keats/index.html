Keats’s last book

Taylor and Hessey announced Lamia, Isabella, The Eve of St. Agnes, and Other Poems on 26 June 1820, and its publication is usually dated 1 July. It was the third and last book of poems Keats published, and it printed “To Autumn”, “Ode to Psyche”, “Ode on Melancholy” and “Hyperion” for the first time.

The publishers put an Advertisement at the front, saying that “Hyperion” had been left unfinished because the reception of Endymion had discouraged its author. Keats had not written it, and in one copy he crossed it out and wrote “This is a lie”.

By then he was seriously ill with tuberculosis. He sailed for Italy with Joseph Severn in September 1820 and died in Rome on 23 February 1821.
