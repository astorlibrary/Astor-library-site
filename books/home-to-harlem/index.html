A best-seller and its critics

Claude McKay was born in Jamaica and was known as a poet, for Harlem Shadows among other books, when he wrote Home to Harlem, his first novel, during the years he spent in Europe and North Africa. Harper and Brothers published it in 1928, and it became a best-seller.

Not everyone welcomed it. W. E. B. Du Bois reviewed it in The Crisis in June 1928, alongside Nella Larsen’s Quicksand, and wrote that it “for the most part nauseates me, and after the dirtier parts of its filth I feel distinctly like taking a bath.” In 1929 McKay received the Harmon Foundation’s gold award for literature.
