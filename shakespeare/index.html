Julius Caesar

Caesar comes home from his victory over Pompey’s sons, and Cassius works on Brutus until he agrees to join the conspiracy. They stab Caesar in the Capitol on the Ides of March. At the funeral Antony’s speech sets the crowd against them, and the conspirators end up dead at Philippi.

Tragedy

Romeo and Juliet

The play first appeared in quarto in 1597 and again in 1599. Against the Montague–Capulet feud, Romeo and Juliet’s secret marriage is undone by Tybalt’s violence, Romeo’s banishment and Friar Lawrence’s failed message. Mercutio gives the tragedy another of its defining voices, one later actors and film-makers have continually rediscovered.

History

Henry VI, Part 3

Printed in 1595 as The True Tragedie of Richard Duke of Yorke, the early text stands behind the later Folio play. Queen Margaret, York, Edward IV, Warwick and Richard carry the Wars of the Roses through Towton and Tewkesbury, pressing the story onwards towards Richard III.

Comedy

Much Ado About Nothing

Printed in quarto in 1600 and in the First Folio in 1623, the comedy sets two courtships against one another in Messina. Beatrice and Benedick talk their way towards love; Hero and Claudio are nearly destroyed by overhearing, false evidence and public shame. Dogberry’s watch uncovers what the court misses, a contrast that stage and screen versions keep finding afresh.

Comedy

The Comedy of Errors

Our first records are a Gray’s Inn performance and the text in the First Folio; behind them lie sources in Plautus. In Ephesus two sets of twins are mistaken for each other, and ordinary troubles with debt and marriage start to look like witchcraft. Recognition finally restores order, though later musical versions have found more room for the joke.

Tragedy

Titus Andronicus

The 1594 quarto and Henslowe’s Rose record bring us close to a Roman revenge tragedy of sacrifice, mutilation and banquet horror. Lavinia, Tamora and Aaron stand at the centre of its violence. The extremity of their stories has also shaped the authorship debate and the play’s difficult return to the modern stage.

Comedy

A Midsummer Night's Dream

The play survives in a 1600 quarto and the 1623 Folio, drawing on Ovid, Chaucer and the Robin Goodfellow tradition. Its Athens, quarrelling fairies and mechanicals have inspired music by Mendelssohn and Britten, Peter Brook’s 1970 RSC production and many screen versions.

Late romance

The Tempest

First printed in the 1623 First Folio, the play draws on reports of the Sea Venture and Bermuda, as well as Montaigne. On Prospero’s island, Miranda, Ariel and Caliban make power inseparable from masque and music. Colonial readings and later screen versions continue to test who controls the island and its story.

Comedy

The Taming of the Shrew

The First Folio text sits beside the related A Shrew and stories by Gascoigne and Ariosto. From Christopher Sly’s strange induction to the marriage bargaining in Padua, Katherina, Petruchio and Bianca make this an uneasy comedy. Its history on stage and screen is also a history of argument.

History

Richard II

This history, written entirely in verse, survives first in a 1597 quarto. Its sources in Holinshed and the later textual problem around the deposition scene open a deeply political play, in which Gaunt and Bolingbroke challenge Richard’s belief in divine kingship. The Essex performance record and later productions show why that conflict mattered.

Comedy

The Merry Wives of Windsor

The 1602 quarto and 1623 Folio bring Falstaff into the domestic world of Mistress Ford and Mistress Page, where each attempt at deception meets a sharper counter-plot. Ford’s jealousy and Anne Page’s suitors add to the confusion, while the buck-basket, the Brentford disguise and Herne’s Oak give it unforgettable comic shapes.

Henry VI, Part 2

Published in 1594 as The First Part of the Contention, the play follows Gloucester, Suffolk and Queen Margaret while York waits and builds his claim. Jack Cade’s rebellion and the battle of St Albans take the rivalry into civil war. Later performance records show how the play returned to the stage.

Henry IV, Part 1

The 1598 quarto divides Holinshed’s history into three worlds: the court, the rebels and Eastcheap. Henry IV, Prince Hal, Falstaff and Hotspur each offer a different idea of honour on the road to Shrewsbury. Screen versions have repeatedly returned to the tension between them.

Tragedy

Othello

A Whitehall performance is recorded in 1604; the play then survives in a 1622 quarto and the 1623 First Folio, alongside Shakespeare’s source in Cinthio. As the action moves from Venice to Cyprus, Iago’s plot closes around Othello, Desdemona and the handkerchief. Screen versions reveal how differently that narrowing tragedy can be seen.

Tragedy

Hamlet

Q1, Q2 and the First Folio preserve substantially different early texts of Hamlet. Behind Elsinore lie the Amleth tradition and the conventions of revenge tragedy; at its centre are Hamlet and Ophelia, both changed by the demands placed upon them. Its stage and screen history shows that performance never leaves the play quite as it found it.

Tragedy

King Lear

The 1608 quarto and 1623 First Folio preserve two distinct early texts of Lear’s division of the kingdom. Cordelia’s story is shadowed by that of Gloucester and Edgar, binding the two families’ suffering together. The play’s sources, Tate’s adaptation, RSC records and screen versions show how radically its ending and emphasis have changed.

History

Richard III

The 1597 quarto and 1623 First Folio draw on More and Holinshed to shape Richard’s rise and fall. Clarence, Lady Anne, the princes and Buckingham mark stages on his way to Bosworth. Cibber’s adaptation, RSC records and screen versions reveal how later performers have remade that journey.

Dark comedy

All's Well That Ends Well

First printed in the 1623 First Folio, the play draws on stories by Boccaccio and Painter. Helena’s pursuit of Bertram, with Parolles nearby, makes an uneasy comedy of rank, merit and the bed trick. Its unsettled ending explains both the problem-play label and the questions visible in its RSC performance history.

Tragedy

Macbeth

The play’s print and source history lead directly to its arguments about ambition, kingship, prophecy and guilt. Its violence and supernatural atmosphere have never belonged to one period alone: stage productions, opera, screen versions and later responses show every age remaking them.

Comedy

As You Like It

Banished by her uncle, the usurping Duke, Rosalind runs away to the Forest of Arden dressed as a young man, Ganymede, with her cousin Celia and the court fool Touchstone. Orlando is in the forest too, hanging bad love poems on the trees. Still in disguise, she offers to cure him of loving Rosalind by letting him practise his wooing on her.

Tragedy

Antony and Cleopatra

Mark Antony rules the eastern third of the Roman world and spends his time in Alexandria with Cleopatra. Called back to Rome, he marries Octavius Caesar’s sister to patch up the peace, then returns to Egypt. It ends with the sea battle at Actium, a last defeat at Alexandria and two suicides.

Tragedy

Coriolanus

Caius Marcius takes the town of Corioli for Rome almost single-handed and is given its name. To be made consul he has to show his wounds to the citizens and ask for their votes, and he cannot bring himself to do it. Banished, he joins the Volscians, his old enemies, and marches on Rome, and only his mother, Volumnia, can turn him back.

Late romance

Cymbeline

Imogen’s secret marriage, Iachimo’s wager, two lost princes and Britain’s threatened war with Rome meet in a play of disguise and false evidence. First printed in the 1623 Folio as a tragedy, it turns towards recognition and repair without forgetting the damage jealousy has done.

Henry IV, Part 2

Rumour, sickness and age change the world after Shrewsbury. Rebellion ends at Gaultree, the dying king reconciles with Hal, and Falstaff waits for the new reign. The 1600 quartos and 1623 Folio lead towards the coronation scene where private friendship meets public rule.

Henry IV, Parts 1 and 2

Read Hal’s movement from Eastcheap and Shrewsbury to the crown as one continuous arc. Hotspur, Falstaff, Henry IV and the Chief Justice give honour, rebellion, friendship and lawful office competing voices before Henry V’s first public decisions change the whole company.

Henry VI, Part 1

After Henry V’s death, Joan leads the French recovery while Talbot embodies an English heroism undermined by faction. The Temple Garden roses point towards civil war. First printed in the 1623 Folio, the play also opens an important modern discussion about collaborative authorship.

History

Henry V

Henry V claims the French crown, takes Harfleur and, with a sick and outnumbered army, beats the French at Agincourt on St Crispin’s Day, 1415. On the way he has his old drinking companion Bardolph hanged for robbing a church, and during the battle he orders the French prisoners killed.

Henry VI, Parts 1, 2 and 3

All three parts of Henry VI, the plays that made Shakespeare’s name in the early 1590s. They cover nearly fifty years, from Henry V’s funeral in 1422 to the murder of Henry VI in the Tower in 1471: the loss of France, and the Wars of the Roses as far as Tewkesbury.

History

Henry VIII

Shakespeare’s last history play, written with John Fletcher in 1613. It follows the falls of the Duke of Buckingham, Queen Katherine and Cardinal Wolsey, and ends with the christening of the baby Elizabeth. On 29 June 1613 a cannon fired during a performance set fire to the Globe’s thatch, and the theatre burned down.

History

King John

King John holds the English throne, and the King of France backs the claim of John’s young nephew, Arthur. A marriage patches up a peace, the Pope’s legate breaks it, and Arthur is captured. John orders Hubert to kill the boy, while Philip the Bastard, Richard the Lionheart’s illegitimate son, comments on everyone’s motives.

Comedy

Love’s Labour’s Lost

The King of Navarre and three of his lords swear to study for three years without seeing women. The Princess of France arrives on business the next day, with three ladies. The men write sonnets in secret, send them to the wrong people and visit the ladies disguised as Russians, and at the end the women give them a year to prove they mean it.

Comedy / problem play

Measure for Measure

The Duke of Vienna hands power to his deputy, Angelo, and stays in the city disguised as a friar. Angelo revives an old law and sentences Claudio to death for getting his fiancée pregnant. When Claudio’s sister Isabella, about to become a nun, pleads for him, Angelo offers to spare him if she will sleep with him.

Comedy

The Merchant of Venice

Antonio borrows three thousand ducats from Shylock so that his friend Bassanio can court Portia, and signs a bond: a pound of his flesh if he cannot repay in three months. His ships are lost. At the trial Portia, disguised as a young lawyer, saves him, and Shylock loses his money and is ordered to become a Christian.

Romance

Pericles, Prince of Tyre

Pericles solves a riddle that exposes King Antiochus’s incest with his daughter, and has to run for his life. In the years that follow he is shipwrecked, marries, loses his wife at sea in childbirth and leaves his baby daughter, Marina, to be brought up in Tarsus. The medieval poet John Gower tells the story as Chorus.

Late romance

The Winter's Tale

Leontes, King of Sicilia, decides without evidence that his wife Hermione has slept with his oldest friend, puts her on trial, and orders their newborn daughter abandoned. His son dies and Hermione is reported dead. Sixteen years pass, a bear chases a courtier offstage, and the play moves to a sheep-shearing feast in Bohemia.

Tragedy / dark satire

Timon of Athens

Timon of Athens gives money, feasts and jewels to anyone who flatters him, until the money runs out. When he asks his friends for help, every one of them has an excuse. He invites them to a last banquet, serves them warm water, and goes to live in a cave outside the city, where he digs up gold.

Late romance / tragicomedy

The Two Noble Kinsmen

Palamon and Arcite, cousins and prisoners of Theseus, see Emilia from their prison window and fall out over her on the spot. Theseus rules that they will fight for her, and the loser will die. Shakespeare wrote the play with John Fletcher in about 1613; it was printed in 1634 and was not in the Folio.

Troilus and Cressida

Seven years into the Trojan War, the Trojan prince Troilus spends one night with Cressida, and the next morning she is handed over to the Greeks in exchange for a prisoner. In the Greek camp, Achilles sulks in his tent and Thersites insults everyone.

Comedy

Twelfth Night

Shipwrecked on the coast of Illyria, and believing her twin brother drowned, Viola dresses as a young man and goes to work for Duke Orsino, who sends her to woo the Countess Olivia for him. Valentine goes to the Duke’s court in Milan and falls in love with the Duke’s daughter, Silvia. Shakespeare in print

Two poems that made his name in print.

Venus and Adonis and The Rape of Lucrece were among Shakespeare’s first named appearances in print. Read beside the plays, they open a different scale of voice, argument and classical story.

Narrative poem

The Rape of Lucrece

Collatine boasts in camp about his wife’s virtue, and Tarquin, the king’s son, rides to her house, is received as a guest, and rapes her in the night. Most of Shakespeare’s poem of 1594 comes after that: Lucrece’s long argument with herself, her letter to her husband, and her death, which leads to the expulsion of the kings from Rome.

Open pageBuy / view the Astor edition

Narrative poem

Venus and Adonis

Venus pulls the young Adonis off his horse and spends a summer’s day trying to make him love her. He wants to go hunting, and the next morning a boar kills him. It was the first work Shakespeare published, in 1593, and it was reprinted more often in his lifetime than anything else he wrote.

Two narrative poems · 1593–94

The Rape of Lucrece and Venus and Adonis

Shakespeare’s two long poems, written while plague had closed the London theatres in 1592–94 and both dedicated to the young Earl of Southampton. In Venus and Adonis the goddess tries to seduce a boy who only wants to go hunting; in Lucrece a Roman wife is raped by the king’s son. Both are here complete, with notes on the same page.
