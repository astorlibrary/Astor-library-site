Shakespeare Apocrypha

Eight specialist annotated editions of plays historically attributed to, associated with or connected to Shakespeare and his theatrical world, arranged in numerical order.

Complete collection

Eight volumes in numerical order.

Every volume has its own book page and remains searchable in the complete Astor catalogue.

Volume I

Edward III

A specialist annotated edition of the anonymous 1596 history play now generally understood to contain a substantial contribution by Shakespeare, although the identity of his collaborator remains unsettled.

Volume II

Arden of Faversham

An annotated edition of the 1592 domestic tragedy based on a Kent murder of 1551, with the competing cases for Shakespeare, Thomas Kyd and unresolved collaboration presented without false certainty.

Open the edition pageBuy / view

Volume III

Locrine

A complete annotated text of the 1595 legendary British tragedy, whose title-page initials W. S. later drew it into Shakespeare’s apocryphal canon despite the absence of a secure author.

Open the edition pageBuy / view

Volume IV

Sir Thomas More

The complete surviving collaborative play and additions, including the three manuscript pages known as Hand D and widely accepted as Shakespeare’s only surviving dramatic writing in his own hand.

Open the edition pageBuy / view

Volume V

Thomas Lord Cromwell

A complete annotated edition of the chronicle play printed in 1602 as written by W. S., following Cromwell from a Putney blacksmith’s house to power under Henry VIII and execution by attainder.

Open the edition pageBuy / view

Volume VI

The London Prodigal

The complete 1605 city comedy about debt, false inheritance, marriage and reform, printed under Shakespeare’s name but not accepted as his work.

Open the edition pageBuy / view

Volume VII

The Puritan

A complete modern-spelling edition of the fast Jacobean city comedy printed as written by W. S. but now generally attributed to Thomas Middleton.

Open the edition pageBuy / view

Volume VIII

A Yorkshire Tragedy

A concentrated ten-scene domestic tragedy based on Walter Calverley’s 1605 attack on his family, printed under Shakespeare’s name but now most strongly attributed to Thomas Middleton.
