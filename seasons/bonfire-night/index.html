Your Bonfire Night reading list

A Victorian Bonfire Night is the centre of this small, focused collection. It brings together nineteenth-century fiction, essays, folklore and reporting with original historical chapters on the Plot, observance, bonfire societies, food, fireworks and policing.

The accompanying Macbeth material offers a separate route into early Stuart concerns about kingship, treason and equivocation. It is contextual reading for the period of the Gunpowder Plot; the play’s action is set in medieval Scotland.

The Astor reading note

From the Plot to the annual observance

The Gunpowder Plot attempted to destroy Parliament and kill James I at the opening planned for 5 November 1605. Its discovery was followed by an annual religious commemoration established by Parliament in 1606. UK Parliament’s historical account records the services and sermons that made the date a recurring public observance.

The Astor anthology follows later changes rather than treating the Victorian celebration as a simple continuation of 1605. It covers the repeal of the statutory observance in 1859, local bonfire societies, commercial fireworks, fuel gathering, disturbances and seasonal food. The historical record also includes anti-Catholic hostility, which needs to be understood as part of the festival’s development.
