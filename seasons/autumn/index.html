Your Autumn reading list

Autumn’s shelf begins with stories that suit an evening’s reading: Irving’s Headless Horseman, American and Victorian ghost fiction, and the investigations of Carnacki. Longer supernatural tales take the reader from the Danube to a remote Irish house.

The selection brings together American Classics, Victorian writing and Modern Classics. The Halloween collection explains how older stories acquired their October associations; A Victorian Bonfire Night follows the changing customs of the Fifth of November. Both give the seasonal reading a documented history.

The Astor reading note

A practical autumn reading sequence

For a short first session, use the Sleepy Hollow close reading alongside Irving’s story. Follow it with one tale from American Ghost Stories or M. R. James. The collections allow several kinds of supernatural writing to be compared without requiring a full novel each week.

Move to A Victorian Bonfire Night around 5 November, or use it at any time as a source-based history of the festival. Its regional examples, newspaper material and accounts of food, fireworks and policing explain why the holiday was never observed in one uniform way.
