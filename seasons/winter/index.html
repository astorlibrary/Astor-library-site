Your Winter reading list

These books make winter weather, northern travel or the winter household part of their subject. Ethan Frome places its central story in snowbound New England; Jack London’s novels follow working animals through the northern gold-rush world; Frankenstein begins and ends within an Arctic expedition.

A second shelf turns towards rooms, firesides and remembered homes. Grahame’s Wild Wood and Mole’s return, Coleridge’s Frost at Midnight, and the opening winter tale within Shakespeare’s late play offer shorter routes through the collection.

The Astor reading note

Follow the season in the text

For a winter reading group, compare what cold weather requires people or animals to do. Transport, food, shelter, work and money are concrete concerns in Ethan Frome and the London novels. Frankenstein uses the Arctic expedition to frame another narrator’s history and to place Walton’s ambition beside Victor’s.

The Winter’s Tale should be read with its changing seasons in view. The play moves from the danger surrounding Leontes’s household to the pastoral scenes after a sixteen-year interval. Its title does not mean the whole action occurs in winter; the contrast between winter storytelling and the later festival is part of the reading.
