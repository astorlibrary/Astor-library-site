Your Love & Valentine’s reading list

This shelf brings together poems, plays and novels in which courtship and intimate relationships are central. Shakespeare’s sonnets provide short individual readings; the comedies offer mistaken identities, overheard conversations and disputed expectations; the novels give relationships their social and economic setting.

The books do not all promise an uncomplicated romance. The sections distinguish comic courtship from works involving loss, coercion or disappointment, so that a gift, reading-group choice or study session can begin with an informed selection.

The Astor reading note

Read the relationship in its setting

A Valentine’s reading can be brief without becoming vague. Sonnet 130 is a useful starting point for examining what a love poem actually says about conventional praise. In Much Ado About Nothing, the overhearing scenes show how a character changes when presented with an account of another person’s feelings.

For the novels and later drama, follow the practical conditions of a relationship: income, inheritance, marriage law, work and the authority of relatives. The Astor book pages identify the particular historical and editorial material supplied with each edition.
