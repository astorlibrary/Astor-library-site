Your Fourth of July reading list

A Fourth of July reading list can hold the celebration of independence alongside the American writers who examined the meaning and limits of freedom. Frederick Douglass’s Narrative is the starting point here, followed by nineteenth-century abolitionist fiction and Twain’s accounts of slavery, identity and moral judgement.

The wider shelf includes American communities, the Civil War, maritime labour and the later pursuit of wealth. These are books for reading around the national anniversary; each retains its own period, subject and publication history.

The Astor reading note

Douglass and the national anniversary

On 5 July 1852, Frederick Douglass addressed an antislavery gathering in Rochester, New York. The speech now commonly known as What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July? examined the contradiction between national celebrations of liberty and the continued enslavement of people in the United States. The Library of Congress provides a historical account and links to contemporary newspaper evidence.

The Astor volume on this shelf is Douglass’s Narrative, first published in 1845, rather than an edition of that later speech. Read it for his account of enslavement, literacy, resistance and abolitionist testimony. The dated distinction matters when using the two works together in teaching or a reading group.
