Your Spring reading list

Spring in these books is usually a particular day. The Mole abandons his spring-cleaning for the river bank; Wordsworth writes to his sister on the first mild day of March; Anne of Green Gables lives through a reluctant Canadian spring; Tess sets out for Talbothays on a thyme-scented May morning; Elizabeth Bennet is at Hunsford, a short walk from Rosings, over Easter.

The poems and plays use the season as a convention to play with. Shakespeare ends Love’s Labour’s Lost with a song for spring and another for winter; Blake’s ‘Spring’ belongs to Songs of Innocence; Milton’s Eden has a perpetual spring that the Fall brings to an end. The pages below gather the editions with the guides and close readings that go with them.

The season in the novels

Grahame opens with the Mole’s spring-cleaning, Montgomery gives a chapter to the Canadian spring, Hardy sends Tess to the dairy in May and Austen brings Darcy to Rosings for Easter. Werther’s first letter is dated 4 May.

The Astor reading note

Two springs side by side

Read Wordsworth’s ‘Lines Written in Early Spring’ beside Blake’s ‘Spring’. Wordsworth sits in a grove and ends with a complaint about what man has made of man; Blake gives it to a child and a lamb and leaves the argument out.

For a longer route, follow the Mole out of his burrow in the first chapter of The Wind in the Willows, then read the Talbothays chapters of Tess, where the spring and summer at the dairy are the happiest stretch of her life.
