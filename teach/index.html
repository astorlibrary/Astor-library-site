Astor Teaching Rooms

The teacher’s desk

A place to open a book, choose the time you have and teach from the words on the page. Each room brings together a short task, a complete lesson, a sequence of lessons and close readings that students can use for themselves.

Free to use

No account, download or prepared slideshow is needed. Put the passage on screen, print the student prompt or work straight from the page.

Start with the writing

These are not collections of activities waiting to be attached to a text. The lesson begins with a precise question about a scene, line or choice of language. Context is introduced when it changes what can be seen in that evidence. A student leaves with something made: an annotation, an argument, a spoken interpretation or the first part of an essay.

Open a room

The first room is complete and ready for teaching. Further rooms will be added as the library grows; the structure will remain familiar, so teachers can move between books without learning a new system each time.

Room 01 · William Shakespeare

Macbeth

Teach prophecy and choice, Lady Macbeth’s invocation, the dagger, equivocation and the collapse of time. The room contains a 15-minute close-reading task, a 50-minute lesson, a three-lesson sequence, six turning points, student-ready questions and an evidence ladder for essays.

Inside every room

Three scales of teaching

Use one passage when time is short. Take a full lesson when a class needs to slow down. Follow the longer sequence when students need to see how an argument changes across a whole work. Teacher notes and student wording are kept separate, so the page can be used at the desk and at the front of the room.
