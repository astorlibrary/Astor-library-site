Period

Modern.

Books from the turn of the twentieth century onwards: Kafka’s The Metamorphosis, Woolf’s Mrs Dalloway and A Room of One’s Own, Chesterton, Yeats, Mansfield and Orwell’s Animal Farm, with ghost stories by M. R. James and Algernon Blackwood and children’s classics from The Railway Children to The Wind in the Willows.

Paperback · L. M. Montgomery

The Blue Castle

Valancy Stirling is twenty-nine, unmarried and ordered about by her family in the Ontario town of Deerwood. Then a doctor’s letter tells her she has a year to live, and she stops doing what she is told. Unusually for L. M. Montgomery, the novel is set in Ontario, not Prince Edward Island. It came out in 1926.

Paperback · William Hope Hodgson

Carnacki, the Ghost-Finder

Carnacki, a Chelsea bachelor with a camera and an ‘electric pentacle’, invites four friends to dinner and then tells them about his latest case. William Hope Hodgson published six of these stories in magazines in 1910 and 1912, and this edition prints them in that order, from the magazine texts.

Paperback · M. R. James

M. R. James: Collected Ghost Stories

M. R. James, Provost of King’s College, Cambridge, and later of Eton, wrote many of his ghost stories to read aloud to friends at Christmas. All thirty-three that he finished are here, from the four original volumes, the 1931 collection and three later stories, with seven pieces James wrote about the ghost story itself.

Paperback · T. E. Lawrence

Seven Pillars of Wisdom

T. E. Lawrence lost most of the first draft of this book at Reading station in 1919 and rewrote it from memory. Astor prints the 1926 text he had privately printed for about two hundred subscribers, and separates his own account of the Arab Revolt from what the documents record.

Paperback · William Hope Hodgson

The House on the Borderland

Two Englishmen on a fishing holiday in the west of Ireland find a ruined house on the edge of a chasm and, in the rubble, a damaged manuscript. Its writer describes pig-faced creatures climbing out of the pit, and a vision in which he watches the sun die. William Hope Hodgson published the novel in 1908.

Paperback · E. Nesbit

The Railway Children

When their father is taken away by two men one night, Roberta, Peter and Phyllis move with their mother to a cottage in the country beside a railway line, and start waving to the 9.15. Nobody tells them for a long time that he is in prison. E. Nesbit’s novel was serialised in 1905 and came out as a book in 1906.

Paperback · Algernon Blackwood

The Wendigo

A moose-hunting party goes into the backwoods north of Rat Portage, in Canada, and the guide, Défago, is called away in the night by something that shouts his name. Algernon Blackwood published the story in 1910. Astor’s text is set from the Project Gutenberg edition and has not been checked against the 1910 printing.

Paperback · Algernon Blackwood

The Willows

Two men canoeing down the Danube in flood tie up for the night on a sandy island among the willows below Pressburg, and cannot leave. Algernon Blackwood had made the same journey by canoe in the summer of 1900. This is the original British text of 1907, printed without footnotes.

1908

The Man Who Was Thursday, G. K. Chesterton

First published in 1908, Chesterton’s novel follows Gabriel Syme from Saffron Park into undercover policing, anarchist disguise and the Council of Days. What begins like a detective story becomes a nightmare allegory shaped by Edwardian politics and the elusive Sunday, with a later life on radio and screen.

1945

Animal Farm, George Orwell

Orwell turns the history of the Russian Revolution and Stalinism into a short, unsettling allegory. Around Boxer and Squealer, propaganda and rewritten commandments slowly alter life on the farm. The story was later retold on stage and screen. Sales availability varies by country because copyright terms differ.

Novel · 1925

Mrs Dalloway

On a Wednesday in June 1923, Clarissa Dalloway walks through Westminster to buy flowers for the party she is giving that evening. In Regent’s Park, Septimus Warren Smith, who fought in the war and hears the dead talking, waits with his Italian wife for an appointment with a Harley Street doctor. The two never meet.

Poetry collection · 1921

Michael Robartes and the Dancer

Fifteen poems by Yeats, printed by the Cuala Press, which his sisters ran, in a small edition dated 1920. ‘Easter, 1916’ and ‘The Second Coming’ are among them. This edition follows the Cuala text and puts the changes Yeats made later in the notes.

Novel · 1908

Anne of Green Gables

Matthew and Marilla Cuthbert, a brother and sister who have never married, send for a boy from an orphanage to help on their Prince Edward Island farm. They get Anne Shirley, eleven, red-haired and unable to stop talking. It was L. M. Montgomery’s first novel, published in 1908.

Essay · 1929

A Room of One’s Own

‘A woman must have money and a room of her own if she is to write fiction.’ Woolf first made the argument in two lectures at Newnham and Girton in October 1928 and published it the following year. On the way she gives Shakespeare a gifted sister, Judith, and asks what would have become of her.

Novel · 1908

The Wind in the Willows

Mole gives up his spring-cleaning, meets the Water Rat on the riverbank and goes for a row. Before long they are trying to save Toad from his passion for motor cars. Kenneth Grahame, Secretary of the Bank of England, began the stories as bedtime tales for his son, and the book came out in 1908.

Novella · 1915

The Metamorphosis

Gregor Samsa, a travelling salesman, wakes one morning changed into a huge insect, and his first worry is that he has missed his train. Kafka’s story was published in 1915. Astor uses Ian Johnston’s translation, which calls the creature a ‘monstrous verminous bug’.

Stories and fragments · 1923

The Doves’ Nest and Other Stories

Katherine Mansfield died in January 1923, and John Middleton Murry published this collection later that year. Six of the stories are finished and four are pieces she left unfinished; the notes say which is which and what state each one was in.
