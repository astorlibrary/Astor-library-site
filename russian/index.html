The Brothers Karamazov

First published serially between 1879 and 1880, The Brothers Karamazov centres on the murder of Fyodor Pavlovitch Karamazov and the lives of his sons Dmitri, Ivan and Alyosha.

The Death of Ivan Ilyich

First published in 1886, The Death of Ivan Ilyich follows a successful Russian official whose apparently ordinary life is overturned by illness and the knowledge of his approaching death.

Paperback · Fyodor Dostoevsky

White Nights

Over four nights on a Petersburg canal embankment, a lonely young dreamer falls in love with Nastenka, who is waiting for another man. Dostoevsky published this short sentimental novel in 1848, four months before his arrest, and revised it in 1860; Constance Garnett’s translation follows the revised text.

Paperback · Nikolai Gogol

The Overcoat

Akaki Akakievitch, a poor copying clerk in St Petersburg, goes without supper for months to pay for a new overcoat, wears it for one day and has it stolen on the way home. Gogol’s story of 1842, in Claud Field’s translation, started a whole line of Russian fiction about poor clerks.

Paperback · Fyodor Dostoevsky

Notes from Underground

“I am a sick man.... I am a spiteful man.” A retired official, writing notes he says nobody will read, argues that people will always choose freedom over their own good, and then tells how at twenty-four he humiliated a young woman called Liza. Dostoevsky’s short novel of 1864, in Constance Garnett’s translation.
