Guide contents

What the guide covers.

The complete guide has 14 slides. It covers:

  1. 01

    Publication in Putnam’s Monthly Magazine in 1853 and in The Piazza Tales in 1856.

  2. 02

    Where Melville was in 1853, and The Lawyer’s Story as a likely source.

  3. 03

    The story in brief, the people in the office and the work of a scrivener.

  4. 04

    The story’s references to law, money, places, books and scripture, and its afterlife in film and philosophy.

Reading notes

Reading the text and its history.

Use these questions alongside the 14 illustrated slides and the work itself.

Work

Find the first refusal

Bartleby first refuses to help examine copies. Use the slide on copying to explain what he was asked to do and why it mattered in a law office.

Narrator

Read the lawyer

Use the slides on Chancery and John Jacob Astor to read the narrator’s account of himself. What does he want the reader to think of him?

Allusion

Follow the references

Find Edwards and Priestley, Petra and the verse from Job in the story. What does each add to the narrator’s attempt to explain Bartleby?

How to use this guide. “Bartleby” first appeared anonymously, in two instalments. Notice where the first instalment might have ended.
