What the guide is for.

A period guide for readers of Jack London: the strike on Rabbit Creek, the stampede over the passes, Dawson City, London’s own winter in the Yukon and the fiction he wrote from it. Thirteen slides.

Read The Call of the Wild or “To Build a Fire” with the guide, and match London’s trails, rivers and camps with the history of the rush.

Guide contents

What the guide covers.

The complete guide has 13 slides. It covers:

  1. 01

    The strike of August 1896, the news of July 1897 and the numbers who set out.

  2. 02

    The Chilkoot and White passes, and the year’s supplies each stampeder carried.

  3. 03

    Dawson City, the end of the rush and London’s winter near the Stewart River.

  4. 04

    London’s Klondike fiction, the words of the trail and other writers of the North.

Reading notes

Reading the text and its history.

Use these questions alongside the 13 illustrated slides and the work itself.

Experience

Follow London’s route

Trace London from San Francisco over the Chilkoot Pass to the Stewart River. Which parts of the journey reappear in The Call of the Wild?

Numbers

Read the figures

Use the table of numbers to compare those who set out with those who found gold. How does London’s fiction treat the many who failed?

Language

Learn the trail’s words

Find cheechako, sourdough and the Outside in London’s stories, and use the guide to read each as the miners used it.

How to use this guide. London found no gold, lost four front teeth to scurvy and left the Yukon in 1898. His Klondike fiction came afterwards, from what he had seen and heard.
