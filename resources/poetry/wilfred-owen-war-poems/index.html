Guide contents

What the guide covers.

The complete guide has 14 slides. It covers:

  1. 01

    The 1920 volume, its contents and the unfinished Preface.

  2. 02

    The five poems printed in Owen’s lifetime, and his life from 1893 to 1918.

  3. 03

    Craiglockhart, Sassoon, and the sources and allusions in the poems.

  4. 04

    Pararhyme, words from the trenches, later editions and the poems’ afterlife.

Reading notes

Reading the text and its history.

Use these questions alongside the 14 illustrated slides and the work itself.

Preface

Read the Preface first

Read “My subject is War, and the pity of War” with the guide, then ask how far a single poem bears it out.

Sources

Find the allusions

Use the slide on sources to read Horace in “Dulce et Decorum Est” and Genesis in “Parable of the Old Men and the Young”.

Sound

Hear the pararhyme

Find pararhymes such as escaped and scooped in “Strange Meeting”, and describe what the changed vowel does to each pair.

How to use this guide. Only five of Owen’s poems were printed while he was alive. Most reached readers after his death, in the edition Edith Sitwell and Siegfried Sassoon prepared in 1920.
