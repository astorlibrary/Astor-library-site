What this guide is for.

Read Richardson’s epistolary novel through fear, hesitation, class, gender and the heroine’s contradictory inner life.

Keep the form of the writing in view. Letters, narrators and claims to truth are part of the argument, not neutral containers for the story.

Before you open it

Three ways into the guide.

The guide is there to support the reading. These notes give you a place to begin and a reason to return to the words on the page.

Epistolary form

Feeling has no safe distance

Letters place the reader close to uncertainty. Pamela repeats herself, revises a judgement and records danger before she can know how an episode will end.

Reception

Readers have always disagreed

The early debate over sincerity, calculation and social ambition is not an obstacle around the novel. It is evidence of how urgently the book unsettled assumptions about virtue and class.

Power

Contradiction needs context

Pamela’s feelings cannot be separated from dependence, confinement and unequal authority. A psychological reading becomes stronger when it measures the conditions under which a choice is made.

A useful way in. Avoid forcing Pamela into a choice between perfect sincerity and complete calculation. Follow when her language changes and what has made another response possible.
