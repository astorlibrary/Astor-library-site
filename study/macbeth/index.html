Shakespeare study

Macbeth, William Shakespeare

This Astor Study Edition presents the complete play with scene summaries that keep cause and consequence connected. Line numbering supports accurate reference; explanatory notes clarify compressed grammar, unusual words, mythological allusion and the military, political and religious language that enters the action.

Longer sections distinguish the historical Macbeth from Shakespeare’s figure, follow the transformation of Holinshed’s chronicle material and place the play in the reign of James VI and I. Equivocation is treated as a precise way in which words can be literally defensible yet designed to mislead, not as a loose synonym for lying.

The study material tracks images across scenes rather than collecting quotations under a theme. Blood first appears as battle honour, then as proof of murder, imagined stain and a nation’s wound. Sleep moves from natural restoration to violated innocence and finally to Lady Macbeth’s restless return to the crime. Performance notes keep the dagger, Banquo’s ghost and the witches open to different staging without pretending every choice means the same thing.

Character work leads into close reading, discussion and essay planning. The central question is not whether Macbeth is ambitious. It is how the play makes us watch him recognise alternatives, choose among them and then use prophecy to protect himself from the meaning of his own choices.

Plot and structure

Five acts, from prophecy to Macbeth’s defeat.

The summary identifies how Macbeth and other characters interpret each prophecy and act on it.

Act I · Imagination

A title arrives; murder follows in thought

The witches greet Macbeth as Thane of Cawdor and future king. The first title is confirmed almost at once. Duncan names Malcolm his heir and plans to stay at Inverness. Lady Macbeth calls on spirits to remake her body for cruelty; Macbeth lists the reasons not to kill his guest, king and kinsman, then accepts the plan.

Act II · Deed

The imagined weapon becomes real blood

A dagger appears to direct Macbeth towards Duncan, but along “the way that I was going”. After the murder, Macbeth cannot return the daggers or say “Amen”. Lady Macbeth restores the props of their false story. Knocking brings the public world to the gate, and Duncan’s sons flee into suspicion.

Act III · Rule

A stolen crown creates its own enemies

Macbeth hires murderers without telling his wife. Banquo is killed but Fleance escapes, preserving the prophecy Macbeth fears. At the feast, Banquo’s ghost occupies Macbeth’s seat and turns private terror into a public failure of kingship. Macbeth decides to seek the witches again and act before thought can intervene.

Act IV · False safety

Warnings are converted into guarantees

The apparitions tell Macbeth to beware Macduff, then give him conditions he treats as impossible: harm from one not born of woman and defeat when Birnam Wood moves. He responds by ordering the slaughter of Macduff’s household. In England, Malcolm tests Macduff before joining grief to political action.

Act V · Unmaking

The body and the promises cease to obey

Lady Macbeth sleepwalks through fragments of the past and later dies. Malcolm’s soldiers carry branches from Birnam Wood; Macduff reveals the sense hidden in “none of woman born”. Macbeth sees equivocation too late. His final resistance retains courage but cannot restore the meaning his rule destroyed.

Critical reading

Analysis of three major speeches.

Each page contains the extract, five notes on specific phrases and a classroom or independent study task.

1.5 · Lady Macbeth

“Unsex me here”

The speech is built from commands: come, unsex, fill, make thick, stop, take, pall. Lady Macbeth treats body and conscience as passages that can be blocked or converted. Milk becomes gall; darkness is asked to hide the act from the knife, heaven and the speaker herself.

Do not reduce “unsex” to a wish to become male. The speech draws on gendered ideas of nurture and pity in order to reject whatever might stand between intention and act. Its pressure comes from the fact that she must ask other powers to perform that remaking.

2.1 · Macbeth

“Is this a dagger”

Macbeth begins with a question and quickly gives an order: “Come, let me clutch thee”. Sight insists on a weapon that touch cannot confirm. He can call it a “false creation” and still watch blood gather on its blade.

The most revealing line may be the least spectacular. The dagger “marshall’st me the way that I was going”. It seems to lead, but towards an action already planned. The vision gives Macbeth’s intention an object outside himself while the grammar returns responsibility to him.

5.5 · Macbeth

“Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow”

Lady Macbeth’s death produces no sustained recollection of a shared past. Instead, Macbeth imagines time creeping through repeated days towards “dusty death”. Life becomes candle, shadow, actor and tale: each figure briefly gives it a form, then removes substance, duration, audience or meaning.

The speech does not have to be Shakespeare’s verdict on existence. It belongs to a man whose future was once everything and whose efforts to command it have emptied his present. The moving wood is reported immediately afterwards, forcing metaphor back into military action.

Language across the play

Six patterns worth following from first use to last.

A motif matters when it changes. Record the speaker, situation and meaning each time rather than making a list of similar words.

Blood

The Captain’s blood belongs to honourable battle. Duncan’s blood becomes material evidence that must be transferred to the grooms. By Act III, Macbeth imagines himself wading through blood; in Act V, Lady Macbeth cannot stop working at an invisible spot. Ask when blood ceases to be something that can be washed away.

Sleep

Banquo resists “cursèd thoughts” in sleep, Duncan is murdered while sleeping and Macbeth believes he has murdered sleep itself. Lady Macbeth’s confident dismissal of the dead as pictures ends in sleepwalking. Natural rest becomes the condition in which suppressed knowledge speaks.

Clothing

New titles first feel like “strange garments”. Macbeth later asks for armour before it is needed, while Angus imagines his royal title hanging like a giant’s robe on a dwarfish thief. Clothing can confer identity, expose a bad fit or become protection from what cannot be faced.

Children

The play sets inheritance against childlessness without giving a simple domestic history. Banquo fathers a promised line of kings; Duncan names Malcolm; Lady Macbeth uses infant imagery in her attack on Macbeth; Macbeth orders the death of Fleance and Macduff’s children. The future he tries to own repeatedly takes the form of another person’s child.

Dark and sight

Macbeth asks stars to hide their fires; Lady Macbeth calls for thick night; Duncan is killed in darkness. Yet concealment produces visions: dagger, ghost and spots of blood. Track the difference between what characters hide from other people and what their own sight returns to them.

Equivocation

The witches speak in riddles before the word is named. The Porter imagines an equivocator who could swear in both scales. The apparitions offer truths arranged to invite false conclusions. Only when Birnam Wood moves does Macbeth identify the “equivocation of the fiend / That lies like truth”.

Characters

Principal relationships and dramatic functions.

The analysis covers prophecy, persuasion, suspicion, grief, testing and resistance in the relationships between major characters.

Macbeth

A successful soldier with an unusually active imagination. He sees moral and practical consequences before acting, which makes knowledge part of his responsibility. After becoming king, he shortens the distance between idea and violence until action is meant to occur before thought.

Lady Macbeth

She reads Macbeth closely, turns his language of desire into a plan and uses love and manhood to challenge refusal. Her early authority is real but not permanent. Macbeth excludes her from later murders, and the speech she once directed outward returns in sleep as broken recollection.

Banquo

He hears promises too and admits that they enter his dreams, but makes a different choice about allegiance. His suspicion threatens Macbeth; his descendants threaten Macbeth’s imagined future. As a ghost or guilty vision, he turns the royal banquet into an exposure of the king.

Macduff

His refusal to attend the coronation marks political distance early. Flight to England leaves his household unprotected, a decision the play allows Lady Macduff to criticise. Grief does not erase responsibility; it becomes part of the action against Macbeth and the hidden fulfilment of the apparition’s words.

Malcolm

Duncan’s named heir learns caution after his father’s murder. In 4.3 he tests Macduff by describing himself as unfit to rule, then reveals the test and the support gathered in England. His closing order promises measure and time after Macbeth’s frantic secrecy, though performance may question how complete that restoration is.

The Weird Sisters

They predict, vanish, stage apparitions and delight in double meanings. Their power is evident, but its limit is crucial: they do not command Duncan’s murder. Productions decide whether they look separate from the human world, emerge from it or resemble the forces Macbeth already carries within him.

Text, sources and first audiences

Relevant textual and historical context.

The section covers the First Folio text, Holinshed’s Chronicles, the reign of James VI and I, and early modern equivocation.

First Folio

A short play with no surviving quarto

The first printed Macbeth is the 1623 Folio text. Its brevity contributes to the play’s speed, though scholars also debate adaptation and the passages involving Hecate. Simon Forman’s 1611 description of a performance confirms that a recognisable version of the tragedy was on stage before print. Textual uncertainty should be named where it matters, not turned into a theory that every difficult line is corrupt.

Chronicle and king

Holinshed, Banquo and James VI and I

Shakespeare drew on Raphael Holinshed’s Chronicles, combining and altering material. The historical Duncan and Macbeth do not match their dramatic versions, and the action of years becomes a tightening sequence. Holinshed’s Banquo is implicated in Duncan’s death; Shakespeare makes him more honourable. That change matters beside the Stuart claim of descent from Banquo and the accession of James to the English throne in 1603.

Equivocation

Words weighed in the Gunpowder Plot period

After the 1605 Gunpowder Plot, the prosecution of the Jesuit Henry Garnet made “equivocation” a charged public term. The Porter’s imagined equivocator is often read against that moment. The play does more than insert a topical reference: from the witches’ opening oppositions to the apparitions, it repeatedly shows statements that can be true in wording and destructive in the conclusions they invite.

Study topics

Three topics for detailed analysis.

Each topic starts with textual evidence and develops an interpretation for discussion or essay writing.

Decision route

Find the alternative in every choice.

At 1.3, 1.7, 2.1, 3.1 and 4.1, write what Macbeth knows, what he fears, what options remain and what he does. Responsibility becomes clearer when the road not taken stays visible.

Marriage route

Track who completes whose sentence.

Follow address, interruption and shared planning from the letter in 1.5 through Duncan’s murder and the separate handling of Banquo. End by placing the sleepwalking scene beside Macbeth’s response to Lady Macbeth’s death.

Stage route

Decide what the audience can see.

Make one consistent production choice for the witches, dagger and Banquo’s ghost, then identify the moment where it no longer works. The failure is useful: the play may not allow one explanation to contain all its supernatural events.

Discussion and essays

Questions for analysis.

Answers should compare at least two scenes and explain the significance of changes in language, action or character.

01

When does possibility become intention?

Compare Macbeth’s first aside, the arrival at Inverness and the end of 1.7. Identify the grammatical change that matters most.

02

How does the marriage distribute responsibility?

Keep persuasion, agreement, action and later secrecy distinct. Test any claim against both Duncan’s and Banquo’s murders.

03

What can the body remember that speech denies?

Follow sleep, hands, blood, hearing and sight from the murder to Lady Macbeth’s final scene.

04

Does the supernatural explain Macbeth?

Set the witches and apparitions beside Banquo’s responses, Macbeth’s decisions and one staging of the dagger or ghost.

05

What makes Macbeth a tyrant rather than merely a murderer?

Consider surveillance, hired violence, public fear, the attack on Macduff’s family and Scotland’s language of sickness.

06

Who says that life signifies nothing?

Read the final speech as Macbeth’s words at a particular defeat, then decide how far the play supports or challenges his conclusion.
