Study editions/Love’s Labour’s Lost

William Shakespeare

Love’s Labour’s Lost

Four men swear off women, food and sleep for three years, and break the oath within a day of four women arriving on state business. What follows is made almost entirely of language: sonnets read aloud in a park while three men hide in the bushes, two letters delivered to the wrong hands, a schoolmaster’s Latin, a Spaniard’s prose, a masque of counterfeit Russians. The women win every exchange, and when a messenger brings news of a death in the last minutes they set the men a year of waiting instead of marrying them. It is the only Shakespeare comedy that ends with nobody married.

c. 1595-96The verse and the rhyming put it beside A Midsummer Night’s Dream and Romeo and Juliet; no record fixes a first performance.
1598A quarto printed by William White for Cuthbert Burby, its title page naming Shakespeare and a performance before the Queen the previous Christmas.
No story sourceThe names come from the French civil wars, the shapes from the academy, the embassy, the masque and the Nine Worthies pageant.
Five acts, nine scenesSeven of them are short. Act IV Scene 3 and Act V Scene 2 hold almost half the play between them.

Who to watch

  • Berowne The lord who argues against the oath, signs it, and breaks it first
  • Rosaline The lady who matches Berowne line for line and sets his penance
  • The King of Navarre Founder of the academy, also called Ferdinand
  • The Princess of France Ambassador for her father, and queen before the play ends
  • Boyet The lord attending the Princess, and the play’s best reader of faces
