Study editions/Richard III

William Shakespeare

Richard III

A man tells the audience in the first forty lines exactly what he intends to do, and then does it while everyone on stage fails to notice. Richard's method is not secrecy but performance: he grieves for the brother he has imprisoned, refuses a crown he has arranged to be offered, and takes the audience into a confidence he offers nobody else. The play's problem is that the confidence works — and that it stops working in Act IV, when the jokes dry up and the women take over the stage.

Where the quotations come from.

Every quotation on this page was located in Globe/Moby text of Richard III before it was published, and carries its act.scene.line, Globe/Moby line numbering. The quarto and Folio texts differ substantially; line numbers here follow the traditional Globe numbering of the conflated text.
