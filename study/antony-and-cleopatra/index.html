Study editions/Antony and Cleopatra

William Shakespeare

Antony and Cleopatra

A triumvir who governs a third of the known world will not leave Alexandria, and Rome sends messengers instead of armies. Shakespeare gives the story forty-two scenes and no settled vantage point: every judgement passed on Antony is passed by someone with an interest in it. After Actium the play stops being about who wins and becomes about who gets to describe the losers, and Cleopatra spends the last act taking that job away from Caesar.

c. 1606–07Close in date to King Lear and Macbeth, and to Shakespeare’s other Plutarch play, Coriolanus.
1623No quarto survives. The play was entered in the Stationers’ Register in 1608, but the Folio is the only early text.
Plutarch’s Life of AntonyIn Thomas North’s English translation of 1579, which Shakespeare follows closely enough that whole phrases survive into the verse.
Five acts, forty-two scenesAct III alone changes location thirteen times; several scenes are under twenty lines long.
