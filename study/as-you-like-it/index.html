Study editions/As You Like It

William Shakespeare

As You Like It

Two women leave a court where one of them is about to be killed, walk into a wood and stay there for three acts. Rosalind, in a young man's clothes, meets the man who is writing poems about her and offers to cure him of love by playing the part of herself. Almost nothing happens in Arden except talking, which is exactly the point: the play is about what people become when the machinery of rank stops holding them in place.

c. 1599The same year as Henry V and Julius Caesar, and the year the Globe opened on Bankside.
1623No quarto. The play was entered in the Stationers' Register in 1600 with a note staying publication.
Lodge's RosalyndeThomas Lodge's prose romance of 1590, which supplies the plot; Touchstone, Jaques and Audrey are Shakespeare's additions.
Five acts, twenty-two scenesEvery scene after 2.3 but two takes place in the forest, and the plot stops moving for most of Acts III and IV.

Where the quotations come from.

Every quotation on this page was located in Globe/Moby text of As You Like It before it was published, and carries its act.scene.line, Globe/Moby line numbering. Line numbers follow the traditional Globe numbering. Much of the play is prose, and prose line numbers vary widely between editions.
