William Shakespeare

All's Well That Ends Well

Helena, the orphaned daughter of a famous physician, has been brought up in the Countess of Rossillon's household and is in love with the Countess's son Bertram, who is far above her in rank. She cures the dying King of France with one of her father's remedies and is allowed to choose a husband from the lords at court. She chooses Bertram, who is ordered into the marriage and leaves for the war in Florence that night, writing that she may call him husband only when she has the ring off his finger and a child of his to show. Helena follows him, finds the young woman he is trying to seduce, and takes her place in his bed. Beside this runs the unmasking of Bertram's companion Parolles, a braggart whom his fellow officers blindfold and frighten into betraying everyone he knows.

Early 1600sNo record fixes the date. Editors have proposed years from about 1602 to 1606, most often placing it close to Measure for Measure.
1623No quarto is known. The First Folio prints it among the comedies, and every modern edition depends on that one text.
Boccaccio, through PainterThe story of Giletta of Narbonne from the Decameron, which reached English readers in William Painter's Palace of Pleasure. Parolles and the Countess have no counterpart there.
Five acts, twenty-three scenesThe action moves from Rossillon to Paris, back to Rossillon, out to Florence and its camp, and home by way of Marseilles.

Characters

  • Helena Daughter of the physician Gerard de Narbon, brought up by the Countess
  • Bertram Count of Rossillon, the Countess's son
  • The Countess of Rossillon Bertram's mother, and Helena's guardian
  • The King of France Ruler of the court at Paris, dying when the play begins
  • Parolles Bertram's companion, a self-styled captain
