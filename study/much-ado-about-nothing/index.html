Study editions/Much Ado About Nothing

William Shakespeare

Much Ado About Nothing

Two couples are made by the same method. Beatrice and Benedick are talked into love by friends who arrange for them to overhear flattering lies; Claudio is talked out of it by a stranger at a window. The play's comedy and its near-catastrophe run on identical machinery, and it takes a pair of incompetent constables to notice the difference.

Noting, not nothingThe two words sounded close in early modern English, and almost every turn of the plot depends on somebody noting — watching, overhearing or reporting.
