Study editions/Titus Andronicus

William Shakespeare

Titus Andronicus

A general comes home from ten years of war, buries his sons, and sacrifices a captive prince to their spirits because Roman piety requires it. The prince’s mother becomes empress within the hour, and the rest of the play is her repayment: a son-in-law murdered, a daughter raped and maimed, two sons beheaded on false evidence, and a hand cut off for nothing. Shakespeare’s first tragedy is not shapeless. It is a machine, and its subject is what a city calls justice once revenge has learnt to wear the forms of law.

c. 1592Probably Shakespeare’s first tragedy, and possibly written with George Peele, whom many attribute the opening act to.
1594The first Shakespeare play ever to reach print. A single copy of the quarto survives, in the Folger Library.
Ovid and SenecaLavinia’s fate follows Philomela in the Metamorphoses; the banquet follows Seneca’s Thyestes. There is no historical Titus.
Five acts, fourteen scenesAct I is one continuous scene in which an emperor is chosen, a prince sacrificed, a son killed by his father and a bride stolen.

Where the material comes from, and where to take it.

Every quotation on this page was located in Globe/Moby text of Titus Andronicus before it was published, and carries its act.scene.line, Globe/Moby line numbering. Line numbers follow the traditional Globe numbering, which includes the fly scene (3.2) found only in the Folio. Modern editions vary by a line or two.
