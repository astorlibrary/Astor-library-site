William Shakespeare

Cymbeline

Cymbeline's daughter Imogen has married a poor gentleman, Posthumus, and her father has banished him. In Rome a stranger named Iachimo bets that he can seduce her, fails, hides in a trunk in her bedroom, and comes away with enough true detail to pass as proof. What follows is a princess walking across Britain in boy's clothes, two lost princes keeping house in a Welsh cave, a Roman invasion, and a last scene that unties every knot in front of the whole cast at once.

Five acts, twenty-nine scenesMore changes of place than anything else in the Folio: court, Rome, a mountain cave, a battlefield, a prison, a tent.

Where it happens

  • Milford Haven The Welsh port named in the forged letter. Imogen rides for it and never arrives; the Roman legions land there instead.
  • The Preseli Hills The mountain country above the haven, where Belarius keeps his cave and the princes hunt for the pot.
  • Rome Philario's house, where the wager is drawn up by lawyers and Posthumus is talked out of his marriage.
  • Lud's Town The legendary name for London. Cloten swears he will set the brothers' heads on its gates, and the play ends marching there to ratify the peace.
  • The Blackfriars playhouse The indoor theatre the King's Men had in hand by 1609, with the machinery to lower Jupiter and his eagle through the roof.
