Study editions/Cymbeline

William Shakespeare

Cymbeline

Imogen, the king’s daughter, has married the man she chose instead of the stepbrother chosen for her, and the play punishes almost everyone for it. Her husband is banished, wagers on her chastity in Rome, loses to a lie built from a mole and a bracelet, and orders her killed. Shakespeare then sends her across Britain in boy’s clothes to a cave in Wales, where her lost brothers are living under other names, and to a headless body dressed in her husband’s suit. The final scene undoes every deception in turn, and the play’s real interest is in how much has to be confessed aloud before anyone can be forgiven.

Where the material comes from, and where to take it.

Every quotation on this page was located in Globe/Moby text of Cymbeline before it was published, and carries its act.scene.line, Globe/Moby line numbering. Line numbers follow the traditional Globe numbering. The only early text is the First Folio of 1623, where the play stands last among the tragedies; modern editions vary by a line or two and some divide Act I differently.
