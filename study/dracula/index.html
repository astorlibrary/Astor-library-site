Dracula

A solicitor’s clerk goes to Transylvania to complete a property purchase and finds his client is not leaving the country alone. Stoker tells the whole thing in journals, letters, telegrams, ships’ logs, newspaper cuttings and phonograph cylinders, so the reader assembles the danger months before the characters manage to compare notes. The hunt is finally won by filing: Mina types the papers into order, and the order shows them where he has gone.

1897In London by Archibald Constable and Company, in yellow cloth with red lettering.
Documents onlyJournals in shorthand, letters, telegrams, a ship’s log, newspaper cuttings, a phonograph diary and a memorandum — no outside narrator at any point.
Notes kept from about 1890Stoker’s working papers show the name and the Transylvanian setting arriving late, over an existing plan.
Twenty-seven chapters and a closing noteRoughly four in the castle, ten in England, and the rest a chase back the way Harker came.

Where the material comes from, and where to take it.

Every quotation on this page was located in Standard Ebooks text of Dracula before it was published, and carries its chapter, with the closing section given as Note. Chapters are numbered but untitled in the original; the closing section is headed Note. Stoker’s characters date their entries by day and month and never give a year.
