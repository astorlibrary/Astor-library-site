Study editions/Richard II

William Shakespeare

Richard II

A king who believes the crown was fixed on him by God seizes his cousin's inheritance to pay for a war in Ireland, and finds that the cousin comes home for the land and stays for the throne. Bolingbroke wins without a battle; Richard loses without a defeat. The play is written entirely in verse, so the man who cannot hold a kingdom can still out-speak everyone in it, and the deposition turns into an argument about what words a king is made of.

Where it happens

  • Coventry The lists where the trial by combat is stopped at the trumpet in 1.3.
  • Westminster Hall, London The single scene of Act IV: Carlisle's prophecy, the resignation of the crown and the broken mirror.
  • Ravenspurgh, Humber estuary Bolingbroke's landing place, long since lost to the sea near Spurn Head.
  • Flint Castle Where Richard comes down from the walls into the base court in 3.3.
  • Pontefract Castle Pomfret in the play: the cell of the prison soliloquy and the murder in 5.5.

Where the material comes from, and where to take it.

Every quotation on this page was located in Globe/Moby text of Richard II before it was published, and carries its act.scene.line, Globe/Moby line numbering. Line numbers follow the traditional Globe numbering. Modern editions vary by a line or two, and quarto and Folio witnesses differ over the deposition passage in 4.1.
