Study editions/King Lear

William Shakespeare

King Lear

An old king asks his three daughters to say how much they love him, and disinherits the one who will not perform. Within two acts he has no soldiers, no roof and no title, and the play spends the next three watching what is left of a man when the offices are taken away. A second family repeats the mistake with sons, and by the end both fathers have learned to see and neither survives the knowledge.

Where the material comes from, and where to take it.

Every quotation on this page was located in Globe/Moby text of King Lear before it was published, and carries its act.scene.line, Globe/Moby line numbering. The Globe numbering follows a conflated text. The 1608 quarto and the 1623 Folio differ in hundreds of readings, and the Folio omits a whole scene that many editions print as 4.3.
