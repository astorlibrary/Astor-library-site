William Shakespeare

King Lear

An old king asks his three daughters to say how much they love him, and disinherits the one who will not perform. Within two acts he has no soldiers, no roof and no title, and the play spends the next three watching what is left of a man when the offices are taken away. A second family repeats the mistake with sons, and by the end both fathers have learned to see and neither survives the knowledge.

Sources

Quotations are taken from the Globe/Moby text of King Lear, and references give the act, scene and line of the Astor Library Edition. The Astor edition prints a conflated text. The 1608 quarto and the 1623 Folio differ in hundreds of readings, and the Folio omits a whole scene that many editions print as 4.3. A few quotations follow the Astor Library Edition’s wording where this text reads differently. The edition page lists the sources for the book’s dates and publication history.
