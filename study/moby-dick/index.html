Herman Melville

Moby-Dick

A schoolmaster with no money and a damp November in his soul signs on to a Nantucket whaler, and finds that the captain means to spend the voyage hunting the white whale that took his leg. Melville keeps interrupting the chase with sermons, stage directions, dictionary entries and a whole natural history of the animal, so that the book acquires the shape of Ishmael's mind rather than Ahab's. Everyone aboard dies except the man telling the story, and he survives on a coffin.
