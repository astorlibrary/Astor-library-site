Study editions/The Comedy of Errors

William Shakespeare

The Comedy of Errors

Two sets of identical twins, separated by shipwreck when they were babies, spend one day in the same city without meeting. Antipholus of Syracuse has come looking for the brother he cannot remember; Antipholus of Ephesus is already settled there, with a wife, a house and a goldsmith's account, and loses all three by teatime. Every disaster follows the same mechanism: a servant is sent on an errand, does it correctly, and reports to the wrong master. Around that Roman machine Shakespeare builds a frame of his own invention — a father under sentence of death at sunset, and the wife he lost at sea waiting inside the abbey he walks past all day.

Early 1590sVerse tests place it among the first plays; the Gray's Inn performance of December 1594 is the latest it can have been finished.
1623No quarto exists. The First Folio prints it among the Comedies under the title The Comedie of Errors.
Plautus, Menaechmi and AmphitruoThe Menaechmi gives the twins, Amphitruo the husband shut out of his own house; the second pair of twins, Egeon, Emilia and the abbey are Shakespeare's.
Five acts, eleven scenesOne city, one afternoon, and about 1,777 lines: the shortest play Shakespeare wrote.
