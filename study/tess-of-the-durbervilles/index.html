Study editions/Tess of the d’Urbervilles

Thomas Hardy

Tess of the d’Urbervilles

A parson tells a haggler on a Dorset road that he is descended from knights, and the boast costs his daughter everything. Hardy tracks Tess Durbeyfield through four employers, two men and one dead child, and arranges the novel so that every disaster arrives through ordinary machinery: a horse on a dark road, a letter under a carpet, a threshing engine that will not stop. The subtitle is an argument, and the book is built to win it.

Seven phasesHardy names them — The Maiden, Maiden No More, The Rally, The Consequence, The Woman Pays, The Convert, Fulfilment — so that the table of contents reads as a sentence about a life.
