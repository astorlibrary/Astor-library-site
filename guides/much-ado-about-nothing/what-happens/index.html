Much Ado About Nothing · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The soldiers’ return, and Claudio falls for Hero

Don Pedro’s company returns from a war that cost almost nothing, and the house fills with young men with time on their hands. Claudio decides within minutes that he loves Hero; Beatrice and Benedick resume a quarrel that predates the play; and Don John, newly and unconvincingly reconciled to his brother, announces that he would rather be disliked than pretend.

1.1
Beatrice asks after Benedick before he arrives; Claudio asks Don Pedro to court Hero for him.
1.2
Antonio brings Leonato a garbled report that the prince loves Hero himself.
1.3
Don John states his character outright and begins looking for something to spoil.

Act II The masked dance, and the gulling of Benedick

At the masked ball the wooing is done by proxy and briefly believed to be a betrayal. Once that is cleared up, Don Pedro proposes the only labour of Hercules he thinks worth attempting: getting Beatrice and Benedick to admit they like each other. The gulling of Benedick, staged in an orchard, works first time.

2.1
Beatrice abuses Benedick to his masked face; Claudio believes briefly that Don Pedro has taken Hero for himself.
2.2
Don John is told how a window, a borrowed name and a willing servant can wreck the match.
2.3
A song, then three men loudly discussing Beatrice’s hopeless love, and a bachelor converted.

Act III Beatrice gulled, and Don John’s deception

Hero and Ursula play the same trick on Beatrice, this time accusing her of pride, and she resolves to give up her scorn as soon as they leave. Meanwhile Don John takes Claudio to see a woman at Hero’s window at night, and the watch overhear Borachio boasting about it and arrest him and Conrade.

3.1
Beatrice overhears herself described as too scornful to be loved, and surrenders in rhyme.
3.2
Benedick’s friends mock his shaved beard and perfume; Don John invites Claudio to see proof.
3.3
Dogberry instructs his men to avoid trouble, and they arrest Borachio bragging about the deception.
3.4
Hero’s women joke about husbands and heaviness on the morning of the wedding.
3.5
Dogberry tries to tell Leonato what his watch has found and is sent away to hold the examination himself.

Act IV Hero accused at the wedding

Claudio waits until the ceremony to denounce Hero, as he said he would. She faints, her father believes the accusers, and a friar proposes to report her dead. Left alone in the empty church, Beatrice and Benedick admit they love each other, and she immediately asks him to kill his best friend.

4.1
The public shaming, the swoon, the Friar’s plan, and ‘Kill Claudio’.
4.2
Dogberry conducts an inquiry he cannot follow and gets the confession anyway.

Act V Grief, confession and a second wedding

Leonato challenges the men who shamed his daughter, and they mock him for being old. The truth comes out because the constables question their prisoners; none of the gentlemen looks into it. Claudio mourns at a tomb, agrees to marry a cousin sight unseen, and is given Hero back.

5.1
Leonato and Antonio challenge Claudio and Don Pedro; Benedick does the same in earnest; Borachio confesses.
5.2
Benedick tries to write a love poem, fails, and learns Hero is cleared.
5.3
Claudio reads an epitaph and hangs a scroll on a tomb that contains nobody.
5.4
Hero unmasks, the lovers are caught out by their own handwriting, and Don John is reported taken.
