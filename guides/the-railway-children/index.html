E. Nesbit

The Railway Children

A study guide

Two men call at a comfortable suburban villa after dinner, the father is taken away in a cab, and his wife and three children move to a cold cottage in the country and learn to manage on very little. The children fill the gap with the railway: they wave at the 9.15, cadge facts from the porter, stop a train with their torn red petticoats and organise a birthday for a man who does not want charity. Meanwhile their mother writes stories to keep them fed and says nothing about where their father is. The plot everyone remembers is the rescue; the book’s real subject is what the children are not told, and how long Nesbit can keep the reader in the same position.

Written c. 1904–05, while Nesbit was living at Well Hall in Eltham, then in Kent; first published 1906.
