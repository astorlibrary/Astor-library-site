The Railway Children · E. Nesbit

What happens

Chapters 1–2 The knock at the door, and Three Chimneys

Two gentlemen are shown into the library after dinner, the father leaves in a cab, and within weeks the family is unpacking by candlelight in a cold house on a hillside. Nesbit gives the reader the exact shape of a catastrophe and withholds its content, so that the move reads as an adventure and also as a fall.

Chapter 1
Peter’s birthday engine bursts, the two gentlemen call, Ruth is dismissed for hinting, and the family arrives after dark at a house with no supper in it.
Chapter 2
The children find the railway, name the trains, and Peter starts taking coal from the station heap until the Station Master catches him and lets him off.

Chapters 3–4 The Green Dragon and the old gentleman

A clean hand waves back from a first-class carriage, and a habit becomes a friendship. When their mother has influenza the children ask a stranger for what the doctor has ordered, get it, and are told afterwards that they must never do such a thing again.

Chapter 3
The daily wave to the 9.15; Peter apologises for the coals; the doctor’s list is painted on a sheet and a hamper comes up from the station.
Chapter 4
Their mother’s anger and apology, Bobbie’s birthday with its flower map of the railway, and her ride in the cab of a moving engine with a broken toy under her arm.

Chapters 5–7 The exile, the landslide and the watches

A shabby foreigner with no ticket is found at the station and taken home, and his story (prison, Siberia, a wife lost somewhere in England) is told to the children as something that happens in Russia. A week or two later they stop a train with their petticoats and are given gold watches for it.

Chapter 5
Peter’s stamps identify the stranger as Russian, their mother interprets, and that night she asks the children to pray for all prisoners and captives.
Chapter 6
Perks explains party politics, the trees walk down the cutting, and six flags torn from two red flannel petticoats stop the 11.29 twenty yards short of the mound.
Chapter 7
A presentation at the station, a short speech from Peter, and Bobbie’s real errand: asking the old gentleman to find the Russian’s wife and child, which he does.

Chapters 8–9 The canal, and Perks’s birthday

These are two episodes about how help lands on the person helped. Peter refuses to be driven off a public bridge by a bargeman and then pulls the bargeman’s baby out of a burning cabin; and the village subscribes to a birthday for Perks, who will not have it until he learns exactly who gave what and why.

Chapter 8
Bill the Bargee takes Peter by the ear; hours later the cabin is alight, Peter carries out Reginald Horace, and the family gains the freedom of the canal.
Chapter 9
Fourteen buns marked A. P., a pram from the post-office, and a furious porter who takes it all back when the labels are read aloud.

Chapters 10–12 The newspaper, and the boy in the tunnel

A pile of old magazines is wrapped in a sheet of newspaper, and Bobbie learns that her father has been convicted of selling State secrets. She tells her mother, keeps it from the others, and then spends a long cold hour in a tunnel holding the hand of an injured boy who does not know any of it.

Chapter 10
The quarrel over the rake, Peter’s torn foot, and the printed column headed End of the Trial that Bobbie reads at the level-crossing.
Chapter 11
Mother tells her about the planted letters and the jealous subordinate; Bobbie writes to the old gentleman; the paperchase runs into the tunnel and one hound does not come out.
Chapter 12
Bobbie stays with Jim in the dark while Peter and Phyllis wake a signalman who has not slept in five days and refuse the two shillings he offers them.

Chapters 13–14 The grandfather, and the 11.54

Jim’s grandfather turns out to be the old gentleman from the 9.15, who has been working on the case, without telling anyone, since he read about it. The whole train waves at the children one September morning, and a tall pale man gets off the 11.54.

Chapter 13
Dr Forrest lectures Peter about how to treat his sisters; Mother becomes matron of a hospital with one patient; at the gate the old gentleman tells Bobbie he has hopes.
Chapter 14
Lessons, rhymes, a village that will not explain itself, and a scream on a platform; then the narrator declines to follow the father through the door.
