The Railway Children · E. Nesbit

Context

Dates

1858
Edith Nesbit born in London The daughter of an agricultural chemist who died when she was three; the family moved often afterwards, in England and on the Continent.
1871
The family settles at Halstead in Kent For about three years, in a house beside the railway. Nesbit wrote warmly about the place in later life, and it is usually identified as a model for the country of this novel, though nothing in the book names a county.
1880
Marriage to Hubert Bland Their son Paul, born the same year, is the boy to whom The Railway Children is dedicated for his knowledge of railways.
1884
The Fabian Society is founded Bland was a founder member and its first treasurer, and Nesbit wrote and spoke for it. The politics of this novel (a writer jailed for a book about the poor, a signalman worked past exhaustion) belong to that household.
1894
The Dreyfus case begins in France A French officer convicted of passing secrets to a foreign power on documents that were not his; the verdict was finally quashed in 1906. The parallel with the father’s case is close, and there is no evidence that Nesbit had any particular trial in mind.
1899
The Story of the Treasure Seekers, and the move to Well Hall Her first great success with the Bastable children, and the move to the moated house at Eltham, then in Kent, where she lived for more than twenty years and where this novel was written.
1904
The Russo-Japanese War Fought from February 1904 to September 1905. It is the war the Russian exile deserts from, and the reason Perks has taken the Japanese side by the time the children meet him.
1905
Serialisation in The London Magazine Thirteen monthly instalments from January 1905, which is why the chapters are so nearly self-contained. The watches the children are given are engraved with the year 1905, the only date the story puts on itself.
1906
Published as a book By Wells Gardner, Darton and Co., with twenty black-and-white plates by C. E. Brock, including the frontispiece.
1924
Nesbit dies in Kent At New Romney, having published some forty books for children as well as poetry, novels and ghost stories for adults.
1968
A BBC television serial The last of the BBC’s versions, with Jenny Agutter as Bobbie. Its railway scenes were filmed at Oakworth on the Keighley and Worth Valley line, where the film went two years later.
1970
Lionel Jeffries’s film Filmed on the Keighley and Worth Valley Railway in West Yorkshire, with Oakworth as the station, Jenny Agutter again as Bobbie and Bernard Cribbins as Perks. It fixed a Yorkshire setting on a book that never names one.
2000
A television film for ITV Made on the Bluebell Railway in Sussex, with Jenny Agutter now playing the mother.
2022
The Railway Children Return A sequel set among evacuees in 1944, bringing back Agutter as Bobbie and returning to Oakworth; evidence of how firmly the film’s Yorkshire has attached itself to the story.

Places

London
The red-brick villa with every modern convenience, the Government Office where the father works, and the city the mother keeps going back to alone. The first page lists the children’s idea of it: Maskelyne and Cook’s, the Pantomime, the Zoological Gardens and Madame Tussaud’s.
Marlow, Buckinghamshire
The only place in England the novel names for the parents’ own past: Father and I used to go on the river at Marlow before we were married. The bargee’s wife names the same bridge as the site of the insult about the puppy-pie.
Halstead, Kent
Where Nesbit lived for about three years as a girl, in a house beside a railway. It is usually named as the model for the country of this book, and the identification rests on her own later affection for the place. The novel gives its station no county and its railway an invented company.
Well Hall, Eltham
The moated house, then in Kent and now in south-east London, where Nesbit was living when the book was written and serialised.
Northumberland
Jim’s home, named once, when the farm bailiff asks where the boy with the broken leg lives. The point is the distance: it is why he is carried up to Three Chimneys instead.
Siberia
The mines the Russian was condemned to for life, reached on foot in a chain of convicts. The novel names nothing more exact than Siberia, and the coordinates here simply mark the middle of it.
Oakworth station, West Yorkshire
Not a place in the book. The station on the Keighley and Worth Valley Railway used for the 1970 film and again in 2022, and the reason most readers now picture the story in Yorkshire.
