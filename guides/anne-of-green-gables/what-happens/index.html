Anne of Green Gables · L. M. Montgomery

What happens

Chapters 1–6 The mistake, and Marilla keeps Anne

Mrs Rachel Lynde sees Matthew drive past in his good clothes and goes to find out why. At Bright River a small girl is waiting on a pile of shingles, and by the time Marilla has decided that the mistake must be corrected, the alternative, a hard-worked household at Mrs Blewett’s, has shown her what correcting it would mean.

Chapters 7–12 Bringing-up begun

Anne is taught to say her prayers, offends Mrs Rachel Lynde about her hair and freckles, and refuses to apologise until an apology can be performed with full dramatic satisfaction. Sunday school, a wreath of buttercups on a hat, and the vow of eternal friendship with Diana Barry all belong to this stretch.

Chapters 13–18 School, Diana and the boy with the slate

Anne confesses to a theft she did not commit in order to attend a picnic, breaks a slate over Gilbert Blythe’s head for calling her carrots, and accidentally makes Diana drunk on Marilla’s currant wine. The croup night at the Barrys’ ends the estrangement and establishes Anne as useful in an emergency.

Chapters 19–23 A run of catastrophes

The Haunted Wood terrifies its own inventor, the liniment cake ruins a minister’s tea, and a dare on Mr Barry’s kitchen roof ends with a broken ankle. Each disaster is also an introduction: Aunt Josephine, Mrs Allan and a village that begins to take Anne’s part.

Chapters 24–29 Puffed sleeves, green hair and the lily maid

Miss Stacy arrives and changes what school can be; Matthew, after a lengthy campaign of embarrassment, buys a dress with puffed sleeves. Anne dyes her hair green, has it cut off, and nearly drowns playing Tennyson’s Elaine, and is pulled out of the water by Gilbert Blythe, whose apology she still will not accept.

Chapters 30–36 Queen’s, the pass list and the Avery

The Queen’s class is organised, Anne and Gilbert tie for first place on the entrance list, and a year in Charlottetown ends with the Avery scholarship and the prospect of a B.A. Marilla and Matthew are proud in their separate, largely silent ways, and Matthew says the one thing he has never said.

Chapters 37–38 Matthew’s death, and the bend in the road

A bank fails, Matthew dies of the shock, and Marilla learns that her eyes will not stand much more use. Anne gives up the scholarship, takes the Avonlea school, and makes her peace with Gilbert at a gate; the novel ends with a road that no longer runs straight.
