Anne of Green Gables · L. M. Montgomery

Context

Dates

1874
Lucy Maud Montgomery born at Clifton, Prince Edward Island The village now called New London, a few miles from Cavendish.
1876
Her mother dies; she is sent to her grandparents at Cavendish The household of an elderly couple and a child not quite their own is the one the novel rearranges.
1905
Montgomery begins the novel She started writing in June, after months of what she called spade work on character and incident.
1907
Contract with L. C. Page and Company Signed on 22 April, with the Boston firm that would publish the book and its sequels.
1908
Anne of Green Gables published The first edition appeared in June; Montgomery recorded receiving her copies on the 20th.
1909
First translation, into Swedish A Swedish edition appeared within a year, the beginning of an international readership.
1911
Montgomery marries and leaves the Island She moved to Ontario as a Presbyterian minister’s wife, and wrote most of the Anne books away from Cavendish.
1919
First film version A silent adaptation, now lost, made a decade after publication.
1925
L. C. Page issues a reset and altered edition L. C. Page issued a newly set and slightly altered edition, which is why the 1908 printing matters as a base text.
1942
Montgomery dies in Toronto She was buried at Cavendish, within sight of the landscape the novel renames.
1985
The Canadian television adaptation Kevin Sullivan’s version fixed the novel’s appearance for a generation of readers and made Prince Edward Island a destination.

Places

Cavendish, Prince Edward Island
The village Montgomery grew up in, and the source of Avonlea, though not a map of it.
New London, Prince Edward Island
Clifton, where Montgomery was born in 1874.
Charlottetown
The provincial capital, where Anne boards for her year at Queen’s Academy.
Gulf of St Lawrence shore
The north-shore coast and dunes that make Avonlea a triangular peninsula with water on two sides.
Boston
Home of L. C. Page and Company, whose 1908 printing fixed the novel’s American spelling and punctuation.
