The Duchess of Malfi · John Webster

What happens

Act I A marriage made in private

Antonio, back from France, compares a well-run court to a fountain and then sketches the three siblings for Delio. The brothers take on Bosola as their spy, under cover of a post in their sister’s stables, and warn her in a duet that sounds rehearsed never to marry again. Within minutes of their leaving she proposes to Antonio, puts her ring on his finger and marries him before a hidden witness.

1.1
Antonio praises the French king’s reformed court; Bosola, lately out of the galleys, rails at the Cardinal who will not reward him.
1.2
Antonio describes Ferdinand, the Cardinal and the Duchess; Ferdinand buys Bosola as an intelligencer with gold and the provisorship of the horse.
1.3
The brothers forbid remarriage; the Duchess woos Antonio while dictating her will and marries him by spoken contract, Cariola listening behind the arras.

Act II The apricots, and the birth

Time has passed and the Duchess is heavily pregnant. Bosola tests her with early apricots; she goes into labour, and Antonio locks the court into its chambers on the pretext of stolen jewels. Bosola picks up the horoscope Antonio has cast for the child and writes to Rome, where the Cardinal is entertaining his mistress and Ferdinand arrives with the letter, raving.

2.1
Bosola mocks an old lady’s cosmetics, meditates on the rotten body and gives the Duchess apricots; she falls into labour.
2.2
Antonio has the gates shut and the officers confined; Cariola brings news of a son.
2.3
Bosola and Antonio meet at night with lantern and sword; Antonio’s nose bleeds on his initials and he drops the child’s nativity.
2.4
In Rome the Cardinal lectures his mistress on women’s inconstancy; Delio offers her gold.
2.5
Ferdinand rages at his sister in images of burning and butchery while the Cardinal tells him to control himself.

Act III Ferdinand’s discovery, and the banishment

Years later there are three children and the rumour is loose. Ferdinand comes into the bedchamber with a key Bosola has had made, hears his sister talking to a husband who has left the room, hands her a dagger and goes. She sends Antonio away under a pretended charge of false accounts, then tells Bosola the truth because he has praised Antonio so warmly. At Loretto the Cardinal exchanges his hat for a soldier’s sword and has the family banished; Bosola returns in a visor to take the Duchess home as a prisoner.

3.1
Antonio tells Delio the court thinks him a thief; Ferdinand assures the Duchess he will believe no rumours.
3.2
Ferdinand overhears the Duchess and gives her a poniard; she dismisses Antonio as a bad steward, then confides in Bosola.
3.3
In Rome the Cardinal is made a general; Bosola reports and Ferdinand learns that the husband is Antonio.
3.4
In a dumb show the Cardinal lays down his hat and robes and has the family banished while churchmen sing; two pilgrims ask by what justice.
3.5
The Duchess sends Antonio and their eldest son towards Milan and is taken by Bosola’s guard, answering him with a fable.

Act IV The Duchess tormented and killed

Ferdinand sets out to drive his sister to despair before he kills her. In the dark he gives her a dead man’s hand wearing her ring and shows her wax figures of her husband and children as corpses; he sends madmen to sing and dance at her door; Bosola comes disguised as an old tomb-maker, then as the bellman. She is strangled with Cariola and two of the children. Ferdinand looks at her face and begins to go mad; Bosola, refused his reward, sees her revive for one word and die.

4.1
The Duchess kisses a severed hand in the dark and is shown the wax figures; Bosola begins to refuse the work.
4.2
Madmen, a dirge and the cords; Ferdinand’s ‘Cover her face’, his denial of his own orders, and Bosola’s change of heart over her body.

Act V The deaths in Milan

In Milan Antonio resolves to visit the Cardinal at midnight and seek a reconciliation. Ferdinand, convinced he is a wolf, attacks his doctor. The Cardinal hires Bosola to kill Antonio and poisons Julia with a kissed Bible when she learns his secret. Bosola, meaning to protect Antonio, kills him by mistake in the dark, then stabs the Cardinal, whose cries go unanswered because he told his courtiers he would only be pretending. Ferdinand and Bosola wound each other, and Delio brings in the Duchess’s son.

5.1
Pescara refuses Antonio’s confiscated land to Delio and gives it to Julia, as fit payment for an injustice.
5.2
Ferdinand tries to throttle his shadow; the Cardinal hires Bosola, and Julia dies of the poisoned book.
5.3
By the ruins under the Cardinal’s window an echo gives Antonio’s words back to him as warnings.
5.4
The Cardinal forbids anyone to answer cries in the night; Bosola, expecting an assassin, stabs Antonio.
5.5
Bosola kills the Cardinal, Ferdinand and Bosola wound each other, and Delio presents the heir.
