Richard II · William Shakespeare

Characters

King Richard II

King of England, grandson of Edward III; first appears 1.1

He begins as a king who thinks the title does the work for him, and becomes, once it is taken away, the best speaker in the play. Everything he loses politically he recovers as language, which is why an audience that would not follow him into battle follows him into prison.

Henry Bolingbroke

Duke of Hereford, later King Henry IV; first appears 1.1

He says very little about what he wants and gets all of it. The play never gives him a soliloquy, so an audience must judge him by timing alone: he lands to claim an inheritance, accepts a crown he never demands, and ends by disowning the murder he asked for out loud.

John of Gaunt

Duke of Lancaster, Richard’s uncle; first appears 1.1

He refuses to avenge his brother’s murder because the king ordered it, and then spends his last scene saying everything he has held back. His speech on England turns out to be a complaint about leases and parchment bonds, and the play takes his estate away before his body is cold.

Duke of York

Edmund of Langley, Richard’s uncle and Lord Governor of England; first appears 2.1

Left to hold the kingdom with no troops and no instructions, he changes sides slowly and in public, which makes him the play’s honest measure of how fast legitimacy moves. By Act V he is denouncing his own son to prove a loyalty nobody asked him for.

Thomas Mowbray

Duke of Norfolk; first appears 1.1

Accused of Gloucester’s murder, he defends himself without ever explaining what happened, because the truth would implicate the king who is judging him. His farewell mourns the English language more than England itself, and he dies offstage in Venice, the first of the play’s exiles.

Earl of Northumberland

Henry Percy, Bolingbroke’s chief supporter; first appears 2.1

He does the work Bolingbroke will not be seen doing: carrying the demands to Flint, pressing Richard to read the list of crimes aloud, ordering him to the Tower. The Henry IV plays begin with the bill for his service arriving.

The Queen

Richard’s wife, Isabel of Valois; first appears 2.1

Shakespeare ages a child bride into an adult woman so that the fall has a private witness. Her grief arrives before its cause does, and she tells Richard to fight instead of talk, which he cannot do.

Bishop of Carlisle

A churchman at the deposition; first appears 3.2

He makes the one speech in the play that names the consequence of what is happening, and is arrested before he finishes hearing the answer. His prophecy of English blood on English ground is also a trailer for the seven history plays Shakespeare had already written or would write.

The Gardener

A servant in the Duke of York’s garden; first appears 3.4

He has no name, one scene and the clearest political analysis in the play: a state is a plot of ground that needs pruning, and Richard has let the weeds take the light. He also knows about the deposition before the Queen does, which says something about how news travels.
