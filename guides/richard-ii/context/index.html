Richard II · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1367
Richard of Bordeaux is born Son of Edward the Black Prince, and grandson of Edward III.
1377
Richard becomes king as a child He succeeds his grandfather Edward III at the age of ten, his own father having died the year before.
1381
The Peasants’ Revolt The young king faces the rebels in person, an episode Shakespeare leaves out of a play that begins seventeen years later.
1398
Bolingbroke and Mowbray are banished The quarrel with which the play opens ends in exile, with no trial by combat.
1399
Gaunt dies, Bolingbroke returns, Richard is deposed The confiscation of the Lancastrian inheritance and the landing at Ravenspurgh fall in the same year as the surrender of the crown.
1400
Richard dies in captivity at Pontefract The play calls the castle Pomfret and gives him a murderer; the historical cause of death is not certain.
1587
Holinshed’s Chronicles reissued The second edition gave Shakespeare the sequence of events for this play and the ones that follow it.
1595
The play is written Usually dated to about this year, which places it at the head of the second sequence of English histories.
1597
The first quarto is printed Printed by Valentine Simmes for Andrew Wise, without the deposition passage in 4.1.
1601
A Richard II play is staged before the Essex rising Supporters of the Earl of Essex paid for a performance of a play about Richard II on the eve of the revolt. The records do not say outright that it was Shakespeare’s, and the subject alone was enough to make it dangerous.
1608
Fourth Quarto adds the deposition scene The title page announces the Parliament scene and the deposing of King Richard as material now in print.
1623
The First Folio The play is printed among the histories, fixing its place at the start of the run to Henry V.

Places

Coventry
The lists where the trial by combat is stopped at the trumpet in 1.3.
Westminster Hall, London
The single scene of Act IV: Carlisle’s prophecy, the resignation of the crown and the broken mirror.
Ravenspurgh, Humber estuary
Bolingbroke’s landing place, long since lost to the sea near Spurn Head.
Flint Castle
Where Richard comes down from the walls into the base court in 3.3.
Pontefract Castle
Pomfret in the play: the cell of the prison soliloquy and the murder in 5.5.
Berkeley Castle
Where York meets Bolingbroke’s advancing force and fails to arrest it.
