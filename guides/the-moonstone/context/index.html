The Moonstone · Wilkie Collins

Context

Dates

1799
The storming of Seringapatam British forces take the city in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War; Collins places the theft of the diamond in the confusion of the assault.
1824
Wilkie Collins born in London The son of the landscape painter William Collins, and later trained for the law at Lincoln’s Inn.
1851
Collins meets Charles Dickens The beginning of a collaboration and a friendship that gave Collins his serial platforms.
1859
The Woman in White begins in All the Year Round Collins’s first great multiple-narrator novel, and the commercial proof of the method used again here.
1868
The Moonstone serialised Thirty-two weekly instalments from 4 January to 8 August, in All the Year Round and in Harper’s Weekly.
1868
Three-volume edition published Tinsley Brothers issue the London book in July, before the weekly serial has finished.
1871
Revised one-volume text Smith, Elder issue a revised text with a new preface for a different market and format.
1877
Collins’s own dramatisation staged He adapted the novel for the theatre himself, cutting the many narrators that give the book its structure.
1889
Collins dies in London He had used laudanum for years against the pain of rheumatic illness, as Ezra Jennings does in the novel.
1934
First sound film version One of a long line of adaptations that keep the theft and drop most of the narrators.
1959
First BBC television serial Seven half-hour episodes, with Patrick Cargill as Sergeant Cuff. The BBC adapted the novel again in 1972, 1996 and 2016.

Places

Seringapatam (Srirangapatna)
Where the diamond passes into English hands during the assault of 1799.
Somnauth (Somnath)
The sacred city the stone was taken from in the eleventh century, and the shrine it returns to in the Epilogue.
Gray’s Inn Square, London
Mr. Bruff’s office, where an Indian visitor asks how long a borrower has to repay a loan on a pledge.
Hampstead
Bruff’s private house, where Rachel takes refuge after her mother’s death.
Brighton
Where the Ablewhites take a furnished house for Rachel after her mother’s death, and where she breaks off her engagement to Godfrey.
Amsterdam
The city where Murthwaite advises the diamond be cut into half a dozen stones, which would have ended the danger and the plot.

Critics

T. S. Eliot

“Wilkie Collins and Dickens”, in Selected Essays (1932)

Eliot called The Moonstone the first, the longest and the best of modern English detective novels, and argued that Collins’s gift was for plot and situation, the theatrical side of fiction, where Dickens’s was for character.
