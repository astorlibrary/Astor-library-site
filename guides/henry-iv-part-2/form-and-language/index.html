Henry IV, Part 2 · William Shakespeare

Form and language

Prose against verse

The switch between prose and verse as a signal of status, mood or place.

Eastcheap and Gloucestershire speak prose; Westminster, Warkworth and Gaultree speak verse. Hal moves between them, and he rejects Falstaff in verse; once the procession has gone, Falstaff talks to Shallow in prose again.

I know thee not, old man. Fall to thy prayers.

King Henry V, 5.5.39

Soliloquy

A character alone on stage speaking thoughts aloud.

The King’s address to sleep, Hal’s speech to the crown, and Falstaff’s prose soliloquies, among them those on Shallow and on sherris-sack, give each of the three men a private voice.

A good wit will make use of anything. I will turn diseases to commodity.

Falstaff, 1.2.209

Foils

Characters placed side by side so that each shows up the other.

Falstaff and the Chief Justice, Falstaff and Prince John, Shallow and Falstaff: each pairing shows a different side of the old knight, and the Chief Justice wins in the end.

I am not only witty in myself, but the cause that wit is in other men.

Falstaff, 1.2.7

Rumour and other abstractions

Giving human form to an idea.

Rumour appears in person to open the play, and the King speaks to sleep as a god who prefers the poor, while Hal addresses the crown as polished perturbation and golden care.

Rumour is a pipe Blown by surmises, jealousies, conjectures

Rumour, 1.0.15

Disease imagery

Imagery of illness and medicine applied to politics.

The commonwealth is sick, the rebels will purge it, the King’s body mirrors the kingdom’s. Falstaff makes the idea a business: a good wit will turn diseases to commodity.

A good wit will make use of anything. I will turn diseases to commodity.

Falstaff, 1.2.209

Dramatic irony

The audience knowing more than a character does.

Falstaff talks about the Prince to Doll while the Prince listens in disguise, and rides to London certain of favour while the audience has just heard Hal promise the Chief Justice the opposite.

Yea, this man’s brow, like to a title-leaf, Foretells the nature of a tragic volume.

Northumberland, 1.1.65
