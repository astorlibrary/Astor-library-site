Henry IV, Part 2 · William Shakespeare

Characters

Prince Hal

Prince of Wales, then King Henry V; first appears 2.2

Less often on stage than in Part 1, and less easy in the tavern. He tells Poins his heart bleeds for his father but that weeping would look like hypocrisy, spies on Falstaff in disguise, and leaves the moment the news of war arrives. At his father’s bedside he takes the crown too soon and has to explain himself. As king he keeps the justice who once imprisoned him and turns away Falstaff in public.

Sir John Falstaff

An old knight, Hal’s companion; first appears 1.2

Older, poorer and more often ill than in Part 1. He owes the Hostess a hundred marks and borrows more, is refused a thousand pounds by the Chief Justice and gets one from Shallow, lets the fittest recruits buy their way out, and claims a knight who surrenders to his reputation. He still talks better than anyone, but the play reminds him of his age again and again: Doll’s death’s-head, Shallow’s dead friends, the chimes at midnight. He expects the new king to make his fortune, and is sent to prison.

King Henry IV

The dying king; first appears 3.1

He does not appear until the third act, awake at one in the morning and sick. He remembers Richard II’s prophecy that Northumberland would turn on him, fears the reign his son will lead, and on his deathbed admits that he came by the crown by by-paths and indirect ways. His last advice is to keep restless minds busy with wars abroad.

The Lord Chief Justice

Chief Justice of the King’s Bench; first appears 1.2

He once sent the Prince to prison for striking him in court. He spends the play trying to bring Falstaff to account, and after the old King’s death expects to lose everything. His defence, that he acted for the King’s own law, persuades Henry V to keep him in office as a father to his youth. It is he who sends Falstaff to the Fleet.

Prince John of Lancaster

The King’s younger son, general at Gaultree; first appears 4.1

Cold, capable and sober. He promises the rebels at Gaultree that their grievances will be redressed, and once their army has scattered he arrests their leaders, keeping to the letter of his word and giving God the credit. Falstaff says he drinks no wine and cannot be made to laugh.

Earl of Northumberland

Hotspur’s father; first appears 1.1

He lay crafty-sick while his son fought at Shrewsbury. Told of Hotspur’s death he tears off his nightcap and calls for order to die, then lets his wife and daughter-in-law persuade him to Scotland, and sends the Archbishop letters instead of men. The sheriff of Yorkshire defeats him in the end.

Archbishop of York (Scroop)

Leader of the second rebellion; first appears 1.3

He makes rebellion a religious cause, gathering followers with the blood of King Richard, and argues at Gaultree that the commonwealth is sick and he means only to purge it. He trusts Prince John’s word, sends his army home, and is arrested for treason.

Justice Shallow

A country justice in Gloucestershire; first appears 3.2

A thin old man who knew Falstaff at Clement’s Inn fifty-five years ago, by his cousin Silence’s count, and talks endlessly of the wild days they had. Falstaff says that every third word is a lie. He presents the recruits, entertains Falstaff twice, and lends him a thousand pounds in the hope of advancement at court.

Mistress Quickly

Hostess of the Boar’s Head in Eastcheap; first appears 2.1

A poor widow, she has Falstaff arrested for a hundred marks and remembers, in exact domestic detail, the day he swore to marry her. Within minutes she has agreed to pawn her plate to lend him more. She has known him twenty-nine years. At the end she is dragged off by the beadles with Doll.

Doll Tearsheet

Falstaff’s companion at the Boar’s Head; first appears 2.4

She fights Pistol with better insults than he has, calls Falstaff a muddy conger, and then sits on his knee and tells him she loves him better than any young man. She is arrested in the last act after a man has been killed.

Pistol

Falstaff’s ancient (ensign); first appears 2.4

A swaggerer who talks in scraps of old plays, Latin and Italian. He is thrown downstairs at the Boar’s Head, rides to Gloucestershire with the news of the King’s death, and goes to the Fleet with Falstaff.

Lady Percy

Hotspur’s widow; first appears 2.3

In one speech she remembers her husband so vividly that the young men of England copied even the thickness of his speech, and tells his father that he broke his word to his son. She persuades Northumberland not to fight.

Francis Feeble

A woman’s tailor, recruited by Falstaff; first appears 3.2

The one recruit who does not try to buy his way out. He answers Falstaff’s jokes about his trade with plain courage.

Rumour

The speaker of the induction; first appears 1.0

A figure painted full of tongues who announces that he has spread false news of a rebel victory at Shrewsbury. The play that follows is full of wrong reports, exaggerated numbers and people who believe what they fear.
