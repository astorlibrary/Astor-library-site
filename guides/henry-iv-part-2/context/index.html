Henry IV, Part 2 · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1399
Richard II deposed Henry Bolingbroke took the throne as Henry IV with the help of the Percys, the debt the rebels of this play keep recalling.
1403
Battle of Shrewsbury Hotspur was killed. The play opens with the false and true reports of the battle.
1405
Archbishop Scrope executed The Archbishop of York and Mowbray were seized after a parley in Yorkshire and beheaded at York.
1408
Northumberland killed at Bramham Moor Defeated by the sheriff of Yorkshire, the victory reported to the King in 4.3.
1413
Henry IV dies in the Jerusalem Chamber He died on 20 March in the room of that name at Westminster Abbey.
1587
Holinshed’s Chronicles, second edition Shakespeare’s main source for the history.
1598
The Famous Victories of Henry the Fifth printed An older play about the wild prince and the Lord Chief Justice, which Shakespeare drew on.
1600
First quarto printed The first copies left out the whole of 3.1, the King’s sleepless night; a reissue that year added it.
1623
First Folio The Folio text, with passages not in the quarto.
1777
Maurice Morgann’s essay on Falstaff An Essay on the Dramatic Character of Sir John Falstaff argued that he is not a coward.
1909
A. C. Bradley on the rejection of Falstaff Oxford Lectures on Poetry reprinted his lecture The Rejection of Falstaff, the classic statement of the case against Hal.
1965
Orson Welles’s Chimes at Midnight A film built from both parts of Henry IV, with Welles as Falstaff, taking its title from 3.2.

Places

Warkworth Castle
Northumberland’s castle, where the induction and first scene are set.
Eastcheap, London
The Boar’s Head tavern.
Westminster
The palace, the Jerusalem Chamber and the coronation route.
Gaultree Forest, Yorkshire
The royal forest north of York where the rebels are tricked.
York
The Archbishop’s palace, where the rebels plan in 1.3.
Gloucestershire
Justice Shallow’s house and orchard.
