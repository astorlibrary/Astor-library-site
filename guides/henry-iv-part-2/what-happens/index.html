Henry IV, Part 2 · William Shakespeare

What happens

Induction and Act I Rumour, and the news of Hotspur’s death

Rumour boasts that he has spread word of a rebel victory at Shrewsbury. At Warkworth, Northumberland hears it from Lord Bardolph, then hears the truth from Morton: his son Hotspur is dead. He rages, then is told the Archbishop of York has risen. In London Falstaff, with a page the Prince has given him, dodges the Lord Chief Justice, who reminds him of the robbery at Gad’s Hill, and complains that he cannot rid his purse of its consumption. At York the Archbishop and his allies debate whether they can fight without Northumberland.

1.0
A figure painted full of tongues explains that he runs before the truth and has spread a false report of Hotspur’s victory.
1.1
Northumberland reads Morton’s face before Morton speaks, and his grief becomes a call for order to die.
1.2
A duel of insults about age and illness, a refused loan of a thousand pounds, and Falstaff’s plan to turn diseases to commodity.
1.3
Lord Bardolph argues from the builder who draws a house he cannot afford; the Archbishop condemns the fickle crowd that turned from Richard to Bolingbroke.

Act II Eastcheap and Warkworth

Mistress Quickly has Falstaff arrested for debt; he talks her out of the action and into lending him more. Prince Hal, tired and ashamed to admit he is sad at his father’s sickness, reads a letter from Falstaff and plans to spy on him. Lady Percy persuades Northumberland not to join the Archbishop, reminding him that he left his own son to die. At the Boar’s Head Doll Tearsheet quarrels with Pistol and makes up with Falstaff, while Hal and Poins, disguised as drawers, overhear Falstaff call the Prince a shallow young fellow. News of the war calls them all away.

2.1
Fang and Snare try to arrest Falstaff; the Hostess recalls the day he promised to marry her; the Chief Justice tells him to pay.
2.2
The Prince talks of small beer and Poins’s shirts, and says his heart bleeds inwardly for his father.
2.3
Hotspur’s widow describes him as the glass the young men of England dressed by, and Northumberland decides for Scotland.
2.4
Pistol’s swaggering, Doll’s tenderness to an old man, the disguised Prince, and the knock at the door that ends the night.

Act III The sleepless King and the old justice

At one in the morning the King envies his poorest subjects their sleep and remembers Richard’s prophecy that Northumberland would turn against him. Warwick tells him the rebels’ numbers are exaggerated and that Glendower is dead. In Gloucestershire Falstaff reaches Justice Shallow, who talks of the wild days he and Falstaff had at the Inns of Court and presents recruits named Mouldy, Shadow, Wart, Feeble and Bullcalf. The two strongest pay Bardolph to be released, and Falstaff lets them go. Alone, he mocks Shallow’s lies about his youth and plans to fleece him.

3.1
The King’s address to sleep, his memory of Richard’s prophecy, and Warwick’s reply that history has a pattern.
3.2
Old Double is dead, the chimes at midnight are remembered, Feeble says a man can die but once, and money changes hands.

Act IV Gaultree and the Jerusalem Chamber

At Gaultree Forest Westmoreland and Prince John meet the rebels. John agrees that their grievances shall be redressed, drinks with their leaders, and waits until their army has dispersed before arresting the Archbishop, Mowbray and Hastings for treason. Falstaff takes a surrendering knight, Colevile, and praises sherris-sack. At Westminster the King collapses on hearing the news. The Prince, sitting by him, thinks him dead, takes the crown from his pillow and puts it on. The King wakes, accuses him of wishing his death, and is reconciled when Hal explains himself; he advises his son to keep the country busy with foreign wars, and asks to be carried to the chamber called Jerusalem to die.

4.1
The Archbishop’s case for rebellion, John’s promise, the rebels’ army sent home, and the arrests.
4.2
A rebel yields to Falstaff’s reputation; John is unimpressed; Falstaff explains why sober young men never come to anything.
4.3.1–138
The King tells Clarence how to manage his brother and faints as good news arrives.
4.3.139–380
Hal takes the crown from the pillow; the King’s rebuke, Hal’s defence, and the last advice of a man who knows how he came by the crown.

Act V The new king, and Falstaff rejected

In Gloucestershire Shallow entertains Falstaff, and Shallow’s man Davy asks favours for a friend who is a knave. At Westminster the Lord Chief Justice expects to be ruined by the new King; instead Henry V confirms him in office and tells his brothers that his wildness has died with his father. In Shallow’s orchard Pistol brings the news of the old King’s death and Falstaff sets out for London, declaring the laws of England at his command. The Hostess and Doll are arrested after a man is killed. At the coronation procession Falstaff calls out to the King, who banishes him and his companions from within ten miles of his person; the Chief Justice sends them to the Fleet.

5.1
Davy’s household business and his plea for William Visor; Falstaff plans to make Hal laugh at Shallow for six fashions.
5.2
The justice’s defence of the day he sent the Prince to prison, and the new King’s reply.
5.3
Silence sings, Pistol arrives with his news, and Falstaff calls himself Fortune’s steward.
5.4–5.5
The beadles take away the Hostess and Doll; the procession passes; I know thee not, old man.
