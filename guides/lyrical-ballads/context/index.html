Lyrical Ballads · William Wordsworth and Samuel Taylor Coleridge

Context

Dates

1795
Wordsworth and Coleridge meet Introduced in Bristol; within two years they were living four miles apart in Somerset.
1797
The Wordsworths move to Alfoxden, near Coleridge The Wordsworths take Alfoxden House, a walk from Coleridge’s cottage at Nether Stowey. Most of the book is written in the twelve months that follow.
1797
Coleridge begins The Ancyent Marinere on a walk Planned in November on a walk towards Lynton, partly to pay for the trip; a friend’s dream of a skeleton ship and a sailor’s account of albatrosses went into it.
1798
Tintern Abbey composed Written in July, in Wordsworth’s head, on the walk back to Bristol from the Wye, and set down on arrival.
1798
First edition published anonymously Joseph Cottle issues five hundred copies in Bristol in September with neither poet named.
1798
Southey’s hostile review in the Critical Review In the Critical Review he calls the Marinere a Dutch attempt at German sublimity, and the volume’s best-known poem is dismissed out of hand.
1798
Wordsworth and Coleridge leave for Germany The two poets leave for Goslar and Ratzeburg; the separation ends the year of daily collaboration.
1800
The second edition Two volumes, Wordsworth’s name alone on the title page, Michael and the Lucy poems added, the Marinere respelled and moved out of first place.
1802
The Preface expanded Wordsworth adds the definitions the book is now remembered by, including poetry as the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings.
1817
Coleridge disputes the Preface in Biographia Literaria Biographia Literaria devotes two chapters to disputing Wordsworth’s theory of poetic diction while praising the poems it produced.
1820
Wordsworth cuts the mocked couplet from The Thorn Wordsworth finally cuts the couplet in which the narrator measures the pond (‘three feet long, and two feet wide’) from The Thorn after twenty years of ridicule.

Places

Alfoxden House, Holford
The house the Wordsworths rented in 1797–98; most of Wordsworth’s contributions were written here.
Nether Stowey
Coleridge’s cottage, three miles across the hills.
Tintern, Monmouthshire
The abbey a few miles below the spot on the Wye where the last poem is set.
Bristol
Joseph Cottle’s shop, where the book was printed and published in 1798.
Lynton, north Devon
The direction of the November walk on which The Ancyent Marinere was planned.
Goslar, Lower Saxony
Where Wordsworth spent the winter after publication, writing the poems that filled the 1800 edition.

Critics

Samuel Taylor Coleridge

Biographia Literaria (1817)

Coleridge described the division of labour behind the book: he was to make supernatural subjects real enough to win “that willing suspension of disbelief for the moment, which constitutes poetic faith”, and Wordsworth to give the charm of novelty to things of every day.

William Hazlitt

“Mr. Wordsworth”, in The Spirit of the Age (1825)

Hazlitt called Wordsworth’s genius a pure emanation of the spirit of the age, and his muse a levelling one: it chose humble people and plain subjects, as the politics of the 1790s had tried to level society.
