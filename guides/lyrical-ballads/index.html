Study guides/Lyrical Ballads

William Wordsworth and Samuel Taylor Coleridge

Lyrical Ballads

A study guide

Two young poets published this anonymously in 1798 and changed what English poetry was allowed to be about. The subjects are people who had been comic or invisible in verse: a discharged soldier, a woman whose child has died, an old huntsman who cannot cut through a root, a boy sent for the doctor who spends the night listening to the owls. The language is deliberately plain, the metre is borrowed from street ballads, and the book ends with a hundred and sixty lines of blank verse above the Wye that set the terms for the next century of poetry.

Written Mostly between March and July 1798, on and around the Quantock Hills, with The Ancyent Marinere begun on a walk in November 1797; first published 1798.
