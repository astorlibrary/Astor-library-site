The Merry Wives of Windsor · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1597
Lord Hunsdon elected to the Order of the Garter The Lord Chamberlain, patron of Shakespeare’s company, was elected in April; many editors connect the Garter speech in 5.5 with the feast.
1598
Henry IV Part 1 printed Falstaff was already a famous stage character; the first part of Henry IV was printed this year and reprinted the next.
1602
First quarto printed A much shorter text than the Folio. Ford’s disguise is called Brooke here; the Folio calls him Broome.
1604
Played at court The Revels Accounts record a performance before King James on 4 November.
1623
First Folio The full text, printed as the third of the comedies.
1702
John Dennis’s story of the Queen’s command Dennis wrote that the play was written at Elizabeth’s command in fourteen days; Nicholas Rowe added in 1709 that she wanted to see Falstaff in love.
1849
Nicolai’s Die lustigen Weiber von Windsor Otto Nicolai’s comic opera, first performed in Berlin.
1893
Verdi’s Falstaff Verdi’s last opera, with a libretto by Boito drawn mainly from this play, first performed at La Scala.
1929
Vaughan Williams’s Sir John in Love An opera setting the play with English folk songs.

Places

Windsor
The town, the Garter Inn and the Fords’ and Pages’ houses.
Windsor Home Park
Herne’s oak, where the last scene is set.
Datchet Mead
Where the buck-basket is tipped into the Thames.
Frogmore
The field where the parson waits for the doctor.
Eton
Where Slender marries a postmaster’s boy.
Brentford
Home of the fat woman whose gown Falstaff wears.

Critics

John Dennis

Epistle to The Comical Gallant (1702)

Dennis, introducing his own adaptation, recorded the tradition that Queen Elizabeth commanded the play and wanted it finished in fourteen days. Nicholas Rowe added in 1709 that she had asked to see Falstaff in love. Neither claim can be confirmed.
