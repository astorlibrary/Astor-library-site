The Merry Wives of Windsor · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I Two letters

Justice Shallow arrives in Windsor to complain that Falstaff has beaten his men, killed his deer and broken open his lodge, and Sir Hugh Evans, the Welsh parson, proposes to settle it by marrying Shallow’s cousin Slender to Anne Page, who has seven hundred pounds. At the Garter Inn Falstaff gets Bardolph a job as a tapster and reveals his plan to make love to Mistress Ford and Mistress Page, who he says control their husbands’ purses. Pistol and Nym refuse to carry his letters and resolve to tell the husbands. At Doctor Caius’s house Mistress Quickly, his housekeeper, promises to help Slender, Caius and Fenton to Anne, and Caius challenges Evans for helping Slender.

1.1
Coats of arms, a proposed marriage, a picked pocket, and Slender tongue-tied in front of Anne.
1.3
Falstaff cuts his costs, describes the wives as his East and West Indies, and dismisses the two followers who will betray him.
1.4
Simple hides in a closet, Caius writes a challenge to Evans, and Quickly takes money from Fenton too.

Act II Wives and husbands

Mistress Page and Mistress Ford discover that their letters are identical except for the names, and plan to lead Falstaff on until he pawns his horses. Pistol tells Ford, and Nym tells Page. Page laughs; Ford asks the Host to introduce him to Falstaff as Master Brook. Mistress Quickly brings Falstaff an appointment with Mistress Ford between ten and eleven. Brook then pays him to seduce Mistress Ford, and Falstaff tells him cheerfully about the appointment. Alone, Ford rages. The Host keeps Caius and Evans apart by sending them to different places.

2.1
Letter for letter, but that the name of Page and Ford differs, and two husbands react in opposite ways.
2.2
Pistol finds the world his oyster, Quickly delivers her message, and Ford in disguise hires the man he suspects.
2.3
Caius waits in a field for Evans while the Host arranges to bring him the wrong way.

Act III The buck-basket

Evans and Caius meet at last, are disarmed by the Host, and make friends in order to be revenged on him. Ford invites the town to see a monster. Falstaff arrives at Mistress Ford’s, Mistress Page runs in with the news that Ford is coming with half Windsor, and they hide Falstaff in a basket of dirty linen that the servants carry out and tip into the Thames at Datchet Mead. Ford searches the house and finds nothing. Anne begs her mother not to marry her to Slender, and would rather be buried alive and bowled to death with turnips than marry Caius. Falstaff, soaked, accepts a second appointment and tells Brook everything.

3.1
The parson sings to keep up his courage, and the Host calls him and the doctor soul-curer and body-curer.
3.3
Falstaff’s courtship, the alarm, the basket and Ford’s fruitless search.
3.4
Fenton admits her father’s money was his first motive, and Slender courts her through his uncle.
3.5
Falstaff describes his alacrity in sinking, and Brook learns about the basket.

Act IV The witch of Brentford

Evans examines young William Page in his Latin while Mistress Quickly hears rude words in every case. At the Fords’ house Falstaff is trapped again. Ford is sure to search the basket, so the wives dress Falstaff in the clothes of the fat woman of Brentford, whom Ford calls a witch, and Ford beats him out of the house. The wives then tell their husbands everything, Ford begs his wife’s pardon, and the four plan a last public shaming at Herne’s oak. Page secretly arranges for Slender to carry off Anne, Mistress Page for Caius to do so, and Anne tells Fenton, who arranges with the Host for a vicar. Three Germans run off with the Host’s horses.

4.1
A parson, a schoolboy and a housekeeper who mishears the grammar.
4.2
The chimney, the kiln-hole, the fat woman’s gown, and Ford’s cudgel.
4.4
The story of the ghost at the oak, and the plan for children dressed as fairies.
4.5–4.6
The Host loses his horses, and Fenton explains how Anne will deceive both her parents.

Act V Herne's oak

At midnight Falstaff waits at Herne’s oak with a buck’s head on, calling on Jove, who became a bull and a swan for love. The wives come, then run at a noise of horns, and the fairies, led by Mistress Quickly as their queen, Evans as a satyr and Pistol as Hobgoblin, test him with tapers, find him corrupt, and pinch him. In the confusion Caius and Slender each carry off a boy in disguise, and Fenton carries off Anne. The husbands and wives reveal themselves and mock Falstaff, who admits he has been made an ass. Slender arrives married to a postmaster’s boy and Caius to another boy; Fenton and Anne arrive married. Page accepts it, and everyone, Falstaff included, goes home to laugh by a country fire.

5.1–5.3
Falstaff promises Brook wonders, and each parent tells a suitor what colour Anne will wear.
5.5
The buck’s head, the fairy queen’s blessing on Windsor Castle, the tapers, and the song of the pinching.
5.5
How like you Windsor wives? The husbands’ list of insults, two wrong brides, and Fenton’s defence of his marriage.
