The Merry Wives of Windsor · William Shakespeare

Characters

Sir John Falstaff

A knight staying at the Garter Inn; first appears 1.1

Short of money, he decides to court two wives together for their husbands’ purses, and sends them the same letter. He is carried out in dirty laundry, thrown in the Thames, beaten as an old woman and pinched by children dressed as fairies, and every time he talks his way back to the next appointment. In the end he admits he has been made an ass and is invited home to supper by one of the husbands.

Mistress Ford (Alice)

Ford’s wife; first appears 1.1

She receives Falstaff’s letter first, carries out the plots in her own house, and has to put up with her husband’s searches while she does it. Her jokes about Falstaff are the play’s sharpest, and she keeps the plan inside the limits of her honesty.

Mistress Page (Meg)

Page’s wife; first appears 1.1

The leader of the plotting, and the author of its motto that wives may be merry and yet honest too. She runs in with false alarms, invents the disguise of the fat woman, and proposes the Herne’s oak masque. She also plans to marry her daughter to Doctor Caius behind her husband’s back, and is beaten by her daughter at her own game.

Master Ford

A citizen of Windsor, jealous of his wife; first appears 2.1

Told by Pistol that Falstaff loves his wife, he disguises himself as Master Brook and pays Falstaff to seduce her, so as to catch her. He searches his house twice, finds nothing, beats an old woman who is Falstaff, and is made a laughing stock until his wife tells him the truth. He asks her pardon and joins the last plot.

Master Page

A citizen of Windsor, Anne’s father; first appears 1.1

Confident in his wife, he laughs at Nym’s warning and at Ford’s searches. He wants his daughter to marry Slender, who has land, and turns Fenton away as too well born and too wild. He ends the play accepting the marriage he tried to prevent.

Anne Page

The Pages’ daughter, with seven hundred pounds; first appears 1.1

She says little, and every word counts. She sees exactly what three hundred pounds a year does for Slender’s faults, would rather be buried alive than marry Caius, and deceives both parents to marry Fenton, the man she has chosen.

Fenton

A young gentleman, Anne’s choice; first appears 1.4

Page objects that he is too high born, that he has spent his money, and that he kept company with the wild Prince and Poins. He admits her father’s wealth was his first motive for wooing Anne, and says he found her worth more than money. He arranges their secret marriage through the Host.

Abraham Slender

Shallow’s cousin, Page’s choice for Anne; first appears 1.1

A timid, foolish young man who wishes he had his book of songs and sonnets to woo with, tells Anne about the bear Sackerson, and says he will marry her if his uncle tells him to, hoping that love will decrease upon better acquaintance. He carries off a boy in white and marries him at Eton.

Doctor Caius

A French physician, Mistress Page’s choice for Anne; first appears 1.4

Hot-tempered and loud, he challenges the parson to a duel for helping Slender, waits for him in the wrong field, and then joins him in revenge on the Host. He carries off a boy in green and marries him at the deanery.

Sir Hugh Evans

A Welsh parson and schoolmaster; first appears 1.1

He tries to make peace between Shallow and Falstaff by a marriage, sings to steady his nerves before a duel, examines young William in Latin, teaches the children their fairy parts and plays a satyr at Herne’s oak. His Welsh English is mocked by everyone, and he gets the last word on Falstaff’s sins.

Mistress Quickly

Doctor Caius’s housekeeper; first appears 1.4

She keeps the doctor’s house single-handed and promises all three of Anne’s suitors her help, taking Fenton’s money as she does so. She carries the wives’ messages to Falstaff and plays the Queen of the Fairies at the oak. She mishears William’s Latin as a string of rude words.

The Host of the Garter

Landlord of the Garter Inn; first appears 1.3

Loud and full of nicknames, bully rook and bully Hector among them, he takes on Bardolph as a tapster, arranges the duel and then sends the duellists to the wrong places, helps Ford become Brook and Fenton marry Anne, and loses three horses to men who say they are Germans.

Justice Shallow

A Gloucestershire justice, Slender’s cousin; first appears 1.1

The same old justice as in Henry IV Part 2, here come to Windsor to complain of Falstaff and to marry his cousin to money. He says he has lived fourscore years and upward.

Pistol

One of Falstaff’s followers; first appears 1.1

He refuses to carry the love letter, tells Ford that Falstaff loves his wife, and later plays Hobgoblin in the fairy masque.
