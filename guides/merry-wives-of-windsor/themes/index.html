The Merry Wives of Windsor · William Shakespeare

Themes

Marriage

Two established marriages are tested by Falstaff and by jealousy, and a new one is fought over. The play compares a husband who trusts too easily, a husband who cannot trust at all, and parents who treat a daughter’s marriage as a deal.

The Fords are reconciled in 4.4, and the play ends with a marriage made by the couple themselves against both parents’ plans.

Letter for letter, but that the name of Page and Ford differs!

Mistress Page, 2.1.54

O, what a world of vile ill-favoured faults Looks handsome in three hundred pounds a year!

Anne Page, 3.4.31

Jealousy

Ford’s jealousy is the most dangerous thing in Windsor: he pays a stranger to seduce his wife, searches his house with half the town behind him, and beats an old woman he thinks is a witch.

It is cured by being made ridiculous. By the end Ford can joke that he will not mistrust his wife until the parson can woo her in good English.

Better three hours too soon than a minute too late.

Ford, 2.2.247

Buck? I would I could wash myself of the buck! Buck, buck, buck! Ay, buck!

Ford, 3.3.120

Money and love

Falstaff courts for money, Page chooses Slender for his land, Mistress Page chooses Caius for his friends at court, and even Fenton admits that Anne’s fortune was his first motive.

Anne’s line about three hundred pounds a year and Ford’s closing couplet, money buys lands, and wives are sold by fate, frame the question the whole plot raises.

Indeed, I am in the waist two yards about, but I am now about no waste; I am about thrift.

Falstaff, 1.3.31

they shall be my East and West Indies, and I will trade to them both.

Falstaff, 1.3.57

Disguise and exposure

Ford becomes Brook, Falstaff becomes a bundle of washing, an old woman and a stag, children become fairies, and two boys are married as Anne.

Each disguise exposes the person wearing it: Ford’s jealousy, Falstaff’s appetite, the parents’ schemes.

Letter for letter, but that the name of Page and Ford differs!

Mistress Page, 2.1.54

you may know by my size that I have a kind of alacrity in sinking

Falstaff, 3.5.10

A knight among citizens

Falstaff assumes that citizens’ wives will be flattered by a knight, and the citizens of Windsor, who have money and sense, prove him wrong.

The last scene turns the court’s own ceremony, the Order of the Garter, into a blessing spoken by Mistress Quickly over Windsor Castle.

they shall be my East and West Indies, and I will trade to them both.

Falstaff, 1.3.57

Now, good Sir John, how like you Windsor wives?

Mistress Page, 5.5.102

English mangled

Evans speaks Welsh English, Caius French English, Mistress Quickly malapropisms, Pistol stage bombast and Nym the word humour. Half the jokes are about how people speak.

Falstaff’s lowest moment is being mocked by one that makes fritters of English.

Have I lived to stand at the taunt of one that makes fritters of English?

Falstaff, 5.5.133
