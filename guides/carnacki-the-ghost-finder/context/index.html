Carnacki, the Ghost-Finder · William Hope Hodgson

Context

Dates

1877
Hodgson born in Essex A clergyman’s son, and the second of twelve children.
1891
Hodgson apprenticed to the sea Eight years afloat, including a Royal Humane Society medal for a rescue in heavy weather.
1899
Hodgson opens a gymnasium in Blackburn He left the sea for physical culture, photography and lecturing; the practical streak in Carnacki comes from here.
1907
Hodgson’s first novel, The Boats of the Glen Carrig The Boats of the Glen Carrig, then The House on the Borderland the following year.
1910
First five stories published in The Idler The Gateway of the Monster, The House Among the Laurels, The Whistling Room, The Horse of the Invisible and The Searcher of the End House.
1912
The Thing Invisible published in The New Magazine The sixth case appears in The New Magazine, and is the most detective-like of them.
1913
First book edition, published by Eveleigh Nash The six stories are published as a book.
1918
Hodgson killed at Ypres A shell killed him in April, aged forty; he had re-enlisted after being invalided out.
1947
Arkham House edition adds three more cases The Haunted Jarvee, The Find and The Hog are printed from manuscripts; later editions often run to nine cases.

Places

Cheyne Walk, Chelsea
Carnacki’s address, where every story is told over dinner.
County Galway
Iastrae Castle, twenty miles north-east of Galway, in The Whistling Room; The House Among the Laurels is also set in the west of Ireland.
Kent
Sir Alfred Jarnock’s house at Burtontree, in South Kent, where The Thing Invisible is set.
Blackburn, Lancashire
Where Hodgson ran his school of physical culture from 1899. The Horse of the Invisible is set in East Lancashire.
Borth, Ceredigion
The seaside village near Aberystwyth where Hodgson lived with his mother and sister from 1904, until he moved to London around 1910.
