Carnacki, the Ghost-Finder · William Hope Hodgson

Form and language

Frame narrative

A story told aloud to a fixed audience inside another story.

The dinner at Cheyne Walk supplies the audience, the interruptions and the closing question. It also lets Hodgson end a case without resolving it: a man telling a story after dinner is allowed to say he never found out.

In response to Carnacki's usual card of invitation to have dinner and listen to a story, I arrived promptly at 427, Cheyne Walk

No. 1, The Gateway of the Monster

The closing formula

A phrase repeated at the same point in every story.

Carnacki’s dismissal closes all six cases. Coming after a hand in the dark or a hoof in the passage, it works as the door of the barrier closing: the evening is sealed and everyone goes home.

Out you go!

Every story

Equipment

A story advanced by the state of its tools.

Chalk, tape, wax, garlic, a camera, a tape measure and a battery. Whether a case ends well depends on whether the circle is complete and the tubes are lit, which gives the supernatural something to break.

the Electric Pentacle, which is a most marvelous 'Defense'

No. 1, The Gateway of the Monster

First-person retrospect

An account with no corroboration beyond the teller.

Carnacki’s photographs are described but not shown, and his friends never visit the houses. The frame makes the whole book one man’s word, and the fraud cases strengthen it: he is the sort who would say so if it were a trick.

that feeling of spiritual sickness, as if some delicate, beautiful, inward grace had suffered, which is felt only upon the too near approach of the ab-human

No. 1, The Gateway of the Monster

Haunted-house conventions

The haunted-house tradition approached as a job of work.

The manor, the castle and the chapel are all conventional, and everything Carnacki does in them is not. The contrast between inherited dread and a man with a tape measure is the collection’s whole comic and dramatic engine.

the sound of a hoof in the dark billiard room we had just left

No. 4, The Horse of the Invisible

Invented sources

Invented archaic orthography used for quoted authority.

Theyre must noe lyght come from wythin the barryier. The spelling is fake and the effect is real: it makes the rules older than the man obeying them.
