Carnacki, the Ghost-Finder · William Hope Hodgson

What happens

The frame The dinners at 427 Cheyne Walk

Every story is set inside the same evening: a card arrives, four friends dine, Carnacki refuses to be drawn until he is ready, tells the whole case without being interrupted, answers a question or two, and then throws them out. The narrator Dodgson records it. The frame gives the book its rhythm and its one joke.

Every story
The usual invitation to dinner and a story, and four men who never decline it.
Every story
The same genial dismissal closes each case, whatever has just been described.

The apparatus Chalk, tape, garlic and a battery

Carnacki’s procedure is the book’s real subject. He measures the room, photographs everything and seals the doors and windows with tape and wax so that no human agent can enter, then builds the defences: a chalk circle, a water circle, bread and garlic, and the Electric Pentacle wired to accumulators. The reader learns the drill and then watches it be tested.

No. 1, The Gateway of the Monster
Hodgson’s invention arrives in the first case: vacuum tubes laid in a pentacle and switched on.
No. 2, The House Among the Laurels
A circle measured with a tape rule, men posted inside it, and their pipes confiscated because the Sigsand MS. forbids light within the barrier.
No. 1, The Gateway of the Monster
A fourteenth-century manuscript quoted in invented spelling and treated as a technical reference.

Cases that are real The room, the ring and the hoof

In three of the six, something is really there. A ring and a bed-chamber produce a hand that hunts; a room in Ireland swells and whistles like a mouth; and in Lancashire a hoof walks through a closed door and down a passage between two men. These are the stories the book is remembered for, and in each the defences are nearly not enough.

No. 1, The Gateway of the Monster
A door that unlocks itself, a hand in the pentacle, and a ring that turns out to be the gateway.
No. 3, The Whistling Room
A room in an Irish castle that whistles, and that Carnacki eventually sees as a vast pair of lips.
No. 4, The Horse of the Invisible
The sound of a great hoof passing between two men in the dark, after a human fraud has already been exposed.

Cases that are not The hoaxes

Carnacki is as pleased to find a hoax as a haunting, and says so. A chapel dagger that stabs by itself turns out to be machinery and a butler; a house among the laurels has a human explanation running alongside the inhuman one; and a family curse is being helped along by somebody who wants a marriage stopped. The detections are fair and the clues are given.

No. 6, The Thing Invisible
A weapon that strikes on its own, explained by mechanism, timing and a man with a reason.
No. 4, The Horse of the Invisible
A human impostor is caught, and the hoof goes on sounding afterwards.
No. 5, The Searcher of the End House
The only case in his own home, with a smell, a child’s footsteps and a long dull vigil that turns.
