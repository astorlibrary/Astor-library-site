Carnacki, the Ghost-Finder · William Hope Hodgson

Characters

Thomas Carnacki

The ghost-finder; first appears No. 1, The Gateway of the Monster

A professional investigator who believes in neither ghosts nor the absence of them until he has measured the room. He is brusque about being questioned, exact about his equipment, and honest when a case collapses into fraud. He is unusual among occult detectives in being frightened, saying so, and going back in anyway.

Dodgson

The narrator, one of the four guests; first appears No. 1, The Gateway of the Monster

He records the evenings and almost never intrudes. His one function is to be the man who asks a question too early in the first story and is politely frozen out, which teaches the reader the rules of the house.

Arkright

The guest who asks the technical questions; first appears No. 1, The Gateway of the Monster

He is how the reader learns the theory: at the end of a case he asks what governs the Unknown Last Line of the Saaamaaa Ritual, and gets an answer about astral vibration and a protective force. Hodgson uses him to deliver exposition without slowing the stories.

The Sigsand MS.

A fourteenth-century manuscript, quoted as a manual; first appears No. 1, The Gateway of the Monster

Hodgson’s invented authority, written in a spelling of his own devising and cited the way an engineer cites a standard. It supplies the rules (no light within the barrier, the five hills, the properties of the circle), and its flat practicality makes the occult in these stories feel like a trade.

The Outer Monstrosities

What the defences are for; first appears No. 1, The Gateway of the Monster

Never described and barely named, they are the ab-human forces the pentacle keeps out. Hodgson gives them a theory instead of a shape: they press on the human from outside, and something inscrutable stands between them and the soul.
