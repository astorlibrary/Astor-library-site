William Shakespeare

Venus and Adonis

A study guide

Venus, goddess of love, falls for the young hunter Adonis as he sets out for the chase. She pulls him from his horse, holds him down, kisses him and argues with him: the god of war once begged for her love, beauty should be used before it fades, and he has a duty to father a child. Adonis blushes, sulks and refuses. His horse breaks free to chase a mare, stranding him with her, and by nightfall she has won one kiss and learned that he means to hunt the boar in the morning. He rejects her love as lust and leaves. At dawn she finds him killed by the boar. His blood becomes a purple and white flower, and Venus prophesies that love will always be joined with sorrow before she flies away to Paphos.

Written 1592–1593; first printed 1593.
