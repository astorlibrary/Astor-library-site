Venus and Adonis · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1567
Golding’s Ovid Arthur Golding’s English translation of the Metamorphoses, which Shakespeare knew alongside the Latin.
1589
Lodge’s Scillaes Metamorphosis An Ovidian love poem in the same six-line stanza, usually counted the first of the fashion for such poems.
1592
The theatres close Plague shut London’s playhouses for most of 1592 to 1594, the years in which Shakespeare wrote both his narrative poems.
1593
First printed Entered for publication on 18 April and printed by Richard Field, a printer from Stratford, with a dedication to the Earl of Southampton.
1594
The Rape of Lucrece Shakespeare’s second narrative poem, commonly taken as the graver labour promised in the first dedication, also printed by Field and dedicated to Southampton.
1598
Meres’s praise Francis Meres wrote that the soul of Ovid lived in mellifluous and honey-tongued Shakespeare, witness his Venus and Adonis and his Lucrece.
1600
Under the pillow In The Return from Parnassus, a Cambridge student play of about 1600, a foolish gallant says he will keep Venus and Adonis under his pillow.
1817
Coleridge’s Biographia Literaria Coleridge used the poem to describe the signs of poetic genius in the young Shakespeare.

Places

Paphos
Venus’s sacred city in Cyprus, where she flies at the end.
London
Printed by Richard Field and sold in St Paul’s Churchyard, the centre of the book trade.
Stratford-upon-Avon
Home town of both Shakespeare and his printer, Richard Field.
