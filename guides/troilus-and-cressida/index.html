William Shakespeare

Troilus and Cressida

A study guide

Seven years into the siege, a Trojan prince gets the woman he wants through her uncle’s brokerage and loses her four days later to a prisoner exchange arranged by her own father. Around that, the Greek command argues about why its best soldier will not leave his tent, and the Trojan council argues about whether Helen is worth what she has already cost. Nobody wins anything. The one formal duel is called off because the fighters turn out to be cousins, the one great death is an ambush by twenty men against one who has taken his armour off, and the last word belongs to a diseased go-between.

Written c. 1601–02; first printed 1609.
