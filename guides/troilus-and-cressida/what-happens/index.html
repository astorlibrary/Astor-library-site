Troilus and Cressida · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The Prologue, Troilus and Pandarus

An armed Prologue announces that the play starts in the middle of the war and asks the audience for nothing. Troilus then refuses to go to the field because he is in love; Cressida listens to her uncle advertise Troilus for a whole afternoon and decides to hold off; and in the Greek camp Ulysses explains that seven years have produced nothing because degree has been abandoned, before proposing a rigged lottery that will put Ajax against Hector and sting Achilles out of his tent.

Prologue
Thirty-one lines on the sixty-nine ships, the six gates of Troy and a play that leaps over the beginning of the story.
1.1
Troilus tells Pandarus he is too weak with love to fight, and complains that he cannot reach Cressida except through her uncle.
1.2
Pandarus praises Troilus to Cressida, then names the Trojan lords as they come back from the field; left alone, she explains why she will show nothing.
1.3
Agamemnon and Nestor make the failure sound noble; Ulysses diagnoses it as the collapse of rank, and Hector’s challenge gives him a way to use it.

Act II The Trojan and Greek councils

Ajax beats Thersites and learns nothing; the Trojan council debates whether to send Helen back; the Greek generals find Achilles still refusing to come out. Hector proves that keeping Helen is wrong in law, nature and arithmetic, and then votes to keep her. Ulysses works Ajax up with flattery until he is ready to be used. On both sides of the plain, men reach a decision their own reasoning has just demolished.

2.1
Ajax strikes Thersites for not reading him the proclamation; Achilles arrives and the railing spreads to him.
2.2
Hector argues that Helen is not worth the cost, Troilus that worth is whatever men put on it; Cassandra interrupts to say Troy will burn, and Hector takes Troilus’s side.
2.3
Thersites curses the whole camp; Achilles will not come out, and Ulysses begins praising Ajax to his face to make Achilles hear it.

Act III The lovers’ night, and the exchange agreed

Pandarus flirts through Helen’s music room on an errand, brings the lovers together in his orchard and takes them to a bed, hearing them swear vows in which each names the proverb their name will become. In the scene after, Calchas asks the Greeks for his daughter in exchange for the prisoner Antenor, and gets her, before either lover knows the request has been made.

3.1
Pandarus makes his excuses to Paris and Helen, sings them a song about love, and covers for Troilus’s absence from supper.
3.2
Troilus and Cressida meet; she confesses she has loved him for months, and all three swear the oaths that will make their names by-words.
3.3
Cressida is promised to the Greeks for Antenor; the generals walk past Achilles, and Ulysses tells him that time throws good deeds into a wallet behind its back.

Act IV The exchange, and a duel called off

Cressida is woken, told, and handed across in a single morning. Troilus gives her a sleeve, takes her glove, and asks her four times to be true. In the Greek camp the whole command kisses her in turn, and Ulysses dismisses her in ten lines. Hector and Ajax then fight to no result because they are cousins, the two armies eat together, and Ulysses offers to walk Troilus to the tent where Diomedes will spend the night.

4.1
Aeneas and Diomedes swear to kill each other later; asked who deserves Helen, Diomedes says she has cost a Greek life for every false drop in her veins.
4.2
The lovers part at dawn; Aeneas knocks, and Cressida learns she is to be traded and swears she will not go.
4.3
A dozen lines in the street: Paris tells Troilus to fetch her, and Troilus says he will deliver her like a priest at an altar.
4.4
Troilus and Cressida exchange tokens; he asks her to be true so often that she asks whether he loves her at all.
4.5
Cressida is kissed by the Greek command; Hector and Ajax break off as kin, the sides feast together, and Ulysses offers Troilus a place to watch from.

Act V Cressida and Diomedes, and Hector’s death

Ten short scenes. Troilus watches Cressida give his sleeve to Diomedes while Ulysses watches Troilus and Thersites watches all three. Hector goes out although his wife, his sister and his father have begged him to stay, kills a man for the armour he is wearing, and is caught with his own off by Achilles and the Myrmidons. Troy keeps the field and has lost everything, and Pandarus is left alone on stage to make his will.

5.1
Thersites brings a letter from Hecuba that keeps Achilles out of the battle; the Trojans come to supper, and Diomedes slips away to Calchas.
5.2
Cressida gives Diomedes the sleeve; Troilus, hidden with Ulysses, refuses at first to believe that the woman he is looking at is Cressida.
5.3
Andromache, Cassandra and Priam beg Hector to stay in; he goes, and Troilus tears up a letter from Cressida without answering it.
5.4–5.7
Excursions, insults and missed meetings: Thersites talks his way out of a fight, Troilus loses his horse to Diomedes, and Achilles instructs his Myrmidons to ring Hector and kill him.
5.8–5.10
Hector takes off his armour and is surrounded; his body is tied to a horse’s tail, Troilus carries the news back, and Pandarus addresses the theatre.
