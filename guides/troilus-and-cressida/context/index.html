Troilus and Cressida · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1385
Chaucer finishes Troilus and Criseyde The long poem that gives Shakespeare the lovers, the uncle and the exchange, and treats all three with a tenderness the play removes.
1474
Caxton prints the Recuyell of the Historyes of Troye The first book printed in English, and the source for most of the war material, including the embassy, the duel and Hector’s death.
1513
Lydgate’s Troy Book printed Pynson’s edition of a poem written a century earlier for Prince Hal; behind the play’s Trojan council and the stress on Hector’s courtesy.
1598
Chapman’s Seven Books of the Iliads The first substantial Homer in English, which put the Greek heroes in front of a London reader at the moment the play was being planned.
1599
A rival Troilus for the Admiral’s Men Henslowe pays Dekker and Chettle for a play on the same story; a fragment of its plot survives, and nothing of its text.
1602
Probably written The usual dating puts it with Hamlet and the other plays of the turn of the century, though no record fixes the year or a first performance.
1603
Entered in the Stationers’ Register James Roberts enters it on 7 February as acted by the Lord Chamberlain’s men, and then does not print it.
1609
Quarto printed, in two states Bonian and Walley print it; some copies advertise performance at the Globe, others replace the title page and add a preface claiming it was never staged.
1623
First Folio Printed between the histories and the tragedies, left out of the catalogue at the front of the volume, and carrying the Prologue for the first time.
1679
Dryden’s Truth Found Too Late A rewriting that makes Cressida faithful and has her kill herself to prove it, and keeps the play off the stage in its own form for two centuries.
1912
William Poel stages it in London The Elizabethan Stage Society production at King’s Hall, Covent Garden, the first professional performance of Shakespeare’s text in England since the seventeenth century.
1938
Michael Macowan’s modern dress production At the Westminster Theatre, with the Greeks in service uniform and Thersites as a war correspondent, opening as the Munich crisis broke.
1960
Peter Hall and John Barton at Stratford Played on a square of sand with the armies half naked; the production that established the play in the modern repertory.

Places

Troy
The besieged city. The play ends with Troilus promising revenge and the walls still standing.
Tenedos
The island where the Prologue lands the Greek fleet before it pitches its tents on the Dardan plain.
Sparta
Menelaus’s kingdom and the marriage Paris broke; the Trojan council of 2.2 is about whether to send Helen back to it.
Ithaca
Agamemnon calls Ulysses the Prince of Ithaca. The island he spends the Odyssey getting back to is a title here and nothing else.
Bankside, London
One state of the 1609 quarto says the play was acted at the Globe; the other prints a preface swearing it never was.
