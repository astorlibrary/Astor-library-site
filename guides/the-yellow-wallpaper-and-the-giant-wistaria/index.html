Charlotte Perkins Gilman

The Yellow Wall-Paper and The Giant Wistaria

A study guide

A woman is brought to a rented country house to recover from what her husband, a physician, calls a slight hysterical tendency. She is forbidden to work or write, put in a top-floor room with barred windows, and left with nothing to look at but the wallpaper. She looks at it. What begins as irritation becomes study, then a pattern, then a figure behind the pattern, then a plan. The companion story in this volume, The Giant Wistaria, puts a second silenced woman in a second old house and lets a party of summer tenants find what is left of her.

Written The Yellow Wall-Paper in two days at Pasadena in June 1890; The Giant Wistaria a little earlier; first published 1892.
