William Shakespeare

Shakespeare’s Sonnets

A study guide

A poet spends seventeen poems telling a beautiful young man to have a child, then changes his argument: the poems themselves will keep him. What follows is a long record of that claim under pressure from absence, from a rival poet, from the young man’s own behaviour, and from a woman the poet desires and does not admire. The sequence ends with poems about lust, lying and self-disgust that are as plain as the early ones are ornate. Read in the 1609 order, it is the most sustained piece of writing Shakespeare left outside the plays, and the only one written in the first person.

Written Between about 1591 and 1604, over more than a decade; first printed 1609.
