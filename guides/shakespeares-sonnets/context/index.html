Shakespeare’s Sonnets · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1591
Sidney’s Astrophil and Stella printed The sonnet sequence becomes the fashionable English form almost overnight; Daniel, Drayton, Spenser and others follow within five years.
1593
Venus and Adonis published Shakespeare’s first printed book, dedicated to the Earl of Southampton, from the period when the earliest sonnets were probably written.
1594
The Rape of Lucrece published A second dedication to Southampton, warmer than the first, and the last time Shakespeare put his name to a dedication.
1598
Francis Meres names the sonnets Palladis Tamia refers to sugared sonnets circulating among Shakespeare’s private friends, so a substantial group existed in manuscript.
1599
Sonnets 138 and 144 printed in The Passionate Pilgrim The Passionate Pilgrim prints early versions of sonnets 138 and 144 under Shakespeare’s name.
1609
Thomas Thorpe publishes the quarto SHAKE-SPEARES SONNETS is entered in the Stationers’ Register on 20 May and published with the dedication to Mr. W.H. It was not reprinted for thirty-one years.
1640
Benson’s edition rearranges the poems John Benson reorders the sequence, runs poems together under invented titles and changes some pronouns from male to female.
1780
Malone restores the 1609 text Edmond Malone prints the sonnets in their quarto order with numbers and notes, the basis of every later edition.
1793
Steevens refuses to reprint them George Steevens leaves the sonnets out of his edition of Shakespeare, saying no act of parliament could make readers face them: the clearest record of eighteenth-century embarrassment.
1889
Wilde’s The Portrait of Mr. W. H. A story about the search for the young man that is also a defence of reading the poems as love poems between men.
1944
The Variorum edition Hyder Rollins collects three centuries of commentary in two volumes and shows how little the biographical questions have been settled.
1997
Duncan-Jones’s Arden edition Katherine Duncan-Jones argues that the 1609 quarto was authorised by Shakespeare, reopening a question that had been treated as closed.

Places

Stationers' Hall, London
Where Thomas Thorpe entered the sonnets in the register on 20 May 1609.
St Paul's Churchyard, London
The bookselling quarter; William Aspley sold one issue of the 1609 quarto here.
The Globe, Bankside
Shakespeare’s company playhouse from 1599, and the public trade the speaker calls a stain on his name.
Titchfield, Hampshire
Seat of the Earl of Southampton, dedicatee of the narrative poems and one candidate for the young man.
Wilton House, Wiltshire
Seat of the Earls of Pembroke; William Herbert is the other long-standing candidate for Mr. W.H.
Stratford-upon-Avon
Where Shakespeare’s family remained through the years the sequence was written.
