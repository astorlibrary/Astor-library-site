Gulliver’s Travels · Jonathan Swift

Context

Dates

1667
Jonathan Swift born in Dublin Educated at Kilkenny and Trinity College, Dublin, and later secretary to Sir William Temple.
1699
The book’s date for Gulliver’s first voyage: 4 May 1699 The fictional first voyage is dated 4 May 1699; the book dates every departure.
1704
A Tale of a Tub and The Battle of the Books published Swift’s first sustained attack on false learning and modern authorship, and an anonymous one.
1713
Swift installed as Dean of St Patrick’s, Dublin The post he held for the rest of his life, and the source of the name by which Ireland knew him.
1724
Swift writes The Drapier’s Letters Swift’s campaign against Wood’s halfpence, written in the voice of a Dublin draper: another invented plain-speaking narrator.
1726
Travels into Several Remote Nations of the World published Benjamin Motte issues it in two volumes on 28 October, with Lemuel Gulliver on the title page.
1727
Keys, sequels and imitations appear Explanatory ‘keys’, spurious continuations and parodies follow within months of publication.
1729
Swift’s A Modest Proposal published The same deadpan applied to Irish famine, by a narrator whose arithmetic is as careful as Gulliver’s.
1735
Faulkner’s Dublin edition The Travels appear in volume III of Swift’s collected works, with the letter to Sympson added.
1745
Swift dies in Dublin He left the bulk of his estate to found a hospital for the mentally ill.
1939
Fleischer Studios’ animated Gulliver’s Travels One of the best-known screen versions, and a clear case of the book surviving as Lilliput alone.
1996
Television adaptation of all four voyages Unusually among adaptations, it keeps Part IV and Gulliver’s damaged return.

Places

Nottinghamshire
Gulliver’s father’s small estate, and the county he retires to at the end.
Emanuel College, Cambridge
Where Gulliver was sent at fourteen before being apprenticed to a London surgeon.
Leyden
Where he studied physic for two years and seven months, the standard training for a ship’s surgeon.
Bristol
The port from which the Antelope sails for the South Sea in 1699.
Nangasac
Nagasaki, the one real country Gulliver visits, reached from Luggnagg in Part III.
Amsterdam
His route home from Japan, and the port whose Dutch traders he treats with a marked and pointed hostility.
