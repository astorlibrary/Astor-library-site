The Winter's Tale · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I Leontes’s jealousy

Polixenes has been visiting for nine months and means to leave; Hermione persuades him to stay because her husband asked her to. Watching them talk, Leontes decides they are lovers, and there is no Iago, no handkerchief and no evidence of any kind. By the end of the act he has ordered his most trusted counsellor to poison a king.

1.1
Camillo and Archidamus praise the friendship of the kings and the promise of the young prince.
1.2
Hermione wins the argument her husband could not, and Leontes watches her hand in another man’s and goes mad in public.

Act II A queen arrested and an oracle sent for

Hermione is taken from her son in the middle of a ghost story and imprisoned; she gives birth in the cell. Paulina carries the baby to the king expecting the sight of it to work, and is called a witch for her trouble. Leontes has already sent to Delphos, and announces that he will believe the god whatever the god says.

2.1
Mamillius begins a winter’s tale and is interrupted by his father’s guards; Hermione is charged and taken away.
2.2
Paulina learns of the birth and decides to carry the child to its father herself.
2.3
Paulina lays the baby at the king’s feet and argues; Antigonus is sworn to carry it to a desert place and leave it.

Act III The oracle, the deaths, and the bear

Hermione defends herself in the clearest speech in the play, the oracle clears her in a single sentence, and Leontes rejects it out loud. His son dies within a minute of the blasphemy and his wife within two. Then the play changes countries, a storm sinks a ship, a bear takes Antigonus, and a shepherd finds a baby and says the first kind thing anyone has said for three acts.

3.1
Cleomenes and Dion describe the sweet air and the deafening voice of the oracle, and hope for a good outcome.
3.2
Hermione speaks for her honour, the oracle is read, Leontes denies it, and Mamillius and Hermione are both reported dead.
3.3
Antigonus leaves the baby on the Bohemian coast and exits pursued by a bear; a shepherd and his son find the child and the wreck.

Act IV Sixteen years, a sheep-shearing and a pedlar

Time appears as a chorus, turns the glass and moves the play on. In Bohemia the foundling is a shepherd’s daughter presiding over a feast, a prince is courting her under a false name, and a thief with a ballad-sheet is working the crowd. Then Polixenes tears off his disguise and threatens everyone, and the young couple run for Sicilia with the help of the man who fled it.

4.1
The chorus turns his hourglass, apologises for the leap and sets the second half in motion.
4.2
Polixenes refuses Camillo leave to go home, and they agree to go in disguise to the shepherd’s cottage where the prince keeps disappearing.
4.3
A pedlar and former courtier sings on the road, picks the Clown’s pocket by pretending to be robbed, and goes to the feast.
4.4
Perdita gives out flowers and argues about art and nature with a disguised king who then forbids the match and threatens her with death.

Act V The recognitions, and the statue

Leontes has spent sixteen years doing penance under Paulina’s supervision. The lovers arrive, the pursuing king arrives, the tokens are produced and the reunion of father and daughter is deliberately kept offstage and reported by gentlemen. The play saves its one visible wonder for last, in a chapel, with a curtain and a piece of music.

5.1
Leontes is urged to remarry and Paulina forbids it; Florizel and Perdita arrive claiming a diplomatic errand.
5.2
Three gentlemen describe the discovery of Perdita’s identity to one another, and the Clown becomes a gentleman born.
5.3
Paulina draws a curtain on a figure of Hermione aged sixteen years, requires the onlookers to awake their faith, and calls for music.
