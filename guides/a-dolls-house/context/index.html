A Doll's House · Henrik Ibsen

Context

Dates

1828
Henrik Ibsen born at Skien His father’s business failed when he was a boy, and money and social standing recur throughout his work.
1867
Peer Gynt published The last of the great verse plays before Ibsen turned to contemporary prose drama.
1877
Pillars of Society published The first of the modern prose plays, and the beginning of the sequence this play belongs to.
1879
Et dukkehjem published in Copenhagen Published on 4 December in a first printing of 8,000 copies.
1879
First performance at the Royal Theatre, Copenhagen On 21 December, with Betty Hennings as Nora.
1880
Ibsen supplies an alternative ending for the German stage In it Nora does not leave; his letter of February 1880 explains that he wrote it himself only to stop somebody else doing it worse.
1881
Ibsen’s Ghosts published Often read as his reply to those who had wanted Nora to stay: a marriage nobody leaves, and what happens in it.
1889
First public British production At the Novelty Theatre, London, on 7 June, with Janet Achurch as Nora and Charles Charrington as Helmer, in William Archer’s translation.
1891
Ibsen returns to Norway After twenty-seven years living in Italy and Germany.
1906
Ibsen dies in Kristiania By then his contemporary plays were established across Europe and had reshaped what a serious stage play could be about.
1910
R. Farquharson Sharp’s translation published Issued by J. M. Dent in Everyman’s Library; it is the English text these quotations are checked against.

Places

Skien, Norway
Ibsen’s birthplace, where his father’s business failed when he was a boy and taught him early what respectability and debt can do to a family.
Rome
Where Ibsen was living when he began the play; he spent twenty-seven years out of Norway.
Amalfi
Where he worked on the play through the summer of 1879, on the coast south of Naples.
Copenhagen
The Royal Theatre, where the play was first performed on 21 December 1879.
Kristiania (Oslo)
The Norwegian capital, and the city Ibsen returned to in 1891 and died in.
London
The Novelty Theatre, where the first public British production opened in June 1889 and started the English argument about Nora.

Critics

George Bernard Shaw

The Quintessence of Ibsenism (1891)

Shaw read Nora’s departure as the end of the idealism that holds the respectable home together: when she discovers that her marriage has been a pretence, the only duty left to her is the duty to herself.

Henrik Ibsen

Speech to the Norwegian Women’s Rights League (1898)

Thanked by the League in 1898, Ibsen disclaimed the honour of having worked for women’s rights on purpose. He said he was not even sure what the movement was, and that his task had been the description of humanity.
