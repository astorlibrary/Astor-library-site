A Doll's House · Henrik Ibsen

What happens

Act I Christmas Eve, with a tree, a promotion and a bond

Nora comes home with parcels and is teased about spending. Mrs Linde arrives looking for work, and Nora tells her the secret she has kept for eight years. Krogstad, who lent the money and holds a bond with a forged signature on it, comes to ask for his job at the bank and makes plain what he will do if he loses it.

Act I, the parcels
The skylark and the squirrel, the macaroons hidden in a pocket, and a household about to have money for the first time.
Act I, the confidence
Nora tells Mrs Linde that she found the money for the journey to Italy herself; Torvald believes her father paid.
Act I, Krogstad’s call
The lender explains what the forged signature is worth to him.
Act I, the curtain
Helmer lectures on how a deceitful parent poisons a household, and Nora sends the children away.

Act II Christmas Day, Doctor Rank and the letter in the box

The tree stands bare in the corner and the letter has not yet come. Nora tries to get Krogstad’s dismissal reversed and fails on a point of Helmer’s pride; Doctor Rank makes his declaration at the worst possible moment; and the letter goes into the box, which Helmer keeps the key to. Nora dances the tarantella to stop him opening it.

Act II, the costume
The fancy-dress costume needs mending, and Mrs Linde begins asking questions about Doctor Rank.
Act II, the appeal
Helmer refuses to keep Krogstad on, and the reason turns out to be that the man calls him by his Christian name.
Act II, Rank
The black cross, and a request Nora can no longer make once he has spoken.
Act II, the letter
Krogstad drops his letter in the box; Nora dances to hold her husband’s attention for twenty-four hours.

Act III After the ball Helmer reads the letter, and Nora leaves

Mrs Linde and Krogstad settle their own past in ten minutes and she stops him withdrawing the letter, on the grounds that the Helmers need the truth more than they need safety. Helmer reads it, calls his wife a hypocrite and a criminal, reads the second letter, declares himself saved, and finds that the crisis has gone the wrong way round.

Act III, the wreckage
Two people who lost each other for money agree to join forces.
Act III, the first letter
Helmer’s fear for his own position, and his plan to keep up appearances.
Act III, the second letter
The bond is returned, Helmer forgives, and Nora changes her dress.
Act III, the table
The first serious conversation of the marriage, conducted sitting down, and the door below.
