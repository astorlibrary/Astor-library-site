A Doll's House · Henrik Ibsen

Characters

Nora Helmer

Torvald’s wife, mother of three; first appears Act I

She has run a secret business for eight years, copying documents at night behind a locked door and calling it fun, while playing the spendthrift child her husband finds charming. The play’s argument is that the performance and the competence are the same woman, and that nobody in the house has ever been curious enough to notice.

Torvald Helmer

A lawyer, newly appointed manager of the bank; first appears Act I

He is not a villain and is barely even unkind; he is a man who has arranged his household as a compliment to himself and cannot imagine it any other way. His refusal to keep Krogstad on turns out to rest on the fact that the man uses his first name, and that detail is the whole diagnosis.

Nils Krogstad

A subordinate at the bank, and Nora’s creditor; first appears Act I

He committed one forgery, was never prosecuted and has been paying for it socially ever since, which makes him the play’s best witness on how respectability actually works. He is threatening for two acts and then, given one thing he wants, becomes reasonable in a page.

Mrs Linde

Nora’s old schoolfriend, a widow looking for work; first appears Act I

She married for money to keep a mother and two brothers alive, was widowed with nothing, and has kept herself by her own work ever since. Her decision to let the letter stand is the hinge of the last act, and she takes it for a reason she states plainly: this house has been living on concealment.

Doctor Rank

The family’s friend and daily visitor; first appears Act I

He is dying of a disease he attributes to his father’s manner of living, and he keeps the fact from Helmer because Helmer cannot bear ugliness. His declaration to Nora removes the one source of money she had left, and his exit is arranged by visiting card so that nobody has to watch.

Anne

The children’s nurse, formerly Nora’s; first appears Act I

She gave up her own child to take a position, which is the same transaction Nora is about to make and the play puts them side by side without comment. She has two short scenes, and they show in advance what the ending will cost.
