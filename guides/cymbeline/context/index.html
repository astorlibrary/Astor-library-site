Cymbeline · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1353
Boccaccio finishes the Decameron The ninth tale of the second day has a merchant wager on his wife’s chastity and a rival win it by being carried into her bedroom in a chest.
1587
Holinshed’s Chronicles, second edition Shakespeare takes Cymbeline, Cassibelan and the quarrel over the Roman tribute from the British books, and reorders the reigns to suit himself.
1608
The King’s Men take the Blackfriars An indoor playhouse with descent machinery in the roof, of the kind the Jupiter scene asks for.
1610
Cymbeline written Composed alongside Pericles, The Winter’s Tale and The Tempest, at the end of Shakespeare’s working life.
1611
Simon Forman writes down what he saw His notebook records the wager, the man in the trunk and the cave in Wales. Forman died that September, so the play had been staged by then.
1623
Printed in the First Folio No quarto survives. The Folio classes it as a tragedy and prints it last, closing the volume.
1634
Played at court for Charles I The Master of the Revels recorded a performance by the King’s players on New Year’s Day, and noted that the king liked it.
1682
D’Urfey rewrites it as The Injured Princess Thomas D’Urfey’s version, subtitled The Fatal Wager, kept the stage in place of Shakespeare’s text for the best part of a century.
1761
Garrick brings the play back to Drury Lane David Garrick played Posthumus himself and returned something close to Shakespeare’s text to the repertory.
1896
Ellen Terry plays Imogen at the Lyceum Henry Irving took Iachimo. Bernard Shaw reviewed it, praised Terry and attacked almost everything else in the play.
1937
Shaw rewrites the last act Cymbeline Refinished replaces the cascade of recognitions with a short scene in which Imogen is markedly less pleased to have her husband back.
2016
The RSC makes Cymbeline a queen Melly Still’s production cast Gillian Bevan as a ruling queen and set the British scenes in a shut-in, decaying country.

Places

Milford Haven
The Welsh port named in the forged letter. Imogen rides for it and never arrives; the Roman legions land there instead.
The Preseli Hills
The mountain country above the haven, where Belarius keeps his cave and the princes hunt for the pot.
Rome
Philario’s house, where the wager is drawn up by lawyers and Posthumus is talked out of his marriage.
Lud's Town
The legendary name for London. Cloten swears he will set the brothers’ heads on its gates, and the play ends marching there to ratify the peace.
The Blackfriars playhouse
The indoor theatre the King’s Men had in hand by 1609, with the machinery to lower Jupiter and his eagle through the roof.
