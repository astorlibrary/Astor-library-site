Cymbeline · William Shakespeare

Characters

Imogen

Cymbeline’s daughter and heir, married to Posthumus; first appears 1.1

She is the most clear-sighted person in the play, though even she mistakes Cloten’s headless body for her husband’s. She stands up to her father in the first scene, refuses Cloten to his face, reads her own death warrant and hands over the sword herself, walks two days into Wales on her own feet, and cooks for the men who turn out to be her brothers. She is slandered, sentenced, drugged and mourned, and she survives all of it.

Posthumus Leonatus

An orphan raised at Cymbeline’s court, and Imogen’s husband; first appears 1.1

Everyone praises him in the first scene, and by the third act he has done the worst thing in the play. He stakes his wife’s fidelity on a bet with a man he met at dinner, believes a bracelet over everything he knows of her, and posts an order to cut her throat. The fifth act is given over to his repentance, and he opens it with the bloody cloth in his hand, wishing the gods had punished him instead of her.

Iachimo

A Roman gentleman who bets he can seduce Imogen; first appears 1.4

He tries the direct route first, is rebuffed, talks his way back into favour, and switches to a trunk. He is dangerous because he never lies about a detail: the tapestry, the chimneypiece, the silver Cupids and the mole are all exactly as he says. He builds a falsehood entirely out of true statements, and by the last act he cannot stop confessing it.

Cymbeline

King of Britain; first appears 1.1

He banishes his son-in-law, loses his daughter, lets his wife run his foreign policy, and spends the middle of the play offstage being wrong about all three. His judgement is not so much restored as overtaken: the Queen dies confessing, his stolen sons walk back into the tent, and at the end he pays the tribute he went to war over, having won the war.

The Queen

Cymbeline’s second wife, and Cloten’s mother; first appears 1.1

She keeps a doctor on hand for poisons, assures Imogen she is not the stepmother of the stories, and means to put her own son on the throne over her stepdaughter’s body. Her speech on Britain’s rocks and roaring waters is the most patriotic thing anybody says in the play. She dies raving, having confessed that she never loved her husband and married his crown.

Cloten

The Queen’s son by her first husband, and Imogen’s suitor; first appears 1.2

He is stupid, violent and obsessed with his own rank, and two lords insult him in asides for a whole scene without his noticing. He is also the engine of the worst thing that happens on stage: told that his rival’s poorest coat is worth more than he is, he puts that coat on to commit a rape and a murder, and loses his head to a boy who has no idea who he is.

Pisanio

Posthumus’s servant, left behind in Britain with Imogen; first appears 1.1

He is handed a letter ordering him to murder his mistress and works out, in the time it takes to read it, that obeying would be the dishonest thing. He shows her the letter, will not take the sword she offers him, invents Fidele, sends a bloody cloth to Rome as false proof of her death, and then holds his tongue through two acts, even when the King threatens him with torture.

Belarius

A lord banished twenty years ago, living in Wales under the name Morgan; first appears 3.3

Two men swore he was in league with Rome, Cymbeline believed them, and he answered by carrying the king’s infant sons out of the nursery. He has raised them well and knows it, and he spends the play watching an inherited nature break through the life he gave them. Handing them back in the last scene costs him the only family he has.

Guiderius

Cymbeline’s elder lost son, raised as Polydore; first appears 3.3

He is the blunt one. He tells Belarius the cave is a debtor’s prison, declines to be impressed when Cloten announces he is the Queen’s son, cuts his head off and throws it in the creek, and says at his own trial that he did it and is glad of it. He cannot sing, so he speaks his half of the dirge over a sister he does not recognise.

Arviragus

Cymbeline’s younger lost son, raised as Cadwal; first appears 3.3

He is the one who talks about the flowers. Where his brother is short, he is lavish: he offers to bleed a whole parish of Clotens to bring the colour back to Fidele’s cheek, and lists the pale primrose, the azured harebell and the leaf of eglantine he means to strew on the grave. He carries his sister’s body on stage in his arms and is told off for wench-like words.
