Cymbeline · William Shakespeare

Form and language

Stage property

An object handled on stage that carries far more weight than its use in the scene explains.

A ring, a bracelet and a box travel the length of the play. The bracelet comes off Imogen’s arm in the dark in 2.2 and is held out again in 5.5 by a kneeling man; the box everybody calls poison is what lets her be buried and then get up.

It is a manacle of love; I'll place it Upon this fairest prisoner.

Posthumus, 1.1.128

Inventory

A list allowed to run on past the point of usefulness, so that the accumulation itself becomes the effect.

Iachimo recites the tapestry of Cleopatra, the chimneypiece of Dian bathing, the fretted roof and the two winking Cupids on the andirons. He admits none of it proves anything, and Posthumus is worn down by the sheer length of the list before the bracelet appears.

Here's a voucher Stronger than ever law could make; this secret Will force him think I have pick'd the lock and ta'en The treasure of her honour.

Iachimo, 2.2.40

Song

A sung passage standing outside the dialogue, free to say what the speaking characters cannot.

Two songs sit either side of the play’s middle. Cloten pays for the lark at heaven’s gate to wake a woman who loathes him, and the brothers speak “Fear no more”, without singing it, over a sister they believe is a dead boy.

Hark, hark! the lark at heaven's gate sings, And Phœbus 'gins arise,

A Musician, 2.3.17

Foils

A character built to throw another’s choices into relief by making different ones.

Cloten and Posthumus are the same build, want the same woman and end up in the same suit. In 4.1 Cloten measures his legs against his rival’s and finds them better, and in 4.2 Imogen looks at the body and cannot tell them apart.

O blessed that I might not! I chose an eagle, And did avoid a puttock.

Imogen, 1.1.147

Prophecy

A statement about the future that shapes the action by being believed, whether or not it is true.

Jupiter leaves a tablet about a lion’s whelp and a lopped cedar on the sleeping Posthumus. It stays unreadable until a soothsayer takes apart Leo-natus and mollis aer in 5.5, by which time everything it promises has already happened.

Whom best I love I cross; to make my gift, The more delay'd, delighted.

Jupiter, 5.4.102

The set piece

A self-contained episode built to a single effect and complete in itself.

The Leonati ghosts circle the prisoner in rhymed fourteeners and Jupiter descends on an eagle throwing a thunderbolt. Nothing in 5.4 moves the plot except the tablet he leaves behind, and the scene needs machinery in the theatre roof.

Fear no more the heat o' th' sun, Nor the furious winter's rages … Golden lads and girls all must, As chimney-sweepers, come to dust.

Guiderius, 4.2.288

Recognition

The moment a character’s real identity or history becomes known, and the scene has to absorb the shock of it.

The last scene does this two dozen times over: the Queen’s confession, the ring, the page who is a wife, the husband who strikes her, the headless man, the banished lord, the stolen princes and the mole on a neck, each landing before the one before it has been taken in.

The heaviness and guilt within my bosom Takes off my manhood. I have belied a lady, The Princess of this country

Iachimo, 5.2.1
