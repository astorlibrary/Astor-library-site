Cymbeline · William Shakespeare

Themes

Proof

Iachimo’s report is accurate in every particular and wrong in its conclusion. The bracelet was on her arm, the mole is under her breast, the andirons are two winking Cupids of silver, and none of it touches the question Posthumus actually asked.

Objects carry the case in the first half and are overturned in the second, when a second mole, on Guiderius’s neck, gives a father back his son.

It is a manacle of love; I'll place it Upon this fairest prisoner.

Posthumus, 1.1.128

But I make my wager rather against your confidence than her reputation

Iachimo, 1.4.92

Belief and doubt

Posthumus goes from staking everything on his wife’s honour to vowing to tear her limb-meal inside a single scene, and the turn happens on a bracelet he has already been told could have been stolen by a servant.

Philario argues for doubt in 2.4 and is brushed aside; by the fifth act Posthumus is making the same case against himself and asking for a cord or a knife.

It is a manacle of love; I'll place it Upon this fairest prisoner.

Posthumus, 1.1.128

O blessed that I might not! I chose an eagle, And did avoid a puttock.

Imogen, 1.1.147

Disguise

Imogen becomes Fidele, Belarius answers to Morgan, the princes are Polydore and Cadwal, Cloten dies in Posthumus’s suit and Posthumus fights in a peasant’s. Almost nobody in Wales is called by their own name.

Disguise keeps people alive until Cloten borrows a suit, after which it starts killing them; the last scene works by taking every borrowed name back.

Our Tarquin thus Did softly press the rushes ere he waken'd The chastity he wounded.

Iachimo, 2.2.13

She hath been reading late The tale of Tereus; here the leaf's turn'd down Where Philomel gave up.

Iachimo, 2.2.45

Service and loyalty

Pisanio is ordered to commit murder by a master he loves, and decides that keeping faith means refusing. The Queen tries to buy him with preferment, Cloten tries to frighten him with a drawn sword, and he goes on serving Imogen through both.

Posthumus himself supplies the verdict in 5.1, saying that every good servant does not all commands; by then Pisanio has been proved right for two acts.

'tis slander, Whose edge is sharper than the sword, whose tongue Outvenoms all the worms of Nile

Pisanio, 3.4.33

You must forget to be a woman; change Command into obedience

Pisanio, 3.4.166

Royal birth

Two boys brought up in a cave stand and fight like princes the moment a battle reaches them, and Belarius says openly that he has never been able to hide it. Shakespeare takes it for granted that breeding will out, and the cave has done them no harm at all.

What starts as Belarius’s private astonishment in 3.3 becomes state business in 5.5, when the succession he stole is handed back over a mantle and a mole.

O blessed that I might not! I chose an eagle, And did avoid a puttock.

Imogen, 1.1.147

His mean'st garment That ever hath but clipp'd his body, is dearer In my respect, than all the hairs above thee

Imogen, 2.3.138

Britain, Rome and the tribute

Cymbeline refuses three thousand pounds a year and goes to war over it, egged on by a queen with her own reasons and a stepson who thinks Britain is a world by itself. Britain wins the battle and then agrees to pay anyway.

Roman arrogance in 3.1 becomes Roman defeat in 5.2, and the settlement pays the money without giving back the victory.

Britain is a world by itself, and we will nothing pay for wearing our own noses.

Cloten, 3.1.12

I' th' world's volume Our Britain seems as of it, but not in't; In a great pool a swan's nest.

Imogen, 3.4.147

Pardon

Posthumus, standing over the man who destroyed his marriage, tells him the only power he has is to spare him. Cymbeline then pardons everybody in the tent, including the army he has just beaten and the boy who beheaded his stepson.

The play withholds punishment from almost everyone. The two who die, Cloten and the Queen, are the two who never ask for anything.

The heaviness and guilt within my bosom Takes off my manhood. I have belied a lady, The Princess of this country

Iachimo, 5.2.1

Whom best I love I cross; to make my gift, The more delay'd, delighted.

Jupiter, 5.4.102
