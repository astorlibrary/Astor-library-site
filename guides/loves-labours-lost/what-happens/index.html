Act I The oath, and Costard arrested

Navarre and three friends sign a three-year statute against sleep, food and the sight of women. Berowne objects to every clause, points out that a French embassy is already on its way, and signs anyway. Within minutes Costard is arrested for being taken with Jaquenetta, and Armado, who reported him, admits he wants her himself.

1.1
The King reads out the rules of his academy; Berowne argues against study, signs, and hears the first case brought under the new law.Key lines “Let fame, that all hunt after in their lives…” · “Navarre shall be the wonder of the world…” · “Small have continual plodders ever won, Save base authority from others’ books.” · “Necessity will make us all forsworn Three thousand…” · “I’ll lay my head to any good man’s…”
1.2
Armado spars with his page Moth, is given Costard to keep, trades a dozen short lines with Jaquenetta, and resolves to write a sonnet.Key line “Assist me, some extemporal god of rhyme…”

Act II The Princess arrives

The Princess of France arrives to settle a debt of a hundred thousand crowns and the ownership of Aquitaine. Navarre will not let her through the gate, so she negotiates standing outside it. Three of the men go to Boyet afterwards to ask the names of the women they have picked out, which tells the audience how much attention the courtship has had.

2.1
Boyet flatters, the Princess will have none of it, the Aquitaine accounts are disputed and adjourned, and three of the four men have to ask Boyet who their ladies are.Key lines “Beauty is bought by judgement of the eye…” · “’Tis deadly sin to keep that oath…” · “Did not I dance with you in Brabant once?” · “Why, all his behaviours did make their retire…”

Act III Costard carries two letters

Armado sends Costard with a letter for Jaquenetta. Berowne, five minutes later, sends the same man with a poem for Rosaline. Nobody checks. The scene ends with Berowne alone, working out in thirty lines of self-disgust how a man who spent Act I mocking lovers has become one, and deciding to write anyway.

3.1
Moth and Armado play with words until Costard is freed and hired twice over; Berowne admits he is in love and curses Cupid at length.Key lines “Remuneration”! O, that’s the Latin word for three farthings. · “This wimpled, whining, purblind, wayward boy, This Signior Junior, giant-dwarf, Dan Cupid”

Act IV The letters misdelivered, and the four men exposed

The Princess hunts a deer and is handed Armado’s letter by mistake. Holofernes and Nathaniel are handed Berowne’s and send it straight to the King. Then, in the park, three of the men in turn read their love poems aloud while the others listen from hiding, and Berowne, caught last, argues the oath away for all of them.

4.1
The Princess shoots for praise and knows it; Costard delivers Armado’s letter to her instead of Jaquenetta, and Boyet reads it out.Key line “Glory grows guilty of detested crimes…”
4.2
Holofernes praises the deer in Latin and English, extemporises a poem on the letter L, and hears Berowne’s sonnet read well enough to know it must go to the King.Key line “The preyful Princess pierced and pricked a pretty pleasing pricket”
4.3
King, Longaville and Dumaine each read a sonnet aloud and each is overheard; Berowne mocks all three, is exposed by his own torn letter, and turns the oath inside out.Key lines “From women’s eyes this doctrine I derive.” · “Let us once lose our oaths to find ourselves…”

4 key lines from Act IV

Act V The Russians, the Nine Worthies and Marcadé’s news

Holofernes casts the Nine Worthies. Warned in advance by Boyet, the women mask and swap the men’s gifts so that every disguised Russian courts the wrong woman. The pageant is heckled to a standstill, Costard announces Jaquenetta is pregnant, and Marcadé arrives with news that the Princess’s father is dead. The women leave, setting each man a year’s task.

5.1
Holofernes, Nathaniel, Armado, Moth, Costard and Dull agree on an entertainment, with the page complaining that the learned men eat words.Key line “They have been at a great feast of…”
5.2
The women exchange tokens and wreck the Muscovite masque; the pageant collapses under heckling; death arrives; four proposals are deferred for a twelvemonth, and two songs close the play.Key lines “That he should be my fool, and I his fate.” · “Henceforth my wooing mind shall be expressed In…” · “I am sorry, madam, for the news I…” · “A time, methinks, too short To make a world-without-end bargain in.” · “A jest’s prosperity lies in the ear Of…” · “Our wooing doth not end like an old play.” · “When icicles hang by the wall…”
