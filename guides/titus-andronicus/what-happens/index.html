Titus Andronicus · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I A funeral, an election and a sacrifice

Everything is set in motion in a single unbroken scene of nearly five hundred lines. Two brothers fight over the empire, the people offer it to Titus, and Titus hands it to Saturninus. Between those decisions he burns Tamora’s eldest son on his family tomb, stabs his own son Mutius for blocking a doorway, and watches the woman he has just wronged crowned Empress of Rome. By the end of the act every later murder has a cause.

1.1
Titus returns with his dead sons and his prisoners, sacrifices Alarbus, refuses the empire, gives it to Saturninus, kills Mutius in a quarrel over Lavinia, and looks on while Saturninus marries Tamora instead.

Act II The hunt, the pit and the forest

The court rides out to hunt and the play moves into the woods, where Aaron has buried a bag of gold and written a letter that will hang two innocent men. Bassianus is stabbed and dropped into a pit, Titus’s sons Quintus and Martius fall in after him and are arrested for the murder, and Lavinia is taken away by Chiron and Demetrius. Marcus finds her at the end, and can only talk round what he sees.

2.1
Aaron stops Chiron and Demetrius fighting over Lavinia and tells them to take her together during the hunt, in woods he calls ruthless, dreadful, deaf and dull.
2.2
Horns, hounds and morning greetings. Demetrius ends the scene telling his brother which doe they are actually hunting.
2.3
Bassianus is murdered and Lavinia dragged off; Aaron leads Quintus and Martius to the pit where the body lies and fetches the emperor to find them standing over it.
2.4
Chiron and Demetrius leave her mutilated and jeering at her to write down who did it. Marcus, alone with her, talks for forty lines about Philomela, lutes and rivers.

Act III The hand, and the fly

Titus pleads for his condemned sons to a street that has already emptied, then lies down and tells his griefs to the stones. Aaron arrives with the emperor’s offer: a hand for two lives. Titus lets him cut it off and gets back two heads and his own hand in scorn. He stops weeping, laughs, and takes his vow. The act closes on a family dinner and a killed fly.

3.1
Titus pleads, loses the argument to an empty street, gives his hand to Aaron, receives his sons’ heads, laughs, and swears revenge; Lucius is banished and goes to raise an army among the Goths.
3.2
At a banquet Titus reads Lavinia’s signs aloud, then attacks Marcus for killing a fly, and forgives him once Marcus says the fly looked like the empress’s Moor.

Act IV Names in the sand, a black child, arrows to the gods

Lavinia chases her nephew for his copy of Ovid, turns to the story of Philomela, and writes her attackers’ names in the sand with a staff held in her mouth. Tamora’s child is born black and Aaron kills the nurse to keep it. Titus, apparently mad, shoots petitions to the gods over the palace wall, and in secret begins to arrange what he intends to do.

4.1
Lavinia finds the tale of Tereus in the Metamorphoses, then writes Stuprum and two names in the sand. Marcus swears the family to revenge; Titus tells him the dam will wake.
4.2
The nurse brings Tamora’s black baby with orders to kill it. Aaron kills her instead, refuses his accomplices, and carries the child away to raise among the Goths.
4.3
Titus sends his kinsmen to dig for Justice in the underworld and to fish for her at sea, then has them shoot letters addressed to the gods into the emperor’s court.
4.4
The emperor rages over the arrows and orders the Clown who delivered a letter to be hanged. Tamora calms him and goes off to play Revenge at Titus’s house.

Act V The banquet

Lucius returns at the head of a Gothic army, takes Aaron prisoner, and buys a full confession with a promise to keep the baby alive. Tamora arrives at Titus’s study disguised as Revenge and leaves her sons behind as hostages to his madness. He cuts their throats, bakes them, serves them to their mother, kills Lavinia and then Tamora, and the survivors count what is left of Rome.

5.1
Caught with the child, Aaron trades the whole plot for the baby’s life and then lists his crimes with relish, wishing only that he had done ten thousand more.
5.2
Tamora comes disguised to Titus’s study; he plays along, keeps Chiron and Demetrius when she goes, binds them, and cuts their throats over a basin Lavinia holds between her stumps.
5.3
Titus serves the pie, kills Lavinia, names the pie’s contents, stabs Tamora and is killed by Saturninus, who is killed by Lucius. Lucius is made emperor, sentences Aaron to be buried alive, and orders Tamora thrown to the birds.
