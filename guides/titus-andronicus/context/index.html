Titus Andronicus · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

8
Ovid completes the Metamorphoses Book VI tells of Tereus, Philomela and the weaving that exposes him, and of the meal Procne serves afterwards. Shakespeare puts the book itself on stage in Act IV.
1581
Seneca His Tenne Tragedies printed in English Thomas Newton’s collection put Thyestes and its banquet of children into the hands of English playwrights and their audiences.
1592
Titus Andronicus written Usually dated to the early 1590s, and often called Shakespeare’s first tragedy. Some arguments for an earlier start rest on Jonson’s later joke about the play’s ageing admirers.
1594
Performed at the Rose on 24 January Philip Henslowe recorded the takings for Titus & ondronicus in his account book, marking it as a new play for Sussex’s Men.
1594
Entered in the Stationers’ Register and printed John Danter entered A Noble Roman Historye of Tytus Andronicus on 6 February and printed the quarto for Edward White and Thomas Millington. It is the first printing of any Shakespeare play, and one copy survives.
1600
Second quarto James Roberts printed Q2 for Edward White; Edward Allde printed a third quarto for White in 1611. Both show the play still selling twenty years on.
1614
Jonson jokes about it in Bartholomew Fair The Induction mocks playgoers who still call Titus Andronicus the best thing going, saying their judgement has stood still for twenty-five or thirty years.
1623
First Folio The play is printed among the tragedies, from a copy of Q3 marked up against a playhouse manuscript. The Folio alone prints the fly scene at 3.2.
1687
Ravenscroft’s adaptation Edward Ravenscroft rewrote it as Titus Andronicus, or The Rape of Lavinia, and his preface began the long tradition of doubting that Shakespeare wrote all of it.
1955
Peter Brook directs Olivier and Vivien Leigh At Stratford, with stylised blood and Laurence Olivier as Titus. The production is generally credited with making the play stageable again after two centuries of embarrassment.
1987
Deborah Warner at the Swan With Brian Cox as Titus, Estelle Kohler as Tamora and Sonia Ritter as Lavinia. The staging let the play be funny, and found that the laughter made the horror worse.
1999
Julie Taymor’s film Titus Anthony Hopkins and Jessica Lange, in a design that mixes Roman architecture with twentieth-century fascism. Harry Lennix’s Aaron brought the part to a wide audience.

Places

Rome
The Capitol, the Pantheon and the Andronici tomb. The play’s Rome belongs to no datable reign, which leaves it a city of rites, elections and burials with no year attached.
The Rose, Bankside
Where Henslowe recorded a performance on 24 January 1594, the earliest firm evidence that the play was being acted.
Stationers' Hall, London
Where John Danter entered the play on 6 February 1594, two weeks after the Rose performance and just before printing it.
Folger Shakespeare Library, Washington
Holds the only surviving copy of the 1594 quarto, found in Sweden in 1904 and sold to Henry Clay Folger the year after.
Swan Theatre, Stratford-upon-Avon
Where Deborah Warner staged the play in 1987, in the production that settled its modern reputation.
