Mrs Dalloway · Virginia Woolf

Context

Dates

1882
Virginia Stephen born in Kensington Into a large literary household; her father Leslie Stephen edited the Dictionary of National Biography.
1895
Her mother dies The first of the bereavements that were followed by breakdowns, and the beginning of her own long history with doctors.
1915
The Voyage Out published Her first novel, in which a Mr and Mrs Dalloway appear as passengers.
1917
The Hogarth Press founded Set up by Leonard and Virginia Woolf with a hand press, which eventually meant she published herself and answered to no editor.
1918
The Armistice Less than five years before the June day in 1923 that the novel describes, and still the event everybody in it is organised around.
1922
Jacob’s Room published The first of her novels to abandon conventional plotting, and the book that made this one possible.
1922
The War Office committee on shell-shock reports Its evidence and recommendations were argued over publicly while Woolf was writing Septimus.
1923
‘Mrs Dalloway in Bond Street’ appears The short story out of which the novel grew, and which supplied its opening walk.
1925
Mrs Dalloway published on 14 May By the Hogarth Press, with a jacket designed by Vanessa Bell.
1925
Woolf’s The Common Reader published Her first collection of essays, which set out in criticism the case her fiction was making in practice.
1927
Woolf’s To the Lighthouse published The next novel, which takes the method of this one and stretches it across ten years and one empty house.
1941
Woolf drowns herself in the River Ouse At fifty-nine, in Sussex, during the second war.

Places

Westminster
Where Clarissa lives and where Big Ben strikes the hours the whole novel is measured by.
Bond Street
The flower shop at the end of the morning walk, and the street where the motor car stops.
Regent's Park
Where Rezia sits with Septimus, and where Peter Walsh falls asleep on a seat.
Harley Street
Sir William Bradshaw’s consulting room, and the clock that shreds the hour into slices.
The Strand
Where Elizabeth’s omnibus takes her, past the Temple, thinking about a profession.
Victoria Street
Crossed early in the walk, and the setting of the novel’s first great catalogue of London noise.
