Mrs Dalloway · Virginia Woolf

What happens

The morning walk Clarissa buys the flowers

Clarissa walks from Westminster to Bond Street for flowers, and the walk keeps opening backwards into Bourton, Peter Walsh and a girl of eighteen at a window. Big Ben strikes; the novel establishes straight away that its present tense is thin and its past is crowded.

The motor car and the aeroplane Septimus enters the book

A backfiring car with a blind drawn down, and then an aeroplane writing an advertisement in smoke, are seen at the same moment by people who do not know each other. Woolf uses both to step from one mind to the next, and Septimus Warren Smith enters the book among the onlookers.

Regent’s Park Rezia and Septimus in the park

Rezia sits with Septimus while he sees Evans coming through the railings and hears sparrows singing in Greek. Her loneliness is given its own sentences: she is an Italian hat-maker in a foreign city with a husband nobody will call ill.

Clarissa at home The green dress, and Peter Walsh’s visit

Clarissa learns she has not been asked to Lady Bruton’s luncheon, takes her green dress upstairs to mend, and is interrupted by Peter Walsh back from India with a pocket-knife he cannot stop opening. Thirty years of an unfinished argument are conducted in twenty minutes of small talk.

Peter walking Peter in the street and the park

Peter leaves the house, invents a romance about a woman in the street, falls asleep in the park and dreams. The section is Woolf’s demonstration that the interior life of a failure is as densely furnished as the hostess’s, and that both are living mostly in 1890.

Septimus, Holmes and Bradshaw Septimus’s war, and Dr Holmes and Sir William Bradshaw

Septimus’s history is given: the volunteer, the officer Evans, the death he congratulated himself on not feeling. Dr Holmes prescribes porridge and a music hall; Sir William Bradshaw prescribes six months in a home in the country, and the novel stops to attack him by name.

Lady Bruton’s luncheon Lady Bruton’s letter to the Times, and Richard’s roses

Richard Dalloway and Hugh Whitbread help Lady Bruton draft a letter to the Times about emigration to Canada. Richard then walks home with roses, intending to say he loves his wife, and does not manage the words.

Miss Kilman, Elizabeth and the Strand Elizabeth and Miss Kilman, and the old woman opposite

Doris Kilman, poor, devout and humiliated, takes Elizabeth to the Army and Navy Stores and loses her over an éclair. Elizabeth rides a bus up the Strand thinking about a profession, and Clarissa, alone, watches the old woman in the house opposite climb the stairs to her bedroom.

The window and the ambulance Septimus’s death

Septimus and Rezia trim a hat together in the only untroubled half-hour they are given, and then Dr Holmes comes up the stairs. Peter Walsh, hearing the ambulance from a distance, calls it one of the triumphs of civilisation without knowing what it is carrying.

The party Clarissa’s party

The Prime Minister comes; Sally Seton arrives as Lady Rosseter with five sons; Peter waits. The Bradshaws are late because of a case, and Clarissa goes alone into a little room to think about a young man she never met, then returns to her own party.
