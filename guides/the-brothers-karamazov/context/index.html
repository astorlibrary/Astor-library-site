The Brothers Karamazov · Fyodor Dostoevsky

Context

Dates

1821
Fyodor Dostoevsky is born in Moscow The second son of an army doctor.
1849
Arrest and mock execution Arrested in April with members of the Petrashevsky circle, Dostoevsky is condemned to death on 22 December, taken to the scaffold, and reprieved at the last moment.
1850
The prison at Omsk In four years of penal servitude Dostoevsky meets Dmitri Ilyinsky, a nobleman wrongly convicted of killing his father, whose story he tells in Notes from the House of the Dead.
1864
Russia’s new court statutes The judicial reform brings public trials, professional advocates and juries; the novel is set soon afterwards, in the mid-1860s.
1878
The death of Dostoevsky’s son Alyosha On 16 May. Dostoevsky could not work for a long time afterwards.
1878
A visit to Optina Pustyn In June Dostoevsky travels with the philosopher Vladimir Solovyov to the monastery of Optina Pustyn and meets the elder Amvrosy, some of whose sayings passed to Zossima.
1879
Serialisation begins The first books appear in the Russian Messenger in January; the novel runs in instalments until November 1880.
1879
Pro and Contra, and The Russian Monk Dostoevsky calls Book V the culminating point of the novel in letters to his editor, Lyubimov, and tells Pobedonostsev in August that Book VI is meant as the answer to its negative side.
1880
The Pushkin speech In June, at the unveiling of the Pushkin monument in Moscow, Dostoevsky’s speech is received with extraordinary enthusiasm.
1880
The novel is finished On 8 November Dostoevsky tells Lyubimov that the Epilogue has been sent: three years’ work, two years in print. The book edition appears in December, in two volumes dated 1881.
1881
Dostoevsky dies In St Petersburg, on 9 February by the Western calendar, a few months after the book edition. His preface had promised a second novel about Alyosha.
1912
Constance Garnett’s translation Published in London by Heinemann, the first English translation, which her preface calls complete, unaltered and unabridged, unlike the French and American versions.

Places

Staraya Russa
The small town where the Dostoevskys spent their summers, which gave its streets and manners to Skotoprigonyevsk.
Optina Pustyn
The monastery Dostoevsky visited in 1878, a model for the monastery and its elders.
Moscow
Dostoevsky’s birthplace; Ivan studies there, and Smerdyakov is trained there as a cook.
St Petersburg
Where Dostoevsky wrote much of the novel and died in 1881; Fetyukovitch is brought from there for the defence.
Omsk
The prison where Dostoevsky knew Dmitri Ilyinsky, wrongly convicted of parricide.
Seville
The setting of Ivan’s poem, The Grand Inquisitor, in the time of the Inquisition.
