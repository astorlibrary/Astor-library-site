Fyodor Dostoevsky

The Brothers Karamazov

A study guide

Fyodor Pavlovitch Karamazov, a rich old buffoon who neglected all his children, is murdered in his house one night, and his eldest son Dmitri, who had quarrelled with him over money and over the same woman, Grushenka, is tried for the crime. Around the murder Dostoevsky sets the three brothers and what each believes: Dmitri, violent, generous and ashamed; Ivan, the clever unbeliever who returns his ticket to a world where children suffer and tells the legend of the Grand Inquisitor; and Alyosha, a novice sent out into the world by his dying elder, Father Zossima. The servant Smerdyakov, rumoured to be Fyodor Pavlovitch’s son, has learned from Ivan that everything is lawful. Around them are the women who love and torment the brothers, a poor captain and his dying son, a band of schoolboys, a monastery and a small town that watches it all. The trial ends in what Dostoevsky calls a judicial error, and the novel closes with Alyosha speaking to the boys at a funeral about what a good memory from childhood can do.

Written 1878–1880; first published 1879.
