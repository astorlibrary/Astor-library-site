Fyodor Dostoevsky

Notes from Underground

A study guide

A retired official of forty, living on a small legacy in a corner of St Petersburg, writes notes that he says nobody will read. In the first part he argues, with spite and brilliance, against the belief that people would behave well if only they understood their own interests: a man will choose to harm himself, he says, simply to prove that he is a man and not a piano-key. In the second he remembers what happened when he was twenty-four: a feud with an officer on the Nevsky, a disastrous farewell dinner, and a night with a young woman called Liza, whom he moves with a speech and then humiliates. He calls himself an anti-hero, and many later writers recognised themselves in him.

Written 1863–1864; first published 1864.
