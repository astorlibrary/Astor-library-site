Nathaniel Hawthorne

The Scarlet Letter

A study guide

A woman is sentenced to wear an embroidered letter for the rest of her life, refuses to name the father of her child, and outlives the sentence without ever being released from it. Hawthorne is less interested in the adultery, which has already happened when the book opens, than in what seven years of public meaning does to four people: the woman who is marked, the minister who is not, the husband who uses diagnosis as revenge, and the child who is made to stand for all of it.

Written 1849–50; first published 1850.
