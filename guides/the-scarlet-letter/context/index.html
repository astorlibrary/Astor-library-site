The Scarlet Letter · Nathaniel Hawthorne

Context

Dates

1642
The novel’s opening scene, set about 1642 The novel places Pearl’s birth in the early 1640s, some fifteen or twenty years after the settlement of Boston.
1649
Governor John Winthrop dies The historical death the novel puts on the night of Dimmesdale’s midnight vigil, with a meteor over the town.
1692
The Salem witch trials John Hathorne sat as one of the examining magistrates; the novelist added a w to the family name.
1804
Nathaniel Hawthorne born in Salem His father, a ship’s captain, died in Surinam four years later.
1837
Hawthorne’s Twice-Told Tales published The collection that made his name among writers, if not yet among book-buyers.
1846
Hawthorne is appointed Surveyor of the Port of Salem Three years in the custom house gave him the material for the book’s introductory sketch.
1849
Hawthorne loses his custom-house post, and his mother dies The dismissal followed a change of administration; the romance was written in the months after both events.
1850
The Scarlet Letter is published in Boston Ticknor, Reed and Fields issued it in March; it sold out quickly enough to need a second printing straight away.
1850
Second edition, with Hawthorne’s new preface Salem had taken ‘The Customhouse’ personally, and Hawthorne declined to withdraw a word of it.
1851
Hawthorne’s The House of the Seven Gables published Written in the Berkshires, where Melville was his neighbour and about to dedicate Moby-Dick to him.
1864
Hawthorne dies at Plymouth, New Hampshire He was buried at Sleepy Hollow Cemetery in Concord, Massachusetts.
1926
Victor Sjöström films the novel with Lillian Gish as Hester The silent version fixed a popular image of the story that stayed in circulation for decades.

Places

Salem, Massachusetts
Hawthorne’s birthplace, the custom house of the frame, and the town his ancestor judged witches in.
Boston, Massachusetts
The colonial settlement of the romance: prison, marketplace, scaffold, Governor’s hall and meeting-house.
King's Chapel Burying Ground
The old churchyard named in the first chapter, and the kind of ground the final slab belongs to.
Amsterdam
Where Hester and her husband lived before she sailed ahead of him to Massachusetts.
Concord, Massachusetts
The Old Manse, where Hawthorne wrote before Salem, and the cemetery where he was buried.
Lenox, Massachusetts
The Berkshire cottage he moved to months after publication, and where Melville rode over to see him.
