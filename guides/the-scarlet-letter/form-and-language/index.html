The Scarlet Letter · Nathaniel Hawthorne

Form and language

Frame narrative

Presenting fiction as a document the narrator merely discovered and edited.

‘The Customhouse’ produces a rag of cloth and Surveyor Pue’s papers, gives the narrator a burn on the chest as authorisation, and never once pretends the reader should believe it.

it seemed to me, then, that I experienced a sensation not altogether physical, yet almost so, of burning heat; and as if the letter were not of red cloth, but red-hot iron. I shuddered, and involuntarily let it fall upon the floor.

The Customhouse

Multiple explanations

Offering natural and supernatural accounts of the same event without deciding between them.

The meteor in Chapter 12 is a sign, an optical effect, or a symptom of a diseased conscience; the mark on Dimmesdale’s chest gets four separate explanations in Chapter 24, including the theory that there was nothing there.

she felt or fancied, then, that the scarlet letter had endowed her with a new sense

Chapter 5

The repeated tableau

Staging the same arrangement of characters in the same place at intervals, so the changes register.

Three scaffold scenes (noon, midnight, noon) with the same three people and a different distribution of knowledge each time.

“I will not speak!” answered Hester, turning pale as death, but responding to this voice, which she too surely recognized. “And my child must seek a heavenly Father; she shall never know an earthly one!”

Hester Prynne, Chapter 3

Emblem

An object made to stand for an idea, in the tradition of the emblem book and the heraldic device.

The book ends on a coat of arms, ‘On a field, sable, the letter A, gules’: the punishment has become the formal blazon of a tombstone.

On the breast of her gown, in fine red cloth, surrounded with an elaborate embroidery and fantastic flourishes of gold-thread, appeared the letter A.

Chapter 2

The intruding narrator

A narrator who steps forward to generalise, moralise or admit uncertainty.

He tells us what Chillingworth’s face is doing and then says he cannot be sure; he draws a moral in Chapter 24 and puts it in quotation marks, as if reporting somebody else’s.

which evidently found something congenial in the soil that had so early borne the black flower of civilized society, a prison

Chapter 1

Symbolic names

Giving characters names that carry a judgement or a quality.

A physician who chills, a minister whose name contains a dimming, and a child named for a thing of great price, bought by selling everything else.

Ye that have wronged me are not sinful, save in a kind of typical illusion; neither am I fiend-like, who have snatched a fiend’s office from his hands. It is our fate. Let the black flower blossom as it may!

Roger Chillingworth, Chapter 14
