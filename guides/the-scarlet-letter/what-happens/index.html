The Scarlet Letter · Nathaniel Hawthorne

What happens

The Customhouse Hawthorne at the Salem Custom House

A surveyor of customs at Salem describes the dozing office he worked in and the parcel he claims to have turned up in the upper storey: a worn scrap of embroidered cloth and some papers about the woman who wore it. He puts the letter on his own breast and feels it burn. Every document in this sketch is invented, and in the sketch Hawthorne is honest about invention.

The Customhouse
The narrator finds the rag of scarlet cloth and Surveyor Pue’s papers, and takes the burn on his chest as a commission.

Chapters 1–8 The prison door and the first scaffold

Hester Prynne comes out of the jail with a three-month-old child and a letter she has embroidered far beyond the sentence. She refuses to name the father while he stands above her on the balcony and asks her to. In the crowd is the husband nobody has seen for two years, who makes her swear to keep his identity secret and settles into Boston as a physician.

Chapter 2
Hester emerges with Pearl and the letter, and the women of the town discuss what a proper punishment would have been.
Chapter 3
Dimmesdale is ordered to exhort her to name her partner; she refuses him to his face.
Chapter 4
In the prison Chillingworth binds her to silence about his name and swears to find the man.
Chapter 8
The magistrates consider removing Pearl; Hester appeals to Dimmesdale, who argues successfully for her.

Chapters 9–12 The physician moves in

Chillingworth attaches himself to the failing minister as doctor and lodger, and begins an investigation conducted entirely through sympathy. Dimmesdale’s sermons grow more powerful as his health collapses; he scourges himself in a locked closet and cannot say the one sentence that would end it. At last he climbs the scaffold at midnight, where nobody can see him confess.

Chapter 10
Chillingworth opens the sleeping minister’s vestment and sees something on his chest; the narrator does not say what.
Chapter 11
The more Dimmesdale accuses himself in the pulpit in general terms, the holier his congregation thinks him.
Chapter 12
A midnight confession with an audience of three, a meteor in the shape of a letter, and a glove left on the scaffold.

Chapters 13–15 Hester seven years on

Hester has become the town’s unpaid nurse, and the townspeople have begun to read her letter as ‘Able’. Hawthorne notes what that respectability has cost: passion turned into thought, a woman who now speculates freely because she has nothing left to lose socially. She confronts Chillingworth, tells him she means to break her promise, and looks at what he has become.

Chapter 13
The letter’s meaning has drifted, and the narrator sets out what the change has cost Hester.
Chapter 14
She releases herself from her oath; he declines to forgive and calls it fate.
Chapter 15
Hester admits to herself that she hates her husband, and Pearl asks what the letter means.

Chapters 16–19 The meeting in the forest

Hester intercepts Dimmesdale on a forest path, tells him who the physician is, and is forgiven. They plan a ship to Europe. She throws the letter into the leaves, lets down her hair, and the sunshine arrives on cue. Then Pearl, on the far bank of a brook, will not cross until her mother puts the letter back on.

Chapter 17
Two people who have not spoken in seven years argue about what their sin actually was.
Chapter 18
The letter comes off and the forest changes lighting.
Chapter 19
Pearl will not recognise a mother without the letter, and screams until it is fastened on again.

Chapters 20–24 Election Day

Dimmesdale walks back through the town nearly deranged with the effort of pretending, writes the sermon of his life in one night, and preaches it. Then he asks Hester and Pearl to help him onto the scaffold in daylight, tells the colony what he is, tears open his shirt and dies. The witnesses immediately begin to disagree about what they saw.

Chapter 21
The whole colony gathers; Hester learns that Chillingworth has taken passage on the same ship.
Chapter 23
The confession, the chest, and Chillingworth’s collapse.
Chapter 24
Several accounts of what was on the minister’s breast, Pearl’s inheritance and marriage abroad, and Hester’s return to resume the letter voluntarily.
