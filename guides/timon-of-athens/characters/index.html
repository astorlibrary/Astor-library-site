Timon of Athens · William Shakespeare

Characters

Timon

A rich Athenian, afterwards a hermit in the woods; first appears 1.1

He gives without keeping accounts and believes the giving has bought him a circle of brothers. The belief is put to the test three times in one act and fails three times, after which he cannot revise it by degrees. The same appetite for absolutes that had him dealing kingdoms to his friends has him wishing leprosy on every Athenian, and it softens only once, for the steward he calls the one honest man.

Flavius

Timon’s steward; first appears 1.2

He keeps the books nobody will open and weeps over them in a back room while the house blazes with lights. The play gives him the two things it treats as unmixed: the scene where he shares his own last money among the servants being turned off, and the scene in the wood where he offers to serve a man who can pay him nothing. Timon’s answer is to hand him gold on condition that he hate everybody with it.

Apemantus

A churlish philosopher; first appears 1.1

He comes to the first banquet in order to be thrown out of it, eats a root at a table of his own and tells the room they are eating their host. He is right about everything and it costs him nothing, which is the charge Timon brings against him in the wood: a man bred to nothing has never had anything taken away and cannot be disappointed by anyone.

Alcibiades

An Athenian captain; first appears 1.1

He asks the senate to spare a soldier who killed a man in anger, and is banished for pressing the point twice. He takes Timon’s gold, marches on Athens, and then does the thing Timon cannot: he separates the men who wronged him from the city they live in, and enters on terms he sets himself and keeps.

Lucullus

A lord of Athens, and the first friend asked; first appears 3.1

He comes downstairs expecting a silver basin and ewer, having dreamt of one the night before. Told that the box is empty and the request is for fifty talents, he explains that he has often dined with Timon on purpose to advise him to spend less, and offers Flaminius three small coins to wink at him and say he saw nobody.

Lucius

A lord of Athens, and the second friend asked; first appears 3.2

He is telling three visitors how shameful Lucullus has been when Timon’s servant arrives at his elbow. Within twenty lines he has remembered a purchase made the day before that leaves him nothing, calls himself a beast for it, and asks the servant to carry his own words back so that the refusal will reach Timon sounding handsome.

Sempronius

A lord of Athens, and the last friend asked; first appears 3.3

Told that the others have already said no, he is affronted where he should be ashamed: he was the first man who ever took a gift from Timon and should have been approached first. The refusal comes dressed as injured honour, and the servant left alone on stage calls it a goodly villainy and a man striving hard to appear foul.

Ventidius

An Athenian whom Timon frees from a debtors’ prison; first appears 1.2

Five talents buy him out before the first scene is over. At the banquet his father has died rich and he offers the money back, and Timon refuses it on the principle that a man who takes payment has not given anything. In Act III, Sempronius mentions in passing that Ventidius has denied him, and he is never seen or spoken of again.

The Poet

A poet with a piece to sell; first appears 1.1

His new work shows Fortune throned on a hill and the followers who let a man slip when she spurns him down, which is the plot of the play he is standing in. He presents it to the man it is about, takes his money, and in Act V walks out to the cave with nothing in his hands but an intent that is coming towards Timon.

The Painter

A painter with a piece to sell; first appears 1.1

He says a thousand moral paintings could show Fortune’s quick blows more plainly than any poem, and then sells Timon a portrait. In the wood he explains that promising is the very air of the time and that performance is a kind of will or testament, and offers an excellent piece he has not started.
