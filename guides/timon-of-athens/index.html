Timon of Athens

A study guide

Timon pays a stranger out of a debtors’ prison, matches a dowry so that his servant can marry above himself, and hands out jewels and horses to men who thank him in verse. His steward has known for months that the land is sold. When the bills arrive and every friend finds a different form of words for no, Timon calls the same guests back, serves them warm water, drives them out of the house and walks beyond the walls, and spends the last two acts cursing the species from a wood where he has dug up more gold than he ever gave away.

Written c. 1604–06; first printed 1623.
