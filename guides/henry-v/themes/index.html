Henry V · William Shakespeare

Themes

Kingship

Again and again the play comes back to what the crown actually consists of, and the answer Henry reaches alone at night is ceremony: forms that buy obedience and cost sleep. Nothing in the victory revises that answer; it is simply not spoken of again once there are people watching.

Set out in the bishops’ account of the reformed prince in 1.1, tested in the dark in 4.1, and closed off by an Epilogue that reduces the whole reign to a short time greatly lived.

May I with right and conscience make this claim?

King Henry V, 1.2.96

But when the blast of war blows in our ears, Then imitate the action of the tiger; Stiffen the sinews, summon up the blood, Disguise fair nature with hard-favour'd rage;

King Henry V, 3.1.5

The justice of the war

The claim is manufactured in Act I by men with a financial motive, blessed by a churchman who offers to bear the sin, and never examined again by anyone at court. It is a common soldier, in the dark, who asks the question the archbishop had a financial interest in settling.

Canterbury’s genealogy in 1.2; Williams’s objection in 4.1; the order to kill the prisoners in 4.6, which nobody on stage condemns.

May I with right and conscience make this claim?

King Henry V, 1.2.96

for many a thousand widows Shall this his mock mock out of their dear husbands; Mock mothers from their sons, mock castles down;

King Henry V, 1.2.286

England, Wales, Scotland and Ireland

The English army contains a Welshman, an Irishman and a Scot who quarrel among themselves while they fight the French, and the play laughs at their accents and takes their professionalism seriously. On the French side, Katharine’s English lesson makes the conquest audible as a problem of language.

The captains’ quarrel in 3.2, the lesson in 3.4, Fluellen’s leek in 5.1 and Henry’s claim in 4.7 that he is Welsh himself.

The game's afoot: Follow your spirit, and upon this charge Cry 'God for Harry, England, and Saint George!'

King Henry V, 3.1.32

From this day to the ending of the world, But we in it shall be remember'd; We few, we happy few, we band of brothers; For he to-day that sheds his blood with me Shall be my brother

King Henry V, 4.3.60

Persuasion

Henry’s speeches do practical work: they are how a tired, outnumbered army comes to agree to fight. The play stages the same skill turned in four directions (law, terror, fellowship and courtship), so that the audience can see it working and notice that it is a technique.

Legal argument in 1.2, terror at Harfleur in 3.3, fellowship at Agincourt in 4.3, and a deliberately clumsy plainness at Troyes in 5.2.

O for a Muse of fire, that would ascend The brightest heaven of invention, A kingdom for a stage, princes to act And monarchs to behold the swelling scene!

Chorus, 1.0.1

And let us, ciphers to this great accompt, On your imaginary forces work.

Chorus, 1.0.17

Ceremony and the private man

Stripped of recognition by a borrowed cloak, Henry discovers that the difference between a king and a soldier is a set of observances that cannot cure a fever or buy an hour’s sleep. The speech is delivered to nobody, and the play never returns to it in company.

4.1: the disguise, the argument, the soliloquy on ceremony and the prayer about his father’s crown, all in one unbroken scene.

Upon the king! let us our lives, our souls, Our debts, our careful wives, Our children and our sins lay on the king! We must bear all.

King Henry V, 4.1.202

And what art thou, thou idle ceremony? What kind of god art thou, that suffer'st more Of mortal griefs than do thy worshippers?

King Henry V, 4.1.212

Memory and fame

The Crispin’s Day speech is about the future tense: old men forgetting, feats remembered with advantages, a story taught to a son. The play is interested in the making of the legend as much as in the battle, which is why it ends by pointing out that everything won here was lost within a generation.

The Chorus builds the legend across five acts; 4.3 predicts its transmission; the Epilogue’s sonnet takes it all back in fourteen lines.
