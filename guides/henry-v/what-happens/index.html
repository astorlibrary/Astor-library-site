Henry V · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The bishops, and the claim to France

Two bishops discuss a bill that would strip the Church of its land and agree that a French war would be a useful distraction. Canterbury then delivers a sixty-line genealogy proving Henry’s right to France, and takes the sin of it on his own head when the king asks. The Dauphin’s gift of tennis balls makes a legal argument personal.

1.0
The Chorus apologises for the stage and invites the audience to supply the horses, the fleet and the two kingdoms.
1.1
Canterbury and Ely count what a parliamentary bill would take from them, and what the king’s new piety is worth.
1.2
The claim is argued at length; the French ambassadors deliver an insult; Henry promises widows.

Act II The traitors, and the death of Falstaff

Henry traps Cambridge, Scroop and Grey into demanding severity for a drunkard and then hands them their own arrest warrants. In Eastcheap, Falstaff dies offstage and is given an obituary by a hostess who cannot manage the word. The men who knew the king when he was a prince pack up and follow the army to France as looters.

2.0
The Chorus reports a country arming, and warns of treason paid for by France.
2.1
Nym and Pistol quarrel over the hostess; word comes that Falstaff is dying.
2.2
The three conspirators argue for harshness and are condemned out of their own mouths.
2.3
The Hostess describes the death; Pistol, Nym and Bardolph leave for France.
2.4
The French king takes the invasion seriously; the Dauphin does not; Exeter delivers the ultimatum.

Act III The siege of Harfleur

The breach speech makes an exhausted army into a company of fathers and sons. When the town still holds, Henry stands at the gates and describes in detail the rape and infanticide that will follow if he has to attack again. The town surrenders, so the audience never learns whether he meant it. Katharine begins learning English; the army begins to get sick.

3.0
The fleet crosses; the Chorus tells the audience to imagine the siege guns.
3.1
‘Once more unto the breach’: the most famous exhortation in English drama.
3.2
Bardolph, Nym and Pistol hang back; the Boy sizes them up; four captains of four nations argue.
3.3
Henry threatens the townspeople of Harfleur; the governor yields.
3.4
Katharine learns the parts of the body from Alice, and finds two of the words obscene.
3.5
The French nobility are appalled that an army they despise has crossed the Somme unopposed.
3.6
Fluellen reports the execution of a thief; Henry confirms it without a pause.
3.7
Noblemen pass the night before the battle praising their own horses.

Act IV The night before Agincourt, and the battle

Disguised in a borrowed cloak, Henry argues with two soldiers about whether a king answers for the souls of the men he leads, loses the argument in substance, and prays alone about his father’s usurpation. In the morning he answers the shortage of men with the Crispin’s Day speech. Before the victory is certain, he orders the prisoners killed.

4.0
The Chorus’s night-piece: two camps, the hum of either army, and a little touch of Harry in the night.
4.1
Henry in disguise debates responsibility with Bates and Williams, quarrels over a glove, and prays.
4.2
The French ride out confident of an easy morning.
4.3
Answering a wish for more men, Henry makes scarcity the point of the speech.
4.4
A Frenchman is ransomed through a boy interpreter; the comedy runs beside the killing.
4.5
The French lords resolve to die sooner than live shamed.
4.6
Told the French have rallied, Henry commands that every soldier kill his prisoners.
4.7
Fluellen and Gower report the killing of the luggage-boys; Henry says he was not angry until this instant.
4.8
The dead are counted, the disparity is enormous, and Henry credits God.

Act V A leek, a treaty and a marriage

Fluellen makes Pistol eat a leek, which is the last the play sees of Eastcheap. Burgundy describes a France gone to weeds while the two courts negotiate, and Henry courts Katharine in the plain-soldier manner of a man who has already been promised her in the treaty. The Chorus then returns to say that their son lost everything.

5.0
London greets the king; the Chorus glances at a general expected home from Ireland.
5.1
Fluellen beats Pistol and makes him eat the leek he mocked.
5.2
Burgundy pleads for peace; Henry woos Katharine; the treaty makes him heir of France. The Epilogue reminds the audience that Henry’s son lost it all.
