King John · William Shakespeare

Characters

King John

King of England, holding a crown his nephew has the better descent to; first appears 1.1

He is quick and certain while there is an embassy to answer or a town to threaten, and lost the moment the question turns to what his title rests on. He wants Arthur dead without saying the words, blames Hubert when he believes it has been done, hands his crown to a legate to buy off an invasion, and dies of poison in an orchard while a messenger is still speaking.

Philip Faulconbridge, the Bastard

Richard the Lionheart’s illegitimate son, knighted as Sir Richard Plantagenet; first appears 1.1

He gives up an estate to be called his father’s son and spends the next four acts saying aloud what the kings will not. At Angers he tells two armies to level the town and settle the quarrel afterwards; in the last scene he kneels to a boy king and speaks the closing lines about England. He also strips the abbeys for John’s war and carries Austria’s head on stage.

Arthur

Duke of Brittany, son of John’s elder brother Geoffrey; first appears 2.1

Everybody argues about his right and nobody asks him anything until Hubert arrives with the irons and the warrant. He says he would rather be out of prison and keeping sheep, and he talks his way out of blinding by remembering a headache and a handkerchief. Then he puts on a ship-boy’s clothes, climbs down a wall, and breaks his neck on the stones.

Constance

Arthur’s mother, widow of Geoffrey Plantagenet; first appears 2.1

She is the one person who will not let the wedding at Angers pass as a settlement, and she says so to both kings on the day itself. When Arthur is taken she sits on the ground in the French tent, pulls her hair down, and tells a cardinal to his face that she is not mad, only grieving. She dies offstage, reported in a messenger’s half-sentence.

Queen Eleanor

John’s mother, widow of Henry II; first appears 1.1

Forty lines into the play she tells her son that possession is doing more for him than right, and pitches it so that only the two of them hear. She sails with the army, picks the Lionheart out of a stranger’s face, and arranges the Blanche marriage that ends the war for an afternoon. Her death reaches John in the same speech as the news of the French fleet.

Hubert de Burgh

John’s chamberlain, given the keeping of Arthur; first appears 3.2

He takes the commission in a scene where the king never says what it is, heats the irons and puts two executioners behind the arras. Alone with the boy he sends them out, and then cannot use the iron either. For that he is called a murderer by the lords, damned by the Bastard, and turned on by the king who wanted it done. Some editions give him the Angers citizen’s lines as well.

Cardinal Pandulph

The pope’s legate in France and England; first appears 3.1

He walks into the wedding celebrations with a question about Stephen Langton and leaves with a war. He excommunicates John, orders Philip to unclasp a hand he has just taken, and later shows the Dauphin how Arthur’s death will hand him the kingdom. He takes John’s crown, gives it back, and then discovers that the invasion he started will not stop for him.

King Philip of France

King of France, Arthur’s declared champion; first appears 2.1

He comes to Angers in Arthur’s name with a great deal of language about the judge in heaven who made him the boy’s guardian, and gives the cause up the same day for five provinces and a marriage. Pandulph makes him break the new league within hours of swearing it. After Angers he drops out of the play, and his son carries the invasion on alone.

Louis the Dauphin

Philip’s son, married to Blanche and later the invader of England; first appears 2.1

He is handed a wife and five provinces at Angers and takes the English claim that comes with her. When John makes his peace with Rome, Louis tells the legate that Rome has sent no men, no money and no munition, and carries on. Melun’s confession on the battlefield reveals that he had already sworn to cut off the heads of the English lords who joined him.

Blanche of Spain

John’s niece, married to the Dauphin as a term of the treaty; first appears 2.1

She is offered to Louis in front of both armies and answers that her uncle’s will in the matter is hers. A few hundred lines later Pandulph has set her husband against her uncle on her wedding day, and she works out aloud that whichever side wins she loses. After that line she says nothing more and never comes back on stage.
