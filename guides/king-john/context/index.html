King John · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1199
John is crowned at Westminster Richard I dies childless and the crown passes to his youngest brother, over the claim of Geoffrey’s son Arthur.
1202
Arthur of Brittany is captured at Mirebeau Taken by John’s forces while besieging his own grandmother Eleanor, he was imprisoned at Falaise and then Rouen, and was never seen again after April 1203.
1213
John surrenders the kingdom to the papal legate After four years of excommunication he accepted Stephen Langton and received England back from Pandulph as a papal fief.
1215
Magna Carta is sealed at Runnymede The event that now defines the reign, and the one the play leaves out entirely.
1216
Louis of France lands in England; John dies at Newark The barons invited the Dauphin over; John lost his baggage train in the Wash and died of dysentery in October, and was buried at Worcester.
1591
The Troublesome Reign of King John is printed An anonymous two-part play with the same unusual sequence of events, generally taken as the source Shakespeare worked from.
1596
Shakespeare writes King John Dated to the middle of the 1590s on style and on its place among the other histories.
1598
Francis Meres lists it in Palladis Tamia His roll-call of Shakespeare’s plays names King John, which fixes the latest possible date of composition.
1623
The First Folio The only early text. It opens the section of histories, ahead of Richard II.
1745
Two rival King Johns in London Garrick played the king at Drury Lane against Colley Cibber’s adaptation Papal Tyranny at Covent Garden, during the year of the Jacobite rising.
1823
Charles Kemble’s antiquarian revival J. R. Planche’s researched thirteenth-century costumes at Covent Garden began the fashion for historically accurate Shakespeare staging.
1899
Herbert Beerbohm Tree’s production A spectacular staging at Her Majesty’s Theatre, from which a short filmed fragment survives, often called the earliest Shakespeare film.

Places

Northampton
The room of state where the play opens, where the Faulconbridge suit is heard, and where John is crowned a second time in Act IV.
Angers
Angiers in the text: the walled town that will accept neither king, and whose citizens propose the marriage that ends the war.
Bury St Edmunds
Saint Edmundsbury, where the English lords take the sacrament with the Dauphin, and on whose altar Louis swore to have their heads.
The Wash
The Lincoln Washes, where the Bastard loses half his force to the tide in the last act.
Swineshead, Lincolnshire
Swinstead in the text: the abbey where a monk poisons John, and the orchard where he dies.
Worcester Cathedral
Where Prince Henry says his father’s body must be interred, and where the historical John’s tomb still stands.
