King John · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I France’s demand, and the Bastard’s choice

Chatillion asks for the crown on Arthur’s behalf and is sent home with a threat. Eleanor then tells her son, in an aside, what his claim really rests on. The rest of the act is a Northamptonshire inheritance case: two brothers arguing over five hundred pounds a year, which Eleanor settles by offering the elder the chance to be called Richard the Lionheart’s son instead.

1.1
France demands the crown for Arthur and gets war. The Faulconbridge brothers bring their suit; the elder gives up the estate, is knighted Sir Richard Plantagenet, and hears from his mother who his father was.

Act II Angers, and the marriage of Blanche and Louis

Everything happens in front of the walls of Angers. Both kings make their case to the townsmen, who decline to choose; a day’s fighting settles nothing; the Bastard proposes that France and England flatten the town together and fight afterwards. A citizen then suggests a wedding, and five provinces and thirty thousand marks buy the peace that the battle could not.

2.1
Claim and counter-claim, a drawn battle, the Bastard’s plan to level the town, the match between Blanche and the Dauphin, and the soliloquy that gives self-interest the name of Commodity.

Act III Pandulph breaks the peace, and John hints at Arthur’s murder

Constance refuses to accept the settlement and sits on the ground to say so. Pandulph arrives, excommunicates John for keeping Stephen Langton out of Canterbury, and orders France to break the league it swore that morning. Battle follows, Arthur is taken, and John works his way round to asking Hubert for the boy’s death in two words.

3.1
Constance curses the wedding day; Pandulph curses John; Philip lets go of England’s hand and Blanche finds she cannot wish either army well.
3.2
The Bastard comes on with Austria’s head; John puts Arthur into Hubert’s keeping.
3.3
The Bastard is sent home to shake money out of the abbeys, and John talks Hubert towards a murder he will not name.
3.4
Constance tears her hair down over Arthur’s capture; Pandulph explains to the Dauphin why the boy’s death will hand him England.

Act IV Hubert and the irons, the second coronation and Arthur’s death

Hubert heats the irons, hides two executioners behind the arras, and then cannot do it. John has himself crowned a second time and is told by his own lords that gilding gold is waste. News arrives of a French landing and of two deaths, Arthur jumps from a wall in disguise and breaks his neck, and the lords ride off to Saint Edmundsbury.

4.1
Arthur talks Hubert out of blinding him, and Hubert sends him into hiding and lets the king believe he is dead.
4.2
The second coronation, Pembroke’s request for Arthur’s release, the report of his death, the loss of Eleanor and Constance, and John turning on Hubert for having understood him.
4.3
Arthur leaps, and dies. The lords find the body, swear revenge, and leave; the Bastard is left holding what is left of England.

Act V John yields to Rome, the French invasion and John’s death

John hands his crown to Pandulph on Ascension Day and takes it back as the pope’s tenant, which fulfils a prophecy he had the prophet hanged for. Louis refuses to be called off. Melun, dying, warns the English lords that Louis has sworn to kill them, and they come home. John is poisoned by a monk; the Bastard loses half his army in the Lincolnshire tides and reaches the orchard in time to watch the king die.

5.1
John submits to Rome, hears that London has let the French in, and hands the conduct of the war to the Bastard.
5.2
Salisbury weeps over his own rebellion; Louis tells Pandulph that Rome has paid for none of this; the Bastard delivers John’s defiance.
5.3
John, sick with fever, leaves the field for Swinstead as word comes that the French supply ships are lost on the Goodwin Sands.
5.4
The wounded Melun tells the English lords they are bought and sold, and they turn back to John.
5.5
Louis, who thinks the day went well, learns that Melun is dead, the lords are gone and his reinforcements are sunk.
5.6
Hubert finds the Bastard in the dark with news that a monk has poisoned the king; the Bastard reports half his force drowned in the Washes.
5.7
John dies in a chair in the open air, Prince Henry succeeds, Pandulph has terms ready, and the Bastard kneels to the new king.
