A Midsummer Night's Dream · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1595
The play probably written Usually placed in the mid-1590s on stylistic grounds, close to Romeo and Juliet; no document fixes the year.
1598
Francis Meres lists the play in Palladis Tamia The earliest external record that it existed, two years before it was printed.
1600
First Quarto, printed for Thomas Fisher The title page says the play had been publicly acted by the Lord Chamberlain’s Men. Most modern editions descend from this text.
1619
Second Quarto, falsely dated 1600 Set from Q1 and belonging to the group of reprints associated with Thomas Pavier.
1623
First Folio Printed among the comedies, from the second quarto, with small changes including the substitution of Egeus for Philostrate in the last act.
1662
Samuel Pepys sees the play and detests it His diary records one of the bluntest hostile verdicts in the play’s reception history.
1826
Mendelssohn composes the overture Written when he was seventeen, and still the music most audiences hear in their heads.
1840
Madame Vestris stages the play at Covent Garden A production that helped establish the Victorian fairy-spectacle tradition.
1842
Mendelssohn’s incidental music Added sixteen years after the overture, and the source of the wedding march.
1935
Reinhardt and Dieterle’s Hollywood film With James Cagney as Bottom and Mickey Rooney as Puck: the play as a studio fantasy.
1960
Britten’s opera premieres at Aldeburgh The libretto begins in the wood and skips Athens, which shifts the balance from law to enchantment.
1970
Peter Brook’s white box production for the RSC Sally Jacobs’s bare white set, trapezes and circus skills replaced painted foliage and changed how the play could look.

Places

Athens
Theseus’s city, where the law gives a father the right to dispose of his daughter.
Sparta
Named by Theseus and Hippolyta when they compare hunting dogs on the morning after.
Crete
Where Hippolyta once heard hounds bay a bear, in the same exchange.
Thessaly
The third of the hunting regions Theseus lists when boasting about his pack.
India
The changeling boy’s country, and the spiced Indian air where Titania’s votaress laughed at the sails.
Fleet Street, London
Thomas Fisher’s shop at the sign of the White Hart, where the 1600 quarto was sold.

Critics

Samuel Pepys

Diary (1662)

Pepys saw the play on 29 September 1662 and called it “the most insipid ridiculous play that ever I saw in my life”, though he enjoyed the dancing and some handsome women.

Jan Kott

“Titania and the Ass’s Head”, in Shakespeare Our Contemporary (1964)

Kott read the play as dark and erotic: the night in the wood releases animal desire, Titania’s love for Bottom is sexual, and the lovers wake ashamed of what they have done. His reading lay behind Peter Brook’s production of 1970.
