A Midsummer Night's Dream · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I Hermia’s sentence and the mechanicals’ play

Theseus is four days from marrying a woman he defeated in battle when Egeus arrives demanding the Athenian law that lets a father dispose of a daughter. Hermia is given until the next new moon, Theseus’s wedding day four days off, to obey, die or enter a convent; she and Lysander agree to run, tell Helena, and Helena tells Demetrius. In a separate room six tradesmen are allocating parts in a play about a double suicide.

1.1
Egeus demands his right; Theseus upholds it; the lovers plan to escape and Helena betrays the plan.
1.2
Quince hands out parts and Bottom offers to play all of them.

Act II Oberon and Titania’s quarrel, and the love juice

Oberon and Titania have fallen out over a stolen Indian boy, and the dispute has stopped the seasons behaving. Oberon sends for a flower whose juice makes the sleeper dote on whatever they first see, intending it for his wife, and adds an instruction about the Athenian youth he has just watched treat a woman badly. Puck anoints the wrong Athenian.

2.1
Oberon and Titania quarrel; Helena pursues Demetrius through the wood; Puck is sent for love-in-idleness.
2.2
Titania is drugged asleep; Puck finds Lysander and Hermia lying apart and anoints the wrong man.

Act III Puck’s mistakes in the wood

Bottom acquires an ass’s head, his friends run, and the fairy queen wakes and falls in love with him. In the other half of the wood both men now love Helena, who is certain she is being mocked, and the two women who grew up together insult each other’s height until the men go off to fight. Oberon has Puck fog the wood and put them all to sleep.

3.1
The rehearsal is interrupted; Bottom is transformed and Titania wakes to adore him.
3.2
Both men court Helena, the two women fall out, and Puck leads the rivals astray in the dark.

Act IV The lovers and Bottom wake up

Oberon has what he wanted, releases Titania and says nothing about how he got it. Theseus finds the four lovers asleep in the wood, overrules Egeus and adds them to his own wedding, although Demetrius, alone of the four, is still under the drug. Bottom wakes last, in the middle of the stage, trying to describe something he has no words for.

4.1
Titania is released; the hunt finds the lovers; Theseus overrules the law; Bottom wakes and gropes for his dream.
4.2
The players mourn their lost leading man until he walks in with news of a wedding.

Act V Pyramus and Thisbe, and the fairies’ blessing

Three couples watch Pyramus and Thisbe, a tragedy of forbidden love and mistaken death performed by men who explain the wall and apologise for the lion. The court heckles it; the play they are mocking is the story they have just escaped. The fairies then come indoors to bless the marriages, and Puck invites the audience to call the whole thing a dream.

5.1
The mechanicals perform; the court comments; Oberon and Titania bless the house; Puck asks for pardon.
