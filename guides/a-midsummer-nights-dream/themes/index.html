Love without reason

The play’s working definition of love is that it cannot be argued with and cannot be trusted: Helena says it looks with the mind, not the eyes, Bottom observes that reason and love keep little company, and a flower does the work of a lifetime’s devotion. The drug does nothing unnatural; it is only quicker.

Through the play Stated as theory in 1.1, demonstrated on the lovers in Acts II and III, and left standing at the end with Demetrius still under the influence.

In the text

Now, fair Hippolyta, our nuptial hour Draws on apace; four happy days bring in Another moon: but, O, methinks, how slow This old moon wanes!

Theseus, 1.1.1

The play starts a clock and immediately complains about it, which is how the whole comedy will run: everything is dated to the moon. The image Theseus reaches for, an old woman withering away a young man’s inheritance, puts impatience and money into the first speech of a play about who may marry whom.

It fell upon a little western flower, Before milk-white, now purple with love’s wound, And maidens call it love-in-idleness.

Oberon, 2.1.166

The drug that drives the rest of the plot gets an origin story in three lines, and its colour is a wound. ‘Love-in-idleness’ is a real country name for the wild pansy, so the enchantment arrives as folklore, which is how the play likes its magic.

Law, fathers and consent

Athens opens with a father asking a duke to enforce his right to kill his daughter, and the duke agreeing that this is the law. Nothing in the play repeals it: in Act 4 Theseus simply overrules Egeus, which leaves the comedy’s happy ending resting on a ruler’s mood.

Through the play Egeus makes his demand in 1.1 and repeats it in 4.1; Theseus overrules him without argument, and Egeus is given nothing further to say.

In the text

I beg the ancient privilege of Athens, As she is mine, I may dispose of her: Which shall be either to this gentleman Or to her death, according to our law

Egeus, 1.1.41

‘Dispose of’ is a term for property, and the alternatives offered are a husband or an execution, in that order and in the same sentence. Egeus is not presented as a monster: he is invoking something he calls ancient and the duke agrees it exists, and that is why the first scene of a comedy feels cold.

Your wrongs do set a scandal on my sex: We cannot fight for love, as men may do; We should be wood and were not made to woo.

Helena, 2.1.240

She names the rule she is breaking and what breaking it will cost, in one couplet. ‘Wood’ puns on wooed and on the old word for mad, and also names the place she is standing in: three meanings in one syllable, all of them about a woman who has come to the wrong place to do the wrong thing.

Transformation

Everyone in the wood is altered and almost nobody notices at the time. The one visible change, Bottom’s head, is the least distressing of them; the play saves its fear for the invisible ones, like a man waking to loathe the woman he loved.

Through the play Titania and Lysander are drugged in 2.2, Bottom transformed in 3.1 and Demetrius drugged in 3.2; three of the four are undone by 4.1, and Demetrius is not.

In the text

reason and love keep little company together now-a-days; the more the pity that some honest neighbours will not make them friends.

Bottom, 3.1.122

The play’s thesis is delivered in prose by the only character who has not been given the drug, and he treats the failure of reason and love as a neighbourly dispute that a bit of mediation might settle. It is also a polite way of telling a queen that she is not making sense.

My Oberon! what visions have I seen! Methought I was enamour’d of an ass.

Titania, 4.1.73

She calls it a vision, he says nothing about what he did, and the boy is never mentioned again by either of them. Her next line, that her eyes now loathe his face, is aimed at a man asleep three feet away, and the comedy depends on the audience laughing without looking at it too closely.

Night, sleep and the morning story

The play’s settlement depends on everybody agreeing to call the night a dream. Theseus produces the sceptical account, Hippolyta doubts it, Bottom cannot fit his experience into words, and Puck finally offers the whole audience the same bargain.

Through the play The lovers wake half-remembering in 4.1; Theseus explains it away in 5.1; the epilogue makes the explanation the audience’s responsibility.

In the text

The eye of man hath not heard, the ear of man hath not seen, man’s hand is not able to taste, his tongue to conceive, nor his heart to report, what my dream was.

Bottom, 4.1.206

The senses have been shuffled, which is exactly right for someone describing an experience the available vocabulary does not cover; the pattern is a garbled echo of St Paul on what the eye has not seen. The mangling is the meaning: Bottom admits that something happened which cannot be tidied into a morning story.

The lunatic, the lover and the poet Are of imagination all compact:

Theseus, 5.1.7

‘All compact’ means made entirely of, so the three are identified with each other outright. The speech is the rational case against everything the audience has just watched, delivered by a character out of Greek myth in a play with fairies in it, and Hippolyta’s reply, that all four tell the same story, is the only answer it gets.

Theatre about theatre

The mechanicals worry about the things real companies worry about (casting, props, whether the audience will be frightened, whether a boy can play a woman) and solve them by explaining everything aloud. Their failure demonstrates the conventions the surrounding play is relying on at that exact moment.

Through the play Rehearsal in 1.2 and 3.1, performance in 5.1, and an epilogue that begs the same indulgence the mechanicals begged for in their prologue.

In the text

Let me play the lion too: I will roar, that I will do any man’s heart good to hear me;

Bottom, 1.2.58

‘Too’ is the joke: he has accepted the lead and wants the other parts as well, including the one with no words. Quince talks him out of it by explaining that a convincing lion would get the company hanged, which is the first of the play’s several reminders that theatre in 1595 was a licensed activity.

And as imagination bodies forth The forms of things unknown, the poet’s pen Turns them to shapes and gives to airy nothing A local habitation and a name.

Theseus, 5.1.14

He intends this as evidence that poets are unreliable, and produces the best description of writing in Shakespeare while doing it. ‘A local habitation and a name’ is what a stage gives a story: an address and a cast list. The sceptic has accidentally stated the terms on which the theatre he is standing in operates.

Who is allowed to decide

Fathers, dukes and fairy kings all get what they want, and each of them calls it correction. The play lets that pattern show by repeating it at three social levels within an hour of stage time.

Through the play Egeus invokes the law, Theseus invokes his sword and his wedding, Oberon invokes a flower; the changeling boy changes hands offstage and is never mentioned again.

In the text

Hippolyta, I woo’d thee with my sword, And won thy love, doing thee injuries; But I will wed thee in another key,

Theseus, 1.1.16

He describes a conquest as a courtship without seeming to notice the difference, and ‘doing thee injuries’ stays in the sentence as a plain fact. ‘In another key’ is a musical term, so the change he promises is one of tone, and the relation stays the same, which is what Oberon and Titania will act out in the wood.

Ill met by moonlight, proud Titania.

Oberon, 2.1.60

Six words establish that this marriage is in the middle of a long argument, and the greeting, ‘Ill met’, is a courtesy said backwards. Everything the fairies do for the rest of the night is a move in a quarrel that began before the audience arrived.
