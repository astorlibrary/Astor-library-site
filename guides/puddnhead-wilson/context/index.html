The Tragedy of Pudd’nhead Wilson · Mark Twain

Context

Dates

1830
The novel’s opening: Roxy switches the babies in 1830 Roxy changes the two babies in the Driscoll nursery; both are about seven months old.
1845
Clemens’s boyhood in Hannibal, Missouri Hannibal, Missouri, where Samuel Clemens grew up watching slaves shipped south, is the model for Dawson’s Landing.
1853
The novel’s murder trial, set in 1853 The murder of Judge Driscoll and Wilson’s finger-print demonstration close the novel’s action.
1880
Faulds and Herschel argue for finger-prints Letters in Nature propose using finger-prints for identification, a decade before Twain wrote the trial.
1892
Galton’s Finger Prints published Francis Galton’s book established that the patterns are unique and unchanging; Twain read about the method while the novel was in progress.
1892
Begun as a farce Twain started a comic story about conjoined twins in Florence, then found the changeling plot taking it over.
1893
Twain cuts the Siamese twins out of the novel He separated the brothers, pulled their story out as Those Extraordinary Twins, and rewrote what remained.
1893
Serialised in The Century The novel ran in The Century Magazine from December 1893 to June 1894, with illustrations.
1894
Published as a book The American Publishing Company issued it with Those Extraordinary Twins appended and Twain’s note explaining the surgery.
1896
Plessy v. Ferguson Two years after publication the Supreme Court upheld segregation in a case turning on a man who was seven-eighths white.
1955
Leslie Fiedler’s defence Fiedler called the novel a great and neglected American book, and modern criticism of it effectively starts here.

Places

Hannibal, Missouri
Twain’s boyhood town on the Mississippi, and the original of Dawson’s Landing.
St Louis, Missouri
Half a day upstream by steamboat; where Tom gambles and pawns what he steals.
New Orleans, Louisiana
The end of the river, and what ‘sold down the river’ means in practice.
The Arkansas cotton country
Where Tom sells his mother, and where she is worked in the fields before she escapes.
Florence, Italy
Where Twain wrote the book, in the winter of 1892–93.

Critics

F. R. Leavis

Introduction to Pudd’nhead Wilson (1955)

Leavis called it a great book, neglected because it was taken for a farce, and argued that its real subject is the society of a slave-holding town and the arbitrariness of the line it draws between free and enslaved.
