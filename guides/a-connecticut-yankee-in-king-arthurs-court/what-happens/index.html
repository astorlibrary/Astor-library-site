A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur’s Court · Mark Twain

What happens

A Word of Explanation, Chapters I-II Warwick Castle, the manuscript and the year 528

A tourist at Warwick meets a stranger who talks about the sixth century as though he has been there, and is handed a manuscript on palimpsest to read overnight. The story inside begins in a Hartford arms factory and goes on under an oak tree in 528, where a man in armour rides up and a child stares at the stranger’s clothes.

A Word of Explanation
The frame narrator meets the man, hears about bullet-holes in chain mail, and is given the manuscript.
Chapter I
Hank Morgan wakes in open country, is taken prisoner by Sir Kay, and assumes he is in an asylum.
Chapter II
Hartford, the arms factory, and a man who can make anything and invent the machine that makes it.

Chapters III-VIII The Round Table and the eclipse

At the Round Table the knights tell Malory’s stories at length while Hank works out what year it is. Sentenced to burn, he remembers a total eclipse due on the 21st of June, 528, and announces that he will put out the sun. The court buys it, the king makes him perpetual minister, and the nation gives him a title that fits an American better than a duke’s would.

Chapter III
Their talk is lifted from Malory, and Hank listens to it as a man listens to people who lie without knowing they are lying.
Chapter VI
A threat delivered in a voice charged with doom, timed to an astronomical event, and believed absolutely.
Chapter VII
Blowing it up with blasting powder and a lightning rod, to settle which magician the country is going to believe.
Chapter VIII
Second only to the king, and called by a name nobody in the sixth century had a use for.

Chapters IX-X Patent office, schools, wires in the woods

Hank builds a modern state underground and out of sight: a teacher factory, a West Point, a newspaper, telephone lines strung at night, soap and matches and stove polish. It is funny, and it is being done by one man with no successor, in a country that has not asked for any of it. The chapters keep the joke going while laying out exactly why it will fail.

Chapter IX
A weekly institution the Boss studies the way a manager studies a market.
Chapter X
The patent office first, then schools, then the rest: his own order of priorities.

Chapters XI-XXXVIII Sandy, the king in disguise, and the slave gang

The middle of the novel takes the Boss out of his workshop and into the country. He rescues princesses who turn out to be pigs, empties Morgan le Fay’s dungeons, crawls into a smallpox hut, and travels in peasant clothes with a king who cannot learn to walk like one. The comedy thins as they go; by the slave gang it has gone, and the book’s opinion of the sixth century has become an opinion about the nineteenth.

Chapter XII
Armour in the sun, with an insect inside it and no way to reach it: the joke the whole novel grew out of.
Chapter XVI
A charming hostess who stabs a page for brushing her knee, and whose dungeons hold men who have forgotten why they are there.
Chapter XVII
The court at its ease, with the gap between its manners and its cruelty on full display.
Chapter XXII
A miracle performed with a pump, fire-works and two assistants, and received as one.
Chapter XXXIII
Hank talks economics to men who cannot see their own position, and loses the argument by winning it.
Chapters XXXIV-XXXVIII
The king and the Boss are sold as slaves, and the rescue arrives on bicycles.

Chapters XXXIX-XLIV The republic, the Interdict, and the cave

Hank unhorses the flower of chivalry with a lasso and then a revolver, proclaims a republic when Arthur dies, and finds the Church has turned the country against him with an interdict. Fifty-two boys and one engineer meet the assembled knighthood behind electric fences and Gatling guns. The Boss wins in an afternoon, is trapped by the dead he has made, and the manuscript ends in somebody else’s handwriting.

Chapter XXXIX
A lasso, then a revolver, and the end of tournaments.
Chapter XLII
Arthur dead, the republic declared, and the whole of England on the other side.
Chapter XLIII
Twenty-five thousand men killed by wire, water and machine guns in a single engagement, described in the flat voice of an engineer.
Chapter XLIV
The Boss is put to sleep by Merlin for thirteen centuries, and Clarence finishes the book for him.
