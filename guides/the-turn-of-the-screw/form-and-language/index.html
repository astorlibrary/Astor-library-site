The Turn of the Screw · Henry James

Form and language

Frame narrative

A story told inside another story, by a narrator with his own interests.

The Christmas-Eve frame gives the story a title, a delay, a champion for the governess and, crucially, no way of checking her: it closes without returning to the fireside.

The story had held us, round the fire, sufficiently breathless, but except the obvious remark that it was gruesome, as, on Christmas Eve in an old house, a strange tale should essentially be, I remember no comment uttered till somebody happened to say that it was the only case he had met in which such a visitation had fallen on a child.

Prologue

Withheld specifics

Withholding the act, the word or the offence that the plot turns on.

Miles’s expulsion, Quint’s freedom and Miss Jessel’s disgrace are all left as gaps, and readers fill them with material James never supplied.

The story won’t tell

Douglas, Prologue

Suspended syntax

Sentences that qualify themselves clause by clause before reaching the verb that matters.

The governess’s prose delays its own conclusions with dashes and parentheses, so that the reader experiences her habit of thought as much as her account of events.

The story had held us, round the fire, sufficiently breathless, but except the obvious remark that it was gruesome, as, on Christmas Eve in an old house, a strange tale should essentially be, I remember no comment uttered till somebody happened to say that it was the only case he had met in which such a visitation had fallen on a child.

Prologue

The reported sighting

An apparition given only as one person’s report, never as an event the narrative confirms.

James never writes that Quint is there; he writes that she saw him, and the difference is the whole argument between a century of readings.

Someone would appear there at the turn of a path and would stand before me and smile and approve.

Chapter III

Leading questions

Producing testimony by supplying the answer inside the question.

Mrs Grose is asked whether the man had no hat, and Flora is asked where Miss Jessel is; one witness agrees and one does not, by the same method.

Doubling

Pairing characters so that one occupies another’s position.

Two governesses at one desk, two children, two dead servants, and a title that promises a second turn of a screw already turned once.

You terrible, miserable woman!

The governess, Chapter XV
