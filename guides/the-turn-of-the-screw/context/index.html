The Turn of the Screw · Henry James

Context

Dates

1843
Henry James born in New York Into a family that moved repeatedly between America and Europe throughout his childhood.
1876
James settles in London He spent most of the rest of his life in England and became a British subject in 1915.
1895
Guy Domville fails at the St James’s Theatre James was booed at his own curtain call and abandoned writing for the stage; the fiction of the following years is markedly more compressed and dramatic.
1897
What Maisie Knew published Another book told through a child’s partial understanding of adults, written immediately before this one.
1898
Serialised in Collier’s Weekly The story reached its first readers in a popular illustrated weekly.
1898
Collected in The Two Magics Published with Covering End, so that the ghost story shared a volume with a comedy.
1908
Revised for the New York Edition James reworked the text and added a preface discussing how the story was made.
1916
James dies in London He had taken British nationality the previous year, during the war.
1954
Britten’s opera premieres in Venice The Turn of the Screw set for chamber orchestra, with Quint written for a tenor who sings, which the novella’s Quint never does.
1961
Jack Clayton’s film The Innocents Deborah Kerr plays the governess, and the screenplay deliberately leaves the ambiguity open.

Places

Harley Street, London
The guardian’s house, where the governess is interviewed and gives her promise.
Essex
Bly is described only as an old family place in Essex; James names no town and draws no map.
De Vere Gardens, Kensington
James’s London flat through the 1890s, the years of Maisie and this story.
Rye, East Sussex
Lamb House, where James lived and worked for the last phase of his career.
New York
James’s birthplace, and the city whose collected edition he revised the story for in 1908.
