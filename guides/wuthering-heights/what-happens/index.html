Wuthering Heights · Emily Brontë

What happens

Chapters 1–3 The tenant, the diary and the hand at the window

A southern gentleman rents Thrushcross Grange, calls on his landlord twice, is savaged by dogs and snowed in. Shut in an old panelled bed he finds a child’s handwriting in the margins of a book and dreams of a hand at the broken pane. The first thing in the novel is a haunting, and it happens to a stranger.

Chapter 1
Lockwood congratulates himself on finding a misanthropist’s heaven, and misreads every person in the house.
Chapter 3
Catherine Earnshaw’s names scratched on a window ledge, her diary in the margins of a sermon, and the ice-cold hand.
Chapter 3
Heathcliff wrenches open the lattice and sobs into the night for her to come in, with Lockwood watching from the passage.

Chapters 4–9 Heathcliff’s arrival, and Catherine’s choice

Mr Earnshaw walks back sixty miles with a child nobody can account for. When he dies, Hindley reduces Heathcliff to a servant. Catherine, bitten by a dog at the Grange, comes back five weeks later a young lady, and tells Nelly she cannot marry the man she says she is.

Chapter 4
The child is brought home as a nameless bundle; the fiddle promised to Hindley is crushed and the whip promised to Cathy is lost.
Chapter 6
The two children look in through the Grange window at the Lintons quarrelling over a lapdog.
Chapter 9
Catherine’s account of her dream, her engagement and her identity; Heathcliff hears half of it and leaves.

Chapters 10–17 Heathcliff’s return, and Catherine’s death

Heathcliff comes back after three years with money nobody can explain and begins collecting: Hindley’s estate at cards, Isabella Linton by elopement, and a last interview with Catherine on the day she dies. Hindley drinks himself to death within six months; Heathcliff is left with the Heights and Hindley’s son to raise crooked.

Chapter 10
Catherine warns Isabella that Heathcliff is ‘an arid wilderness of furze and whinstone’, and is not believed.
Chapter 15
The last meeting: mutual accusation, and the charge that she has killed them both.
Chapter 16
Catherine dies; Heathcliff, outside, asks to be haunted for as long as he lives.
Chapter 17
Isabella escapes south; Hindley dies; Heathcliff lifts Hareton onto a table and promises to twist him.

Chapters 18–26 The second generation

Twelve years pass in a paragraph. Young Cathy is raised inside the park wall and gets out; Linton Heathcliff is fetched back from London and installed at the Heights as a bargaining asset. His father, who has no affection for him at all, keeps him alive just long enough to be married.

Chapter 18
Cathy reaches the Heights for the first time and meets a cousin she is not allowed to know about.
Chapter 20
Linton is delivered to his father, who inspects him like stock and dislikes what he sees.
Chapter 21
Heathcliff explains to Nelly exactly what the boy is for: the Grange, by law, without a blow struck.

Chapters 27–31 The marriage taken by force

Cathy and Nelly are locked into the Heights until the wedding takes place; Cathy escapes in time to be at Edgar Linton’s bedside when he dies; Linton dies a few weeks later, leaving everything to his father. Heathcliff tells the story of opening Catherine’s coffin as though it were an anecdote.

Chapter 27
Linton weeps and admits he is a traitor, and lets the door be locked anyway.
Chapter 29
Heathcliff describes bribing the sexton, and the eighteen years of being followed by something he cannot see.
Chapter 31
Lockwood’s second visit: Hareton with a book he cannot read, and Cathy’s contempt for him.

Chapters 32–34 1802, and Heathcliff’s death

Lockwood comes back in September and finds the two young people reading together and the gate unlocked. Heathcliff, with everything he set out to acquire, discovers he cannot be bothered to enjoy it; he stops eating, stops sleeping and dies with the window open. The book ends beside three graves in mild weather.

Chapter 32
Flowers planted in the Heights garden, and a reading lesson conducted as a quarrel.
Chapter 33
Heathcliff tells Nelly his old enemies have not beaten him and he no longer wants to lift his hand.
Chapter 34
His death, his burial without a service, and Lockwood at the three headstones on the slope.
