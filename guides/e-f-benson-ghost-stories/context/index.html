E. F. Benson Ghost Stories · E. F. Benson

Context

Dates

1867
E. F. Benson is born On 24 July, at Wellington College in Berkshire, where his father, Edward White Benson, was the first headmaster.
1882
The Society for Psychical Research is founded Its first president is Henry Sidgwick, the brother of Benson’s mother; it sets out to investigate thought-transference, apparitions, mediums and hypnotism.
1886
Phantasms of the Living Edmund Gurney, F. W. H. Myers and Frank Podmore publish their two-volume study of telepathy and of apparitions seen at a moment of crisis, especially death.
1893
Dodo Benson’s first novel, a society comedy, comes out in the spring, while Benson is in Athens studying the precinct of Asclepios below the Acropolis.
1895
Winters in Egypt In the early months of 1895 and 1896 Benson joins his sister Margaret at Luxor, where she has leave to excavate the temple of Mut at Karnak.
1896
Archbishop Benson dies Benson’s father dies of heart failure on 11 October in the church at Hawarden, where he is staying with Gladstone.
1905
The Angel of Pain Benson’s novel grew, he wrote in 1912, from the story The Man Who Went Too Far.
1906
Two stories in the Pall Mall Magazine The Dust-Cloud appears in January, announced as E. F. Benson’s Motor Story, and The Bus-Conductor in December, subtitled A Modern Ghost Story.
1912
The Room in the Tower and Other Stories Seventeen stories published by Mills & Boon, some reprinted from magazines and the rest new; a second edition follows the same year.
1914
Britain declares war on Germany On 4 August. The first story of Visible and Invisible begins that evening.
1920
Lamb House, Rye Benson takes a sub-lease of Henry James’s old house in Rye, where he had first stayed in the winter of 1916–17. Rye becomes Tilling in his comic novels and in two of these stories.
1923
Visible and Invisible Twelve stories published by Hutchinson; Machaon and Roderick’s Story are set in Tilling.
1927
Lovecraft’s survey In Supernatural Horror in Literature, H. P. Lovecraft picks out The Man Who Went Too Far and, from Visible and Invisible, Negotium Perambulans and The Horror-Horn.
1934
Mayor of Rye Benson is elected mayor of the town, and serves three years running.
1940
Benson dies On 29 February, in London; he is buried in Rye.
1945
Dead of Night Ealing’s film credits Benson among the authors of its stories, and its hearse driver speaks the conductor’s line, just room for one inside.

Places

Wellington College, Berkshire
Where Benson was born in 1867, while his father was headmaster.
Athens
Where Benson studied at the British School of Archaeology in the 1890s; Machaon draws on the precinct of Asclepios below the Acropolis.
Luxor
Where Benson spent winters with his sister Margaret, and the setting of At Abdul Ali’s Grave.
Rye, East Sussex
Benson’s home at Lamb House from 1920, and the Tilling of Machaon and Roderick’s Story.
Forest Row, Ashdown Forest
Where the narrator of The Room in the Tower is met at the station before his stay in the Ashdown Forest.
Dunwich, Suffolk
The crumbling cliffs near the Combe-Martins’ house in The Dust-Cloud.
Brora, Sutherland
The post-town of Gavon, where Sandy and Catrine are buried in Gavon’s Eve.
Sloane Square, London
Where the bus crashes at the end of The Bus-Conductor.
Dover Street, Piccadilly
The tube station of In the Tube.
Penzance, Cornwall
Polearn, the imaginary village of Negotium Perambulans, lies two miles off the road from Penzance to the Land’s End.
