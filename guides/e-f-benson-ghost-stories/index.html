E. F. Benson

E. F. Benson Ghost Stories

A study guide

E. F. Benson wrote these stories, he said in 1912, to give their readers some pleasant qualms and a few uncomfortable moments before bed. The Room in the Tower and Other Stories (1912) and Visible and Invisible (1923) bring the ghost story up to date: a phantom motor on a Norfolk road, a hanged man confessing down a telephone, a scientist’s gramophone that plays back the memories of the dead, a suicide foreseen and then re-enacted in the Tube. Older horrors are there too: a vampire in a tower room, a slug-like Thing from a carved church panel, Pan in the New Forest, a witch at a Highland pool. Many stories borrow the language of the psychical research of the day, with its sensitives, mediums and theories of telepathy, and several are comic, from a house whose ghosts are as ordinary as the post to a dead man who catches his medium cheating. The last, Roderick’s Story, closes the collection with a dying man who finds the other side delightful.

Written About 1905–1923; first published 1912.
