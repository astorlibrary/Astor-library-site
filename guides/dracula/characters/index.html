Dracula · Bram Stoker

Characters

Count Dracula

A Transylvanian boyar, and a purchaser of London property; first appears Chapter I

He appears in person in perhaps a dozen scenes and is offstage for most of the book, which is why he works. He is a historian of his own family, a careful reader of Bradshaw and the Law List, and his invasion of England is carried out by conveyancing, shipping agents and carters.

Jonathan Harker

Newly qualified solicitor, the novel’s first narrator; first appears Chapter I

He goes east to complete a purchase and keeps taking notes after the notes stop being professional: recipes, train times, then the fact that his host has no reflection. His shorthand journal is the document everything else is later measured against, and for four months nobody believes a word of it, including him.

Mina Harker

Assistant schoolmistress, then the hunt’s archivist; first appears Chapter V

She learns shorthand and typewriting to be useful to her husband and ends up as the only person who can see the whole case, because she is the one who puts it in order. The men then decide to protect her by leaving her out, and every disaster in the second half follows from that decision.

Lucy Westenra

Mina’s friend, engaged to Arthur Holmwood; first appears Chapter V

She receives three proposals in one day and asks in a letter why a girl cannot marry three men. The novel then spends five chapters draining her, killing her, burying her and killing her again, and the second death is carried out by the man she was going to marry with the other two watching.

Professor Abraham Van Helsing

Dutch physician, lawyer and philosopher, Seward’s old teacher; first appears Chapter IX

He arrives with a diagnosis he will not state, which is a medical judgement and also a device for keeping the reader waiting. His English is wrong in a consistent and characterised way, and his argument against his own profession (that science wants to explain everything and dismisses whatever it cannot) is the book’s defence of its own plot.

Dr John Seward

Head of the asylum next to Carfax; first appears Chapter V

He dictates his diary onto wax cylinders, the most modern thing in the novel, and the reason nobody can search his account until Mina types it out. He is a careful clinician who keeps a man in a cell while failing entirely to hear what the man is telling him.

R. M. Renfield

A patient at Seward’s asylum; first appears Chapter V

He eats flies, then feeds them to spiders, then the spiders to sparrows, and keeps a ledger of the lives he has accumulated. He is the only person in England who knows Dracula has arrived, and he is the only person nobody thinks to ask. He dies because he will not let his master in a second time.

Quincey Morris

A Texan, one of Lucy’s three suitors; first appears Chapter V

He talks in a stage American that even the other characters notice, proposes to Lucy by asking her to go down the long road with him in double harness, and is the one who adds Winchesters to the party’s arms because the Count comes from wolf country. He dies in the snow below the castle at sunset, and the last child born to the group carries his name.
