Dracula · Bram Stoker

What happens

Chapters I-IV Harker at Castle Dracula

Harker records meals, train times and local customs with a clerk’s precision, and keeps recording them as the food becomes strange, the peasants press crucifixes on him and his host turns out to cast no reflection. The documentary habit makes the horror land: nothing is asserted, everything is minuted.

Chapters I-II
The journey through Bistritz and the Borgo Pass; Dracula’s welcome at the threshold; the shaving mirror.
Chapter III
The Count’s history of his race, the crawling descent of the wall, and the three women in the unused room.
Chapter IV
Fifty boxes of earth, the letters Harker is forced to post-date, and his resolve to climb down the precipice.

Chapters V-VIII Lucy and Mina at Whitby, and the wreck of the Demeter

The novel switches to Lucy and Mina’s correspondence, three proposals of marriage in one day, and a seaside holiday. Then a storm drives an empty Russian schooner onto the sands at Whitby with its dead captain lashed to the wheel, and a large dog leaps ashore. Lucy starts walking in her sleep.

Chapters V-VI
Lucy’s three proposals, Seward’s phonograph diary, Renfield’s ledger of flies and spiders, and the Whitby churchyard.
Chapter VII
The cutting from the Dailygraph and the Demeter’s log, kept until the last crewman is gone.
Chapter VIII
Lucy found on the seat above the harbour with two small punctures; Mina is called to Jonathan’s sickbed in Budapest.

Chapters IX-XIII Lucy’s illness, death and return

Seward cannot account for anaemia without blood loss and sends for his old teacher. Van Helsing knows straight away, treats Lucy with garlic and the blood of four men, and is undone by a mother who takes the flowers away and opens the window. Lucy dies, is buried, and children on Hampstead Heath begin coming home with small wounds.

Chapters IX-X
Van Helsing arrives; the first transfusions; the garlic that a well-meaning mother removes from the room.
Chapters XI-XII
The wolf Bersicker, Mrs Westenra’s death, and Lucy’s last change of voice at the moment of dying.
Chapter XIII
The funeral, the newspaper reports of the bloofer lady, and Van Helsing’s plan to cut off her head.

Chapters XIV-XVIII Mina assembles the documents

The novel’s real turning point is clerical. Mina transcribes Jonathan’s shorthand, copies Seward’s cylinders, and puts every document into one chronological sequence, at which point five people can finally see what the reader has seen since Whitby. Lucy is dealt with in her tomb, and the group agrees to hunt.

Chapters XIV-XV
Mina’s transcription; Van Helsing reads Jonathan’s journal; the tomb is found empty and then occupied.
Chapter XVI
The woman in the churchyard, the child she carries, and what is done at the tomb by daylight.
Chapters XVII-XVIII
The papers are brought together at Purfleet; Van Helsing sets out what a vampire can and cannot do.

Chapters XIX-XXIII The hunt for the boxes, and the attack on Mina

The men decide to protect Mina by excluding her from the work, and the decision is what exposes her. Renfield is flung down and killed for trying to keep the Count out; upstairs, the men break in on a scene that Seward describes and Mina has to live with afterwards. She is marked on the forehead by the communion wafer.

Chapters XIX-XX
Carfax is sterilised box by box; Mina is kept from the meetings and begins to sleep heavily.
Chapters XXI-XXII
Renfield’s confession and death; the scene in the Harkers’ bedroom; the burn on Mina’s forehead.
Chapter XXIII
Piccadilly, the last box, and Dracula’s promise that his revenge has only started.

Chapters XXIV-XXVII and the Note The chase to Transylvania, and the closing note

Mina’s hypnotic trances give the group a compass and her own condition gives them a clock. They work out the route by shipping schedules and river maps, split into parties, and reach the castle road as the sun goes down. The closing note, written seven years later, admits that hardly one document in the record is authentic.

Chapters XXIV-XXV
Mina is hypnotised at dawn and dusk; the party waits at Varna for a ship that puts in at Galatz instead.
Chapter XXVI
The river journey, the horse-borne pursuit, and Van Helsing and Mina crossing the last miles alone.
Chapter XXVII and Note
The three women dealt with at the castle, the fight for the cart below the castle, Quincey’s death, and a postscript about proof.
