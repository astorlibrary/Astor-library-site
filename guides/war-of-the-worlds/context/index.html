The War of the Worlds · H. G. Wells

Context

Dates

1866
H. G. Wells born at Bromley He later trained in science in South Kensington under T. H. Huxley.
1877
Schiaparelli maps the canali of Mars The Italian word for channels was widely read in English as canals, and the idea of Martian engineering entered popular astronomy.
1894
Percival Lowell opens his observatory at Flagstaff Built largely to study Mars; the novel opens with a light seen on the planet at the 1894 opposition.
1895
The Time Machine published Wells’s first scientific romance, and the beginning of four years that also produced Moreau, The Invisible Man and this book.
1896
The Island of Doctor Moreau published Its interest in the boundary between men and animals runs straight into the artilleryman’s argument about ants.
1897
Serialised in Pearson’s Magazine British readers met the invasion in monthly instalments; an American serial ran in Cosmopolitan.
1898
First book edition, William Heinemann Harper and Brothers issued the American book edition the same year.
1938
Orson Welles broadcasts the story on CBS Howard Koch’s adaptation moved the first landing to Grovers Mill, New Jersey, and used the form of a news bulletin.
1946
Wells dies in London By then he was known as much for his histories and political writing as for the scientific romances.
1953
George Pal’s film released Directed by Byron Haskin, it relocated the invasion to mid-century America and replaced the tripods with flying machines.
1978
Jeff Wayne’s musical version released A concept album narrated by Richard Burton, which kept the novel’s structure of a witness reporting after the event.
2005
Steven Spielberg’s film released Refocused on one family in flight, and on the refugee traffic that occupies the brother’s chapters.

Places

Horsell Common, Woking
Where the first cylinder falls, into a sandpit on ordinary common land.
Ottershaw
Ogilvy’s observatory, where the narrator watches the flashes on Mars.
Leatherhead
Where the narrator leaves his wife with her cousins, and the town he is later told was destroyed with every soul in it.
Shepperton
Where artillery brings down a fighting-machine and the Thames is boiled by the wreck.
Putney Hill
The house where the artilleryman explains the underground future of the human race.
Primrose Hill
The Martian redoubt over dead London, where the invaders are found killed by bacteria.
