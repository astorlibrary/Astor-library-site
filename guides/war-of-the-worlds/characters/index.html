The War of the Worlds · H. G. Wells

Characters

The narrator

A writer on philosophical subjects, living near Woking; first appears Book I, I

A man whose profession is explaining things and who spends the whole invasion failing to. He is never given a name, never holds a weapon that works and never makes a decision that alters events; what he does instead is look carefully and write it down years afterwards, which is the only kind of authority the book allows anyone.

The narrator's brother

A medical student in London; first appears Book I, XIV

He exists so that the book can leave Surrey. His chapters are the only place where the invasion is seen as a problem of crowds, trains, money and roads, and he is also the only character who does something plainly useful: he gets two women out alive.

Ogilvy

An astronomer at Ottershaw; first appears Book I, I

He sees the first jet of gas leave Mars and tells the narrator that the chances against anything manlike there are a million to one. He is the first man to reach the cylinder and one of the first killed by the Heat-Ray, which is the book’s briskest way of dealing with confident expertise.

The curate

A clergyman met in the ruins of Weybridge; first appears Book I, XIII

He meets the invasion with a theology that cannot hold it and collapses into repetition, appetite and noise. Wells does not let the narrator feel superior for long: the two men eat the same rations, and the killing in the scullery is done for the very practical reason that the curate can be heard.

The artilleryman

A survivor of a gun battery destroyed on the common; first appears Book I, XI

Twice met and twice differently judged. On the second meeting he lays out a whole underground civilisation of able men who will learn to fly a fighting-machine, and the narrator is carried away by it until morning, when the plan turns out to consist of a short tunnel, a stolen larder and a game of cards.

The Martians

The invaders; first appears Book I, IV

Heads and hands with no digestive system, moving in machines because Earth’s gravity is too heavy for their bodies. They are never given speech, motive or negotiation; Wells presents them as a species whose planet is dying, neither devils nor monsters, doing what our own species has done to other populations.

The narrator's wife

Taken to her cousins at Leatherhead when the fighting begins; first appears Book I, I

She is driven to relatives for safety as soon as the fighting begins and is off the page for most of the book, which is why the last line works. The narrator counts her among the dead, and she counts him among the dead, for longer than either of them is actually missing.
