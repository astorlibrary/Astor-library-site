The word ‘nothing’

The word runs through both plots like a fault line: Cordelia’s answer, Lear’s reply, Edmund’s ‘nothing’ as he pockets a forged letter, the Fool’s arithmetic of noughts. The play sets speech that performs feeling against speech that measures it, and shows a state making irreversible decisions on the strength of the first.

Through the play A refusal in 1.1, a joke through Act I, a fact by Act III, when Lear has nothing left to compare with, and an answer in 5.3 where a father asks a dead daughter to speak.

In the text

Unhappy that I am, I cannot heave My heart into my mouth: I love your majesty According to my bond; no more nor less.

Cordelia, 1.1.90

Cordelia offers a bond, an obligation that can be relied on, in place of a quantity that cannot be checked.

Nothing will come of nothing: speak again.

King Lear, 1.1.89

Lear applies a law of physics to affection, and gives her another turn, so the disaster is a decision he had the chance to undo.

Why should a dog, a horse, a rat, have life, And thou no breath at all? Thou’lt come no more, Never, never, never, never, never!

King Lear, 5.3.314

Five times “never”, and the metre stops doing anything except repeat.

Sight, blindness and judgement

Kent tells Lear to see better in the first scene, and the play spends five acts taking the metaphor literally. Gloucester says he stumbled when he saw; Lear, mad, tells a blind man that a man may see how this world goes with no eyes. Understanding in this play is consistently something that arrives after the organ of understanding has been destroyed.

Through the play Figurative in Act I, appallingly literal in 3.7, and reversed in 4.6, where the blind man and the mad king conduct the play’s clearest analysis of power.

In the text

I have no way, and therefore want no eyes; I stumbled when I saw:

Gloucester, 4.1.19

Gloucester made his mistake while he could still see: sight was never how he understood anything.

Lest it see more, prevent it. Out, vile jelly!

Cornwall, 3.7.84

An eye is destroyed for what it might yet witness, on stage, in front of the audience.

Howl, howl, howl, howl! O, you are men of stones: Had I your tongues and eyes, I’ld use them so That heaven’s vault should crack.

King Lear, 5.3.262

Lear accuses the men around him of having tongues and eyes and not using them.

Nature and the unnatural

Everyone in the play appeals to nature and they mean incompatible things by it. Lear calls on it to make his daughter sterile; Edmund makes it his goddess and means appetite without law; Gloucester reads it in eclipses. The storm is the argument brought on stage, and it answers nobody.

Through the play Invoked as authority in Acts I and II, unanswering in Act III, and reduced in 3.4 to a bare forked animal that owes the worm no silk.

In the text

Thou, nature, art my goddess; to thy law My services are bound. Wherefore should I Stand in the plague of custom, and permit The curiosity of nations to deprive me,

Edmund, 1.2.1

Edmund makes nature his goddess and means appetite without law. His case against being ruled by birth order and legitimacy is a good one.

Blow, winds, and crack your cheeks! rage! blow!

King Lear, 3.2.1

Lear gives orders to weather that cannot obey, because giving orders is the only register he has.

As flies to wanton boys, are we to the gods. They kill us for their sport.

Gloucester, 4.1.38

Gloucester’s theology comes from private disaster, and the play does not endorse it: at the end he dies of joy.

Justice and authority

The play holds two trials. The first is a mock one in a farmhouse, where a mad king arraigns a joint-stool; the second is a duel between brothers decided by force. In between, Lear works out that a dog is obeyed in office and that the difference between the justice and the thief is a change of places, perhaps the most radical political speech Shakespeare ever gives a king.

Through the play Ceremonial in 1.1, parodied in 3.6, dismantled in 4.6, and finally left to a chivalric combat that settles a private quarrel and saves nobody.

In the text

Poor naked wretches, whereso’er you are, That bide the pelting of this pitiless storm,

King Lear, 3.4.28

The first time Lear thinks of anyone who is not in the room, and admits he has taken too little care of them.

And the creature run from the cur? There thou mightst behold the great image of authority: a dog’s obeyed in office.

King Lear, 4.6.150

Authority reduced to a dog obeyed in office: the play’s most exact political sentence, from a king who has lost his reason.

The wheel is come full circle: I am here.

Edmund, 5.3.178

Edmund rejected Fortune for nature, and at the end describes his fall as Fortune’s wheel, almost pleased by its neatness.

Age, need and dependence

The quarrel that drives Lear onto the heath is about a number: a hundred knights, then fifty, then twenty-five, then what need one. The daughters’ case is not obviously wrong, and the play knows it. What Lear finds in answer is that need is not the measure of a life, an argument he can only make once he has lost the argument.

Through the play Negotiated downwards through Act II, answered in 2.4, and turned outwards in 3.4, where the same reasoning is applied to people who never had anything to lose.

In the text

Who is it that can tell me who I am?

King Lear, 1.4.200

Without the retinue and the obedience, Lear has no test to apply to himself. The Fool answers: Lear’s shadow.

O, reason not the need: our basest beggars Are in the poorest thing superfluous: Allow not nature more than nature needs, Man’s life is cheap as beast’s.

King Lear, 2.4.259

His best argument, made for a hundred knights he does not need. It outlasts its occasion: need is not the measure of a life.

I am a very foolish fond old man, Fourscore and upward, not an hour more nor less; And, to deal plainly, I fear I am not in my perfect mind.

King Lear, 4.7.61

Lear speaks plainly at last, in the manner he punished Cordelia for in the first scene.

Service and loyalty

The play’s decency is almost entirely in its servants. Kent sits in the stocks for a master who banished him; an unnamed servant of Cornwall’s draws a sword to stop a blinding and dies for it; an old tenant leads Gloucester when his family cannot. Rank organises the tragedy, but obligation runs the other way.

Through the play Punished in 2.2, fatal in 3.7, and finally the only bond left standing in 5.3, where Kent follows his master out of the play.

In the text

See better, Lear; and let me still remain The true blank of thine eye.

Kent, 1.1.160

Kent asks to stay the true mark of the king’s eye, and calls the king by his name, which in this court is the offence.
