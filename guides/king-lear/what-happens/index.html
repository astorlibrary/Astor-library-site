King Lear · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The division of the kingdom

Lear makes the handover of a kingdom into a public competition in flattery, and punishes the one daughter who will not compete. The same act shows Edmund forging a letter and persuading his father of the opposite of the truth. Both fathers act on speech alone, and both are wrong within a hundred lines of hearing it.

1.1
Cordelia says nothing, is disinherited and married to France; Kent objects and is banished.
1.2
Edmund persuades Gloucester that Edgar plots against him, using a document he wrote himself.
1.3
Goneril tells her steward to treat the old king and his knights coldly on purpose.
1.4
Kent returns in disguise, the Fool arrives, and Lear curses Goneril with sterility.
1.5
The Fool works on Lear’s mistake; Lear names the fear that will occupy the rest of the play.

Act II Kent in the stocks, and Lear’s knights taken away

Edmund stages an attack on himself and inherits his brother’s place. Kent is put in the stocks, an insult to the king through his servant, and Lear arrives to find the two sisters auctioning his household downwards from a hundred knights to none. He walks out into the weather sooner than accept the last offer.

2.1
A staged skirmish sends Edgar into flight and makes Edmund the loyal son.
2.2
A servant of the king is punished by a duke, and Gloucester’s protest is overruled.
2.3
Edgar decides that the only safe identity left to him is that of a mad beggar.
2.4
The sisters reduce the retinue by arithmetic; Lear goes out into the storm.

Act III The storm on the heath, and Gloucester’s blinding

Lear shouts at the elements, then does something he has never done before: he thinks about people who have no shelter on any night of the year. The scenes alternate between the heath and Gloucester’s castle, so the king’s disintegration is cut against the machinery of a coup, and the act ends with a duke pulling out an old man’s eyes in his own house.

3.1
Kent tells a gentleman of the division between the dukes and of forces landing.
3.2
Lear commands the weather, and the Fool answers him in riddles and rhyme.
3.3
Gloucester confides his loyalty to the king to the son who immediately reports it.
3.4
Lear meets Poor Tom, and tears off his own clothes to match him.
3.5
Edmund is made Earl of Gloucester for the information he has just given.
3.6
Lear arraigns a joint-stool as Goneril before a beggar, a fool and a disguised earl.
3.7
Cornwall puts out Gloucester’s eyes; a servant kills him for it and is killed.

Act IV Gloucester’s fall, and Lear and Cordelia at Dover

A blind man is guided towards a cliff by the son he disowned, and survives a fall that never happens. A mad king wanders through a field preaching about justice to a man with no eyes. The act ends with the one scene of repair the play allows: Cordelia’s army has landed, and Lear wakes up in clean clothes not sure whether he is dead.

4.1
Gloucester asks a beggar to take him to Dover, not knowing he has hired his son.
4.2
Albany names his wife a fiend; news arrives of Cornwall’s death.
4.3
A gentleman describes how Cordelia received the letters, in a scene the Folio does not print.
4.4
Cordelia sends a hundred soldiers to find her father in the high-grown corn.
4.5
Regan questions Oswald about Goneril’s letters to Edmund.
4.6
The fall that is not a fall, then Lear crowned with weeds on justice, authority and adultery.
4.7
Lear comes back to himself, kneels to Cordelia, and asks her to forget and forgive.

Act V The battle, and the deaths of Cordelia and Lear

The British army defeats the French, so the reunion of Act IV lasts one scene. Edmund’s order for Cordelia’s death is carried out while he lies dying from the duel, and his reprieve comes too late. The play gives no consolation: the last thing Lear does is ask the people round him to look at his daughter’s lips.

5.1
Edmund calculates between two sisters; Edgar delivers a letter to Albany.
5.2
Lear and Cordelia are taken; Edgar tells his father that ripeness is all.
5.3
Prison, a challenge, a duel, two dead sisters, a reprieve too late, and Lear with Cordelia’s body.
