The Man Who Was Thursday · G. K. Chesterton

Characters

Gabriel Syme

Poet, philosophical policeman, and the Thursday of the title; first appears Chapter I

A man who became a defender of order because he was brought up among people who thought disorder was interesting. He is brave in a particular way (terrified at every stage and never once stopping), and the whole plot comes from his refusal to break a promise he should never have made.

Sunday

President of the Central Anarchist Council; first appears Chapter V

Enormous, cheerful, and impossible to place. He hires the policemen and chairs the anarchists; he flees across London leaving nonsense notes; and at the end he says he is the peace of God and declines to explain the rest. Chesterton’s own account was that Sunday is Nature as it appears from behind, which explains the back of the book without quite explaining the face.

Lucian Gregory

The anarchist poet of Saffron Park; first appears Chapter I

Red-haired, theatrical and, it turns out, the only person in the book who means it. He loses his seat on the council by being honest about his convictions in front of a man who is lying about his, and returns at the end as the accuser, the one figure the resolution cannot absorb.

The Secretary (Monday)

The council’s secretary, with a crooked smile; first appears Chapter V

The most frightening of the six on first meeting and the most serious at the end. He is the one who will not accept the explanation: he can forgive God his anger but not his peace, which is the strongest objection the book raises against its own resolution.

Professor de Worms (Friday)

An ancient German nihilist, pursuing Syme across London; first appears Chapter V

A decaying old man who moves at a crawl and cannot be escaped, which makes the longest chase in the book also the slowest. He is a young actor in a false beard, frightened of Syme, and his unmasking is the moment the novel’s method becomes clear: every terror is a colleague.

Dr Bull (Saturday)

The council’s doctor, behind black spectacles; first appears Chapter V

Sinister for exactly as long as his glasses stay on. When they come off he is a cheerful young man with an ordinary face, and Syme’s terror is revealed to have been a reaction to a pair of lenses. The novel’s argument about masks is nowhere neater.

The Marquis de St. Eustache (Wednesday)

A rich, insolent Frenchman with a bomb and a duel to fight; first appears Chapter V

The most conventional villain in the book and therefore the one whose unmasking is funniest: he cannot be wounded because he is wearing so much disguise, and he produces his police card mid-duel. After it he is the most useful man in the pursuit.

Rosamond Gregory

Gregory’s sister, the girl with gold-red hair; first appears Chapter I

She appears twice, at the beginning and at the very end, and says almost nothing. Her function is structural: she is the ordinary daylight the nightmare is measured against, and the last image of the book is her cutting lilac before breakfast.
