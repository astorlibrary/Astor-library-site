The Man Who Was Thursday · G. K. Chesterton

Context

Dates

1874
Chesterton born in Kensington Into the comfortable London he would spend his career defending against people who found it boring.
1894
Chesterton at the Slade School of Art At art school he went through the crisis of doubt and solipsism he later said the novel was written about.
1901
Chesterton marries Frances Blogg The dedicatory poem to E. C. Bentley describes the period the book looks back on as ended by marriage and a creed.
1904
Chesterton’s first novel, The Napoleon of Notting Hill A fable in which the boroughs of London go to war.
1905
Chesterton publishes Heretics Chesterton’s argument with the fashionable pessimists, which he put into a story three years later.
1908
The Man Who Was Thursday published Issued by J. W. Arrowsmith with the subtitle A Nightmare; Orthodoxy followed in the same year.
1922
Chesterton received into the Catholic Church Fourteen years after the novel readers most often take as a statement of his faith.
1926
First stage adaptation produced in London The adaptation was by Mrs Cecil Chesterton and Ralph Neale; the novel has been staged and broadcast many times since.
1936
Chesterton dies; his Autobiography published The Autobiography, published that year, insists the book was a nightmare about the doubt of the eighteen-nineties, and not a theological statement.
1938
Orson Welles’s Mercury Theatre radio adaptation The Mercury Theatre on the Air adapted the novel for radio in September, two months before the War of the Worlds broadcast.

Places

Bedford Park, Chiswick
The aesthetes’ red-brick suburb Chesterton renamed Saffron Park.
Leicester Square
Where the Central Anarchist Council takes breakfast on a balcony, in public.
Soho
The streets of the Professor’s slow pursuit.
Dover
The crossing point for the chase into France.
Calais
Where the detectives land, and where the countryside turns against them.
