The Man Who Was Thursday · G. K. Chesterton

What happens

Chapters I-II Syme and Gregory, and the election to the Council

In a red-brick suburb full of opinions, the anarchist poet Lucian Gregory loses an argument to a man who says he is a poet of law. Stung, Gregory takes his opponent underground to prove that his anarchism is real, and Syme, having sworn on his honour to say nothing, swears Gregory to the same silence and is elected to the seat Gregory wanted.

Chapter I
A sunset suburb, a garden argument about trains and chaos, and a red-haired poet who claims the artist and the anarchist are the same man.
Chapter II
An underground room reached through a public house, walls packed with rifles and revolvers, and a vote that makes a policeman the anarchist called Thursday.

Chapters III-V The Council of Days

Breakfast on a Leicester Square balcony, in full view of the street, because the safest place to plot is in the open. Syme meets the six others and their president; the book goes back to explain how he became a policeman at all; and he discovers that what they fear at this table is being seen through, more than arrest.

Chapter III
Sunday’s face grows larger as Syme approaches it, and the conspiracy turns out to eat a very good breakfast in public.
Chapter IV
The book doubles back: a policeman in a dark room, a force of philosophical detectives, and the sentence that explains the whole plot: they go to artistic tea-parties to detect pessimists.
Chapter V
Sunday announces that there is a spy at the table. Every man at it has the same reason to be frightened, and none of them knows that yet.

Chapters VI-X One by one, the anarchists turn out to be policemen

Gogol goes first, exposed at the table by the blue card in his waistcoat pocket. Then the Professor pursues Syme across London and lays a blue card of his own on the table; Dr Bull takes off his black spectacles and becomes an ordinary young man; and the Marquis, fought in a French field, turns out to be an inspector under a false face. Each unmasking is funnier than the last and leaves the plot with fewer enemies and no explanation.

Chapter VI
Gogol is unmasked as a Scotland Yard man, and Sunday enjoys it more than anyone.
Chapter VII
A chase through Soho at the speed of an old man’s shuffle, and all the more frightening for it.
Chapter VIII
The Professor makes Syme swear he is not in the police, then announces that he is: all day he has only been trying to catch up with a colleague.
Chapter IX
Dr Bull’s dark glasses come off and a pleasant young man appears underneath: the horror was all in the glasses.
Chapter X
A duel in France with a man who cannot be cut, because he is padded with disguise, and who is, inevitably, on the same side.

Chapters XI-XIII The chase through France

With the council exposed, the countryside itself takes up the pursuit: a French town, then a mob, then what looks like the whole earth in arms against a handful of detectives. The Secretary is the last to declare himself a policeman, which leaves nobody on the other side at all, so the survivors turn and chase Sunday instead, through London, on a fire engine, on an elephant and finally in a balloon.

Chapter XI
The reversal the title of the chapter states, played straight and at speed.
Chapter XII
Every window and hedge seems to hold an enemy, and the world takes on the logic of a dream in which nothing can be trusted to stay what it is.
Chapter XIII
Sunday escapes through London leaving absurd notes behind him, and the chase becomes a comic phantasmagoria that nobody in it can explain.

Chapters XIV-XV Sunday’s garden, and waking

The six are dressed as the days of creation and seated in a garden, where Sunday tells them he is the Sabbath and the peace of God. The Secretary refuses to forgive him for it; Gregory arrives as the only real anarchist and accuses them of never having suffered; and Syme, asked whether he can drink of the cup, wakes in a lane at dawn beside the man he argued with in chapter one.

Chapter XIV
Each man learns what he was a mask for, and the thriller reveals itself to have been a theology all along.
Chapter XV
Gregory’s charge, Sunday’s question, the face that fills the sky, and a girl cutting lilac before breakfast.
