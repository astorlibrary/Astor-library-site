The House on the Borderland · William Hope Hodgson

What happens

The frame Two anglers, a ruin and a damp book

Hodgson spends his opening pages establishing how the story reaches us: a fishing holiday in Kraighten, a hamlet forty miles from the nearest railway station, a house reduced to rubble above a pit, and a small thick book dug out of the debris. The narrator who reads it warns that the account is stiff and abrupt and that he has left it alone, which licenses everything strange that follows.

Author’s Introduction
The editor describes handling the manuscript: damp pages, close handwriting, and the last few leaves blank.
The Finding of the Manuscript
Two men fish a river in the west of Ireland, find a garden gone wild and a house thrown down, and dig the book out of the wreck.

The first vision A green jade house, in an arena of gods

The Recluse is carried out of his body across a plain and into an amphitheatre of mountains, where he finds his own house rebuilt in green jade at enormous scale, ringed by the gods and monsters of every mythology. He is shown it, and not told anything. The vision establishes what the rest of the book assumes: the house stands where two orders of reality touch.

The Plain of Silence
The first seizure takes him from his study to a silent plain under a red sun.
The House in the Arena
His own house, identical in every particular except colour and size, at the centre of a ring of watching figures.
The Earth
He comes back to his study nearly a day and a night later, and his sister, used to his absences, asks no questions.

The swine-things The siege by the swine-things

The middle of the novel is a straight siege story and a very good one. Creatures with human bodies and swine faces come out of the chasm, and the Recluse defends the house room by room with a rifle and a barricade while his sister is kept ignorant. Hodgson is exact about ammunition, doors, cellar trapdoors and the sound of things moving underground.

The Thing in the Pit
A face at the edge of the chasm, and a dog that will not go near it.
The Swine-Things
The creatures cross the garden at night; the windows and doors become the plot.
The Trap in the Great Cellar
The pit runs under the house, and the last defence is a stone slab.

The Sea of Sleep Meeting his lost love

Between the siege and the ending, the book turns quiet. The Recluse is taken to a still sea under a vast white orb and meets the woman he loved and lost, and the only tenderness in the novel is here. The darkness that comes across the orb, which she calls the Sun of Darkness, is the same darkness that will end the book.

The Sea of Sleep
A meeting with no explanation offered and none asked for.
The Noise in the Night
The light is eaten across and she has to go before it closes.

The end of the solar system The death of the sun

Time accelerates. Days flicker, the sun becomes a band of fire, the years run out, and the Recluse sits in his study while the earth slows, the sun reddens and dies, a green star draws the system into itself, and the last light goes. His dog crumbles to dust beside him. The manuscript ends in the middle of a word, and a footnote observes that the pen has trailed off across the paper.

The Slowing Rotation
The sun’s arc speeds up until light and dark blur into a grey band.
The Green Star
A green sun grows in the sky and the earth’s orbit begins to change.
The End of the Solar System
The sun goes out, the earth travels across its dark face, and the cold arrives.
The Dark Sun
The Celestial Globes, the Dark Sun, and a sentence that stops.
