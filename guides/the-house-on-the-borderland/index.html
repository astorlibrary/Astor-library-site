William Hope Hodgson

The House on the Borderland

A study guide

Two anglers in the west of Ireland find a ruined house above a chasm and a damp manuscript in the rubble. It was written by a man who lived there alone with his sister and his dog, and it describes two things: a siege by swine-faced creatures that come up out of the pit, and a set of visions in which the writer sits in his own chair while the sun races across the sky, slows, dies, and goes out. The second half is one of the strangest passages in English fiction (no plot, no dialogue, one man watching the solar system end), and it stops mid-word.

Written 1907, in the burst of work that followed Hodgson’s years at sea; first published 1908.
