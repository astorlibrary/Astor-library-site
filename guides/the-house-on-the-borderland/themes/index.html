The House on the Borderland · William Hope Hodgson

Themes

Isolation

The Recluse keeps no servants, hates his neighbours, protects his sister from the facts and talks only to a dog. The diary exists because of that, and the whole book is the record of a solitude that has no outlet except paper.

He begins alone by choice, is besieged alone by necessity, and ends alone in a universe with no other living thing in it.

The peasantry, who inhabit the wilderness beyond, say that I am mad.

The Plain of Silence

I have one friend, a dog; yes, I would sooner have old Pepper than the rest of Creation together.

The Plain of Silence

Deep time

Hodgson takes the geological and astronomical timescales that Victorian science had made familiar and puts one man inside them, conscious, in his own study. The horror lies in a rate of change more than in any event.

Days blur into a band, years into a flicker, and by the last chapters the sun is a dead face the earth crawls across.

he literally and actually crumbled into a mouldering heap of bones and dust

that little hill of ash

Unexplained horror

No one in the book understands what has happened. The Recluse reports, the editor annotates, and neither offers a cause for the pit, the creatures or the visions.

The footnotes get more confident as the events get less explicable, until the last one is reduced to describing the movement of a pen.

I read, and, in reading, lifted the Curtains of the Impossible that blind the mind, and looked out into the unknown.

Author’s Introduction

The Plain of Silence

Religion

The introduction says the story carries a shadowed picture of what may be given the accepted titles of Heaven and Hell, and the arena of gods and the Sea of Sleep supply both. No religion is named and no comfort is offered.

The vision of the gods comes early and is never returned to; what the book ends with instead is physics.

an enormous amphitheatre of mountains

The House in the Arena

a stupendous structure built apparently of green jade

The House in the Arena

The pit

The chasm is a real feature of the ground, measured at about a hundred and fifty feet, and it is also the way through. Hodgson gets his effects by treating the supernatural as geology.

It is a curiosity in the first chapters, a source in the middle, and by the cellar chapters it is directly under the floor.

For a couple of centuries, this house has had a reputation, a bad one

The Plain of Silence

in no particular, save in color and its enormous size, did the lonely structure vary from this house in which I live

The House in the Arena

The damaged manuscript

The text arrives damaged: damp, abrupt, gapped, and finally broken off. Every uncertainty in the story can be blamed on the document, which is exactly why the document is presented so carefully.

The frame introduces the damage, the footnotes measure it, and the last word is unfinished.

I read, and, in reading, lifted the Curtains of the Impossible that blind the mind, and looked out into the unknown.

Lost love

The one relationship in the book is with someone lost before it starts, met again only in a vision, and lost a second time to an advancing darkness.

The Sea of Sleep chapters supply the loss that the cosmic ending then repeats on the scale of a solar system.
