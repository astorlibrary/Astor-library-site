The Wendigo · Algernon Blackwood

Context

Dates

1869
Blackwood born in Kent Into the evangelical household whose certainties his supernatural fiction goes on questioning.
1890
Blackwood emigrates to Canada He emigrated at twenty, failed at dairy farming and hotel-keeping near Toronto, and learned the bush he writes about here.
1892
Blackwood a struggling reporter in New York Years as a reporter and model, often destitute; the wilderness in his fiction is remembered from real weeks of it.
1899
Blackwood returns to England He returned at thirty and began writing the stories that used the country he had left.
1907
Blackwood’s The Willows published The Danube story that made his reputation and set the pattern this one follows.
1910
The Wendigo published In The Lost Valley and Other Stories, the collection that consolidated the reputation.
1927
Lovecraft praises Blackwood in Supernatural Horror in Literature He puts The Willows first among Blackwood’s stories and this one next: less well proportioned, but still very effective.
1934
Blackwood begins broadcasting his stories on the BBC His readings on the radio, and later on television, made him known as the Ghost Man.
1951
Algernon Blackwood dies He died at eighty-two, with the Canadian stories still the work most anthologised.

Places

Rat Portage, now Kenora
The railway town the hunting party outfits from.
Lake of the Woods
The island-scattered water the story’s Fifty Island Water is drawn from.
Toronto
Where Blackwood farmed, kept a hotel and failed at both in the early 1890s.
Montreal
The Quebec world Défago has strayed from, and the French-Canadian bush trade it supplied.
Aberdeen
Dr Cathcart’s city, and the source of the story’s rationalism.
