Algernon Blackwood

The Wendigo

A study guide

A moose-hunting party splits up, and a divinity student is sent off with a French-Canadian guide who does not want to go where they are going. In the night the guide smells something, weeps, and runs into the bush calling about his burning feet of fire. What is left behind is a set of tracks that begin as a man’s and stop being one. The rest of the story is a psychologist explaining it all away while it happens to him too, and it ends with a thing walking into camp that the party’s Indian cook does not stay to look at twice.

Written Out of Blackwood’s years in Canada in the 1890s, in the run of tales that followed The Willows; first published 1910.
