Carmilla · Sheridan Le Fanu

Characters

Laura

The narrator, nineteen at the time; first appears Chapter I

The motherless daughter of an Englishman, brought up in a remote Styrian castle by two governesses, with only occasional visitors of her own age. She is lonely, romantic and honest about her own confusion: she feels attraction and repulsion towards Carmilla, and says so. Her name is first spoken in chapter VIII, by her father, as she begins to fade. She writes the story years later for a correspondent, and by the time the Prologue is written she has died.

Carmilla

The guest; also Millarca, and Mircalla, Countess Karnstein; first appears Chapter I

Beautiful, languid and secretive, she will say only that her name is Carmilla, that her family is ancient and noble, and that her home lies to the west. She takes a cup of chocolate and eats nothing, never joins the family prayers, hates funerals and is fierce when crossed. Her love for Laura is sincere by its own lights, and it is also hunger: she talks of Laura dying into her, of a cruelty that is also love, of Laura coming with her to death. She is a Karnstein countess buried a century and a half before, who became a vampire, the Baron explains, after one had preyed on her.

Laura’s father

An Englishman retired from the Austrian service; first appears Chapter I

Kind and growing old, he retired on a pension and bought the schloss as a bargain. He reads Shakespeare aloud to keep up the family’s English, prides himself on being something of a physician, and at Laura’s pleading offers the stranger’s daughter a home. He explains the local deaths by natural causes and Carmilla’s disappearance by sleepwalking, and keeps what the doctor tells him from Laura. His late wife was descended from the Karnsteins.

General Spielsdorf

An old soldier, guardian of Bertha Rheinfeldt; first appears Chapter II

An old friend of Laura’s father, childless, and a man who generally requires proof. At a masked ball he let himself be charmed into taking charge of a stranger’s daughter, and he curses his own conceited incredulity for what followed. When he reaches the schloss, ten months have aged him by years, and he is bent on the pious sacrilege of finding the vampire’s grave. He tells his story on the drive to Karnstein and finishes it in the ruined chapel.

Bertha Rheinfeldt

The General’s niece and ward; first appears Chapter II

A charming girl whom Laura never meets. She was to have come to the schloss with the General for a visit of some weeks; instead he writes that she is dead. His story shows her meeting Millarca at the ball, taken with her at first sight and begging him to invite her home, and then suffering exactly the dreams and sensations that Laura is suffering as she listens. She died without suspecting the cause.

The Countess

The lady in black velvet who poses as Carmilla’s mother; first appears Chapter II

Stately, commanding and theatrical, she appears twice: at Count Carlsfeld’s masquerade, where she intrigues the General with incidents from his past, and at Laura’s gate after the carriage accident. Each time she pleads urgent business of life and death, leaves her daughter with strangers and insists on secrecy. A grinning woman in a turban watches from her carriage window, and at the ball she is attended by a gentleman in black whose face is the palest the General has ever seen.

Madame Perrodon

Laura’s governess, from Berne; first appears Chapter I

Fat, middle-aged, good-natured and romantic, she has looked after Laura since infancy and in part made up to her for the loss of her mother. She thinks Carmilla the prettiest creature she ever saw. The doctor tells her never to leave Laura alone, and she is standing in the chapel passage when Carmilla vanishes, and sitting up in Laura’s room that night.

Mademoiselle De Lafontaine

The finishing governess; first appears Chapter I

Her father was German, and so she is assumed to be psychological, metaphysical and something of a mystic. She believes that the full moon acts on dreams, on lunacy and on nervous people. She alone noticed the grinning woman in the carriage, and she passes on a servant’s story of a female figure in the lime-tree walk.

Baron Vordenburg

A scholar of vampirism living on a pittance in Gratz; first appears Chapter XV

Tall, narrow-chested and stooping, with gold spectacles and old black gloves far too wide for his hands, he knows every work on vampires and has drawn rules from a digest of the judicial cases. His ancestor was the Moravian nobleman, in truth a native of Upper Styria, who had loved Mircalla in his youth and hid her tomb to save her body from outrage. That ancestor’s tracings, notes and confession lead the Baron to the grave.

The hunchback

A travelling pedlar and showman; first appears Chapter IV

He comes to the schloss about twice a year with his fiddle, his magic-lantern, a salamander and a mandrake stitched together from the dried parts of animals, and a dog that will not cross the drawbridge. He sells the girls charms against the oupire and offers to file down Carmilla’s sharp tooth, which provokes the second and last show of temper Laura ever sees in her.
