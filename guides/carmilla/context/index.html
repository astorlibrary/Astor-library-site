Carmilla · Sheridan Le Fanu

Context

Dates

1725
Peter Plogojowitz An imperial official in Habsburg Serbia reports the opening of a peasant’s grave at Kisiljevo and the staking of a body believed to be a vampire.
1732
Visum et Repertum An Austrian army surgeon’s report on the vampire cases at Medveđa, in Serbia, is reprinted and discussed across Europe. Laura’s commissions innumerable draw on this kind of record.
1746
Calmet’s treatise on revenants and vampires Dom Augustin Calmet’s Dissertations on apparitions and on the revenants and vampires of Hungary, Bohemia, Moravia and Silesia, a source Le Fanu drew on. It tells of a Moravian village, Liebava, freed from a vampire by a Hungarian stranger who watched from the church tower and took its linen, as the woodman’s story does. Henry Christmas’s English translation, The Phantom World, appeared in 1850.
1814
Joseph Sheridan Le Fanu is born in Dublin Into a family of Huguenot descent; he later owned and edited the Dublin University Magazine.
1816
Coleridge publishes Christabel A poem in which a mysterious lady, taken in by a motherless girl and her father, casts a spell over her. Critics have long compared it with Carmilla.
1864
Uncle Silas Le Fanu’s best-known novel, another story of an isolated young woman and a menacing guardian.
1871
Carmilla begins in The Dark Blue The first of four monthly parts appears in December 1871 in a new London magazine, illustrated by D. H. Friston and Michael Fitzgerald.
1872
In a Glass Darkly The serial ends in March, and Carmilla is collected with Green Tea, The Familiar, Mr Justice Harbottle and The Room in the Dragon Volant as cases from the papers of Doctor Hesselius.
1873
Le Fanu dies in Dublin In February, the year after In a Glass Darkly appeared.
1897
Bram Stoker’s Dracula The most famous vampire novel came a quarter of a century later, from another Dublin writer, whose early notes for it set the story in Styria.
1914
Dracula’s Guest A story from Stoker’s papers, published after his death, in which a traveller finds the tomb of a Countess Dolingen of Gratz in Styria.
1932
Carl Theodor Dreyer’s Vampyr A film based on In a Glass Darkly, which takes the bones of its vampire plot from Carmilla and a burial alive from The Room in the Dragon Volant.
1970
The Vampire Lovers Hammer Films’ adaptation, the first of three loosely based on the story.

Places

Graz (Gratz), Styria
The little capital from which the picture cleaner’s son brings the portrait of Mircalla, where the doctor who diagnoses Bertha lives, and where Baron Vordenburg lives on a pittance.
The Styrian forest
An imagined landscape: the schloss, the ruined village of Karnstein three miles west, and General Spielsdorf’s schloss nearly twenty miles away.
Dublin
Where Le Fanu was born, worked as a journalist and editor, and died in 1873.
London
Where The Dark Blue was published, and where Richard Bentley and Son issued In a Glass Darkly.
