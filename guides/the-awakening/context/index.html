The Awakening · Kate Chopin

Context

Dates

1850
Kate O’Flaherty born in St Louis Of Irish and French Creole descent; her father was killed in a railway accident when she was five.
1870
Marriage to Oscar Chopin, and a move to New Orleans Her years in Louisiana, from 1870 to 1884, gave her nearly all the material of her fiction.
1882
Oscar Chopin dies Chopin ran the family business for a time and then returned to St Louis with six children.
1890
At Fault, Chopin’s first novel, published Published at her own expense, and little noticed.
1893
The hurricane destroys Chênière Caminada The Gulf community Edna visits with Robert had been devastated a few years before the novel was written.
1894
Chopin’s Bayou Folk published The Louisiana stories that made her reputation as a regional writer, which is the reputation The Awakening broke.
1897
Chopin begins the novel, then titled A Solitary Soul Her second story collection, A Night in Acadie, came out later that year, while she was at work on the book.
1898
The manuscript finished Dated 21 January 1898, more than a year before it reached print.
1899
Published by Herbert S. Stone & Company On 22 April, in Chicago. Reviewers judged the book on moral grounds and said little about how it was written.
1902
Removed from Evanston Public Library A documented removal. The often-repeated claim that St Louis libraries banned the book has no evidence behind it.
1904
Chopin dies in St Louis On 22 August, five years after the novel appeared and with her third story collection still unpublished.
1969
Per Seyersted’s biography and collected works The scholarly edition and critical study that returned the novel to print and to serious reading.

Places

Grand Isle, Louisiana
The barrier-island resort where the novel opens and closes: Creole New Orleans families summering in a row of cottages.
Chênière Caminada
The fishing settlement west of Grand Isle that Edna and Robert cross to for mass, destroyed by the hurricane of 1893.
New Orleans
The Esplanade Street house, the atelier, the races and the pigeon house around the corner.
St Louis, Missouri
Chopin’s birthplace, the city she returned to as a widow, and where she wrote the novel.
Chicago
Where Herbert S. Stone & Company published the book in April 1899.

Critics

Willa Cather

“A Creole “Bovary””, in The Pittsburgh Leader (1899)

Cather admired Chopin’s style but regretted that she had spent it on so trite and sordid a theme. She compared Edna with Emma Bovary, a woman who asks more of romantic love than it can give.

Per Seyersted

Kate Chopin: A Critical Biography (1969)

Seyersted’s biography, and his edition of Chopin’s complete works in the same year, brought the novel back into print and into teaching after decades of neglect. He read Edna’s awakening as a woman’s claim to a life of her own.
