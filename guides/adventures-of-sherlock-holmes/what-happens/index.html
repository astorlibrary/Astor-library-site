The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes · Arthur Conan Doyle

What happens

A Scandal in Bohemia Irene Adler and the photograph

A king wants a photograph recovered; Holmes fakes a fire to make its owner reveal the hiding place, and Irene Adler, who has already recognised him in disguise, walks out of the country with it. The collection opens by showing the method working perfectly and still failing.

The Red-Headed League Jabez Wilson’s copying job and the bank tunnel

A pawnbroker is paid handsomely to copy out an encyclopaedia because of the colour of his hair. The comedy of the premise is what stops the reader asking the only question that matters, which is what his assistant does in the cellar all day.

A Case of Identity Mary Sutherland’s vanished fiancé

Mary Sutherland’s suitor vanishes on the way to the altar. The solution turns on a typewriter’s worn letters and on an income of a hundred a year, and Holmes finds that he can prove everything and prosecute nothing.

The Boscombe Valley Mystery A son accused of his father’s murder

A son is charged with his father’s murder on the strength of an overheard cry. Holmes reads the ground instead of the testimony, and uncovers a history of blackmail carried from the Australian goldfields into an English estate.

The Five Orange Pips Orange pips, and a client Holmes cannot save

Seed pips posted from abroad precede three deaths in one family. Holmes identifies the organisation, works out the ship and its route, and the client is drowned before any of it can be used. It is the one case in the collection where Holmes fails outright to protect his client.

The Man with the Twisted Lip A City gentleman’s double life as a beggar

A City gentleman disappears in an opium den above the river, and a hideous beggar is found in his rooms. The answer is an income: begging in the correct make-up pays better than journalism, and the two men are one.

The Adventure of the Blue Carbuncle A hat, a goose and a stone

A battered hat and a Christmas goose lead Holmes through the poultry markets to a jewel worth a fortune. Having solved it, he opens the door and lets the thief run, on the grounds that the season and the man’s terror have done the work of a court.

The Adventure of the Speckled Band A locked room and a stepfather's estate

Helen Stoner’s sister died two weeks before her wedding; Helen is now moved into the same bedroom, and is due to marry. The bell-rope that rings nothing and the ventilator that ventilates nothing point at a will, an income and a doctor with a menagerie.

The Adventure of the Engineer’s Thumb A night job with a hydraulic press

A young engineer is hired for an enormous fee to service machinery in the dark, discovers what it is really pressing, and escapes with a hand short of a thumb. It is the story in which Holmes arrives after everything and solves nothing in time.

The Adventure of the Noble Bachelor A bride who leaves during the wedding breakfast

An aristocrat marries American money and the bride disappears before the meal. Holmes works it out from a discarded dress and a note written on a hotel bill, and the answer is a first husband, with no crime at all.

The Adventure of the Beryl Coronet The broken coronet and the banker’s son

A coronet pledged as security for a loan is found broken in a son’s hands. The father’s certainty about which of the young people in his house is dishonest turns out to be the case’s central error, and the physical evidence is a matter of what a piece of gold will and will not do.

The Adventure of the Copper Beeches A governess asked to cut off her hair

Violet Hunter takes a post on conditions so strange she writes to Baker Street about them. She observes, records and reasons before Holmes reaches the house, and the collection ends by naming the countryside as more dangerous than the city.
