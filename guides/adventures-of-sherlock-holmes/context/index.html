The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes · Arthur Conan Doyle

Context

Dates

1859
Arthur Conan Doyle born in Edinburgh Into an Irish Catholic family; he took his medical degree at Edinburgh University.
1876
Doyle enters the Edinburgh medical school There he encountered Dr Joseph Bell, whose habit of diagnosing a patient’s trade and travels from their appearance is the acknowledged model for Holmes’s method.
1887
Holmes and Watson first appear, in A Study in Scarlet Holmes and Watson’s first appearance, in Beeton’s Christmas Annual, to very little notice.
1890
Doyle’s The Sign of the Four published The second novel, commissioned at a Lippincott’s Magazine dinner in 1889 that also produced Wilde’s The Picture of Dorian Gray.
1891
The Strand Magazine begins the stories with ‘A Scandal in Bohemia’ ‘A Scandal in Bohemia’ appears with Sidney Paget’s illustrations, and the magazine’s circulation climbs with each number.
1892
The twelve stories collected as a volume Published by George Newnes in October, in the order they had appeared.
1893
Doyle kills Holmes at the Reichenbach Falls ‘The Final Problem’ closes the second series; readers cancelled subscriptions in numbers the magazine did not forget.
1901
The Hound of the Baskervilles begins serialisation Set before the Falls, so that the detective could return without being resurrected.
1903
Doyle brings Holmes back in ‘The Adventure of the Empty House’ ‘The Adventure of the Empty House’ explains the Reichenbach disappearance and restarts the series.
1927
The last Holmes story published The Case-Book closes a sequence of fifty-six short stories and four novels written across forty years.
1930
Doyle dies at Crowborough By then better known to himself for his spiritualist writings than for the detective.

Places

Baker Street, London
The consulting room every case passes through, and the fixed point the collection returns to twelve times.
Covent Garden
The poultry market where Holmes traces the goose that swallowed the blue carbuncle.
Upper Swandam Lane, by the Thames
The riverside district east of London Bridge where the opium den of ‘The Man with the Twisted Lip’ is placed.
Stoke Moran, Surrey
The Roylott house in ‘The Speckled Band’, set by Doyle on the western border of Surrey near Leatherhead.
Winchester, Hampshire
The city Holmes and Watson travel to in ‘The Copper Beeches’, looking out at the isolated houses that prompt his last speech.
Boscombe Valley, Herefordshire
The estate and pool of the Boscombe case, where the ground gives the answer that the witnesses cannot.
