Three Men in a Boat · Jerome K. Jerome

Context

Dates

1215
Magna Carta is agreed at Runnymede In June, on the meadows between Staines and Windsor. Jerome has King John rowed to the island opposite and sign it there, and declines to say which side of the water is right.
1859
Jerome Klapka Jerome is born in Walsall On 2 May. He grew up in poverty in east London, and worked as a railway clerk, actor, schoolmaster and journalist before he made a living from books.
1878
HMS Pinafore opens in London Gilbert and Sullivan’s comic opera, whose First Lord’s song Harris muddles with the Judge’s song from Trial by Jury in chapter VIII. The First Lord was created by George Grossmith, co-author of The Diary of a Nobody.
1885
Parliament passes the Thames Preservation Act Meant to keep the river open for pleasure boating as riverside owners tried to fence off banks and backwaters, the grievance behind J.’s rage at notice-boards and chains.
1886
Jerome’s Idle Thoughts of an Idle Fellow published Jerome’s book of comic essays, named on the title page of Three Men in a Boat as his earlier work. Its manner, reflective and self-mocking, is the narrator’s voice here.
1888
Jerome marries Georgina Marris In June. The couple spent their honeymoon on the Thames, and the serial began that autumn.
1888
The serial begins in Home Chimes Running from September 1888 to June 1889, according to the Jerome K. Jerome Society’s bibliography, before the book appeared.
1889
J. W. Arrowsmith publishes the book in Bristol In August, with illustrations by A. Frederics and a preface praising its hopeless and incurable veracity. The same firm published The Diary of a Nobody three years later.
1900
Three Men on the Bummel, the sequel, published The sequel sends J., George and Harris on a bicycling tour of Germany.
1909
Arrowsmith advertises 202,000 copies in twenty years This is the publisher’s count of copies printed; nobody audited sales, and the unauthorised American editions could not be included.
1926
Jerome publishes My Life and Times The memoir in which he recalls the book’s origins and its hostile reviews. Some of the reviews he quotes have not been traced to their original issues, so his account is best read as recollection.
1927
Jerome dies at Northampton On 14 June, after a stroke suffered on a motoring tour.
1956
Ken Annakin’s film A British colour film with David Tomlinson, Jimmy Edwards and Laurence Harvey as the three men.
1975
BBC television adaptation by Tom Stoppard Directed by Stephen Frears, with Michael Palin, Tim Curry and Stephen Moore in the boat.

Places

Kingston upon Thames
The boat waits just below the bridge; Harris takes the sculls and J. the tiller-lines. They reach it from Waterloo on a train that turns out to be the Exeter mail.
Hampton Court Palace
The old river wall J. wants to paint, the maze where Harris gets twenty people lost, and the lock where George and J. are photographed feet first.
Runnymede and Magna Charta Island
The party’s first camp is at Picnic Point near the island, and next morning J. imagines King John being rowed across to it in 1215.
Marlow
Where they leave the boat by the bridge and put up for the night, and where, on an earlier trip, not even a walk out of town could escape the paraffin oil.
Streatley and Goring
The drowned woman had come down by train to Goring. The three stay two days at Streatley to get their washing done, and meet the plaster trout on the way back from a walk to Wallingford.
Oxford, Folly Bridge
The top of the journey, reached after the difficult water below Iffley; Montmorency’s idea of heaven, because it is full of dogs to fight.
Pangbourne
Where the three men leave the boat at the Swan in the rain, tell the boatman they will be back at nine, and creep off to the railway station.
The Alhambra, Leicester Square
The music hall where the deserters, in dirty flannels and battered hats, are mistaken for a troupe of contortionists, before supper and the final toast.
