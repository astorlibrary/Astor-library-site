Three Men in a Boat · Jerome K. Jerome

Form and language

Bathos

A drop from the elevated to the trivial, usually at the end of a sentence or passage.

The book’s signature. A page of moonlight ends with Harris asking about rain; a sermon on the boat of life breaks off with I beg your pardon; the barons at Runnymede give way to a frying-pan polished with George’s wet shirt. Jerome has to write the fine passage well enough to carry the reader some distance, or the fall would not be felt.

we fall asleep beneath the great, still stars, and dream that the world is young again … Harris said: “How about when it rained?”

Chapter II

The comic list

A list whose items escalate, clash or run on past the point of sense.

The chapter arguments are lists; so are the diseases in the British Museum, the luggage on the doorstep, the Waterloo platforms, the helpers round Uncle Podger’s chair and the geometric shapes of the pineapple tin. The last item usually carries the joke.

I plodded conscientiously through the twenty-six letters, and the only malady I could conclude I had not got was housemaid’s knee.

Chapter I

Digression and anecdote

A departure from the main narrative into a story, reflection or piece of information suggested by the moment.

The journey is a thread on which anecdotes hang: Uncle Podger, the cheeses, Harris in the maze, the comic song, the trout. The book’s own chapter arguments list them like items on a programme, and the narrator often forgets he is steering while he tells them.

“There you are,” he would say, stepping heavily off the chair on to the charwoman’s corns, and surveying the mess he had made with evident pride. “Why, some people would have had a man in to do a little thing like that!”

Uncle Podger, Chapter III

Mock-heroic

Treating a trivial matter in the language and form of epic, war or tragedy.

The pineapple tin is a battle with a relic kept afterwards; the tent is a life-and-death struggle; notice-boards and steam launches provoke fantasies of murder and burial; the paraffin oil is renounced by an awful oath under a blasted oak.

There may be tow-lines that are a credit to their profession—conscientious, respectable tow-lines—tow-lines that do not imagine they are crochet-work, and try to knit themselves up into antimacassars the instant they are left to themselves.

Chapter IX

Personification

Giving human motives, feelings or speech to animals and objects.

Montmorency speaks his objections outright, and the equipment has characters too: the tow-line with its unrespectable habits, the kettle that sulks when watched, the hoops that attack from behind, and the tin that grins.

There’s nothing for me to do. Scenery is not in my line, and I don’t smoke. If I see a rat, you won’t stop; and if I go to sleep, you get fooling about with the boat, and slop me overboard. If you ask me, I call the whole thing bally foolishness.

Montmorency, Chapter I

Deadpan

Reporting absurd events in a level, reasonable voice that never admits they are absurd.

J. describes a horse terrified into four miles an hour by cheese, a train that agrees to be another train, and a man who has had every disease since boyhood, all in the tone of a sensible witness. Understatement is its common form: Harris, in the middle of a mob, feels he has to a certain extent become unpopular.

I plodded conscientiously through the twenty-six letters, and the only malady I could conclude I had not got was housemaid’s knee.

Chapter I

The unreliable narrator

A narrator whose account the reader learns to read against, because he misjudges himself or what he reports.

J. is honest about events and wrong about himself. He claims energy, expertise and fairness in the same sentences that show idleness, incompetence and self-interest, and the book leaves the reader to set his opinions side by side, as with the steam launches of chapters XIII and XVI.

From my earliest infancy I have been a martyr to it. As a boy, the disease hardly ever left me for a day. They did not know, then, that it was my liver. Medical science was in a far less advanced state than now, and they used to put it down to laziness.
