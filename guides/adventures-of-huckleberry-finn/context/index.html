Adventures of Huckleberry Finn · Mark Twain

Context

Dates

1835
Samuel Langhorne Clemens born in Florida, Missouri The family moved to Hannibal on the Mississippi four years later.
1839
The Clemens family settles in Hannibal A river town in a slave state, and the model for St Petersburg in both Tom Sawyer and this book.
1857
Clemens begins training as a Mississippi steamboat pilot Four years on the river gave him the geography, the vocabulary and eventually the pen name.
1865
The Thirteenth Amendment abolishes slavery in the United States The novel is set roughly a generation earlier, but written and published well after emancipation, and in the middle of its reversal in the South.
1876
The Adventures of Tom Sawyer is published, and Huck’s manuscript is begun Twain wrote the first stretch at Quarry Farm near Elmira and then put it aside for years.
1880
A brief return to the manuscript Work resumed and stalled again; the Grangerford and river material belongs to this uneven period.
1883
Life on the Mississippi is published and the novel is finished A trip back down the river seems to have set the second half going, and it was written quickly.
1884
Chatto & Windus publish the British edition in December The London issue came out some weeks before the American one, partly to secure copyright.
1885
Charles L. Webster & Company publish the American edition in February Twain’s own subscription house, with illustrations by E. W. Kemble.
1885
The Concord public library removes the book from its shelves The stated objections were to coarseness and bad grammar; Twain professed himself delighted by the sales it would bring.
1910
Mark Twain dies at Redding, Connecticut By then the book was established, and the arguments about its last ten chapters had already begun.

Places

Hannibal, Missouri
Twain’s boyhood town on the west bank, and the original of the village Huck runs from.
Cairo, Illinois
The mouth of the Ohio, and the turning they miss in the fog: the one place where they could have gone north.
St Louis, Missouri
The city downstream that the steamboats and the slave trade both run through.
New Orleans, Louisiana
The market Jim is to be sold into, and the direction the river carries them the whole way.
Elmira, New York
Quarry Farm, the hilltop study where the manuscript was begun in 1876 and finished in 1883.
Hartford, Connecticut
The house Twain was living in while the book was written and published by his own firm.

Critics

Ernest Hemingway

Green Hills of Africa (1935)

Hemingway wrote that “all modern American literature comes from one book by Mark Twain called Huckleberry Finn”, but told readers to stop where Jim is stolen from the boys: “That is the real end. The rest is just cheating.”

Lionel Trilling

Introduction to Adventures of Huckleberry Finn (Rinehart) (1948)

Trilling called it one of the world’s great books, praised the river as a god and Huck’s plain, exact prose as a model for American writing, and defended the ending as formally apt in returning Huck to anonymity.

Leo Marx

“Mr. Eliot, Mr. Trilling, and Huckleberry Finn”, in The American Scholar (1953)

Marx answered both Trilling and T. S. Eliot, who had also defended the ending. He argued that the long farce of Tom Sawyer’s plan to free a man who is already free betrays the moral seriousness of the voyage and reduces Jim to a comic stereotype.
