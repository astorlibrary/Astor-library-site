Mark Twain

Adventures of Huckleberry Finn

A study guide

A boy escapes a father who beats him by faking his own murder, and finds on an island in the river a man who has run because he is about to be sold away from his wife and children. They float south on a raft, which takes them further into slave country with every mile. Twain gives the whole book to Huck’s voice, so that everything cruel in the society comes to the reader through a narrator who has been taught to think it ordinary, and the novel’s central act of conscience is one Huck believes will damn him.

Written 1876–83; first published 1884.
