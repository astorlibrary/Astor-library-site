Adventures of Huckleberry Finn · Mark Twain

What happens

Chapters 1–7 Widow, Pap and a faked murder

Huck is being civilised by the Widow Douglas and schooled by Miss Watson when his father reappears to claim him and the money the last book left him. Locked in a cabin across the river and beaten, he saws his way out, kills a pig, scatters the blood and hair, and arranges the scene so that the whole village will go looking for a body.

Chapter 1
Prayer, the good place and the bad place, and a boy who would rather go where the company is better.
Chapter 6
Pap takes him across the river and keeps him locked in while he drinks.
Chapter 7
A murder is staged so convincingly that nobody will look for a runaway.

Chapters 8–11 Jackson's Island

Huck finds he is not alone on the island: Miss Watson’s Jim has run because he overheard that he was to be sold down to New Orleans for eight hundred dollars. The two escapes become one, although only one of them is a crime. A flooded house drifts past with a dead man in it whom Jim will not let Huck look at.

Chapter 8
Jim admits he has run off; Huck promises not to tell, knowing people would call him a low-down Abolitionist for it.
Chapter 11
Huck goes ashore in a dress, gives himself away by the way he throws, and learns that men are searching the island.

Chapters 12–16 The fog, and the slave hunters

A wrecked steamboat, a night of borrowed provisions, and then the two decisive scenes of the first half: a fog that separates them, after which Huck tries to persuade Jim the whole night was a dream, and a skiff carrying two men hunting runaways, whom Huck turns away with a story about smallpox. They pass Cairo in the dark without knowing it.

Chapter 15
Jim’s grief at losing Huck is turned into a joke, and then answered.
Chapter 16
Huck cannot give Jim up, decides he has done wrong, and cannot work out why doing right would feel any better.

Chapters 17–18 The Grangerfords

Separated from Jim, Huck is taken in by a family of unfailing courtesy who have been shooting their neighbours for thirty years over a lawsuit nobody can now explain. He is given a parlour with an elegy-writing dead daughter’s paintings in it, watches two boys he likes killed at a woodpile, and goes back to the raft without describing what he saw.

Chapter 18
Buck is shot; Huck climbs a tree and afterwards says he will not tell everything that happened.

Chapters 19–30 The duke and the king

Two confidence men take over the raft, and the journey becomes a tour of what small river towns will pay to see. A camp meeting is robbed, a drunk is shot dead in the street in front of a crowd that wants to lynch his killer and is talked out of it, Shakespeare is mangled for money, and finally the pair impersonate the brothers of a dead man to steal his daughters’ inheritance.

Chapter 22
A man who has just committed murder faces down a mob by telling them what they are.
Chapter 26
Huck decides to steal the money back because Mary Jane’s trust is more than he can go on taking.

Chapters 31–32 Huck’s letter to Miss Watson

The king sells Jim for forty dollars. Huck tries to pray, finds he cannot pray a lie, writes to Miss Watson to tell her where Jim is, and feels clean for the first time in his life. Then he sits thinking about the journey, looks at the paper, and decides. He arrives at the Phelps farm and is taken for somebody else entirely.

Chapter 31
A letter is written, held, and torn up, and the boy tearing it believes he has chosen damnation.

Chapters 33–43 Tom Sawyer’s escape plan

Tom arrives, discovers that Jim is being held in a shed, and refuses to simply open the door. Three weeks of rope ladders, sawn bedposts, anonymous letters, rats and a grindstone follow, because the books say so. The escape ends with Tom shot in the leg, Jim giving up his own freedom to stay with Tom and help the doctor, and the news that Miss Watson freed him in her will two months earlier.

Chapter 36
Tom rejects every practical method on the grounds that it is not in the authorities.
Chapter 40
Jim comes out of hiding to help the boy who has been using him, knowing what it will cost.
Chapter the Last
Jim is told he has been free all along, Tom pays him forty dollars, and Huck decides to leave before anyone can adopt him.
