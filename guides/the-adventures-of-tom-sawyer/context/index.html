The Adventures of Tom Sawyer · Mark Twain

Context

Dates

1835
Samuel Langhorne Clemens born at Florida, Missouri The family moved to Hannibal on the Mississippi four years later.
1839
The Clemens family moves to Hannibal The river town, its cave, its hill and its social order supply nearly everything St Petersburg is made of.
1861
The Civil War begins The book, written afterwards, looks back at a Missouri slaveholding town and never says so directly.
1876
First edition published in London Chatto & Windus issued the book on 9 June, ahead of any authorised American printing.
1876
Pirated in Toronto, and published in the United States Belford Brothers’ unauthorised Canadian edition appeared on 29 July; the American Publishing Company’s illustrated edition followed in December.
1876
True W. Williams illustrates the American edition His pictures shaped how the book was sold by subscription; they are discussed in the Astor edition but not reproduced.
1884
Adventures of Huckleberry Finn published in Britain The American edition followed in 1885; the sequel gives the narration to the boy Tom treats as an accessory.
1894
Pudd’nhead Wilson published Twain goes back to the same river town and puts slavery, identity and the law at the centre.
1910
Mark Twain dies at Redding, Connecticut By then Tom Sawyer had long outsold its first, disappointing season.
1938
The Technicolor film adaptation Produced by David O. Selznick, it fixed the fence, the cave and the funeral as the story’s three scenes for a mass audience.

Places

Hannibal, Missouri
The river town Clemens grew up in, and the source of St Petersburg’s streets, church, school and social order.
Cardiff Hill (Holliday’s Hill)
The hill above the town, Cardiff Hill in the novel, up which Huck follows Injun Joe to the Widow Douglas’s house.
Mark Twain Cave, south of Hannibal
The limestone cave behind McDougal’s cave, where Tom and Becky are lost and Injun Joe is sealed in.
Hartford, Connecticut
Twain’s home while he wrote the book, and the base of the American Publishing Company that issued the December 1876 edition.
London
Chatto & Windus published the true first edition here in June 1876, before American readers could buy it.
Toronto
Belford Brothers’ unauthorised edition of July 1876 reached readers ahead of the authorised American book.

Critics

William Dean Howells

The Atlantic Monthly (1876)

Howells, Twain’s friend and editor, praised the book’s picture of boyhood and of a Missouri river town, and thought the boy’s mind was represented with a truth few writers had attempted.
