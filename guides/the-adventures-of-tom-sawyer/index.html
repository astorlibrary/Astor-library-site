Mark Twain

The Adventures of Tom Sawyer

A study guide

A boy in a slaveholding river town dodges school, trades a whitewashing job for other boys’ treasures, and then watches a murder in a graveyard at midnight. Twain keeps the two halves in the same book on purpose: the comedy of Sunday-school tickets and dead cats runs straight into a perjured trial, a man left to starve behind a cave door, and twelve thousand dollars that buys a boy into respectability he does not want. The narrator is an adult looking back with affection and no illusions, and the Conclusion admits that the story stops because its hero is about to stop being a boy.

Written Drafted in the early 1870s at Hartford and Quarry Farm; first published 1876.
