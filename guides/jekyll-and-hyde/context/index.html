Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde · Robert Louis Stevenson

Context

Dates

1788
Deacon William Brodie is hanged in Edinburgh A respected cabinetmaker and town councillor by day and a burglar by night; Stevenson grew up with the story and later wrote a play about him.
1850
Robert Louis Stevenson is born in Edinburgh Into a family of lighthouse engineers, in a city of respectable New Town squares and old closes.
1859
Darwin publishes On the Origin of Species One source of the late-Victorian habit of describing the worse half of a man as an earlier one.
1885
The novella is written at Bournemouth By Stevenson’s own account it began in a dream and was drafted at great speed; the household story that he burned a first version has been repeated ever since.
1886
Published in London by Longmans, Green and Co. Issued as a cheap shilling shocker in January and taken up almost immediately by pulpits and reviewers.
1886
Stevenson’s Kidnapped published Published the same year as Jekyll and Hyde, at the height of Stevenson’s most productive period.
1887
Richard Mansfield stars in the authorised stage version Thomas Russell Sullivan’s adaptation, first at the Boston Museum; the transformation is performed in front of the audience, reversing Stevenson’s method.
1888
Mansfield brings the play to London during the Whitechapel murders The coincidence fixed a link between the story and the idea of a respectable Londoner who kills, and the production closed early.
1894
Stevenson dies at Vailima in Samoa Aged forty-four, with Weir of Hermiston unfinished.
1931
Rouben Mamoulian’s film for Paramount Fredric March won an Academy Award for the dual role, and the film’s subjective camera made the transformation the centrepiece.

Places

Soho, London
Hyde’s rooms, reached through a fog that Utterson describes as a district of some city in a nightmare.
Cavendish Square
Lanyon’s house, the address of orthodox and prosperous medicine.
Regent’s Park
Where Jekyll changes into Hyde on a bench, in daylight, without taking anything.
Bournemouth
Skerryvore, the house where Stevenson wrote the novella in 1885.
Edinburgh
Stevenson’s own divided city, and the town where Deacon Brodie kept two lives in one reputation.
