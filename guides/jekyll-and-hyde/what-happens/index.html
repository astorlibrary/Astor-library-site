Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde · Robert Louis Stevenson

What happens

Story of the Door; Search for Mr. Hyde The door, and Jekyll’s will

Enfield tells Utterson about a man who trampled a child and paid her family with a cheque signed by someone respectable. The door he went in by turns out to belong to Jekyll’s house, and the will Utterson has been keeping in his safe leaves everything to the same man. The lawyer goes looking, and the novella’s method is set: everything arrives second-hand.

Story of the Door
A Sunday walk, a neglected door, and Enfield’s account of the trampling and the cheque.
Search for Mr. Hyde
Utterson rereads the will, fails to get anything from Lanyon, and waits in the street until Hyde appears.

Dr. Jekyll Was Quite at Ease; The Carew Murder Case; Incident of the Letter Jekyll’s assurance, and the Carew murder

Jekyll tells Utterson he can be rid of Hyde whenever he chooses, and for some months appears to have done it. Then a maid watching from an upstairs window sees Hyde beat an elderly MP to death in the street with a cane. Jekyll produces a letter of farewell from the murderer, and Utterson’s clerk notices that the handwriting is his friend’s with a different slope.

Dr. Jekyll Was Quite at Ease
After dinner, Jekyll asks Utterson to let the matter sleep and promises he can end it at will.
The Carew Murder Case
Sir Danvers Carew is clubbed to death in the moonlight; Utterson takes the police to Soho.
Incident of the Letter
Jekyll shows a letter supposedly from Hyde; Mr Guest reads the hand and Utterson keeps quiet.

Remarkable Incident of Dr. Lanyon; Incident at the Window Lanyon’s death, and Jekyll at the window

Lanyon, a sceptic who has mocked Jekyll’s science for years, is found looking like a man with a death sentence and refuses to say why. He dies within weeks, leaving a sealed envelope not to be opened until Jekyll is gone. Weeks later Jekyll is talking to Utterson from an upper window when his face changes and the sash comes down.

Remarkable Incident of Dr. Lanyon
Lanyon’s collapse, his refusal to hear Jekyll’s name, and the envelope left for Utterson.
Incident at the Window
A conversation from the court below ends in an expression the two men below cannot discuss.

The Last Night A servant, a lawyer and an axe

Poole comes to Utterson because the voice behind the cabinet door has been sending out for a drug for a week and is not his master’s. The two of them break the door down and find a body still twitching in clothes too large for it. The novella’s climax is entirely made of sounds heard through wood and objects examined afterwards.

The Last Night
The weeping voice, the rejected chemicals, the axe, a suicide on the cabinet floor, and Jekyll’s papers on the table.

Dr. Lanyon’s Narrative; Henry Jekyll’s Full Statement of the Case Lanyon’s narrative and Jekyll’s statement

Lanyon describes what he saw when he handed over a drawer of powders at midnight, and stops short of saying more. Jekyll then explains everything, from a youthful double life to the batch of impure salt he can no longer replace. The explanation is also a defence, and it arrives when nobody can cross-examine it.

Dr. Lanyon’s Narrative
The urgent letter, the drawer, the visitor in oversized clothes, and the transformation Lanyon will not describe twice.
Henry Jekyll’s Full Statement of the Case
The theory of the two natures, the first draught, the pleasures, the murder, and the loss of control.
