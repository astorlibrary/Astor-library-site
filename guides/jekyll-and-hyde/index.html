Robert Louis Stevenson

Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde

A study guide

A lawyer notices that his oldest friend has left everything in a will to a man nobody can describe, and sets out to find him. Stevenson keeps the secret from the reader for eight chapters by the simple method of following the wrong man: Utterson investigates blackmail because blackmail is the worst thing his profession can imagine. The explanation, when it comes, is a confession written by the person with the strongest reason to shape it.

Written 1885; first published 1886.
