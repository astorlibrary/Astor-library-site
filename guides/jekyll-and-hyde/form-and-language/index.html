Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde · Robert Louis Stevenson

Form and language

Multiple narrators

A story assembled from several voices, each limited to what that person knows.

Eight chapters follow Utterson, who is wrong; the last two are written by Lanyon and Jekyll, who are right and dead. The truth arrives only when it can no longer be tested.

It sounds nothing to hear, but it was hellish to see. It wasn’t like a man; it was like some damned Juggernaut.

Richard Enfield, Story of the Door

Delayed revelation

Withholding the central fact so that earlier scenes must be reread once it lands.

Stevenson never shows a transformation until chapter nine. Every adaptation that opens with one is telling a different story, because the novella’s suspense depends on the reader sharing Utterson’s blindness.

the moment I choose, I can be rid of Mr. Hyde.

Dr Henry Jekyll, Dr. Jekyll Was Quite at Ease

Doors and thresholds

Physical barriers used to carry an idea about what is concealed behind them.

A blistered door with neither bell nor knocker, a cabinet door broken with an axe, a window shut mid-sentence: the plot advances by things being locked, forced or closed.

It was two storeys high; showed no window, nothing but a door on the lower storey and a blind forehead of discoloured wall on the upper; and bore in every feature, the marks of prolonged and sordid negligence.

Story of the Door

Legal and professional diction

The vocabulary of contracts, cases and clinical reports used for uncanny material.

The title itself is a case report, the will’s phrasing is quoted verbatim, and the confession is a statement of the case: the supernatural is handled entirely in the language of offices.

disappearance or unexplained absence for any period exceeding three calendar months

Search for Mr. Hyde

Understatement

Saying less than the situation warrants, so that the omission carries the weight.

Enfield’s it was hellish to see, Lanyon’s refusal to name what he witnessed, and Utterson’s habit of noticing something and going home are all places where Stevenson stops short of description.

I let my brother go to the devil in his own way.

Gabriel John Utterson, Story of the Door

Doubling

Pairing characters or spaces so that each exposes something about the other.

Jekyll and Hyde are the obvious pair, but so are the two fronts of one house, the two doctors, and Utterson and Enfield, who walk together every Sunday without speaking.

the unjust might go his way, delivered from the aspirations and remorse of his more upright twin; and the just could walk steadfastly and securely on his upward path

Dr Henry Jekyll, Henry Jekyll’s Full Statement of the Case
