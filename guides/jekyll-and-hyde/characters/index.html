Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde · Robert Louis Stevenson

Characters

Gabriel John Utterson

Lawyer, and the novella’s investigating consciousness; first appears Story of the Door

Dry, incurious about other people’s vices and loyal past the point of prudence, he is the perfect filter for this story because he cannot imagine the answer. He keeps looking for a blackmailer, which is the worst thing a respectable London career can produce, and the search is honest work aimed in the wrong direction.

Dr Henry Jekyll

Physician, and the author of the final statement; first appears Story of the Door

Large, well made, fifty, celebrated for his charities, and privately annoyed that a man of his standing cannot also be a man of his appetites. His experiment does not create a second nature; it removes the inconvenience of having one body carry both, and his confession keeps trying to make that sound like a scientific result.

Edward Hyde

The younger, smaller figure who uses Jekyll’s back door; first appears Story of the Door

Everyone who meets him agrees that he is deformed and nobody can say where. He is smaller and younger than Jekyll because, on Jekyll’s own account, that side of the man had less exercise. Apart from two acts of violence, almost everything he does in the book is administrative: signing cheques, taking rooms, fetching chemicals.

Dr Hastie Lanyon

Physician, Jekyll’s contemporary and critic; first appears Search for Mr. Hyde

Hearty, conventional and certain that Jekyll went wrong in mind ten years ago, he is the book’s representative of orthodox science. What kills him is what he learns, and his narrative stops exactly where the scene stops being explicable, leaving the rest to be told by the guilty party.

Richard Enfield

A well-known man about town, Utterson’s distant kinsman; first appears Story of the Door

He supplies the first story and then declines to discuss it ever again, which sets the novella’s rule that respectable men stop asking at the point where the answer would be interesting. His account of the trampling is the only time in the book a reader sees Hyde act before knowing who he is.

Poole

Jekyll’s butler of twenty years; first appears Search for Mr. Hyde

He works out the truth before any professional man in the book does, and he does it by listening: the voice behind the door is not his master’s, and the figure he glimpses among the crates is the wrong size. Having no theory to defend, he is free to believe his ears.

Sir Danvers Carew

An elderly Member of Parliament; first appears The Carew Murder Case

He appears for one paragraph, asking a stranger for directions with old-fashioned courtesy, and is beaten to death for it. His killing makes a private worry into a public case, and it is the only event in the novella narrated as it happens, through a witness at a window.
