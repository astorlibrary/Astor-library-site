The Willows · Algernon Blackwood

Characters

The narrator

The Englishman telling the story, some years after the event; first appears Section I

He is the story’s resistance: every time something happens he supplies a cause (wind, current, tiredness, a trick of the light), and each explanation is thinner than the last. Blackwood uses him as a pressure gauge, because the reader measures the danger by how hard this sensible man is working to deny it.

The Swede

His companion, the practical one; first appears Section I

He is introduced as a man with no imagination whatever, which is why his conclusions carry weight. He works out the situation by observation (the paddle, the hollows, the direction of the sound), and the theory he arrives at is stranger than anything the narrator has been suppressing. He is the only character who tries to do something, and what he tries is to be taken instead.

The others

Whatever is in the willows, never named and never shown; first appears Section II

They are given no shape, no motive and no face. The story’s discipline is that everything the reader gets is indirect: a sound, a missing paddle, hollows in sand, marks in flesh. The Swede’s one piece of information about them is the most disturbing thing in the book: they have nothing to do with mankind, and this island is a coincidence.

The man in the boat

A Hungarian peasant, on the river for ten seconds; first appears Section I

He shouts a word across the water that neither canoeist catches and crosses himself as the current takes him past. He is the only local in the story, he is not asked and cannot be, and his gesture is the one piece of outside testimony the reader gets.

The body in the roots

A drowned man, found on the last morning; first appears Section V

He is the story’s only casualty and its only evidence. Caught in the willow roots at the edge of the main stream, he hums when touched, turns over in the current, and shows the marks that prove the Swede right. Then the river takes him before he can be buried, which leaves the two men with nothing to show anybody.
