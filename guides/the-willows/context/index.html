The Willows · Algernon Blackwood

Context

Dates

1869
Blackwood born in Kent Into a strictly evangelical household he spent his adult life reacting against.
1892
Blackwood’s years in Canada and New York Farming, hotel-keeping, and years of poverty and reporting that gave him the wilderness settings of his best work.
1900
Blackwood canoes down the Danube Blackwood canoed down the river through the marshes below Vienna, the trip this story is built from.
1906
The Empty House, Blackwood’s first collection, published He was thirty-seven; The Willows followed a year later.
1907
The Willows published In The Listener and Other Stories, the collection that made his reputation.
1908
Blackwood’s John Silence published The psychic-detective stories that sold well and that Blackwood later thought less of than this one.
1910
Blackwood’s The Wendigo published Its companion piece, doing for the Canadian bush what this does for the Danube.
1927
Lovecraft ranks it first Supernatural Horror in Literature names it first among Blackwood’s stories, the one in which his art and restraint are at their best.
1934
Blackwood becomes a BBC broadcaster He became famous all over again as a broadcaster telling ghost stories on the BBC.
1951
Blackwood dies He died on 10 December 1951. The story has stayed in print in horror anthologies ever since.

Places

Vienna
Where the canoe journey begins, and the last city before the marshes.
Bratislava, then Pressburg
The town the canoeists pass before the river loses its channel.
The Danube marshes
The willow-covered flood plain below Pressburg, marked Sümpfe on the old maps.
Budapest
The destination they are trying to reach, named in the first sentence.
Shooter's Hill, Kent
Blackwood’s birthplace, and the evangelical household the story’s cosmology argues with.
