Thomas Paine

Common Sense and The American Crisis

A study guide

Thomas Paine landed in Philadelphia from England at the end of 1774, and just over a year later he published the pamphlet that argued Americans out of their loyalty to the king. Common Sense, which appeared without its author’s name in January 1776, says that society is a blessing and government at best a necessary evil, that monarchy and hereditary succession are absurd, that an island cannot govern a continent, and that the time for independence has come. The enlarged edition of February adds an Appendix, answering the king’s latest speech, and an address to the Quaker leaders who had declared for the Crown. The American Crisis begins in December 1776, during the retreat across New Jersey, with “These are the times that try men’s souls”, and follows the war in sixteen papers: open letters to British generals, commissioners and ministers and to the people of England, calls for courage and for taxes, and, in April 1783, a farewell announcing that the times that tried men’s souls are over.

Written 1775–1783; first published 1776.
