Common Sense and The American Crisis · Thomas Paine

Context

Dates

1737
Thomas Paine is born On 29 January (1736 in the old reckoning), at Thetford in Norfolk, the son of a Quaker staymaker.
1774
Paine reaches Philadelphia Dismissed from the excise, Paine sails for America with a letter of introduction from Benjamin Franklin and arrives on 30 November.
1775
Lexington and Concord The first fighting of the war, on 19 April, which Paine says ended any hope of reconciliation.
1776
Common Sense Published by Robert Bell in Philadelphia on 10 January, without the author’s name; the enlarged Bradford edition follows on 14 February.
1776
The Declaration of Independence Congress adopts the Declaration on 4 July.
1776
The first Crisis Begun at Newark during the retreat across New Jersey and printed in the Pennsylvania Journal on 19 December, a week before Trenton.
1777
Secretary for foreign affairs On 17 April Congress elects Paine secretary to its Committee of Foreign Affairs. He resigns on 8 January 1779 after his public attack on Silas Deane.
1780
The Crisis Extraordinary Published on 4 October, it argues that Americans pay a fraction of the taxes the English pay and must fund the war.
1781
Mission to France Paine sails from Boston in February with John Laurens to seek French money, and returns from Brest in June.
1783
The last Crisis The thirteenth paper appears on 19 April, eight years after Lexington, and a Supernumerary Crisis follows from New York on 9 December.
1791
Rights of Man Paine publishes the first part in London in 1791 and the second in February 1792, and is elected to the French National Convention.
1793
The Luxembourg prison On 28 December Paine is imprisoned in Paris during the Terror.
1809
Paine dies On 8 June, at Greenwich village in New York.

Places

Thetford, Norfolk
Where Paine was born in 1737.
Philadelphia
Where Common Sense was printed in 1776, and where most of the Crisis papers were written and published.
Fort Lee, New Jersey
The garrison on the Hudson where the retreat of November 1776 began.
Newark, New Jersey
Where Paine began the first Crisis during the retreat.
Trenton, New Jersey
Where Washington’s army won its victory on 26 December 1776, a week after the first Crisis appeared.
Lancaster, Pennsylvania
Where Paine dated the fifth Crisis in March 1778, while the British held Philadelphia.
