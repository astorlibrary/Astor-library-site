The Comedy of Errors · William Shakespeare

Form and language

Doubling

Building a plot out of two figures who cannot be told apart, so that every entrance can be a mistake.

Plautus used one pair of twins. Shakespeare uses two, which means errors can cross between master and servant as well as between brother and brother, and it means the last scene needs four actors on stage who look like two.

One of these men is genius to the other; And so of these, which is the natural man, And which the spirit? Who deciphers them?

Duke, 5.1.330

Dramatic irony

Letting the audience hold a piece of information every character on stage lacks.

The audience is told in the first scene that two sets of twins exist, and then watches everyone else work without that fact for four acts. The jokes are in the gap between the lines, which is why the play reads thin and plays fast.

For know, my love, as easy mayst thou fall A drop of water in the breaking gulf, And take unmingled thence that drop again Without addition or diminishing, As take from me thyself, and not me too.

Adriana, 2.2.115

Line-for-line exchange

Dialogue in which speakers alternate single lines, often rhyming, so that the argument becomes a contest of form.

Adriana and Luciana open 2.1 trading rhymed couplets on liberty in marriage, and the Abbess breaks Adriana down in 5.1 by cutting her answers shorter and shorter until she runs out of them.

A man is master of his liberty; Time is their master, and when they see time, They'll go or come.

Luciana, 2.1.7

Wordplay

Puns and quibbles pressed until the word gives way.

Marks on a pate against marks in gold, a sconce that is a head and a fort, a rope’s end that is also an end in view, and a bond that is a debt and a set of cords gnawed through with the teeth.

The clock hath strucken twelve upon the bell; My mistress made it one upon my cheek.

Dromio of Ephesus, 1.2.45

Catalogue

A list allowed to run well past its point, until the accumulation becomes the joke.

Dromio of Syracuse maps a kitchen maid onto Europe country by country in 3.2, and lists everything a sergeant is in 4.3. Dromio of Ephesus answers a question about his working life in 4.4 with a catalogue of beatings sorted by weather.

They say this town is full of cozenage, As nimble jugglers that deceive the eye, Dark-working sorcerers that change the mind, Soul-killing witches that deform the body

Antipholus of Syracuse, 1.2.97

The set piece

A self-contained episode built to a single effect, playable almost as its own short scene.

The lock-out at 3.1 is the clearest example: two servants shouting through a door in rhyming fourteeners while a husband, a goldsmith and a dinner guest stand in the street. It works on stage whatever a production does with the rest of the play.

The classical unities

Keeping a play to one place, one day and one line of action.

One city, one afternoon, one mistake repeated. This and The Tempest are the only plays Shakespeare wrote to that Roman rule, and here it supplies the pressure: nobody has time to compare notes, so each error survives long enough to cause the next.
