The Comedy of Errors · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

200 BC
Plautus writes the Menaechmi, about this time A Roman farce about identical twins separated as children, one of whom arrives in the city where the other lives.
1472
Plautus printed at Venice Giorgio Merula’s edition, the first in print, put the Latin comedies into circulation across Europe and in time into Elizabethan grammar schools.
1592
Written, probably in the early 1590s Verse tests and the heavy use of rhyme group it with the earliest plays; no record survives of its first performance.
1594
Played at Gray’s Inn on 28 December Gesta Grayorum describes an evening of such disorder that the revels were remembered afterwards as the Night of Errors.
1595
William Warner’s English Menaechmi printed Entered in the Stationers’ Register in June 1594. Shakespeare could read the Latin, so whether he used the translation is disputed.
1604
Performed at court on 28 December A second festive-season record, ten years to the day after Gray’s Inn, showing the play still in the company’s repertory.
1623
First Folio The only early text. It prints among the Comedies as The Comedie of Errors, with no quarto behind it and few textual problems.
1734
See If You Like It; or, ’Tis All a Mistake An anonymous two-act farce at Covent Garden, cut down from Plautus and Shakespeare together.
1762
Thomas Hull’s The Twins Hull’s alteration was played at Covent Garden on 24 April 1762 and, so far as the bills show, not again; it survived in print.
1855
Samuel Phelps at Sadler’s Wells Phelps played the full text in place of the Georgian alterations; reviewers called it crude and extravagant, and the house laughed anyway.
1938
The Boys from Syracuse opens on Broadway Rodgers and Hart’s musical, directed by George Abbott, became the play’s most successful afterlife and dropped its title entirely.
1976
Trevor Nunn’s RSC musical Music by Guy Woolfenden, lyrics by Nunn himself, choreography by Gillian Lynne, Judi Dench as Adriana; it transferred to the Aldwych and won the Olivier Award for Best New Musical.

Places

Ephesus
Where the whole play takes place: a Greek trading city on the coast of Asia Minor, famous in Shakespeare’s day for magic and for St Paul’s letter.
Syracuse
Egeon’s home, and the city whose merchants Ephesus has barred on pain of death.
Epidamnum
Modern Durrës, the port Egeon traded with and was sailing home from when the ship broke up; also the setting of Plautus’s Menaechmi.
Corinth
The fishermen of Corinth took Emilia’s half of the family; Antipholus of Ephesus was brought from there to Ephesus by Duke Menaphon.
Epidaurus
The second ship sighted after the storm, named by Egeon as it bore down on the wreck.
Gray's Inn, London
Where the play was staged on 28 December 1594, on the night the Inn’s own revels collapsed into chaos.
