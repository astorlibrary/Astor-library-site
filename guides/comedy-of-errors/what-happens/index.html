The Comedy of Errors · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I Egeon’s story, and the first mistake

The play opens with an execution scheduled for sunset. Egeon of Syracuse has landed in Ephesus against the trade law, and buys an hour of the Duke’s attention by explaining how he came to be looking for his family: a wife, two sons and two bought servants, torn apart when the ship struck a rock and each half of the mast was picked up by a different vessel. He is given one day to raise a thousand marks. The second scene puts his surviving son ashore in the same city, where a servant he does not own hands him the first mistake.

1.1
Egeon is condemned under a law about Syracusan merchants, tells the story of the wreck that scattered his household, and is given until evening to find the ransom.
1.2
Antipholus of Syracuse sends his Dromio to the inn with a thousand marks, then meets the other Dromio, who wants him home to a dinner he knows nothing about, and is beaten for it.

Act II A wife claims the wrong husband

Adriana waits for a man who does not come, and argues with her unmarried sister about whether a wife owes more obedience than a husband owes attendance. Her servant comes back beaten for the second time that day. Then she finds the wrong Antipholus in the street, delivers the most serious speech in the play to a complete stranger, and takes him home to dinner with the door bolted behind them.

2.1
Adriana and Luciana quarrel in couplets about liberty in marriage; Dromio of Ephesus reports that his master denies house, wife and gold.
2.2
Antipholus of Syracuse beats his own Dromio for a conversation he never had, then is claimed as a husband by a woman he has never seen, and decides to play along.

Act III The locked door

The husband who belongs in the house arrives at his own door with a merchant, a goldsmith and an appetite, and is shouted down through it by his brother’s servant while his wife tells him to go away. Talked out of breaking in with a crowbar, he walks off to dine with a courtesan and promises her the gold chain his wife was to have had. Meanwhile his brother is courting his wife’s sister.

3.1
Antipholus of Ephesus is shouted down through his own front door by his brother’s Dromio, Luce the kitchen maid and Adriana, argued out of forcing it, and goes off to dine at the Porpentine and give the chain away out of spite.
3.2
Antipholus of Syracuse makes love to Luciana in rhyme; Dromio flees Nell’s claim on him; Angelo hands over the chain to a man who never ordered it.

Act IV Debt, arrest and a conjurer

Money makes a domestic muddle into a legal one. Angelo is pressed by his own creditor, presses Antipholus of Ephesus for a chain the man never received, and has him arrested in the street. The bail is fetched by the wrong servant and handed to the wrong master. The courtesan reports her ring stolen. By the end of the act a schoolmaster and conjurer called Pinch has declared master and man possessed, and both are tied up in a dark cellar.

4.1
Angelo, squeezed for a debt he cannot pay, has Antipholus of Ephesus taken by an officer over a chain that is at that moment round his brother’s neck.
4.2
Dromio of Syracuse brings Adriana the news of an arrest and carries her bail money away to a prisoner who is not his master.
4.3
Greeted by name, offered goods and handed gold all over town, Antipholus of Syracuse decides that Ephesus is full of witches and will not give the Courtesan her ring.
4.4
Antipholus of Ephesus beats his Dromio for bringing a rope instead of bail, is examined by a conjurer, and is bound and carried home as a madman.

Act V Both pairs on stage together

Everyone arrives in the same square. The Syracusans take sanctuary in the priory; the Ephesians gnaw through their bonds and come to the Duke for justice; Egeon, walking to the block, recognises a son who does not recognise him. Then the Abbess brings the second pair out through the gate, the two Antipholuses stand side by side, and she turns out to be Egeon’s wife. Four acts of taking a family apart are undone in the last hundred lines.

5.1
Sanctuary, an execution procession, two flatly contradictory sworn accounts of the same day, and the moment both pairs finally stand in the same square.
