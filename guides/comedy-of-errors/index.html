William Shakespeare

The Comedy of Errors

A study guide

Two sets of identical twins, separated by shipwreck when they were babies, spend one day in the same city without meeting. Antipholus of Syracuse has come looking for the brother he cannot remember; Antipholus of Ephesus is already settled there, with a wife, a house and a goldsmith’s account, and loses all three by teatime. Every disaster follows the same mechanism: a servant is sent on an errand, does it correctly, and reports to the wrong master. Around that Roman machine Shakespeare builds a frame from the old romance of Apollonius of Tyre: a father under sentence of death at sunset, and the wife he lost at sea, who has been abbess in the same city all along.

Written Early 1590s; first printed 1623.
