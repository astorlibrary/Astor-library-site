Arthur Conan Doyle

The Hound of the Baskervilles

A study guide

Dr James Mortimer brings Sherlock Holmes an eighteenth-century manuscript about a spectral hound that has haunted the Baskervilles since the wicked Hugo, and the story of Sir Charles Baskerville, found dead in his yew alley with, some distance off, the footprints of a gigantic hound. The heir, Sir Henry, arrives from Canada, receives a warning made of words cut from The Times, and loses two boots in his hotel. Holmes sends Watson to Dartmoor with him, where Watson meets the naturalist Stapleton and his beautiful sister, follows a butler signalling on to the moor, and sees a stranger standing on a tor. The stranger is Holmes. Together they expose Stapleton, a Baskerville under a false name with a real hound painted with phosphorus, who vanishes into the Grimpen Mire.

Written 1901; first published 1901.
