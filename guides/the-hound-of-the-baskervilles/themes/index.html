The Hound of the Baskervilles · Arthur Conan Doyle

Themes

Superstition

A family legend is set against a detective who has hitherto confined his investigations to this world. Mortimer half believes in the hound; the peasants describe it with hell-fire; Sir Henry is shaken by its cry; Watson clings to his common sense. The legend is finally explained by a dog from a dealer in the Fulham Road and a paste of phosphorus, but the book gets much of its power from the fear it claims to dispel.

The manuscript of 1742; a realm in which detectives are helpless; the devil’s agents of flesh and blood; the cry on the moor; common sense; phosphorus; the family ghost laid.

standing over Hugo, and plucking at his throat, there stood a foul thing, a great, black beast, shaped like a hound, yet larger than any hound that ever mortal eye has rested upon.

Chapter II

that which is clearly known hath less terror than that which is but hinted at and guessed.

Chapter II

Proof

Holmes knows early who the murderer is, and the problem is proving it. A death from fright leaves no mark; a hound that never bites cannot be linked to its master; no jury would believe the story. The case is built from small material facts, a boot, a cut newspaper, cigar ash, a smell of white jessamine, and finally from a trap in which Sir Henry is the bait.

The stick; the Times leader; two boots; the cigar ash; a photograph from York; it is not what we know, but what we can prove.

you are a conductor of light. Some people without possessing genius have a remarkable power of stimulating it.

Sherlock Holmes, Chapter I

Mr. Holmes, they were the footprints of a gigantic hound!

Dr James Mortimer, Chapter II

Inheritance and the family curse

Everything turns on who inherits: a baronetcy, an entailed estate and a fortune of nearly a million from South Africa. The legend makes inheritance a curse passed down the generations for Hugo’s sin, and the real danger is an heir, the disgraced younger brother’s son, who wants the estate. Sir Henry means to restore the grandeur of his line; Stapleton means to take it.

The warning to his sons; seven hundred and forty thousand pounds; the entail; the Desmonds; the portrait; the three ways of claiming the property.

standing over Hugo, and plucking at his throat, there stood a foul thing, a great, black beast, shaped like a hound, yet larger than any hound that ever mortal eye has rested upon.

Chapter II

There is no devil in hell, Mr. Holmes, and there is no man upon earth who can prevent me from going to the home of my own people

Sir Henry Baskerville, Chapter IV

Heredity and the throwback

Like many books of its time, this one is interested in what the body inherits. Mortimer writes on atavism and reversion and reads skulls; Selden’s face looks like a prehistoric savage’s; Holmes sees Stapleton in Hugo’s portrait and calls him a throw-back, physical and spiritual. Doyle takes these ideas half seriously and half as comedy, as in Mortimer’s wish for a cast of Holmes’s skull.

Some Freaks of Atavism; the covetable skull; the Celt and the Ivernian; the evil yellow face; the throw-back in the portrait.

It is not my intention to be fulsome, but I confess that I covet your skull.

Dr James Mortimer, Chapter I

you would feel that his presence there was more natural than your own.

Chapter VIII

The moor

Dartmoor is the book’s great presence: vast, barren, granite-flecked, scattered with the huts of prehistoric men and hiding the Grimpen Mire, where a false step means death. It carries Watson out of modern England, shelters a convict and a detective, and at the end swallows the murderer. The moor is where the legend becomes believable.

The first sight of the moor; the mire and the pony; the stone huts; the man on the tor; the fog like a sea; for ever buried.

A false step yonder means death to man or beast.

Jack Stapleton, Chapter VII

It is so vast, and so barren, and so mysterious.

Jack Stapleton, Chapter VII

Disguise and false identity

Almost no one is who they seem. Stapleton wears a false beard in London, gives the cabman Holmes’s name, has been Vandeleur, and is really a Baskerville; his wife passes as his sister; Holmes hides as a tourist in a hut; Selden dies in Sir Henry’s clothes. Holmes says the first quality of a criminal investigator is to see through a disguise.

The black beard; You have been driving Mr. Sherlock Holmes; the sister who is a wife; the tourist in the tweed suit; the dead man in the ruddy tweed; the portrait.

As you value your life or your reason keep away from the moor.

Chapter IV

Go straight back to London, instantly.

Beryl Stapleton, Chapter VII

Women under a man’s control

The two women closest to the crime are both Stapleton’s instruments. His wife passes as his sister under threats and blows, and Laura Lyons writes the fatal letter because he has promised to marry her. Each knows or suspects part of the truth and is silenced by fear or hope. Mrs Barrymore, loyal to a murderous brother, completes a picture of women bound to dangerous men.

The warning on the path; the curious lisp; the letter signed L. L.; Prove it to me!; the weal across her neck; mind and soul tortured.

As you value your life or your reason keep away from the moor.

Chapter IV

Go straight back to London, instantly.
