The Hound of the Baskervilles · Arthur Conan Doyle

Context

Dates

1859
Arthur Conan Doyle is born On 22 May, in Edinburgh.
1887
A Study in Scarlet Sherlock Holmes and Dr Watson first appear, in Beeton’s Christmas Annual.
1893
The Final Problem In The Strand Magazine for December, Doyle sends Holmes over the Reichenbach Falls with Professor Moriarty.
1901
The Hound in The Strand The first of nine monthly parts appears in August, with a footnote crediting Fletcher Robinson with the story’s inception.
1902
The first edition George Newnes publishes the novel on 25 March, dedicated to Robinson; the American edition follows on 15 April.
1903
The Empty House Holmes returns in a new story, explaining that he never fell at Reichenbach at all.
1930
Conan Doyle dies On 7 July, at his home in Sussex.
1939
Basil Rathbone’s Hound Twentieth Century-Fox films the novel with Basil Rathbone as Holmes and Nigel Bruce as Watson.
1959
Hammer’s Hound Hammer Films makes the first colour film of the story, with Peter Cushing as Holmes.

Places

Baker Street, London
Where Mortimer brings the case, and where Holmes explains it at the end of November.
Paddington Station
Where Watson, Sir Henry and Mortimer take the ten-thirty train for Devon.
Princetown, Dartmoor
The great convict prison from which Selden escapes, fourteen miles from the Hall on Holmes’s map.
Dartmoor
The granite moor, with its tors, stone huts and bogs, on which Doyle placed the invented Baskerville Hall, Grimpen and the Grimpen Mire.
Hindhead, Surrey
Where Doyle lived and signed the dedication to Robinson.

Critics

Edmund Wilson

““Mr. Holmes, They Were the Footprints of a Gigantic Hound!””, in The New Yorker (1945)

Wilson, who had attacked detective fiction as a habit-forming drug, made an exception for Holmes: the stories last, he argued, because of Doyle’s imagination and style and the life of Baker Street, not because of their puzzles.
