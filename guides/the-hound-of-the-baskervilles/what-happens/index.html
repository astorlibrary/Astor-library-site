The Hound of the Baskervilles · Arthur Conan Doyle

What happens

Chapters I–V Baker Street

Holmes and Watson read a stick left by a visitor, who turns out to be Dr Mortimer of Grimpen. He reads them the legend of Hugo Baskerville and the hound, and tells them how Sir Charles died in the yew alley, running, with the footprints of a gigantic hound nearby. Sir Henry arrives from Canada to a warning pasted from The Times and a missing boot. A bearded man shadows him from a cab and escapes, and the cabman reports that his fare gave his name as Sherlock Holmes.

Chapter I
A Penang lawyer, a curly-haired spaniel, and a doctor who covets Holmes’s skull.
Chapters II–III
The manuscript of 1742, the Devon County Chronicle, the footprints, and a map of the moor studied all day in a cloud of shag.
Chapters IV–V
Words cut from a Times leader, two stolen boots, a spy in a hansom, and a cabman with a message.

Chapters VI–VII Baskerville Hall

Watson travels to Devon with Sir Henry and Mortimer. Soldiers watch the roads for Selden, an escaped murderer. At the gloomy Hall the black-bearded butler Barrymore lies about a woman sobbing in the night. On the moor Watson meets the naturalist Stapleton, who shows him the Grimpen Mire swallowing a pony, and hears a long moan across the moor. Stapleton’s sister, mistaking Watson for Sir Henry, urges him to go straight back to London.

Chapter VI
A mounted soldier on the skyline, twin towers, a dining-hall of ancestors, and a sob in the night.
Chapter VII
The great Grimpen Mire, a moth called Cyclopides, and a warning on a moorland path.

Chapters VIII–IX Watson’s reports

Watson’s letters to Holmes describe the moor and its prehistoric huts, old Frankland and his telescope, and Sir Henry’s growing love for Miss Stapleton, which her brother furiously interrupts before apologising. Watson and Sir Henry catch Barrymore signalling from a window: the convict Selden is Mrs Barrymore’s younger brother, and they feed him on the moor. Hunting Selden, the two men hear the hound’s cry, and Watson sees a tall figure standing on a tor against the moon.

Chapter VIII
Stone huts, a litigious neighbour, and a candle carried down a corridor at two in the morning.
Chapter IX
An interrupted proposal, a light answered on the moor, a convict’s face over a rock, and a man on the tor.

Chapters X–XI The man on the tor

Barrymore reveals that Sir Charles went to the gate to meet a woman with the initials L. L., and that another man is hiding on the moor. Watson finds Laura Lyons in Coombe Tracey; she admits writing to Sir Charles but says she never kept the appointment. From Frankland’s roof Watson spots a boy carrying food, follows him to a stone hut, finds a note reporting his own movements, and waits in the dark for the tenant. It is Holmes.

Chapter X
L. L., a burnt letter, a lost spaniel, and another man upon the moor.
Chapter XI
Mrs Lyons at her typewriter, Frankland’s telescope, and a well-known voice at the door of the hut.

Chapters XII–XIII Death on the moor

Holmes tells Watson that Miss Stapleton is Stapleton’s wife and that the case is murder. A scream sounds across the moor, and they find a body in Sir Henry’s clothes: it is Selden, wearing the baronet’s old wardrobe, run down by the hound. Stapleton arrives, masks his disappointment, and hears that Holmes is going home. At the Hall Holmes sees Stapleton’s face in the portrait of Hugo. Laura Lyons, shown proof that Stapleton is married, confesses that he dictated her letter.

Chapter XII
The sister who is a wife, a yell of horror, a ruddy tweed suit, and a beard.
Chapter XIII
The wicked Hugo’s portrait, a pretended departure, a photograph from York, and Lestrade on the London express.

Chapter XIV The hound

Holmes, Watson and Lestrade lie in wait near Merripit House while Sir Henry dines there. Fog rolls in from the mire. As Sir Henry walks home the hound bursts out of the fog, glowing with fire, and Holmes shoots it dead at his throat. Upstairs they find Mrs Stapleton bound and beaten. Next morning she leads them to the path through the mire, where they find Sir Henry’s old boot; Stapleton is never seen again.

Chapter XIV
A creak at the out-house, a white fog like a sea, and a coal-black hound with flaming jaws.
Chapter XIV
A woman tied to a beam, guiding wands, a boot marked Meyers, Toronto, and the bones of a spaniel.

Chapter XV A retrospection

In Baker Street at the end of November, Holmes explains the case. Stapleton was the son of Rodger Baskerville, who had fled to South America; he had embezzled money in Costa Rica, run a school in Yorkshire as Vandeleur, and come to Devon to kill the two lives between him and the estate. He bought the hound in London, hid it on an island in the mire, and used his wife and Laura Lyons as tools. Holmes can only guess how Stapleton meant to claim his inheritance, and takes Watson to the opera.

Chapter XV
Ross and Mangles of the Fulham Road, a scent of white jessamine, the servant Anthony, and a box for Les Huguenots.
