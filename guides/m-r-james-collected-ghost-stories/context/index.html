M. R. James: Collected Ghost Stories · M. R. James

Context

Dates

1862
M. R. James born in Kent The son of an Evangelical clergyman; he grew up in the Suffolk village of Great Livermere, which stands behind several of the stories.
1882
M. R. James goes up to King’s College, Cambridge He arrived as an undergraduate and stayed for most of his life, becoming Provost in 1905.
1893
First two stories read aloud at Cambridge Canon Alberic’s Scrap-book and Lost Hearts were read aloud to friends at King’s; the form was oral before it was printed.
1895
M. R. James publishes his first manuscript catalogue His catalogues of medieval manuscripts in Cambridge libraries are still in use, and supply the fiction’s furniture.
1904
Ghost Stories of an Antiquary published Eight stories published by Edward Arnold, with a preface naming the real settings.
1911
More Ghost Stories of an Antiquary published Casting the Runes and The Tractate Middoth among them; the second volume is where the method is at its most confident.
1919
A Thin Ghost and Others published Five stories written during and after the war, drier and bleaker than the earlier work.
1925
A Warning to the Curious published The title story, about a buried crown on the Suffolk coast, is the one many readers put first.
1931
The Collected Ghost Stories published Everything gathered in one volume, with four stories added; the book has never been out of print since.
1936
M. R. James dies at Eton His last piece appeared in The London Mercury in the year he died.
1968
Jonathan Miller’s BBC film Whistle and I’ll Come to You Michael Hordern plays the professor. The film went out in May; the BBC’s A Ghost Story for Christmas began three years later, and James adaptations became part of British Christmas television.

Places

Great Livermere, Suffolk
The village where James grew up, and the flat country behind most of the stories.
King's College, Cambridge
Where the stories were read aloud at Christmas, and where James was Provost from 1905.
Felixstowe, Suffolk
The seaside town James says he had in mind for Oh, Whistle, and I’ll Come to You.
Aldeburgh, Suffolk
The coast of A Warning to the Curious, with its martello tower and shingle.
St Bertrand de Comminges
A real Pyrenean town, and the setting of the first story in the first volume.
Viborg, Denmark
Named in the preface as a real setting; Number 13 takes place in its Golden Lion hotel.
