M. R. James: Collected Ghost Stories · M. R. James

Themes

Curiosity

A few of the dead in these stories earn it, like Mr Abney or Karswell, but more are ruined for doing their jobs well: cataloguing, translating, excavating, buying at the right price. The learning is the fault.

Dennistoun buys the scrap-book in the first story, Parkins pockets the whistle, Wraxall reads up on Count Magnus, and by A Warning to the Curious the title says it outright.

As for the fragments of ostensible erudition which are scattered about my pages, hardly anything in them is not pure invention

Preface, 1904

It ought to mean, ‘Who is this who is coming?’ Well, the best way to find out is evidently to whistle for him.

“Oh, Whistle, and I’ll Come to You, My Lad”

Documents

A manuscript, a diary, a Latin inscription, a plan of a well: the plot usually runs through a piece of paper that the scholar can read and the reader is shown.

James prints the evidence (ciphers, abbreviations, bad Latin) so that the reader does the research alongside the victim and reaches the answer at about the same time.

St Bertrand de Comminges is a decayed town on the spurs of the Pyrenees, not very far from Toulouse, and still nearer to Bagnères-de-Luchon.

Touch

Many of his frights are tactile: a mouth under the pillow, hair, damp linen, something that is the wrong shape in the dark. Seeing it whole is nearly always withheld.

The mouth with teeth that Dunning finds under his pillow in Casting the Runes is the purest case; later stories also work through sound and presence, and A Warning to the Curious ends in a chase along the shingle.

most of which were read to friends at Christmas-time at King’s College, Cambridge

Preface, 1904

coarse black hairs, longer than ever grew on a human hand; nails rising from the ends of the fingers and curving sharply down and forward, grey, horny and wrinkled

Canon Alberic’s Scrap-book

Churches

Cathedrals, abbeys, prayer-books and Templar graves fill the stories, and the church in them is an archive, never a refuge. Consecrated ground is where the dangerous things were put, on purpose.

The clergy in the stories are scholars first; when something is finally stopped, it is by a rule of the thing’s own, and prayer has little to do with it.

Belief

The servants and villagers are right about the house, and the educated men in the armchairs are wrong. James was a conservative who gave the people without Latin the best information in story after story.

Their warnings are ignored in the early stories and acted on far too late in the later ones, which is the only real moral progress in the book.

East Anglia

Flat coast, shingle, martello towers, sea fog, gorse, a golf links. The landscapes are real and specific, and the horror is put down in them like a fence post.

The named places thicken as the stories go on, until A Warning to the Curious is effectively a walking route along the Suffolk coast with something behind you on it.

St Bertrand de Comminges is a decayed town on the spurs of the Pyrenees, not very far from Toulouse, and still nearer to Bagnères-de-Luchon.
