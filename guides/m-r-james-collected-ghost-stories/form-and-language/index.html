Form and language

Documents

A story told through, or around, a manuscript, diary or letter that the narrator claims to have seen.

Almost every story arrives with paperwork attached: a diary, a catalogue entry, an inscription in bad Latin. The apparatus is a joke a scholar would enjoy and a device that makes the impossible part look checkable.

As for the fragments of ostensible erudition which are scattered about my pages, hardly anything in them is not pure invention

Preface, 1904

Partial glimpses

Withholding the full description of the horror.

The face of crumpled linen is a face and a fabric and nothing else; James says the man could not or would not tell what expression he read on it. The refusal is the effect.

coarse black hairs, longer than ever grew on a human hand; nails rising from the ends of the fingers and curving sharply down and forward, grey, horny and wrinkled

Canon Alberic’s Scrap-book

Scholarly understatement

Keeping the tone of a common-room anecdote over appalling material.

Mr Wraxall compares it to the tentacle of a devil-fish, then continues with his notes. The flatness makes a reader lean in.

St Bertrand de Comminges is a decayed town on the spurs of the Pyrenees, not very far from Toulouse, and still nearer to Bagnères-de-Luchon.

Landscape

Using real towns, coasts and buildings instead of invented Gothic ones.

Felixstowe behind the golf links, St Bertrand de Comminges, Viborg. James’s 1904 preface records that St Bertrand de Comminges and Viborg are real places and that he had Felixstowe in mind for the Whistle story.

St Bertrand de Comminges is a decayed town on the spurs of the Pyrenees, not very far from Toulouse, and still nearer to Bagnères-de-Luchon.

The trigger object

A small, purchasable thing that carries the whole plot.

A scrap-book, a mezzotint, a whistle, a runic slip, a crown. The horror is usually attached to something that can be picked up, and often to something that has been bought or dug up.

It ought to mean, ‘Who is this who is coming?’ Well, the best way to find out is evidently to whistle for him.

“Oh, Whistle, and I’ll Come to You, My Lad”

Servants’ dialect

Writing out the pronunciation and idiom of the people who are not scholars.

The housekeeper’s account of the marks on the door in Lost Hearts is set down exactly as she says it. It is where James puts his best comic writing and his most reliable information.

Gothic conventions updated

Inherited gothic furniture (tombs, abbeys, curses) operated in a recognisably modern world.

Trains, golf, hotel bills, magic lanterns and a Bradshaw sit beside Templar graves. The stories are set almost in the reader’s present, which is why the medieval part bites.
