M. R. James: Collected Ghost Stories · M. R. James

Characters

The antiquary

The recurring central figure: a scholar who looks too closely; first appears Canon Alberic’s Scrap-book

He is a cataloguer, a professor, a clergyman with a hobby, a collector on holiday. He is competent, unimaginative and safe until his own expertise takes him one step further than the local people will go, and the step is always a professional one: buying, reading, digging, translating.

Parkins

Professor of ontography, in “Oh, Whistle, and I’ll Come to You, My Lad”; first appears “Oh, Whistle, and I’ll Come to You, My Lad”

A precise, priggish young academic who announces that he does not believe in such things and then finds the sheets on the spare bed disarranged. James makes him faintly ridiculous first, so that the reader has laughed at him before being asked to be frightened for him.

Dennistoun

The Cambridge collector of Canon Alberic’s Scrap-book; first appears Canon Alberic’s Scrap-book

He buys a manuscript from a sacristan who will not meet his eye, at a price so low it should have warned him, and takes it back to his hotel room to work through by candlelight. He is the first of the type and the luckiest of them.

Mr Karswell

The abbot of Lufford, in Casting the Runes; first appears Casting the Runes

A bad scholar with real powers and a thin skin, who answers a hostile review by passing his victim a slip of runic characters. He is James’s one true villain, and the story he appears in is the only one where the trap can be reversed.

The people who live there

Servants, vergers and villagers who know better; first appears Canon Alberic’s Scrap-book

Sacristans, housekeepers, innkeepers, sextons, a boy on a golf links. They know what is in the wood, the well or the barrow, they say so once, and they are not asked again. James gives them his best dialogue, and being right never does them any good.

The thing itself

Whatever has been waiting: never explained, rarely seen whole; first appears Canon Alberic’s Scrap-book

It is made of the wrong material (crumpled linen, matted hair, a tentacle, a mouth in the wrong place), and it is usually felt before it is seen. James withholds the full sight on principle, and the stories that show most are the ones that frighten least.
