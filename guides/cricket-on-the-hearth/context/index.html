The Cricket on the Hearth · Charles Dickens

Context

Dates

1812
Dickens born in Portsmouth Born on 7 February at Landport, the son of a clerk in the Navy Pay Office.
1843
A Christmas Carol published The first of the five Christmas books, issued by Chapman and Hall in December.
1844
The Chimes published The second Christmas book, written in Genoa during Dickens’s year in Italy and more openly political than the first.
1845
A periodical called The Cricket is proposed and dropped Dickens suggested to John Forster a cheap weekly under that title, with the cricket as the voice of the fireside; the idea went into the Christmas book instead.
1845
The Cricket on the Hearth published Bradbury and Evans issued it shortly before Christmas, dedicated to Lord Jeffrey and illustrated by five artists; the title page is dated 1846.
1845
On the London stage within weeks Albert Smith’s adaptation, prepared with Dickens’s agreement, opened at the Lyceum in December, and rival versions followed at other theatres.
1846
Dickens edits the Daily News Bradbury and Evans backed the new Liberal daily, which first appeared on 21 January; Dickens gave up the editorship within weeks.
1846
The Battle of Life published The fourth Christmas book, another domestic story with no ghosts in it.
1853
Dickens reads the Cricket in public He read it at Birmingham Town Hall in the last week of December, alongside A Christmas Carol, in the first public readings he gave.
1859
Boucicault’s Dot opens in New York Dion Boucicault’s adaptation gave Joseph Jefferson the part of Caleb Plummer, which became one of his best-known roles.
1870
Dickens dies at Gad’s Hill He died on 9 June, three months after his farewell reading in London.
1896
Goldmark’s opera Das Heimchen am Herd Karl Goldmark’s opera based on the story was first performed at the Vienna Court Opera.

Places

Portsmouth
Dickens’s birthplace.
Devonshire Terrace, Marylebone
Dickens’s London home from 1839 to 1851.
Whitefriars, off Fleet Street
Bradbury and Evans’s printing works; the 1846 title page gives the publishers’ address as 90 Fleet Street and Whitefriars.
Lyceum Theatre, Wellington Street
Where Albert Smith’s stage version opened in December 1845.
Birmingham Town Hall
Dickens read the Cricket here in December 1853.
