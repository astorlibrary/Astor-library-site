Charles Dickens

The Cricket on the Hearth

A study guide

A carrier brings home an old deaf passenger he found by the roadside, and his young wife cries out when the stranger comes near the fire. Two days later a toy merchant shows him, through a counting-house window, the same man without his white wig and with an arm round her waist. John Peerybingle spends the night between a loaded gun and his own hearth, and by morning blames himself for the marriage. Beside this, a poor toymaker has spent years describing a comfortable house and a kind employer to his blind daughter, and both fictions come apart on the same wedding morning.

Written 1845; first published 1845.
