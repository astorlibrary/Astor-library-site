All's Well That Ends Well · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I A ward in love above her station

The Countess’s household is in mourning, and her son Bertram leaves for Paris to become the King’s ward. Helena, left behind, admits to herself that she loves him and that he is as far above her as a star. At court the King is dying of a fistula his doctors have given up on. Back at Rossillon the Countess learns of Helena’s love from her steward, draws a confession out of her, and gives her leave to take her dead father’s remedy to Paris.

1.1
The Countess blesses Bertram before he leaves. Alone, Helena says she has forgotten her father’s face and remembers only Bertram’s, trades jokes about virginity with Parolles, and ends the scene resolved to act.
1.2
The King lets his young lords serve on either side of the war between Florence and Siena, praises Bertram’s dead father at length, and asks how long it is since the famous physician of Rossillon died.
1.3
The Clown asks leave to marry Isbel. The Steward reports that he overheard Helena confessing her love to herself, and the Countess tests her with the word mother until she kneels and admits it.

Act II The cure and the choosing

Helena persuades the King to try her remedy, staking her life on curing him within two days, and asks as her fee a husband of her choosing. The cure works. She passes along a line of young lords, chooses Bertram, and is refused in front of the court because she is a poor physician’s daughter. The King overrules him and has the marriage performed that night. Bertram decides on the spot to go to the Tuscan wars, sends Helena home to his mother with a message, and rides away before the marriage is consummated.

2.1
Bertram is kept at court as too young for the war. Lafew brings in Helena, who wears down the King’s refusal in rhymed couplets and names her price.
2.2
The Clown shows the Countess his answer that fits every question at court, and is sent to Paris with a letter for Helena.
2.3
Lafew and Parolles marvel at the cure. Helena chooses Bertram, the King lectures him on virtue and title and then threatens him into consent, and Lafew picks a quarrel with Parolles that exposes him as all scarves and no substance.
2.4–2.5
Parolles tells Helena that Bertram must put off his marriage rights. Lafew warns Bertram about Parolles, and Bertram sends Helena away with a letter for his mother, refusing her a parting kiss.

Act III Letters, flight and a pilgrim in Florence

Bertram’s letters reach Rossillon. One tells his mother he has wedded Helena without bedding her; the other sets Helena his two impossible conditions. The Countess disowns her son, and Helena leaves the house at night, writing that she has gone as a barefoot pilgrim to Saint Jaques. Bertram is made general of the Duke of Florence’s horse. Helena turns up in Florence, lodges with a widow whose daughter Diana is being pursued by Bertram, tells the widow who she is, and pays her to help with a plan to put herself in Diana’s place.

3.1
The Duke complains that the French king will not send help, and is told that young Frenchmen will come of their own accord.
3.2
The Countess reads that her son has run away. Helena reads his conditions aloud, and alone she blames herself for sending him into the line of musket fire.
3.3–3.4
The Duke gives Bertram his command. At Rossillon the Steward reads Helena’s sonnet-letter, and the Countess orders him to write to Bertram that his wife has gone.
3.5
The Widow, Diana and Mariana watch the soldiers pass. A pilgrim who says she is bound for Saint Jaques hears Diana describe the French count who is courting her, and the man who carries his messages.
3.6–3.7
The French lords persuade Bertram to let Parolles try to recover the lost drum so that they can ambush him. Helena gives the Widow gold and sets out the terms for the night to come.

Act IV Parolles captured, and the bed-trick

Parolles, out alone at night inventing wounds to explain his failure, is seized by his own side speaking nonsense and believes he has been taken by the enemy. Bertram gives Diana his ancestral ring and agrees to a silent hour in her dark chamber. The lords hear that a peace has been concluded and that Helena has died at the shrine. Later that night Parolles, still blindfolded, sells every secret he can think of while Bertram listens. Helena sets out for the King, and at Rossillon Lafew arranges for Bertram to marry his daughter.

4.1
Parolles talks himself through the lies he will need, is blindfolded by soldiers speaking gibberish, and offers to reveal the whole Florentine camp to save his life.
4.2
Diana argues Bertram out of his family ring by using his own words about honour, and tells him to come to her window at midnight and not to speak.
4.3
The lords discuss Bertram’s seduction and the report of Helena’s death. Parolles, questioned from a note, lies about troop numbers, slanders both Dumaine brothers and Bertram, and is shown who has been listening.
4.4
Helena tells the Widow and Diana that she is believed dead and that they must go to the King, who she hears is at Marseilles.
4.5
Lafew and the Countess mourn Helena and agree a match with Lafew’s daughter. The Clown announces Bertram’s return with a patch of velvet on his face.

Act V The ring, and Helena’s return

Helena misses the King at Marseilles and follows him to Rossillon. Parolles, ragged and smelling of misfortune, is taken in by Lafew. The King forgives Bertram and approves the new match until he sees the ring Bertram offers as a token for Lafew’s daughter: it is the one the King once gave Helena. Bertram lies about it, Diana arrives with a petition claiming him as her husband, and he lies about her too. Diana answers every question with a riddle until Helena enters, pregnant, with the ring and the letter that set the conditions.

5.1
The King has already left. Helena gives her petition to a gentleman of the court, who is going to Rossillon.
5.2
The Clown holds his nose at Parolles. Lafew gives him a coin, asks after his drum and promises he shall eat.
5.3
The ring, Bertram’s arrest, Diana’s petition, Parolles as a witness, the riddle of one that’s dead is quick, and Helena’s entrance.
Epilogue
The actor who has played the King steps out of the play to ask the audience for applause, in the same rhymed couplets the play has used for its bargains.
