William Shakespeare

All's Well That Ends Well

A study guide

Helena, the orphaned daughter of a famous physician, has been brought up in the Countess of Rossillon’s household and is in love with the Countess’s son Bertram, who is far above her in rank. She cures the dying King of France with one of her father’s remedies and is allowed to choose a husband from the lords at court. She chooses Bertram, who is ordered into the marriage and leaves for the war in Florence that night, writing that she may call him husband only when she has the ring off his finger and a child of his to show. Helena follows him, finds the young woman he is trying to seduce, and takes her place in his bed. Beside this runs the unmasking of Bertram’s companion Parolles, a braggart whom his fellow officers blindfold and frighten into betraying everyone he knows.

Written Early 1600s; first printed 1623.
