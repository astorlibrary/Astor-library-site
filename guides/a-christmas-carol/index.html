Charles Dickens

A Christmas Carol

A study guide

A moneylender who has organised his whole life around refusal is visited in one night by his dead partner and three spirits, and shown his own childhood, the households he has never entered and a grave nobody visits. Dickens calls the divisions staves, as if they were verses of a carol, because the book is meant to be performed, and what matters in the end is that Scrooge pays, visits and turns up the next morning; feeling sorry is not enough.

Written 1843; first published 1843.
