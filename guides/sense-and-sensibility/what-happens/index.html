Sense and Sensibility · Jane Austen

What happens

Chapters 1–12 The Dashwoods leave Norland for Barton Cottage

Old Mr Dashwood’s will ties up Norland, and the second chapter reduces a deathbed promise to nothing across a single fireside conversation. The Dashwood women take Barton Cottage on a relation’s charity, Marianne sprains her ankle in the rain, and Willoughby arrives carrying her downhill.

Chapters 13–22 Willoughby leaves, and Lucy’s secret

Willoughby and Marianne behave as though they were engaged and never say that they are; then he leaves for London without explanation. Lucy Steele, watching Elinor closely, discloses her four-year engagement to Edward Ferrars in a conversation designed to look like a confidence.

Chapters 23–36 London, and Willoughby’s letter

In town Marianne writes to Willoughby and waits; he cuts her at a party and returns her letters with a note his fiancée dictated. Colonel Brandon supplies the history of the two Elizas, and at Mrs Ferrars’s dinner Lucy is singled out for kindness while Elinor is pointedly slighted.

Chapters 37–44 Marianne’s illness, and Willoughby’s confession

When the engagement comes out and Edward is disinherited for keeping to it, Elinor finally tells Marianne how long she has known about Lucy and what it has cost to say nothing. At Cleveland Marianne walks in wet grass until she is dangerously ill, and Willoughby arrives at eight on a stormy evening to explain himself to the only person who will listen.

Chapters 45–50 Marianne’s recovery, and the two marriages

Marianne reconsiders her whole conduct without abandoning her capacity to feel. A servant’s report that Mr Ferrars is married sends the household into mourning for the wrong brother, and the novel closes by arranging incomes at Delaford and a parsonage nearby.
