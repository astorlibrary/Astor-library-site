Sense and Sensibility · Jane Austen

Context

Dates

1775
Jane Austen born at Steventon In the Hampshire rectory where she wrote the first versions of three of her six novels.
1795
Early version, Elinor and Marianne, probably written Family tradition places an early version of this story in the mid-1790s, probably told in letters. No manuscript survives.
1797
First Impressions offered to a publisher and refused The novel that became Pride and Prejudice was declined unseen, and Austen published nothing for another fourteen years.
1809
The Austens settle at Chawton The move to a settled house in Hampshire begins the period in which every one of Austen’s novels was revised or written.
1811
Sense and Sensibility published Printed for the author by C. Roworth, published by T. Egerton in three volumes, and advertised at fifteen shillings in boards.
1813
The first edition sells out; a second follows In the same year Pride and Prejudice came out as ‘by the author of Sense and Sensibility’, so the anonymous first book became the name readers knew her by.
1814
Mansfield Park published Begun in 1811, the year Sense and Sensibility came out, and written entirely at Chawton.
1817
Austen dies at Winchester Aged forty-one, with none of her novels published under her own name.
1833
Bentley’s Standard Novels reprint Sense and Sensibility re-enters the market in a cheap uniform edition, and Austen’s novels stop going out of print.
1971
First BBC television adaptation The beginning of a long serial tradition that gives the social network around the sisters more room than a feature film can.
1995
Ang Lee’s film, from Emma Thompson’s screenplay Thompson as Elinor, Kate Winslet as Marianne, Alan Rickman as Brandon; it won the Academy Award for Adapted Screenplay and the Golden Bear at Berlin.
2000
Kandukondain Kandukondain, a Tamil film adaptation Rajiv Menon’s Tamil-language film relocates the sisters, the prospects and the delayed recognition to an Indian setting.
2008
BBC serial adapted by Andrew Davies A three-part version directed by John Alexander. It opens on Willoughby’s seduction of Eliza, which the novel keeps back until Brandon tells Elinor about it.

Places

Sussex
Norland Park, where the Dashwoods had been settled for generations and where the widow and her daughters stay on for several months as guests of the heir.
Devonshire
Barton Cottage and Barton Park: a house taken on very easy terms from a relation, which is a polite name for charity.
London
Berkeley Street and the drawing rooms where Marianne waits for a reply, and is cut in public when it comes.
Somersetshire
Combe Magna, Willoughby’s own small estate, and Cleveland, where Marianne walks in wet grass until she is dangerously ill.
Dorsetshire
Delaford, Colonel Brandon’s property, with the living he gives away and the mulberry tree Mrs Jennings cannot stop describing.
Plymouth
Where the Steele sisters have their connections, and the town whose name is enough to place them socially the moment it is mentioned.
