The Merchant of Venice · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I Antonio’s sadness, Portia’s caskets and the bond

Antonio cannot say why he is unhappy. Bassanio needs money to court an heiress whose father has left her unable to choose her own husband, and Shylock lends it on a forfeit of a pound of flesh, proposed and accepted as a piece of friendly humour by men who have just insulted each other.

1.1
Antonio’s friends try to explain his melancholy; Bassanio asks for a further loan to go to Belmont.
1.2
Portia reviews her suitors with Nerissa and complains of a lottery she cannot refuse.
1.3
Shylock recalls years of public abuse, then lends interest-free against a pound of Antonio’s flesh.

Act II Morocco and Arragon, and Jessica’s flight

Morocco and Arragon choose gold and silver and are beaten by their own assumptions about worth. In Venice, Jessica leaves her father’s house dressed as a boy, taking money and jewels with her, and the Christian gentlemen of the Rialto treat Shylock’s public grief as street entertainment.

2.1
Morocco asks not to be judged by his complexion and is told the terms of the lottery.
2.2
The clown argues with his own conscience and leaves Shylock for Bassanio.
2.3
Jessica calls her father’s house hell and asks Launcelot to carry a letter to Lorenzo.
2.4
Lorenzo learns what gold and jewels she will bring with her.
2.5
Shylock, going out to supper against his judgement, tells Jessica to shut the windows.
2.6
Jessica throws down a casket and escapes in boy’s clothes, ashamed of the disguise.
2.7
Morocco opens the golden casket and finds a skull and a rhyme about glittering.
2.8
Salanio performs Shylock’s cries about his daughter and his ducats for laughs.
2.9
Arragon takes what he deserves and is handed the portrait of a blinking idiot.

Act III Bassanio chooses lead, and Antonio’s ships are lost

Bassanio rejects ornament, chooses lead and wins Portia within the hour. In the same act Shylock, taunted in the street about his daughter, answers with the play’s most famous demand for common humanity and ends it by promising revenge. News arrives that Antonio’s ships are gone.

3.1
Salarino and Salanio bait Shylock; Tubal brings news of Jessica’s spending and of a wrecked argosy.
3.2
Bassanio chooses correctly; the rings are given; a letter from Antonio interrupts the betrothal.
3.3
Shylock refuses to hear Antonio and repeats that he will have his bond.
3.4
Portia sends for legal papers from Doctor Bellario and tells Nerissa they will go disguised.
3.5
Launcelot jokes that converting Jews will raise the price of pork; Jessica changes the subject.

Act IV The trial

The longest scene in the play. Shylock refuses three times the money, Portia arrives disguised as a lawyer from Padua and preaches mercy, and then wins by reading the bond more strictly than its holder. Within forty lines Shylock has lost his estate, his livelihood and his religion.

4.1
Mercy is argued, the bond is enforced to the letter, and an alien statute is produced against the plaintiff.
4.2
Portia and Nerissa, still disguised, collect the rings their husbands swore never to part with.

Act V Belmont, and the rings

Lorenzo and Jessica trade stories of lovers who came to bad ends, the wives expose the business of the rings, and Antonio learns that three of his ships are safe after all. Shylock is mentioned only as the source of a deed of gift. The comedy closes with everybody paired except the merchant it is named after.

5.1
Music, the ring quarrel, the revelation of the disguise, and news that Antonio’s argosies have come home.
