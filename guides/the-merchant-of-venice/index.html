William Shakespeare

The Merchant of Venice

A study guide

A merchant borrows money he does not need, on terms he thinks are a joke, so that a friend can go courting. The comedy that follows has a courtroom at its centre, and the clever rescue there is also a confiscation: Shylock loses his money, his profession and his religion in the space of forty lines, and the play then moves to a moonlit garden and never mentions him again.

Written c. 1596–1597; first printed 1600.
