The Taming of the Shrew · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1566
Gascoigne’s Supposes performed at Gray’s Inn An English version of Ariosto’s I Suppositi, and the source of the Bianca plot’s disguises and substituted fathers.
1573
Supposes printed Because it was in print, the borrowing can be traced.
1591
The Taming of the Shrew probably written Usually placed around 1590–92 among the earliest comedies; nothing fixes the year.
1594
The Taming of a Shrew, a closely related play, printed A closely related play with different names and wording, reprinted in 1596 and 1607. Source, memorial report, adaptation or early draft: the question is still open.
1610
Fletcher’s The Woman’s Prize, or The Tamer Tamed Usually dated around 1609–11. Petruchio remarries after Katherina’s death and is resisted by a second wife, which shows the play was being answered within its own generation.
1623
First Folio The first secure printed text of Shakespeare’s Shrew, with the Induction and no return to Sly.
1631
A quarto printed from the Folio text Not an independent witness; it confirms that the Folio was the version in circulation.
1667
John Lacy’s Sauny the Scot A Restoration adaptation in prose that expanded Grumio into a Scottish servant and made Petruchio harsher.
1754
Garrick’s Catharine and Petruchio Three acts, no Sly and almost no Bianca, and it was the version most audiences saw for well over a century.
1929
Sam Taylor’s film with Mary Pickford and Douglas Fairbanks Widely cited as the first sound Shakespeare film; Pickford’s wink during the last speech became part of the play’s argument with itself.
1948
Kiss Me, Kate opens on Broadway Cole Porter’s musical puts the play inside a backstage quarrel, a twentieth-century replacement for the abandoned Sly frame.
1978
Michael Bogdanov’s RSC production Sly disrupted the auditorium before the Padua story began, so the taming plot came across as a problem and not a farce.
2019
Justin Audibert’s gender-reversed RSC production Set in a matriarchal Elizabethan England, with Petruchia and Katherine, so that the hierarchy itself became visible as a construction.

Places

Padua
Baptista’s city, described as a nursery of arts and run as a marriage market.
Verona
Petruchio’s home, which he has left to wive it wealthily in Padua.
Pisa
Lucentio’s city, and Vincentio’s: the identity a passing pedant is hired to counterfeit.
Venice
Where Petruchio goes for rings and fine array between the betrothal and the wedding.
Mantua
The pedant’s home, and the city he is told it is dangerous to have come from.
Barton-on-the-Heath
Sly’s Burtonheath: a Warwickshire village a few miles from Stratford, where Shakespeare had relatives by marriage.

Critics

George Bernard Shaw

The Pall Mall Gazette (1888)

After Augustin Daly’s company played the comedy in London, Shaw wrote that its last scene, with Katherine’s speech of submission, was disgusting to modern feeling, and urged men not to sit through it in the company of a woman.
