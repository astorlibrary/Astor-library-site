The Taming of the Shrew · William Shakespeare

Themes

Marriage and money

Nobody in Padua marries without a negotiation. Baptista will not release Bianca until Katherina is sold, Gremio bids his tapestries against Tranio’s imaginary ships, Petruchio states his price in his first scene, and the last act is a wager laid on wives. The romance is real but it is always conducted over a contract.

Baptista’s condition in 1.1, the dowry settled in 2.1, the auction for Bianca in the same scene, and twenty thousand crowns added as a second dowry in 5.2.

To see fair Padua, nursery of arts

Lucentio, 1.1.2

Her elder sister is so curst and shrewd That till the father rid his hands of her, Master, your love must live a maid at home,

Tranio, 1.1.171

Taming

The method is deprivation dressed as care: no food, no sleep, no clothes, every refusal delivered as concern for her comfort. Petruchio borrows the vocabulary of hawking to describe it, which is the play at its most disquieting: the language assumes a creature that can be reduced to obedience by hunger and then flown.

Announced as an intention in 2.1, explained to the audience in 4.1, tested on the road in 4.5 and displayed as a finished result in 5.2.

I come to wive it wealthily in Padua; If wealthily, then happily in Padua.

Petruchio, 1.2.71

For I am he am born to tame you Kate, And bring you from a wild Kate to a Kate Conformable as other household Kates.

Petruchio, 2.1.275

Disguise and substitution

A servant becomes his master, a master becomes a tutor, a suitor becomes a music teacher, a passing pedant becomes a father, and a tinker becomes a lord. Identity in this play is a matter of clothes and repetition, the same assumption the taming plot depends on.

Lucentio and Tranio change places in 1.1; the false Vincentio is recruited in 4.2 and exposed in 5.1; Sly’s transformation in the Induction sets the pattern for all of them.

Ye are a baggage: the Slys are no rogues; look in the chronicles; we came in with Richard Conqueror.

Sly, Induction 1.2

Come, madam wife, sit by my side and let the world slip: we shall ne'er be younger.

Sly, Induction 2.139

The obedience speech

Forty-four lines on a wife’s duty to her lord, delivered in front of men who have just bet money on whether she would come when called. Nothing in the text settles the tone, which is why the speech has been played as surrender, as irony, as a private arrangement between two people and as a public humiliation.

Katherina refuses to be silenced in 4.3, agrees to call the sun the moon in 4.5, and in 5.2 tells the other two wives that ‘our lances are but straws’.

Thus in plain terms: your father hath consented That you shall be my wife; your dowry 'greed on; And, Will you, nill you, I will marry you.

Petruchio, 2.1.268

Why, gentlemen, you do me double wrong, To strive for that which resteth in my choice: I am no breeching scholar in the schools; I'll not be tied to hours nor 'pointed times, But learn my lessons as I please myself.

Bianca, 3.1.16

Naming and contradiction

Petruchio’s chief weapon is insisting on his own version of the words: she is Kate, the food is burnt, the cap is a bauble, the sun is the moon. Katherina’s resistance is also verbal, and the marriage is settled by who gets to decide what things are called, more than by force.

The wit duel of 2.1 is fought on equal terms; by 4.5 one party is naming and the other assenting; the last speech is the naming done in her own voice.

Asses are made to bear, and so are you.

Katherina, 2.1.199

For I am he am born to tame you Kate, And bring you from a wild Kate to a Kate Conformable as other household Kates.

Petruchio, 2.1.275

Watching and performance

Almost everything in the play is performed for an onstage audience: Sly watches the comedy, Gremio reports the wedding, Hortensio spies on Bianca, and the obedience speech is delivered to a room of spectators who have money on it. The Induction makes watching a subject before the main plot has begun.

The frame is set up in the Induction and abandoned after 1.1; the wager scene reinstates the same arrangement, with the husbands as the lord and Katherina as Sly.
