The Taming of the Shrew · William Shakespeare

What happens

Induction A tinker is told he is a lord

Christopher Sly is thrown out of an alehouse for breaking glasses and falls asleep. A lord returning from hunting decides, as a joke, to have him carried home, dressed in fine clothes and told that his old life was a fifteen-year delusion. Sly holds out for about a hundred lines and then accepts it, and a company of players is brought in to perform the Padua comedy for him.

Induction 1
Sly refuses to pay for the glasses, passes out, and is found by a lord with an idea.
Induction 2
Sly is waited on, given a page dressed as his wife, and settles down to watch a play.

Act I Baptista’s condition, and the suitors’ disguises

Baptista announces that Bianca may not marry until her elder sister is off his hands, which makes Katherina an obstacle before the audience has heard her speak twenty lines. Lucentio falls in love at first sight and swaps clothes with his servant so that he can enter the house as a tutor. Petruchio arrives from Verona and says plainly that he has come to marry money.

1.1
Baptista sets his terms; Katherina is insulted in public; Lucentio and Tranio exchange identities.
1.2
Petruchio states his price, hears about Katherina’s temper, and is undeterred; Hortensio disguises himself as a music master.

Act II Petruchio woos Katherina

Katherina has tied up her sister and is striking her when their father walks in, which is the one scene that shows what the household is actually like. Petruchio then meets her, matches her insult for insult for a hundred lines, tells her father they have privately agreed to marry, and settles the dowry. The wedding is fixed for Sunday before Katherina has consented to anything.

2.1
Katherina beats Bianca; Petruchio conducts a wit duel, declares a match, and bargains with Baptista; Tranio outbids Gremio for Bianca.

Act III The lessons, and the wedding

Two disguised suitors compete for Bianca’s attention under cover of a Latin lesson and a music lesson. Petruchio arrives hours late for his own wedding in deliberately ridiculous clothes, behaves outrageously in church, and then removes his wife before the dinner, announcing that she is his property and pretending to rescue her from thieves.

3.1
Lucentio courts Bianca through a construed Latin sentence; Hortensio through a gamut; Bianca sets the terms.
3.2
Petruchio comes late and badly dressed, marries Katherina, and carries her off before the feast.

Act IV Petruchio’s house, and the false Vincentio

At Petruchio’s house the food is sent back, the bed is pulled apart and a tailor is abused, all in the name of Katherina’s comfort. Petruchio explains the method to the audience in falconry terms. On the road back to Padua he makes her agree that the sun is the moon; in Padua a passing pedant is recruited to impersonate Lucentio’s father, and the real one arrives.

4.1
Servants are beaten, the meat is thrown out, and Petruchio sets out his plan alone on stage.
4.2
Hortensio gives up on Bianca and resolves to marry a widow; Tranio recruits a pedant to play Vincentio.
4.3
Katherina insists on her right to speak; the tailor’s work is destroyed; the journey is cancelled over the time of day.
4.4
The pedant settles the marriage terms as Vincentio, and Lucentio and Bianca slip away to be married.
4.5
Katherina agrees that the sun is the moon, then greets old Vincentio as a budding virgin.

Act V The wager

The real Vincentio meets his impostor and is nearly arrested; the secret marriage is revealed and forgiven. At the wedding feast the three husbands bet on which wife will come when sent for. Katherina comes, fetches the other two, and delivers forty-four lines on a wife’s duty to a room of men who have just staked money on her behaviour.

5.1
The impersonation collapses; Lucentio confesses the marriage; the fathers are reconciled.
5.2
The wager is laid and won, and Katherina speaks on obedience before the company.
