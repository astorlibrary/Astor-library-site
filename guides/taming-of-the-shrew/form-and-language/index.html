The Taming of the Shrew · William Shakespeare

Form and language

The frame

A story enclosing another story, here left open at one end.

Sly is set up as the play’s audience and then simply dropped. The related 1594 quarto A Shrew brings him back at the end, which is why eighteenth-century editors borrowed that ending and why modern directors still have to choose.

Ye are a baggage: the Slys are no rogues; look in the chronicles; we came in with Richard Conqueror.

Sly, Induction 1.2

Disguise plot

Comedy driven by exchanged and counterfeited identities.

Inherited from Gascoigne’s Supposes, itself from Ariosto: a servant in his master’s gown, two suitors disguised as tutors and a stranger hired to play a father, all running together and colliding in 5.1.

Falconry imagery

Sustained comparison of a person to a hawk being trained.

Petruchio’s soliloquy in 4.1 uses the technical vocabulary exactly, sharp, full-gorged, lure, man my haggard, watch her, so the audience hears the method in the language of a manual.

My falcon now is sharp and passing empty; And till she stoop she must not be full-gorged, For then she never looks upon her lure.

Petruchio, 4.1.169

Stichomythia

Rapid exchange of single lines between two speakers.

The wooing scene in 2.1 runs on it for over eighty lines: joined-stool, ass, jade, buzzard, each answer taking the previous line’s word and turning it round. It is the only sustained conversation in which Katherina is a match for anyone.

Asses are made to bear, and so are you.

Katherina, 2.1.199

Soliloquy

A character alone on stage, telling the audience what they are doing.

Petruchio gets two and Katherina none. His speech at the end of 4.1 gives the audience the whole strategy and then asks whether anybody knows a better way, which makes the spectators accomplices.

My falcon now is sharp and passing empty; And till she stoop she must not be full-gorged, For then she never looks upon her lure.

Petruchio, 4.1.169

Wordplay

Puns and quibbles used as attack and defence.

Kate, cate and Kates in 2.1; a cap that is a custard-coffin, a bauble and a silken pie in 4.3. Again and again the play shows that whoever controls the joke controls the scene.

Ye are a baggage: the Slys are no rogues; look in the chronicles; we came in with Richard Conqueror.

Sly, Induction 1.2
