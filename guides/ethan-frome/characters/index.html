Ethan Frome · Edith Wharton

Characters

Ethan Frome

A farmer and sawmill owner, twenty-eight in the reconstructed winter; first appears Prologue

He studied engineering for a year at Worcester and came back to nurse first his father, then his mother, then the wife he married because the silence after his mother’s funeral was unbearable. Every exit is costed in the book and none of them clears: he cannot sell the farm, cannot raise fifty dollars, and cannot leave without abandoning a sick woman.

Zenobia (Zeena) Frome

Ethan’s wife, seven years older than him; first appears Chapters I–III

She came to nurse his mother and stayed as his wife, and her illness, never diagnosed by the book, is the only authority a woman in her position has. Wharton gives her almost no interior life and a great deal of power, and the epilogue reverses the two so completely that the reader has to reconsider everything said about her.

Mattie Silver

Zeena’s young cousin, working in the house for her keep; first appears Chapters I–III

Her father’s failure left her with fifty dollars and no training; shop work and stenography both broke her health, so she is at the Fromes because there is nowhere else. The novel is careful not to make her a great beauty or a great mind; she is simply the only warm thing in the house.

The engineer

An unnamed visitor sent to Corbury Junction on a job; first appears Prologue

He is never named and never explains his own life, and he states at the outset that the story came to him in pieces that did not agree. The central chapters are his own vision of the story, which means every judgement the reader makes about Zeena arrives through a stranger who spent one night in the house.

Harmon Gow

Former driver of the Bettsbridge stage; first appears Prologue

He knew the families along the route before the trolley and the railway came, and he gives the narrator the accident, the dates and the single sentence on which the whole reconstruction rests. His information stops exactly where the village’s sympathy stops.

Mrs Ned Hale

The narrator’s landlady, formerly Ruth Varnum; first appears Prologue

She was the first person into the house after the accident and has said almost nothing about it since. Her reticence is what gives the epilogue its force: when she finally speaks she delivers the harshest verdict in the book, and the book ends on it.

Andrew Hale

A builder, Ethan’s chief customer; first appears Chapters VIII–IX

He pays for lumber three months after delivery and is himself short of money because he is fitting out a house for his son’s marriage. His refusal of an advance is delivered kindly and is the exact point at which every remaining exit closes.

Denis Eady

The grocer’s son, with his father’s horses to drive; first appears Chapters I–III

He has money, a cutter and no obligations, which makes him the version of Ethan’s life that was available to somebody else. He offers Mattie a lift home from the dance and is refused, and afterwards he functions mainly as the future the Fromes cannot compete with.
