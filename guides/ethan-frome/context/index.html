Ethan Frome · Edith Wharton

Context

Dates

1862
Edith Newbold Jones born in New York Into the old moneyed society she would spend a career anatomising.
1885
Marriage to Edward Robbins Wharton A match that became increasingly unhappy and ended in divorce in 1913.
1902
The Mount completed at Lenox, Massachusetts Wharton designed the house and gardens herself and lived in the Berkshire hill country this book is set in.
1904
A coasting accident at Lenox A documented sledding accident in the town, often connected with the novel’s ending, though Wharton did not identify it as her source.
1905
Wharton’s The House of Mirth published The novel that made her reputation, and the first of her studies of a woman destroyed by an economy she is not allowed to work in.
1911
Ethan Frome published By Charles Scribner’s Sons, after appearing in Scribner’s Magazine.
1913
Wharton’s The Custom of the Country published Published two years later, and as ruthless about money in drawing rooms as this book is about money on a hill farm.
1920
Wharton’s The Age of Innocence published Written after the war, from France, looking back at the New York of her childhood.
1921
Wharton wins the Pulitzer Prize for The Age of Innocence The first woman to receive it, for The Age of Innocence.
1937
Wharton dies in France At Saint-Brice-sous-Forêt, north of Paris, where she had lived for years.

Places

Lenox, Massachusetts
Where Wharton built The Mount and learned the Berkshire winters that Starkfield is made from.
Pittsfield, Massachusetts
The nearest real town of any size to Wharton’s Berkshire country, and the model for the errands the Fromes take to Bettsbridge.
Mount Greylock
The highest point in Massachusetts, in the hill country whose isolated farms Wharton took her setting from.
Paris
Where Wharton was living when she wrote the French exercise, Hiver, that supplied the situation of this book.
New York
The city of Scribner’s Magazine, which serialised the novella, and the society Wharton usually wrote about instead.
