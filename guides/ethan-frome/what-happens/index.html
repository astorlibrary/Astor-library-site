Ethan Frome · Edith Wharton

What happens

Prologue The narrator comes to Starkfield

Held in Starkfield by a carpenters’ strike, the narrator notices a ruined man at the post office and starts asking. Harmon Gow supplies the accident, Mrs Ned Hale supplies a reticence, and a snowstorm finally puts him inside the Frome kitchen. There the book goes back twenty-four years.

Chapters I–III Waiting outside the dance

Ethan stands in the cold watching Mattie Silver dance with Denis Eady, and walks her home by the coasting hill and past the Frome graves. Zeena is awake when they get in, and by the third chapter she has announced a trip to Bettsbridge to see a new doctor.

Chapters IV–V An evening alone, and the cat breaks the dish

With Zeena away, Ethan and Mattie have supper in a kitchen that briefly feels like a different life. The cat knocks Zeena’s red glass pickle-dish off the table, and Ethan puts the pieces back on the shelf intending to buy glue in the morning.

Chapters VI–VII Zeena’s return, and the broken dish found

Zeena comes back with a diagnosis and a hired girl already engaged, which means Mattie must leave in the morning. Ethan has no money and no argument, and when the pieces of the dish are discovered the quarrel stops being about anybody’s health.

Chapters VIII–IX Mattie leaves, and the sled ride

Ethan drafts a letter he cannot send, sets out to ask Andrew Hale for an advance and turns back on the road, and drives Mattie to the station by way of the sledding hill. She asks him not to leave her, and then asks him for something else.

Epilogue The kitchen, twenty-four years on

The narrator is shown into the room the three of them share, and the two women in it are not arranged as anyone would have predicted. Mrs Hale afterwards says the thing nobody in Starkfield has said aloud for a quarter of a century.
