Hamlet · William Shakespeare

Characters

Hamlet

Prince of Denmark, son of the murdered king; first appears 1.2

A student called home into a job he has no evidence for and no appetite for. His delay is usually called a flaw, but again and again the play shows him doing the work of proof: testing the Ghost, staging the play, reading the commission. What he cannot do is act while he is still thinking, and the two things almost never stop at the same time.

Claudius

King of Denmark, the dead king’s brother; first appears 1.2

The most competent politician in the play and the only character who tells the audience outright that he is guilty. He settles a foreign crisis by letter, manages a rebellion by talking to it, and never once mishandles a room, which is why the prayer scene hurts: it is the one thing he cannot negotiate.

Gertrude

Queen of Denmark; first appears 1.2

She is given very few lines in which to be anything other than the subject of other people’s speeches about her. The play never says whether she knew about the murder, and the closet scene is the one place where she is asked directly and answers in a way that settles nothing. Her clearest moment is the speech describing Ophelia’s drowning.

Ophelia

Polonius’s daughter; first appears 1.3

Every scene she appears in before her madness has been arranged by a man: her father tells her what to feel, her brother tells her what to fear, and she is placed in a corridor with a book to be watched. When she loses her reason she is finally allowed to speak first and at length, and the court cannot stop her singing.

Polonius

Counsellor to the king; first appears 1.2

A man who mistakes talking for thinking and surveillance for care. His famous advice to Laertes is a string of maxims delivered by somebody who immediately sends a spy after him. He dies because he is hiding in a room where he has no business, which is the only end the play could reasonably give him.

Laertes

Polonius’s son; first appears 1.2

He does in one scene what Hamlet cannot do in four acts: hears of his father’s death, raises a mob and breaks into the palace. The play uses him to show that speed is not justice: he is willing to cut throats in a church, and Claudius steers him within minutes of his arrival.

Horatio

Hamlet’s fellow student; first appears 1.1

The only person in Elsinore who wants nothing from Hamlet, and therefore the only one told the truth. He is a sceptic in the first scene who has to concede that the Ghost is real, and a survivor in the last who is ordered to stay alive and tell the story, a job the play leaves him doing after the lights go down.

The Ghost

The spirit of the dead King Hamlet; first appears 1.1

It comes in armour, refuses to speak to anyone but its son, and gives an account of its own murder that no living witness can confirm. The play deliberately leaves its status open: a soul in purgatory, a devil exploiting grief, or a projection. Everything Hamlet does from Act II onwards is an attempt to work out which.

Rosencrantz and Guildenstern

Hamlet’s schoolfellows, hired by the king; first appears 2.2

They are summoned by name, given a task neither of them quite admits to, and are never separated by the text into two distinguishable people. Their function is to show that friendship at this court is a form of employment; their deaths are reported in a single line by a messenger who has no idea who they were.

Fortinbras

Prince of Norway; first appears 4.4

He is talked about for four acts and appears for a few minutes, which is exactly the point: he is the other son of a dead king, and he is busy. Hamlet watches his army march past to fight over ground not worth farming, and the play then hands Denmark to him because everyone with a better claim is dead on the floor.
