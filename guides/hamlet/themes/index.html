Hamlet · William Shakespeare

Themes

Revenge and delay

The play takes a kind of story in which a son is told to kill, and asks what would have to be true before killing were justified. Hamlet has nerve enough (he stabs through a curtain without hesitating); his problem is the standard of proof. The Ghost is testimony without evidence, and no court in Denmark is going to hear it.

Commanded in 1.5, doubted through Act II, tested in 3.2, deferred in 3.3, and finally performed in 5.2 by a man who is already dying and no longer choosing.

Seems, madam! nay it is; I know not 'seems.'

Hamlet, 1.2.77

There are more things in heaven and earth, Horatio, Than are dreamt of in your philosophy.

Hamlet, 1.5.160

Seeming and performance

Hamlet’s first substantial line rejects the word ‘seems’, and he spends the rest of the play acting: a feigned madness, a play staged as an experiment, a rewritten commission sealed with his father’s signet. Claudius is doing the same thing from the other side of the throne, and only the audience gets to see both scripts.

Set out in 1.2, adopted as policy in 1.5, professionalised in 3.2, and abandoned in Act V, where Hamlet stops performing and starts reporting.

Seems, madam! nay it is; I know not 'seems.'

Hamlet, 1.2.77

Frailty, thy name is woman!

Hamlet, 1.2.147

Watching and being watched

Almost no private conversation in this play is actually private. Reynaldo is sent to spy on Laertes, Ophelia is used as bait, Polonius hides twice, Rosencrantz and Guildenstern are briefed, and the king watches a play while being watched watching it. Elsinore meets dissent by listening to it.

A habit in Act II, a policy in Act III, and lethal in 3.4, where the listener behind the curtain is killed for listening.

This above all: to thine ownself be true, And it must follow, as the night the day, Thou canst not then be false to any man.

Lord Polonius, 1.3.78

Something is rotten in the state of Denmark.

Marcellus, 1.4.91

Death and the body

The play is unusually interested in what happens to bodies: poison in an ear, a corpse dragged to a lobby, a king going on progress through the guts of a beggar, a skull that Hamlet sniffs and puts down. Hamlet’s metaphysics stay unresolved, but his physiology is exact, and the graveyard is where the two finally meet.

Abstract in the soliloquies of Acts I and III; literal from 3.4 onwards, and entirely material by 5.1, where death is a trade with tools and songs.

O, that this too too solid flesh would melt Thaw and resolve itself into a dew!

Hamlet, 1.2.130

There are more things in heaven and earth, Horatio, Than are dreamt of in your philosophy.

Hamlet, 1.5.160

Madness, real and performed

Hamlet announces in advance that he will pretend, which ought to settle the question and does not: the pretence gives him licence to say things that are far too accurate to be mad and far too wild to be safe. Ophelia’s madness is unannounced, unstrategic and hard to interpret, and the court’s response to it is to keep watching.

Declared in 1.5, diagnosed by Polonius in Act II, weaponised in 3.1, and set beside the real thing in 4.5.

O, that this too too solid flesh would melt Thaw and resolve itself into a dew!

Hamlet, 1.2.130

How strange or odd soe'er I bear myself, As I perchance hereafter shall think meet To put an antic disposition on,

Hamlet, 1.5.163

Memory and record

The Ghost’s actual demand is ‘Remember me’, and Hamlet answers by treating his mind as a notebook to be wiped and rewritten. The play is full of records: tables, letters, commissions, songs, an old jester remembered by a skull. Hamlet’s dying charge is that Horatio report him and his cause aright.

A vow in 1.5, a joke about writing it down moments later, and a formal commission to Horatio in 5.2: the play ends by appointing its own narrator.

My tables,--meet it is I set it down, That one may smile, and smile, and be a villain;

Hamlet, 1.5.103

O, what a noble mind is here o'erthrown!

Ophelia, 3.1.147
