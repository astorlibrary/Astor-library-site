Revenge and delay

The play takes a kind of story in which a son is told to kill, and asks what would have to be true before killing were justified. Hamlet has nerve enough (he stabs through a curtain without hesitating); his problem is the standard of proof. The Ghost is testimony without evidence, and no court in Denmark is going to hear it.

Through the play Commanded in 1.5, doubted through Act II, tested in 3.2, deferred in 3.3, and finally performed in 5.2 by a man who is already dying and no longer choosing.

In the text

There are more things in heaven and earth, Horatio,
Than are dreamt of in your philosophy.

Hamlet, 1.5.160

It is usually quoted as mysticism; in position it is an argument with a sceptic who has already seen the ghost and is still reasoning. Note the address: Hamlet uses his friend’s name to make the rebuke personal, and the line’s authority depends on a spirit shouting from beneath the floorboards.

O, my offence is rank it smells to heaven;

King Claudius, 3.3.36

This is the moment the audience stops needing the Ghost: the confession comes from the murderer, unprompted, with nobody listening. Shakespeare gives the proof to the theatre and withholds it from Hamlet, who walks in forty lines later and sees only a man at his devotions.

Seeming and performance

Hamlet’s first substantial line rejects the word ‘seems’, and he spends the rest of the play acting: a feigned madness, a play staged as an experiment, a rewritten commission sealed with his father’s signet. Claudius is doing the same thing from the other side of the throne, and only the audience gets to see both scripts.

Through the play Set out in 1.2, adopted as a plan in 1.5, turned into a method with the players in 3.2, and given up in Act V, where Hamlet stops performing and starts reporting.

In the text

Seems, madam! nay it is; I know not ‘seems.’

Hamlet, 1.2.77

He objects to a verb, which tells you what kind of hero this is going to be. The irony is scheduled three scenes later, when he decides to put on an appearance deliberately, and the play never lets him have the word back.

The lady protests too much, methinks.

Queen Gertrude, 3.2.213

Asked to judge a play about a widow’s promise, Gertrude gives a critic’s verdict on the acting, and not a wife’s answer to the charge. Whether that is composure or blindness is the closest the play comes to telling us what she knows, and it declines to decide.

Watching and being watched

Almost no private conversation in this play is actually private. Reynaldo is sent to spy on Laertes, Ophelia is used as bait, Polonius hides twice, Rosencrantz and Guildenstern are briefed, and the king watches a play while being watched watching it. At Elsinore, the answer to suspicion is to listen in.

Through the play A habit in Act II, a policy in Act III, and lethal in 3.4, where the listener behind the curtain is killed for listening.

In the text

Something is rotten in the state of Denmark.

Marcellus, 1.4.91

It is given to a minor soldier, which matters: the corruption is visible from the guard post before anyone at court admits it. ‘Something’ is deliberately vague: nobody on stage yet knows what, and the audience has not been told either.

Denmark’s a prison.

Hamlet, 2.2.237

Three words of prose, offered as a flat statement of fact to two men who have just been hired as warders without quite realising it. The exchange that follows makes it a philosophical game about thinking making it so, which is how Hamlet avoids being pinned down by people fishing for symptoms.

Death and the body

The play is unusually interested in what happens to bodies: poison in an ear, a corpse dragged to a lobby, a king going on progress through the guts of a beggar, a skull that Hamlet sniffs and puts down. Hamlet’s metaphysics stay unresolved, but his physiology is exact, and the graveyard is where the two finally meet.

Through the play Abstract in the soliloquies of Acts I and III; literal from 3.4 onwards, and entirely material by 5.1, where death is a trade with tools and songs.

In the text

O, that this too too solid flesh would melt
Thaw and resolve itself into a dew!

Hamlet, 1.2.130

Three verbs of dissolving in a row, each one weaker than the last, so the wish loses force as it is spoken. Nothing here is about revenge or a ghost: the speech comes before he knows anything, so the despair is there before the plot begins.

Alas, poor Yorick! I knew him, Horatio: a fellow of infinite jest, of most excellent fancy:

Hamlet, 5.1.169

It is the first death in the play he can speak about without a theory, and the reason is that he has a name and a memory to attach to the bone. The scene is in prose, and the joking goes on around him: the play’s most affecting moment about mortality is set in a workplace where corpses are routine.

Madness, real and performed

Hamlet announces in advance that he will pretend, which ought to settle the question and does not: the pretence gives him licence to say things that are far too accurate to be mad and far too wild to be safe. Ophelia’s madness is unannounced, unstrategic and hard to interpret, and the court’s response to it is to keep watching.

Through the play Declared in 1.5, diagnosed by Polonius in Act II, turned on Ophelia in 3.1, and set beside the real thing in 4.5.

In the text

How strange or odd soe’er I bear myself,
As I perchance hereafter shall think meet
To put an antic disposition on,

Hamlet, 1.5.163

The madness is announced in advance, under oath, as a wardrobe decision: ‘put on’ is the verb used for clothes. That advance notice keeps the question alive for four acts: everything strange Hamlet does afterwards has been pre-authorised, so nothing he does can settle the matter.

O, what a noble mind is here o’erthrown!

Ophelia, 3.1.147

She mourns Hamlet as a loss while he is still alive, and she does it in a formal lament built of three-part lists: eye, tongue, sword. The speech also establishes what he was like before the play began, information the audience has no other way of getting.

Memory and record

The Ghost’s actual demand is ‘Remember me’, and Hamlet answers by treating his mind as a notebook to be wiped and rewritten. The play is full of records: tables, letters, commissions, songs, an old jester remembered by a skull. Hamlet’s dying charge is that Horatio report him and his cause aright.

Through the play A vow in 1.5, a joke about writing it down moments later, and a formal commission to Horatio in 5.2, who is asked to tell the story.

In the text

My tables,—meet it is I set it down,
That one may smile, and smile, and be a villain;

Hamlet, 1.5.103

He has just been told his uncle is a murderer and his response is to reach for a notebook. Once written down, unbearable testimony becomes a manageable proposition, and the stage direction that follows, in which he actually writes, makes the intellectual habit physical.

Which have solicited. The rest is silence.

Hamlet, 5.2.336

His last act is administrative: a vote, a request for an accurate account, and then nothing. ‘The rest’ means both what remains to be said and what remains of him, and the man who could not stop talking hands the talking over and stops.
