Hamlet · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The Ghost, and Claudius’s court

The Ghost walks twice before Hamlet is told about it, so the audience knows Denmark is disturbed before it meets the man who will be blamed for disturbing it. Claudius disposes of mourning, marriage and a foreign war in a single speech; by the end of the act Hamlet has a murder accusation from a source he cannot classify, and a plan to behave strangely on purpose.

1.1
Sentries and Horatio see the Ghost and connect it to Denmark’s rearmament against Fortinbras.
1.2
Claudius does the state business fluently; Hamlet will not join the performance, and hears that his father has been seen.
1.3
Laertes leaves for Paris with a lecture from Polonius, who then forbids Ophelia to see Hamlet.
1.4
Hamlet meets the Ghost in the cold and follows it against his friends’ advice.
1.5
Poison in the ear, a command to revenge, an exemption for Gertrude, and an oath of silence.

Act II Polonius’s spies, and the players

Polonius sends a man to France to spread lies about his own son in order to hear the truth about him, which is how the whole court works. Rosencrantz and Guildenstern are hired to do the same to Hamlet. The act ends with Hamlet inventing a better version of the same technique: a play staged to be watched by one man.

2.1
Polonius teaches an informer to invent small vices; Ophelia describes Hamlet’s silent visit.
2.2
The schoolfellows are recruited, the Norwegian crisis is settled offstage, and the players give Hamlet an idea.

Act III The Mousetrap, and the killing of Polonius

Two scenes of arranged watching sit either side of the Mousetrap. Claudius reacts, which Hamlet reads as proof, and then kneels to pray, which Hamlet reads as a bad moment to kill him. Within a hundred lines Hamlet does kill, through a curtain, without looking, and the man behind it is Ophelia’s father.

3.1
Hamlet’s most famous soliloquy is spoken into a trap, and Ophelia is the bait he then turns on.
3.2
Advice to the players, then a murder play; Claudius rises and calls for light.
3.3
Claudius finds he cannot repent; Hamlet postpones the killing on theological grounds.
3.4
Polonius dies behind the arras, and the Ghost returns to a room where only Hamlet can see it.

Act IV Ophelia’s madness, and Laertes’s return

Hamlet is exported. While he is away the play shows what happens to the people he has damaged: Ophelia goes mad and drowns, Laertes comes home with a mob behind him, and Claudius spends one long scene making a young man’s grief into a poisoned foil. The act runs on offstage events reported by letters and messengers.

4.1
Gertrude tells Claudius of the killing and describes her son as mad.
4.2
Hamlet refuses to say where the body is and tells Rosencrantz what a courtier is for.
4.3
Claudius, working in public calm, sends Hamlet to England with a sealed order for his death.
4.4
Fortinbras marches for a worthless patch of Poland; Hamlet measures himself against it.
4.5
Madness set to old tunes and flowers, then Laertes at the door with an armed crowd.
4.6
Horatio receives the letter that returns Hamlet to Denmark by accident.
4.7
Claudius arranges the unbated sword and the poisoned cup; Gertrude reports the drowning.

Act V The graveyard, and the duel

A man who digs graves for a living sings at it, and Hamlet finally talks about death without metaphor, holding the skull of somebody he knew. The duel is a rigged fencing match that goes wrong in every direction; four people die of a plot meant to kill one, and the last Danish voice in the play gives the throne to a Norwegian.

5.1
Gravediggers argue law and precedence; Yorick’s skull; a funeral turns into a fight in the grave.
5.2
Hamlet tells how he rewrote the commission, accepts the match, and the poison finds everyone.
