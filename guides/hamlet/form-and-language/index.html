Hamlet · William Shakespeare

Form and language

Soliloquy

A character alone on stage, thinking aloud to the audience.

Hamlet has seven, and they do different jobs: self-disgust in 1.2, self-accusation in 2.2, abstract argument in 3.1, and in 4.4 a comparison with an army he can see from where he stands.

O, that this too too solid flesh would melt
Thaw and resolve itself into a dew!

Hamlet, 1.2.130

Metatheatre

A play that draws attention to its own nature as performance.

A troupe of actors is hired, given a lecture on acting, and used to conduct an experiment on a spectator, inside a theatre, watched by spectators.

O, what a rogue and peasant slave am I!

Hamlet, 2.2.513

Foils

A character constructed to throw another’s choices into relief by making different ones.

Laertes and Fortinbras are both sons of dead fathers. Laertes comes back from France and storms the palace to avenge Polonius; Fortinbras, checked by his uncle, marches his army against a worthless patch of Poland. The play shows them in consecutive scenes, 4.4 and 4.5, so that Hamlet’s hesitation is measured twice.

How all occasions do inform against me,
And spur my dull revenge!

Hamlet, 4.4.31

Prose against verse

Shifting between metrical speech and prose to mark status, sanity or intimacy.

Hamlet drops into prose with the players, the spies and the gravediggers, and rises into verse when he is alone; Ophelia’s madness arrives as prose broken by snatches of song.

Though this be madness, yet there is method in 't.

Lord Polonius, 2.2.202

Disease imagery

A sustained figurative field that describes a political situation as a body’s illness.

Something is rotten in the state of Denmark, the world is an unweeded garden, and Gertrude’s guilt an ulcer skinned over; the murder itself is a poison poured into a body, so the metaphor and the plot share a mechanism.

Something is rotten in the state of Denmark.

Marcellus, 1.4.91

Hendiadys

Joining two nouns with ‘and’ where grammar would expect one noun modifying another.

‘The book and volume of my brain’ and ‘the expectancy and rose of the fair state’ are typical: the figure doubles a thought instead of completing it, and the language hesitates the way its hero does.
