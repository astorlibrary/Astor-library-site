Antony and Cleopatra · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I Antony in Egypt, recalled to Rome

Rome watches Egypt through other people’s eyes and does not like what it reports. Antony hears of his wife’s death and of Pompey’s fleet, decides to leave, and discovers that leaving Cleopatra is a scene she intends to direct. Caesar meanwhile sets out the charge sheet in private.

1.1
Philo tells a fellow officer what to look for, and the lovers walk on and perform exactly it.
1.2
A soothsayer reads the women’s palms; Antony learns that Fulvia is dead and resolves to break his Egyptian fetters.
1.3
Cleopatra stages illness, accusation and forgiveness in one scene and lets Antony go on her own terms.
1.4
Caesar itemises Antony’s failings to Lepidus, then praises the soldier Antony once was when he needs him back.
1.5
Cleopatra fills the time with opium, memory and messengers, and dismisses her younger self as salad days.

Act II A marriage, a treaty and a drunken galley

The triumvirs patch their quarrel with Antony’s marriage to Caesar’s sister, and Enobarbus tells the Romans what they have just bargained against. Pompey is bought off at Misenum, and the peace is celebrated by three rulers of the world dancing on a boat while a pirate offers to cut the cable.

2.1
Pompey counts on Antony staying in Egypt, and immediately hears that he is on his way to Rome.
2.2
Antony agrees to marry Octavia; minutes later Enobarbus describes Cleopatra on the Cydnus to the men who arranged it.
2.3
Told that his guardian spirit is always beaten by Caesar’s, Antony decides to go back to Egypt.
2.4
Lepidus and the others arrange to meet at Mount Misenum.
2.5
Cleopatra hales the bringer of the marriage news up and down, then calls him back to describe Octavia.
2.6
Pompey accepts Sicily and Sardinia; Enobarbus and Menas measure each other in prose afterwards.
2.7
Menas offers to cut the cable and make Pompey lord of the world; Pompey wishes it had been done without asking.

Act III Actium, and Cleopatra’s flight

The alliance dissolves in short scenes and reported news: Lepidus is deposed, Octavia goes home to plead with her brother, Antony insists on fighting at sea and then follows Cleopatra’s fleeing sails. Defeat is immediately fought over as a story, and Enobarbus begins to calculate what loyalty to a falling man is worth.

3.1
A subordinate wins a victory and explains why winning too well is bad for a career.
3.2
Enobarbus and Agrippa mock the triumvirs’ tears while the marriage is sealed.
3.3
Cleopatra cross-examines the messenger until Octavia is short, low-voiced and dull.
3.4
Antony lists Caesar’s slights; Octavia asks to mediate between husband and brother.
3.5
Eros reports that the third triumvir has been used and then imprisoned.
3.6
Antony’s public enthronement of Cleopatra and her children gives Rome its pretext; Octavia arrives without ceremony.
3.7
Every soldier argues for land; Antony fights by sea because Caesar dares him to.
3.8
Caesar tells Taurus not to engage on land until the sea is decided.
3.9
Antony sets his squadrons where he can see the battle.
3.10
Enobarbus and Scarus watch Cleopatra’s sixty ships turn and Antony’s flagship follow.
3.11
Antony tells his followers to take his treasure and go, then forgives Cleopatra in the same breath as blaming her.
3.12
Antony’s ambassador is a tutor; Caesar refuses his terms and sends Thidias to work on Cleopatra.
3.13
Antony has Caesar’s envoy flogged for kissing Cleopatra’s hand, calls for another supper, and Enobarbus decides to leave.

Act IV A victory, Enobarbus’s desertion, and Antony’s death

The night before Antony wins a day on land, the music of a god leaving him is heard under the stage; the sea fight that follows loses him the rest. Enobarbus dies of his own defection. Told a false report of Cleopatra’s death, Antony asks Eros to kill him, botches the attempt himself, and is hauled up into the monument to die by degrees.

4.1
Antony’s challenge to single combat is dismissed as the noise of a man out of options.
4.2
Antony makes his servants weep by thanking them as though tonight were the end.
4.3
Soldiers on watch hear hautboys beneath them and take it for Hercules leaving Antony.
4.4
Cleopatra helps buckle his armour and is told she is a better squire than she knows.
4.5
Antony hears of the desertion and sends his treasure after him with a kind message.
4.6
Caesar puts the deserters in the vanguard; Enobarbus receives the treasure and turns away to die.
4.7
Scarus fights with new wounds and Antony’s forces drive Caesar back to camp.
4.8
Antony presents the wounded Scarus to Cleopatra and asks the city to make its armour ring.
4.9
Enobarbus asks the moon to witness his repentance and dies before the watch can speak to him.
4.10
Antony resolves to fight by water once more.
4.11
Caesar tells his army to hold the valleys while the fleets meet.
4.12
The Egyptian ships surrender without a fight and Antony calls Cleopatra a triple-turn’d whore.
4.13
Cleopatra takes refuge and sends word that she is dead, to see what it does.
4.14
Antony watches his shape dissolve like cloud, orders Eros to kill him, and falls on his own sword when Eros will not.
4.15
The dying Antony is lifted to Cleopatra, gives advice she refuses to take, and she resolves on a Roman death.

Act V Caesar’s victory, and Cleopatra’s death

Caesar has won and needs Cleopatra alive to walk in Rome. The last act is a negotiation conducted by both sides in bad faith, until Cleopatra decides that a basket of figs is a better ending than a procession. She dies dressed for a coronation, and Caesar is left arranging the funeral of a story he did not write.

5.1
Caesar weeps for the rival he has just destroyed and in the next breath asks how to stop Cleopatra killing herself.
5.2
Cleopatra is taken, lies to Caesar about her treasure, dreams Antony into a colossus and dies by the asp.
