White Fang · Jack London

Context

Dates

1876
Jack London born in San Francisco He grew up poor around Oakland and the bay, the city whose slums he gives to Jim Hall.
1897
London goes north to the Klondike He wintered in the Yukon in the first season of the rush and came home the following summer with scurvy and notebooks, and no gold.
1898
The novel’s only date: Fort Yukon, summer 1898 White Fang reaches Fort Yukon in the summer of 1898, with thousands of gold-hunters going upriver to Dawson: the only calendar date in the book.
1903
The Call of the Wild published Its success fixed London’s public identity and created the appetite for a second dog book.
1905
London buys land at Glen Ellen The beginning of the Sonoma ranch where he lived for the rest of his life; the novel’s Californian chapters were written by a man settling into the country himself.
1906
White Fang serialised and published Six monthly instalments in Outing from May to October, then the Macmillan book with colour plates by Charles Livingston Bull.
1907
Roosevelt attacks the ‘nature fakers’ The President, in a magazine interview, singled out the fight between the bulldog and the wolf-dog as the kind of thing no naturalist could believe.
1908
London replies in ‘The Other Animals’ Published in Collier’s in September, the essay defends his dogs as creatures of instinct and simple reasoning, and denies that he gave them human thought.
1916
London dies at Glen Ellen He was forty, and had published some fifty books in seventeen years.
1925
First film adaptation A silent feature built around the dog star Strongheart, beginning a long line of screen versions.
1946
A Soviet film version London’s standing as a socialist author made him one of the most widely read Americans in the Soviet Union, and the novel was filmed there soon after the war.
1991
The Disney live-action film It enlarges the human story, gives the dog a young prospector for a companion and leaves out California and Jim Hall altogether.
2018
An animated feature A French-made animated version told largely from the animal’s side, released internationally by a streaming service.

Places

Mackenzie River
The country of Grey Beaver’s people. The cub is born beside a small stream that flows into it in summer, and the camp’s canoes and sleds move along it.
Great Slave Lake
Where Three Eagles is bound when he takes Kiche aboard his canoe in payment of Grey Beaver’s debt.
Fort Yukon, Alaska
The old Hudson’s Bay Company post at the mouth of the Porcupine, just under the Arctic Circle, where White Fang sees his first white men and is sold for whisky.
Dawson City, Yukon
Where the Fighting Wolf is exhibited in a cage for fifty cents in gold dust and fought in the woods outside town, out of sight of the mounted police.
San Francisco
The landing place from the North, all cable cars and towering buildings, and the slum in which Jim Hall was a child.
Santa Clara Valley, California
Sierra Vista, Judge Scott’s estate, lies here, in the same valley as the Judge Miller’s place from which Buck was stolen in the earlier book.
San Quentin State Prison
Where Jim Hall spends three years in the incorrigible cell, an iron tomb, before killing three guards and escaping.
