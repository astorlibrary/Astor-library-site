White Fang · Jack London

What happens

Part 1 The Wild

Two men, six dogs and a coffin are followed down a frozen river by a starving wolf pack that takes the dogs one by one, lured out by a she-wolf who knows the ways of camps. White Fang is not yet born. The part exists to show the Wild from the position of the meat, so that every later chapter about a hunting cub is read by someone who has already been hunted.

Chapter 1
Bill and Henry haul the body of Lord Alfred towards Fort McGurry; Bill feeds seven dogs where there should be six, and in the morning Fatty is gone.
Chapter 2
Frog and Spanker are taken, the she-wolf shows herself in daylight and decoys One Ear from the traces, and Bill goes after her with three cartridges and does not come back.
Chapter 3
Henry, alone with two dogs, slings the coffin into a tree and holds the pack off from inside a ring of fire until a party of men finds him asleep in the ashes.

Part 2 Born of the Wild

The pack breaks up, old One Eye wins the she-wolf by killing his rivals, and a litter is born in a cave above a stream. Famine leaves one grey cub alive. He learns fear before he has met anything to be afraid of, tumbles out through the wall of light into a world of ptarmigan, hawks and weasels, and arrives at a law in three words.

Chapter 1
The famine ends, the pack divides, and One Eye and a young leader together kill an ambitious three-year-old before One Eye kills the young leader as well.
Chapter 2
The she-wolf finds a cave and whelps; One Eye waits out a porcupine and a lynx and carries home what the lynx leaves.
Chapter 3
The one grey cub of the litter takes the cave mouth for a wall of light; a second famine kills his brothers and sisters, and the lynx kills his father.
Chapter 4
He falls through the wall, eats a nest of ptarmigan chicks, is beaten by the hen, nearly drowned in a stream and nearly killed by a weasel.
Chapter 5
The she-wolf robs the lynx’s lair, the lynx comes for the cave, and mother and cub kill her between them; the cub draws the conclusion.

Part 3 The Gods of the Wild

The cub meets five Indians on the way to the stream, his mother crawls to them at the sound of her name, and he is named and owned in a single speech. In Grey Beaver’s camp he learns clubs, fire, laughter, property and the sled, is made an outcast by the puppy pack under Lip-lip, and comes back of his own accord when he might have stayed wild.

Chapter 1
Grey Beaver recognises Kiche, names the cub and claims him; White Fang meets Lip-lip, burns his nose on a fire, and is laughed at.
Chapter 2
Kiche is handed to Three Eagles to settle a debt, and White Fang, swimming after her canoe, takes his first real beating and learns never to bite a god.
Chapter 3
Lip-lip turns every young dog in the camp against him, and he learns to fight without warning and to go for the throat.
Chapter 4
He hides when the camp moves in the autumn, finds that freedom is cold and silent, and runs for more than thirty hours down the river bank to crawl to Grey Beaver’s fire.
Chapter 5
Harnessed in Mit-sah’s team behind a persecuted Lip-lip, he learns what property is, and defends Mit-sah against a gang of boys.
Chapter 6
He faces down old Baseek over a shin-bone, is slashed by a mother who has forgotten him, lives wild through the famine, kills Lip-lip, and goes back to the village.

Part 4 The Superior Gods

Made sled leader and hated for it, he travels with Grey Beaver to Fort Yukon in the summer of 1898 and is sold for whisky to Beauty Smith, who exhibits him as the Fighting Wolf. A bulldog he cannot throw gets him by the throat, and a young mining expert pulls the jaws apart, buys him by force and begins, with great patience, to unmake what the pit has made.

Chapter 1
Running at the head of a team that wants to tear him down, he crosses the Rockies to Fort Yukon and sees his first white men.
Chapter 2
Beauty Smith, the fort’s cook, buys him from a drunk Grey Beaver with bottles; he gnaws free twice and is twice handed back and beaten.
Chapter 3
Caged, goaded and exhibited at Dawson, he fights dogs, wolves and a full-grown lynx, and never loses his feet.
Chapter 4
Tim Keenan’s bulldog Cherokee gets a grip and keeps it; Weedon Scott knocks Beauty Smith down, levers the jaws open with a revolver and pays a hundred and fifty dollars.
Chapter 5
He kills a sled dog and lays open Scott’s hand, and is reprieved because he knows what a rifle is and has the sense to get out of its way.
Chapter 6
Scott’s hand comes down without hurting; liking becomes love; White Fang sickens when Scott goes away, and mauls Beauty Smith when he comes to steal him.

Part 5 The Tame

He breaks through a cabin window to follow Scott aboard the steamer and is taken to the Scott family estate in California, where he has to learn that chickens, cats, children and other dogs are all under the protection of the gods. He pays for his place by killing the convict who comes for the Judge, and ends the book in the sun with puppies climbing on him.

Chapter 1
Locked in the cabin, he goes through the window and is waiting on the deck of the Aurora; Scott gives in.
Chapter 2
San Francisco appals him, a baggage car carries him south, and at Sierra Vista he is met by a deer-hound and by a sheepdog, Collie, who knows a wolf when she sees one.
Chapter 3
He learns the household, kills fifty Leghorn hens in one night, is taught better, and wins Scott a bet against the Judge by sitting an afternoon in the chicken yard.
Chapter 4
He learns to be laughed at, fetches help when Scott’s horse falls and breaks his leg, barking for the second and last time in his life, and runs in the woods with Collie.
Chapter 5
Jim Hall escapes from San Quentin to kill the Judge who sentenced him; White Fang kills him on the stairs, takes three bullets, and is named the Blessed Wolf.
