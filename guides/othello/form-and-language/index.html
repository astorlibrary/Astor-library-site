Othello · William Shakespeare

Form and language

Soliloquy and aside

Speech directed past the other characters, to the audience.

Iago has more of them than anybody, and uses them to recruit: he explains the next move before making it, so the audience watches the trap being built and cannot warn anyone.

I follow him to serve my turn upon him:

Iago, 1.1.42

Dramatic irony

The audience knows something a character on stage does not.

Sustained for four acts by the word ‘honest’. Every time Othello calls Iago honest, the phrase means one thing on stage and another in the auditorium, and the gap is the tension.

I follow him to serve my turn upon him:

Iago, 1.1.42

The stage property

An object handled on stage that accumulates meaning as it changes hands.

The handkerchief passes through five pairs of hands, and Othello never asks any of them how it travelled. Its power is entirely a function of who is holding it and when it is seen: Iago’s argument made portable.

Trifles light as air Are to the jealous confirmations strong As proofs of holy writ:

Iago, 3.3.323

Broken verse

Metrical collapse used to register a mind losing order.

Othello begins in long, balanced periods, collapses in 4.1 into broken prose (“Noses, ears, and lips”), and recovers the grand manner one last time for his final speech.

Reputation, reputation, reputation! O, I have lost my reputation! I have lost the immortal part of myself, and what remains is bestial.

Cassio, 2.3.243

Animal imagery

Language that reduces people to beasts and appetite.

Iago introduces it under Brabantio’s window with rams and horses, and by Act IV Othello has adopted it (“Goats and monkeys!”), which is how the play shows the infection taking.

Even now, now, very now, an old black ram Is tupping your white ewe.

Iago, 1.1.88

Double time

Two incompatible timescales running together, one fast enough for the plot and one long enough for the accusations to be possible.

The Cyprus action takes about thirty-six hours, which leaves Desdemona no opportunity to be unfaithful; yet Othello is sure the act of shame has been committed a thousand times. Audiences do not notice, and that is the trick.
