Othello · William Shakespeare

Characters

Othello

A general in the service of Venice; first appears 1.2

Indispensable to the state and never fully part of it, he is the most controlled speaker in the play until the middle of Act III and one of the least controlled afterwards. He is not credulous in general (his answer to Brabantio before the senate is calm and persuasive); what destroys him is a standard of proof that he hands over to somebody else.

Desdemona

Brabantio’s daughter, Othello’s wife; first appears 1.3

She marries against her father, argues her own case before the Senate, and then finds that every quality she showed in Act I is used against her: constancy becomes persistence, generosity becomes appetite. Her worst mistake is a piece of ordinary decency: pressing Cassio’s suit because she believes he has been wronged.

Iago

Othello’s ensign; first appears 1.1

He gives more reasons for what he does than any other Shakespearean villain and none of them holds: the promotion, the rumour about Emilia, the claim that he too loves Desdemona. What is consistent is method. He almost never lies outright; he times, he hesitates, he declines to say things, and lets other people finish his sentences for him.

Emilia

Iago’s wife, Desdemona’s attendant; first appears 2.1

She hands her husband the handkerchief without asking why, and spends the last act making up for it. Her long speech in 4.3 is the only sustained argument in the play against the sexual double standard, and it is spoken by a woman who has been married to Iago for years and has clearly learned it from him.

Michael Cassio

Othello’s lieutenant; first appears 1.2

Promoted over Iago’s head and entirely unequipped to survive him. He cannot drink, he is courteous to women in a way that can be made to look like something else, and his idea of a disaster is the loss of a reputation. The play never suggests he has done anything wrong, which is why the plot works.

Roderigo

A Venetian gentleman in love with Desdemona; first appears 1.1

Iago’s purse and his rehearsal space. Every technique used on Othello is tried first on Roderigo, who is told to sell his land, follow the wars and wait, and does. He is the only character who accuses Iago to his face before Act V, and he is killed in the dark for it.

Brabantio

A Venetian senator, Desdemona’s father; first appears 1.1

He has entertained Othello at his house for years and still cannot imagine his daughter choosing him, so he reaches for witchcraft. Defeated in the Senate, he leaves with a warning that Iago will be quoting in effect for the rest of the play: a daughter who deceived her father may deceive a husband.

Bianca

A woman of Cyprus, attached to Cassio; first appears 3.4

She is given the handkerchief to copy, brings it back in a jealous temper, and thereby supplies the visible object Othello has been demanding. The play lets her be openly in love and openly angry, and then uses her reputation to discredit her in front of the men who need a witness.
