Othello · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The marriage defended before the Senate

Iago begins the play complaining about promotion and ends it with a plan, and in between a private marriage becomes a matter of state. Brabantio accuses Othello of witchcraft before the Senate; Othello answers with an account of courtship and Desdemona answers with a statement of divided duty. They win, and the reward is a posting to a war.

1.1
Iago and Roderigo wake Brabantio with the most racially loaded language in the play.
1.2
Othello meets an armed party with a joke about dew and rust, and is summoned by the Duke.
1.3
Othello and Desdemona defend the marriage; Cyprus is threatened; Iago tells Roderigo to fill his purse.

Act II Cyprus, and Cassio’s disgrace

The Turkish fleet is destroyed by weather before a shot is fired, which strands a garrison with nothing to do. Iago uses the holiday to get Cassio drunk, start a brawl and have him stripped of his lieutenancy, then offers, as a friend, to help him get it back by asking Desdemona.

2.1
Separate ships arrive through the storm; Iago watches Cassio take Desdemona’s hand.
2.2
A herald announces feasting to celebrate a war that never happened.
2.3
Drink, a stabbing, a cashiered lieutenant, and a piece of advice that starts the plot.

Act III The temptation scene, and the handkerchief

In about five hundred lines Iago moves Othello from ‘I do love thee’ to a demand for a wife’s death, without producing a single fact. The handkerchief drops by accident, is picked up by Emilia and planted; by the end of the act it has stopped being cloth and become a document.

3.1
Cassio arranges his approach to Desdemona through Emilia.
3.2
Six lines of routine business: Othello sends letters to the Senate and goes to look at the fortifications.
3.3
Insinuation, repetition, a dropped handkerchief and a demand for ocular proof.
3.4
Othello asks for it, invents or reports its history, and will not hear about Cassio.

Act IV The overheard conversation, and Othello strikes Desdemona

Iago stages a conversation about Bianca and lets Othello overhear it as a conversation about Desdemona. Othello, who has already fallen into a fit, then strikes his wife in front of a Venetian envoy. The act ends in a bedroom with two women talking honestly about marriage while one of them prepares the bed she will be killed in.

4.1
A staged conversation, the handkerchief produced by the wrong woman, and a blow struck in public.
4.2
Othello questions Emilia, then Desdemona, treating his own house as a brothel.
4.3
Desdemona remembers her mother’s maid; Emilia sets out the case for wives.

Act V Desdemona’s murder, and Emilia’s truth

Roderigo is finally spent and killed; Cassio is wounded in the dark. Othello comes to the bedchamber with a speech about cause and light, and kills his wife as though carrying out a sentence. Emilia takes the whole structure apart in about forty lines, and pays for it.

5.1
Iago’s last improvisation: two attacks in the dark and a witness silenced.
5.2
The killing, Emilia’s testimony, Iago’s silence, and Othello’s last account of himself.
