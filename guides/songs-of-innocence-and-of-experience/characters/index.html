Songs of Innocence and of Experience · William Blake

Characters

The Piper

The singer and writer of the Songs of Innocence; first appears Introduction (Innocence)

Piping down the valleys, this shepherd-musician is pictured on the frontispiece with a child floating above him. At the child’s request he plays a song about a Lamb, sings it, and then writes his songs in a book with a pen made from a hollow reed, so that every child may hear them. The staining of the clear water with ink is often read as the first loss of innocence.

The child on the cloud

The Piper’s visionary listener; first appears Introduction (Innocence)

A laughing child who appears on a cloud and asks the Piper to pipe a song about a Lamb, weeps with joy to hear it, tells him to write it down, and vanishes. The child is both audience and muse for the Songs of Innocence.

The Bard

The prophetic voice of the Songs of Experience; first appears Introduction (Experience)

Poet and prophet, he sees present, past and future and has heard the Holy Word that walked among the ancient trees in Eden. In the Introduction to Experience he calls the lapsed soul and the fallen Earth to arise before daybreak. As the Ancient Bard he invites youth to see the opening morn, where doubt and reason’s clouds have fled.

Earth

The fallen, chained Earth who answers the Bard; first appears Earth’s Answer

Raising her head from darkness with her locks covered in grey despair, she answers the Bard’s call with anger. She is imprisoned on a watery shore by Starry Jealousy, a cruel, selfish father of men who makes love hide in darkness, and she asks him to break the heavy chain that binds free love.

The little black boy

Speaker of The Little Black Boy; first appears The Little Black Boy

Born in the southern wild, he has learned from his mother that God lives in the sun and that black bodies are only a cloud that will vanish when souls have learned to bear the beams of love. He imagines shading the English boy from God’s heat until he can bear it, so that the white boy will love him at last. His hope and his wish to be loved are among the most debated lines in the book.

The little black boy’s mother

The teacher in The Little Black Boy; first appears The Little Black Boy

She sits with her son under a tree before the heat of day and teaches him that God gives his light and heat away, that we are put on earth to learn to bear the beams of love, and that black bodies and sunburnt faces are a cloud, like a shady grove.

Tom Dacre’s friend

Speaker of The Chimney Sweeper in Innocence; first appears The Chimney Sweeper (Innocence)

A child sold by his father before he could say sweep, so that he could only cry weep! He comforts little Tom Dacre when his curly head is shaved, tells Tom’s dream of an angel freeing the sweepers, and ends with the lesson that if all do their duty they need not fear harm, a moral the poem leaves the reader to judge.

Tom Dacre

A little chimney sweeper; first appears The Chimney Sweeper (Innocence)

A small boy who cries when his head, curled like a lamb’s back, is shaved. That night he dreams that thousands of sweepers locked in coffins of black are freed by an angel with a bright key, to run down a green plain and wash in a river. He wakes happy and warm on a cold morning.

The sweep in the snow

Speaker of The Chimney Sweeper in Experience; first appears The Chimney Sweeper (Experience)

A little black thing among the snow crying weep! in notes of woe. His parents have gone to church to pray; because he was happy on the heath they clothed him in the clothes of death. Because he still dances and sings they think they have done him no injury, and have gone to praise God and his Priest and King, who make up a heaven of our misery.

The nurse of Innocence

Speaker of Nurse’s Song in Innocence; first appears Nurse’s Song (Innocence)

Her heart is at rest while the children laugh on the green. When the sun goes down she calls them home, and when they beg to play while it is still light she lets them play until the light fades.

The nurse of Experience

Speaker of Nurse’s Song in Experience; first appears Nurse’s Song (Experience)

Hearing whisperings in the dale, she remembers her own youth and her face turns green and pale. She calls the children home in the same words as her counterpart, and then tells them their spring and day are wasted in play, their winter and night in disguise.

The two-day-old infant

The baby of Infant Joy; first appears Infant Joy

Only two days old and without a name, this baby answers its mother that it is happy and its name is Joy. The poem is a dialogue of blessing.

The struggling infant

The baby of Infant Sorrow; first appears Infant Sorrow

Born while its mother groans and its father weeps, it leaps into a dangerous world like a fiend hid in a cloud, struggles against its swaddling bands, and decides to sulk upon its mother’s breast. It is Infant Joy seen from the other side.

The Clod of Clay

The first singer in The Clod and the Pebble; first appears The Clod and the Pebble

Trodden by cattle’s feet, the clod sings that love seeks not itself to please but gives its ease for another and builds a Heaven in Hell’s despair.

The Pebble

The second singer in The Clod and the Pebble; first appears The Clod and the Pebble

Lying in the brook, it warbles that love seeks only self to please, binds another to its delight, and builds a Hell in Heaven’s despite. The poem gives the two views equal stanzas and no verdict.

The little vagabond

Speaker of The Little Vagabond; first appears The Little Vagabond

Poor and cold, this child tells his mother that the church is cold and the ale-house warm, and that if the church gave ale and a pleasant fire the congregation would sing and pray all day, and God would rejoice like a father and make peace with the Devil and the Barrel.
