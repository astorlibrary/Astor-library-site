Songs of Innocence and of Experience · William Blake

What happens

Songs of Innocence The Piper’s songs

A Piper, asked by a child on a cloud for a song about a Lamb, pipes, sings and then writes his songs in a book for every child to hear. The first songs are pastoral and joyful: a shepherd watching his flock, children playing on an echoing green until the sun goes down, a child asking a lamb who made it, a laughing song, a spring song, and a newborn baby who calls itself Joy.

Introduction
The child on the cloud, the song about a Lamb, and a pen made from a hollow reed.
The Lamb
A child’s catechism: the maker of the lamb calls himself a Lamb.
The Ecchoing Green; Spring; Laughing Song
Old John under the oak, a lamb that licks a child’s neck, and a chorus of Ha, Ha, He!
Infant Joy
A baby without a name who calls itself Joy.

Songs of Innocence Children and their guardians

Some Songs of Innocence already look at children in the care of adults. A little black boy repeats his mother’s lesson that his black body is a cloud that will vanish. A small chimney sweeper, sold by his father, tells how his friend Tom Dacre dreamed of an angel who freed the sweepers from coffins of black. Charity children flow into St Paul’s like Thames waters, a nurse lets the children play until dark, and a lost boy is led home by God.

The Little Black Boy
A mother’s lesson under a tree, and a boy who will shade the English child from the heat.
The Chimney Sweeper (Innocence)
Tom Dacre’s shaved head, a dream of coffins of black, and an angel with a bright key.
Holy Thursday (Innocence)
Thousands of children in red and blue and green, and their aged guardians.
Nurse’s Song (Innocence); The Little Boy Lost and Found
Play until the light fades, and a child led through the fen by God in white.

Songs of Innocence Divine care

The last group of Innocence imagines a world held together by love. Mercy, Pity, Peace and Love are God, and also the human form. Angels move through the night guarding the sleeping animals, and the lion weeps tears of gold. A glow-worm lights a lost ant home, and the maker of all sits by every cradle and weeps with every weeping child.

The Divine Image
The human form divine, in heathen, Turk or Jew.
Night
Angels by every bed, and a lion guarding the fold.
A Cradle Song; A Dream; On Another’s Sorrow
A mother’s lullaby, a lost emmet, and a God who becomes a man of woe.

Songs of Experience The Bard and Earth

Experience opens with a prophetic Bard calling the lapsed soul and the fallen Earth to return. Earth answers that she is chained by a jealous, selfish father of men who binds free love. A Clod of Clay and a Pebble sing two opposite songs about love. Lyca, a seven-year-old lost in the desert, is carried by lions to their caves, and the Voice of the Ancient Bard calls youth to see the opening morn.

Introduction (Experience)
The Holy Word among the ancient trees, and Earth called to arise.
Earth’s Answer
Starry Jealousy, a heavy chain, and free Love bound.
The Clod and the Pebble
A Heaven in Hell’s despair, and a Hell in Heaven’s despite.
The Little Girl Lost and Found; The Voice of the Ancient Bard
A child asleep in the desert, a lion’s palace, and the image of truth new-born.

Songs of Experience The answering poems

Several Songs of Experience take the title or situation of a song of Innocence and answer it. Holy Thursday asks how children fed with a cold and usurous hand can be holy. The Chimney Sweeper’s parents have gone to church while he cries in the snow. The nurse’s face turns green and pale as she watches children play. A newborn leaps into a dangerous world and sulks on its mother’s breast. The Human Abstract shows pity itself depending on poverty.

Holy Thursday (Experience)
Babes reduced to misery in a rich and fruitful land.
The Chimney Sweeper (Experience)
A child clothed in the clothes of death, and God, Priest and King.
Nurse’s Song (Experience); Infant Sorrow
A nurse’s bitter memory, and a baby like a fiend hid in a cloud.
The Human Abstract; A Divine Image
Pity that needs the poor, and Cruelty with a human heart.

Songs of Experience Love, jealousy and the garden

Many Songs of Experience show love damaged by secrecy, jealousy and prohibition. An invisible worm destroys a rose with its dark secret love. A garden of love has become a chapel with Thou shalt not written over the door and graves where the flowers were. A sunflower longs for the sun, a girl is punished for loving in the open, and a man waters his secret anger until it bears a poisoned apple.

The Sick Rose
Crimson joy, and dark secret love.
The Garden of Love
A chapel on the green, and priests binding joys and desires.
A Poison Tree
Anger watered with tears and sunned with smiles.
Ah! Sun-flower; My Pretty Rose Tree; The Lilly; A Little Girl Lost; To Tirzah
A sweet golden clime, a thorny rose, and love called a crime.

Songs of Experience The Tyger and London

The Tyger asks what kind of maker could frame a creature of such fearful symmetry, and whether he who made the Lamb made the tiger too. London walks through chartered streets hearing mind-forg’d manacles in every voice, the sweeper’s cry blackening the church and the soldier’s sigh running in blood down palace walls. A little vagabond prefers the warm ale-house to the cold church, and a boy who questions the priest is burned.

The Tyger
Forests of the night, a furnace, an anvil, and stars throwing down their spears.
London
Marks of weakness, marks of woe, and the Marriage hearse.
The Little Vagabond; A Little Boy Lost
A pleasant fire for the soul, and a child burned in a holy place.
