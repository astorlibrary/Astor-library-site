William Blake

Songs of Innocence and of Experience

A study guide

Blake wrote, etched, printed and coloured these short songs himself. The Songs of Innocence of 1789 are sung by a Piper at a child’s request: lambs and shepherds, children at play, a little black boy, a chimney sweeper who dreams of an angel, and a God who shares every sorrow. In 1794 Blake joined them to the darker Songs of Experience under a new title, Shewing the Two Contrary States of the Human Soul. A Bard calls to a fallen Earth, a sweep in the snow blames God, Priest and King, a rose is sick, a garden of love is full of graves, a tiger burns in the forests of the night, and London’s streets echo with mind-forg’d manacles. Many poems answer one another across the two books.

Written About 1784–1794; first published 1789.
