Songs of Innocence and of Experience · William Blake

Context

Dates

1757
William Blake is born On 28 November, at 28 Broad Street, Golden Square, London, the son of a hosier.
1772
Apprenticed to an engraver At fourteen Blake is apprenticed to the engraver James Basire, and spends much of his time drawing tombs in Westminster Abbey.
1787
Robert Blake dies In February Blake’s younger brother and pupil dies; Blake inherits his notebook, and later says Robert’s spirit revealed to him the method of relief etching.
1789
Songs of Innocence One of the first books Blake made by what he called illuminated printing, etched, printed and coloured by hand.
1793
Songs of Experience and the prospectus Blake completes the etching of Songs of Experience and advertises both books, at five shillings each, from 13 Hercules Buildings, Lambeth.
1794
The combined Songs A general title-page joins the two books: Songs of Innocence and of Experience, Shewing the Two Contrary States of the Human Soul.
1818
Blake’s own order Around this time Blake writes out the order in which the Songs ought to be paged and placed; copies still vary.
1824
Charles Lamb hears The Tyger Lamb writes that he has heard of Blake’s poems but never seen them, and calls the tiger poem glorious.
1827
Blake dies On 12 August, at Fountain Court off the Strand; he is buried in Bunhill Fields.
1839
The first printed edition Twelve years after Blake’s death, J. J. Garth Wilkinson prints the Songs in ordinary type for the first time, leaving out The Little Vagabond.
1863
Gilchrist’s Life of Blake Alexander Gilchrist’s biography, finished after his death, revives interest in Blake and reprints plates of the Songs.

Places

28 Broad Street, Soho
Where Blake was born in 1757, the son of a hosier.
13 Hercules Buildings, Lambeth
The address on Blake’s prospectus of 1793, where the Songs of Experience were finished.
St Paul’s Cathedral
The high dome of Paul’s, into which the charity children flow like Thames waters in Holy Thursday.
Fountain Court, Strand
Where Blake lived from 1821 and died in 1827.
Bunhill Fields
The Dissenters’ burial ground where Blake was buried.
