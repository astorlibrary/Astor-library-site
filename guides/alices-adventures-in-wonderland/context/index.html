Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland · Lewis Carroll

Context

Dates

1832
Charles Lutwidge Dodgson born at Daresbury in Cheshire The eldest son of a clergyman, and the third of eleven children.
1855
Dodgson is appointed Mathematical Lecturer at Christ Church, Oxford He held the post for a quarter of a century and lived in college for the rest of his life.
1862
Dodgson first tells the story on a river trip, 4 July Dodgson tells the first version of the story to the three Liddell sisters on a boat trip towards Godstow.
1864
Alice’s Adventures Under Ground given to Alice Liddell Written out and illustrated by Dodgson himself; the published book more than doubles it.
1865
The first printing is withdrawn Tenniel objected to the quality of the reproduction of his illustrations.
1866
The replacement impression reaches readers Dated 1866, this is the effective first published edition.
1871
Through the Looking-Glass appears Published in December and dated 1872, again with Tenniel’s illustrations.
1890
The Nursery ‘Alice’ Carroll’s own shortened version for very young children, with coloured plates.
1897
The last substantial revision of the text New settings incorporate Carroll’s final corrections to the standard wording.
1898
Carroll dies at Guildford He died in January at his sisters’ house, The Chestnuts.
1907
The book comes out of copyright in Britain New illustrated editions followed straight away, among them Arthur Rackham’s.
1948
The Under Ground manuscript comes to the British Museum Bought by American subscribers and presented to Britain; it is now British Library Add MS 46700.
1951
Disney’s animated Alice in Wonderland It folds material from Through the Looking-Glass into Wonderland and fixes the book’s popular image for a century.

Places

Christ Church, Oxford
Dodgson’s college for the whole of his adult life, and the Liddells’ home in the Deanery.
Folly Bridge, Oxford
Where the boat was hired for the excursion of 4 July 1862.
Godstow
The upstream destination of that afternoon’s row, where the party took tea on the bank.
Daresbury, Cheshire
Carroll’s birthplace, and the county that supplies the Cheshire Cat’s grin.
Guildford
The Chestnuts, his sisters’ house, where he died in January 1898.
