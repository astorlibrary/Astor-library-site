Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland · Lewis Carroll

Form and language

Nonsense logic

Reasoning that is formally valid but proceeds from premises nobody has agreed to.

The Cat’s proof that it is mad, and its directions to Alice, are both watertight; the trouble is entirely in the starting point.

At last the Dodo said, “everybody has won, and all must have prizes.”

Chapter III

Parody

Reproducing the shape of a known text while emptying or reversing its content.

Alice’s recitations bend improving verse into crocodiles and lobsters, and the joke works even for readers who no longer know the originals, because the failure lies in Alice’s memory.

“Speak English!” said the Eaglet. “I don’t know the meaning of half those long words, and, what’s more, I don’t believe you do either!”

Chapter III

Puns and literalism

Treating a word’s other sense, or its plainest sense, as the one that was meant.

A long tale becomes a long tail, a school tortoise taught us, and Alice’s ‘I can’t take more’ is answered with the impossibility of taking less than nothing.

“Curiouser and curiouser!” cried Alice (she was so much surprised, that for the moment she quite forgot how to speak good English)

Chapter II

Free indirect thought

Narration that carries a character’s own phrasing without announcing it as speech.

Alice’s judgements leak into the narrator’s sentences: the “poor little thing” who sits down and cries is being pitied by the narrator and by herself at the same moment.

“and what is the use of a book,” thought Alice “without pictures or conversations?”

Chapter I

Dream frame

A narrative enclosed by a falling asleep and a waking.

The frame ends the book without explaining it: the sister’s second dream repeats Wonderland on purpose, which is not what waking from a nightmare normally does.

Comic repetition

A phrase or event repeated until the repetition itself becomes the joke.

“Down, down, down”, the Queen’s standing order for beheadings and the Dormouse’s treacle all work by recurrence more than by any single funny line.

“Do cats eat bats? Do cats eat bats?” and sometimes, “Do bats eat cats?”
