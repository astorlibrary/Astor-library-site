Books 1–4 Telemachus’s journey

The hero of the poem does not appear for four books. Instead we get the hole he has left: a house being eaten by his wife’s suitors, and a son of twenty who has never had to act. Athene sends Telemachus abroad to ask after his father, and what he brings back is less news than an education in how a great house ought to be run.

Book 1
Zeus complains that mortals blame the gods for their own folly; Athene, disguised as Mentes, stirs Telemachus to act.Key lines “Tell me, O Muse, of that ingenious hero…” · “See now, how men lay blame upon us…” · “Go, then, within the house and busy yourself…”
Book 2
Telemachus denounces the suitors in public, Antinous tells the story of Penelope’s web, and the boy sails by night.Key line “we could see her working on her great…”
Book 3
Nestor receives the strangers with sacrifice and talk, and tells how the Greek fleet broke up after Troy.
Book 4
Husband and wife tell rival stories about Odysseus at Troy; in Ithaca the suitors plan to murder Telemachus on his way home.

Books 5–8 From Calypso’s island to the Phaeacian court

Odysseus enters his own poem weeping on a beach, refuses immortality, builds a raft, is wrecked by Poseidon and crawls ashore naked among strangers. The Phaeacians test the rules of hospitality from the host’s side, and their blind bard sings the Trojan war back to a guest who turns out to have been in it.

Book 5
Hermes orders Calypso to release Odysseus; he turns down eternal life, sails for seventeen days and is wrecked within sight of land.Key line “I am quite aware that my wife Penelope…”
Book 6
A princess doing the laundry meets a naked castaway, who talks his way to safety with a speech about marriage.Key line “there is nothing better in this world than…”
Book 7
Odysseus is received as a suppliant and promised a ship home before anybody has asked his name.
Book 8
Demodocus sings of Troy and of Ares and Aphrodite; the guest weeps twice, and Alcinous finally asks who he is.Key line “He wept as a woman weeps when she…”

Books 9–12 The wanderings, told by the wanderer

Everything most readers think of as the Odyssey is here: the Cyclops, Circe, the dead, the Sirens and Scylla. All of it is narrated by Odysseus himself, after dinner, to hosts who are about to give him presents. He leaves Troy with twelve ships and ends the story alone, and the poem leaves the reader to decide how much of the account to take on trust.

Book 9
Odysseus blinds Polyphemus under the name Noman, then shouts his real name from the ship and earns Poseidon’s curse.Key lines “I am Ulysses son of Laertes…” · “Cyclops, you ask my name and I will…” · “Cyclops, if any one asks you who it…”
Book 10
The bag of winds is opened in sight of home; eleven ships are destroyed; Circe turns men to pigs and then keeps them a year.
Book 11
Tiresias prophesies the way home and the journey after it; Odysseus meets his mother, Agamemnon and Achilles.Key lines “you must take a well made oar and…” · “Thrice I sprang towards her and tried to…” · “I would rather be a paid servant in…”
Book 12
Odysseus hears the Sirens tied to the mast, loses six men to Scylla, and loses everyone else when the crew eat the Sun’s cattle.Key line “No one ever sailed past us without staying…”

Books 13–16 Odysseus back in Ithaca, in disguise

The Phaeacians put Odysseus ashore asleep, and he wakes not knowing where he is. From here the poem slows right down. Athene turns him into an old beggar, he shelters with the swineherd Eumaeus, and father and son meet in a hut, where the first thing they do after weeping is count the enemy.

Book 13
Odysseus lies to a shepherd boy who is really Athene; she is delighted, and the two of them plan the suitors’ deaths.Key line “that is why I cannot desert you in your afflictions…”
Book 14
Eumaeus feeds a beggar he takes for a stranger and refuses to believe the beggar’s news that his master is coming home.Key line “Stranger, though a still poorer man should come here…”
Book 15
Telemachus leaves Sparta, slips past the ambush, and Eumaeus tells the story of how he was sold as a child.
Book 16
Athene restores Odysseus’s looks for a moment; Telemachus takes him for a god, then believes him, and they plot.Key line “There is no other Ulysses who will come hereafter.”

Books 17–20 The beggar in the hall

Odysseus goes into his own house in rags and is kicked, pelted and laughed at in it. These books are a long audit of loyalty: the dog knows him and dies, the nurse knows him and is silenced, the goatherd and the maids insult him, and Penelope questions him by firelight without, apparently, knowing who he is.

Book 17
The old hound recognises his master from the dung heap; inside, Antinous throws a footstool at the beggar.Key line “As soon as he saw Ulysses standing there…”
Book 18
Odysseus boxes the resident beggar for the suitors’ amusement and warns the one decent suitor, Amphinomus, to leave.
Book 19
Penelope and the beggar talk alone; Eurycleia washes his feet and knows the scar; Penelope announces the contest of the bow.Key lines “as soon as she began washing her master…” · “There are two gates through which these unsubstantial fancies proceed…”
Book 20
A sleepless night, thunder from a clear sky, and the prophet Theoclymenus sees the hall running with blood while the suitors laugh.

3 key lines from Books 17–20

Books 21–24 The bow, the bed and the truce

The beggar strings the bow nobody else can bend, and the hall becomes a battlefield with the doors locked. After the killing comes a second test, and this time Odysseus is the one who has to pass it: Penelope’s trick about the bed. The final book goes down to the dead and out to Laertes’ farm, and stops a civil war by divine order.

Book 21
The suitors fail to string the bow; Odysseus strings it like a lyre and shoots through the twelve axes.Key line “strung it as easily as a skilled bard…”
Book 22
Antinous dies first with a cup at his lips; all the suitors are killed, and twelve maidservants are hanged.Key lines “rejoice in silence; restrain yourself…” · “as thrushes or doves beat against a net…”
Book 23
Penelope will not accept him until he proves he knows how their bed was built; then the night is lengthened for them.Key lines “There was a young olive growing within the…” · “As the sight of land is welcome to…”
Book 24
The suitors’ ghosts reach Hades, Odysseus tests his father in the orchard, and Athene halts the suitors’ kin.Key lines “The fame, therefore, of her virtue shall never die…” · “Ulysses, noble son of Laertes…”
