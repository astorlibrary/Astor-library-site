Homer

The Odyssey

A study guide

A war poem has a sequel about getting home, and it turns out that getting home is the harder job. Odysseus has been gone twenty years; his son has grown up without him, his wife is holding off a hundred and eight suitors with a piece of weaving, and he himself is sitting on a beach crying. The poem spends half its length on the voyage everyone remembers and the other half in one house on one island, where a man dressed as a beggar has to find out who is still loyal to him before anybody finds out who he is. Its hero wins by lying, and the poem admires him for it; the last great test is set by his wife, after all the monsters.

Written Composed in Greek, probably in the late eighth or early seventh century BCE; Samuel Butler’s English prose translation appeared in 1900; first printed 1488.
