The Death of Ivan Ilyich · Leo Tolstoy

Context

Dates

1828
Leo Tolstoy is born at Yasnaya Polyana His family estate near Tula, where he lived for most of his life.
1864
The judicial reforms of Alexander II The statutes of 1864 created courts independent of the administration, with public trials and juries. Ivan Ilych is among the new men recruited to them.
1869
War and Peace completed Published in parts from 1865; Anna Karenina followed in 1875–1877.
1880
The story’s hardest year Ivan Ilych is passed over for promotion, falls into debt, and then obtains a post worth five thousand roubles through a change at the ministry.
1881
Ivan Ilyich Mechnikov dies at Tula A judicial official in Tula and brother of the biologist Ilya Mechnikov. Accounts of his reflections on his deathbed reached Tolstoy through his sister-in-law Tatyana Kuzminskaya.
1882
A Confession Tolstoy’s account of the crisis that made him ask what meaning his life had, written 1879–1882; banned in Russia, it was first printed in Geneva in 1884.
1882
Ivan Ilych dies on 4 February, in the story The date on the black-bordered notice in chapter I.
1884
Tolstoy notes a plan for the death of a judge His diary for 27 April records that he wants to begin something new: either the death of a judge or the notes of a madman.
1886
The Death of Ivan Ilyich is published The manuscript was dated 25 March; the story appeared that year in the twelfth volume of Tolstoy’s Works, published by Sofia Tolstaya.
1927
Heidegger’s Being and Time A footnote in the discussion of death points to Tolstoy’s story as a picture of the collapse of the everyday assumption that dying is what happens to someone else.
1935
The Maudes’ translation in the Oxford Centenary Edition In the volume Iván Ilých and Hadji Murád, with an introduction by Aylmer Maude.
1952
Kurosawa’s Ikiru Akira Kurosawa’s film about a Tokyo official with stomach cancer, which borrows the story’s situation and moves it to post-war Japan.

Places

Yasnaya Polyana
Tolstoy’s estate, where the family spent the summers in the years he wrote the story.
Tula
Where Ivan Ilyich Mechnikov, the judicial official whose death lies behind the story, served and died in 1881.
Khamovniki, Moscow
The Tolstoys’ Moscow house, where the family spent winters from 1882.
Kursk
Where an acquaintance gets into Ivan Ilych’s railway carriage with the news of a change at the ministry that makes his fortune.
St Petersburg
The capital of ministries, where Ivan Ilych goes in 1880 to find a post with a salary of five thousand roubles.
