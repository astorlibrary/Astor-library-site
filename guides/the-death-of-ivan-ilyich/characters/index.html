The Death of Ivan Ilyich · Leo Tolstoy

Characters

Ivan Ilych Golovin

A Member of the Court of Justice, forty-five; first appears Chapter I

The second son of a Petersburg official, capable, cheerful and correct, he did at every stage what people of good position considered right. He is a good judge who never abuses his power but enjoys having it, a husband who manages his marriage like an office, and a man whose greatest pleasure is bridge. Illness strips all this away. He rages, lies to himself, clings to hope, envies the healthy and longs to be pitied like a sick child, and at the very end he pities others and dies without fear.

Praskovya Fedorovna

Ivan Ilych’s wife, née Mikhel; first appears Chapter I

The most attractive girl in the set when Ivan Ilych married her, she became jealous, demanding and quarrelsome once the children came, and husband and wife settled into veiled hostility broken by short truces. In his illness she decides that it is his own fault, wishes him dead and fears losing his salary, and takes charge of doctors and communion. At the funeral she weeps, chooses a grave plot at two hundred roubles, and asks how to get more from the government.

Gerasim

The butler’s young assistant, a peasant; first appears Chapter I

Clean, fresh and cheerful, grown stout on town food, he carries out his master’s excretions without a sign of disgust, lifts him easily, and holds his legs on his shoulders through whole nights. He is the only person who does not lie about Ivan Ilych’s dying. We shall all come to it, he says, so why grudge a little trouble? At the funeral he is still briskly doing what needs to be done.

Peter Ivanovich

Ivan Ilych’s colleague and old schoolfellow; first appears Chapter I

He studied law with Ivan Ilych and considers himself under obligations to him, but his first thought on hearing of the death is a transfer for his brother-in-law. At the funeral he crosses himself in an intermediate direction, struggles with the pouffe, feels a moment of terror that the same could happen to him, reassures himself that it happens only to other people, and gets to his bridge after all.

Schwartz

A colleague, elegant and playful; first appears Chapter I

With his Piccadilly whiskers and slim figure in evening dress he wears an air of elegant solemnity that contrasts with his playfulness. At the funeral his wink says that Ivan Ilych has made a mess of things, not like you and me, and his whole bearing says that a death is no reason to miss an evening’s bridge. During the illness his jocularity reminds Ivan Ilych of what he himself was ten years before.

Lisa

Ivan Ilych’s daughter; first appears Chapter I

Handsome, healthy and in love with the examining magistrate Petrishchev, she is impatient with illness, suffering and death because they interfere with her happiness. She comes in to say goodbye before the theatre in full evening dress, breaks an unbearable silence by looking at her watch, and after he tells her he will soon free them all of himself she asks her mother why they should be tortured. At the funeral she looks at Peter Ivanovich as if he were to blame.

Vasya

Ivan Ilych’s schoolboy son, called Volodya in chapter I; first appears Chapter I

A frightened boy of thirteen or fourteen with dark shadows under his eyes, whose schooling has been a subject of dispute. His father sees that he is the only one besides Gerasim who understands and pities him. At the very end the boy catches his father’s flailing hand and kisses it, and that is the moment Ivan Ilych falls through into the light. The story calls him Volodya (Vladimir) and also Vasya, an inconsistency Tolstoy left in and the translation keeps.

The doctor

The celebrated doctor, and the others who follow him; first appears Chapter IV

He puts on with Ivan Ilych the air of importance Ivan Ilych puts on with prisoners, discusses floating kidney, chronic catarrh and appendicitis, and will not answer the one question that matters. Later the regular doctor arrives fresh and cheerful, warms his hands, and asks what sort of a night the patient has had. Ivan Ilych knows all of it is pretence and submits to it as he used to submit to lawyers’ speeches.
