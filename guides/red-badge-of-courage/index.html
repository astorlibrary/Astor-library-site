Stephen Crane

The Red Badge of Courage

A study guide

A farm boy enlists expecting Greek battles and finds a muddy camp full of rumour. When the fighting comes he holds, then runs, then wanders among the wounded until another fleeing soldier cracks his skull with a rifle butt, and his regiment reads the injury as evidence of courage. Crane, who was born six years after the war ended and had never seen a battle, wrote the whole thing from inside one frightened private’s head and never names the battle, the cause or, for most of the book, the private.

Written 1893–94; first published 1895.
