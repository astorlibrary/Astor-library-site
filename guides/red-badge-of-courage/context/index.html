The Red Badge of Courage · Stephen Crane

Context

Dates

1861
The American Civil War begins The war Crane’s novel is set in, and which he was born too late to see.
1863
The Battle of Chancellorsville Fought in Virginia in early May: confused fighting in woodland, and a Union defeat. Long associated with the novel, though Crane never names it.
1865
The war ends Veterans’ reminiscences, many of them published in magazines Crane read, became a large part of American periodical writing.
1871
Stephen Crane born in Newark, New Jersey The fourteenth child of a Methodist minister, and six years too late for the war he made his name on.
1893
Crane’s Maggie: A Girl of the Streets published Crane’s first novel, printed at his own expense and largely unsold.
1894
A shortened version syndicated in newspapers The story reached its first readers cut down for the press, before any publisher would take the full book.
1895
Published by D. Appleton and Company The book made Crane famous in his early twenties, in Britain as well as in America.
1897
Crane shipwrecked off the Florida coast The sinking of the Commodore, on a gun-running voyage to Cuba, produced ‘The Open Boat’.
1898
Crane reports from Cuba as a war correspondent Reporting the Spanish-American War finally gave him the experience readers had assumed lay behind the novel.
1900
Crane dies at Badenweiler Of tuberculosis, in a German sanatorium, aged twenty-eight.
1951
John Huston’s film With Audie Murphy, one of the most decorated American soldiers of the Second World War, as Henry; the studio cut it heavily before release.

Places

Chancellorsville, Virginia
The battle of May 1863 most often matched to the novel’s circumstances, though the text names nothing.
Fredericksburg, Virginia
The town on the Rappahannock beside which the armies of the novel’s sort of campaign were camped through the winter.
Newark, New Jersey
Crane’s birthplace, 1871.
New York
Where D. Appleton and Company published the book in 1895, and where Crane had been living as a struggling reporter.
Badenweiler, Germany
The spa town where Crane died of tuberculosis in 1900, five years after the novel appeared.
