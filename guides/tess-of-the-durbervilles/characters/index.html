Tess of the d’Urbervilles · Thomas Hardy

Characters

Tess Durbeyfield

A field-woman, sixteen at the opening; first appears Phase the First, Chapter II

Hardy gives her the intelligence of a National-school education and the position of an unskilled labourer, and the gap between the two is where the novel lives. She is judged by everyone she meets on evidence she was never allowed to present, and the subtitle exists because Hardy expected his readers to join in.

Alec d’Urberville

Son of a Stoke family that purchased the name; first appears Phase the First, Chapter V

His ancestry is bought and his power is entirely current: money, a gig, a poultry farm, a mother who cannot see. He converts, preaches, relapses and proposes with the same unembarrassed energy, and blames Tess for each turn.

Angel Clare

A parson’s youngest son, learning farming; first appears Phase the First, Chapter II

He has rejected his father’s church and kept every one of its judgements. The cruelty of the wedding night is something stranger than ordinary hypocrisy: he has truly convinced himself that the woman in front of him is a different person from the one he proposed to.

Joan Durbeyfield

Tess’s mother; first appears Phase the First, Chapter III

She sends her daughter to claim kin in a clean dress and never explains why a clean dress might matter. Hardy is precise about the gap between them: the mother’s world is ballads and a fortune-telling book, the daughter’s is the Sixth Standard, and two hundred years lie between the two.

John Durbeyfield

A haggler of Marlott; first appears Phase the First, Chapter I

He is given a title and no money, and spends the rest of his short life being carried home. His death is the event that empties the cottage, because the lease was on his life: the novel’s cruellest joke is that the ancestry which ruined his daughter also fails to house her.

Marian

A dairymaid at Talbothays, later at Flintcomb-Ash; first appears Phase the Third, Chapter XVII

She loves Angel, loses him without resentment, and is the one who finds Tess work when nobody else will. Her drinking at the starve-acre farm is treated as a fact about the work and no fault in her.

Izz Huett

A dairymaid at Talbothays; first appears Phase the Third, Chapter XVII

Asked by Angel to come to Brazil with him, she agrees, and then tells him the truth about how much Tess loved him, which ends the proposal. It is the most disinterested act in the book and nobody thanks her for it.

Liza-Lu

Tess’s younger sister; first appears Phase the First, Chapter III

She is barely characterised until the last pages, when Tess asks Angel to marry her, a request Hardy neither endorses nor refuses. She walks away from Wintoncester holding his hand, which is as close to a future as the book offers.
