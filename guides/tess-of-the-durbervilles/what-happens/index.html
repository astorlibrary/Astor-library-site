Tess of the d’Urbervilles · Thomas Hardy

What happens

Phase the First The Maiden

A chance meeting on the road tells John Durbeyfield he is a d’Urberville, and the family’s drunken celebration puts Tess in charge of the horse. Prince is killed, the family loses its living, and Tess is sent to claim kin from a household that bought the name.

Chapters I–III
Parson Tringham’s antiquarian courtesy makes a haggler a knight, and the club-walking gives Angel Clare his first, unused sight of Tess.
Chapters IV–V
Tess takes the beehives to market at night, falls asleep, and the mail-cart kills the horse; the guilt sends her to Trantridge.
Chapters VI–XI
Alec installs her in the poultry farm, follows her through the fog after the dance at Chaseborough, and the novel’s central assault is narrated in the dark and in the dark of the prose.

Phase the Second Maiden No More

Tess walks home, refuses Alec’s offer of support, and works the harvest with her child at the field edge. Sorrow is baptised by his mother when the parson is refused and buried in the unconsecrated corner with the drunkards and the stillborn.

Chapters XII–XIII
She goes back to Marlott and asks her mother why she was never warned.
Chapters XIV–XV
The harvest scene, the home baptism, the refused burial, and the two years of silence that follow.

Phase the Third The Rally

At Talbothays the work is skilled, the valley is fertile, and for a while Tess is simply good at her job. Angel Clare, learning dairying before emigrating, watches her across three months of summer and decides that she is Nature itself, which is the beginning of the trouble.

Chapters XVI–XIX
A larger landscape, real skill at the milking, and the harp heard across the garden.
Chapters XX–XXIV
The dairy’s three other milkmaids love Angel too, and the novel does not lose sight of them; Angel takes her in his arms at the milking.

Phase the Fourth The Consequence

Tess refuses Angel repeatedly and then accepts, tries four times to tell him, and finally writes it down. The letter goes under the carpet as well as under the door, and the confession she owes is postponed to the wedding night, where he makes his own first.

Chapters XXV–XXX
Angel’s family, his brothers’ respectability, and the ride to the station where Tess begins and abandons her history.
Chapters XXXI–XXXIV
The note that is never read, the wedding at last, and Angel’s confession of his own forty-eight hours in London.

Phase the Fifth The Woman Pays

Angel hears the same offence he has just confessed and finds it a different thing entirely. He leaves for Brazil; Tess sends money home, is turned away from dairy work, and ends at Flintcomb-Ash pulling swedes in the rain and feeding a threshing machine.

Chapters XXXV–XXXVII
The confession, the sleepwalking, the coffin, and the separation dressed as a temporary arrangement.
Chapters XXXVIII–XLIV
The starve-acre farm, Marian and Izz, and the walk to Emminster that ends with Tess overhearing the Clare brothers and turning back.

Phase the Sixth The Convert

Alec reappears as a ranting preacher and abandons the pulpit within a fortnight of seeing her again, telling her the backsliding is her fault. He follows her to the rick, offers to keep her family, and waits for her father’s death to do the arguing for him.

Chapters XLV–XLVII
The conversion, the pursuit across the uplands, and the confrontation on the threshing rick.
Chapters XLVIII–LII
The unanswered letter, John Durbeyfield’s death, the loss of the cottage, and the night in the churchyard at Kingsbere.

Phase the Seventh Fulfilment

Angel comes back from Brazil thin and altered, and finds Tess installed in a boarding house at Sandbourne under another man’s protection. She kills Alec, the two of them hide for five days in an empty house in the New Forest, and the police take her at dawn on the stone at Stonehenge.

Chapters LIII–LVI
The return, the landlady’s staircase, and the spreading stain on the ceiling below.
Chapters LVII–LIX
The empty house in the New Forest, the sleep on the altar stone, the arrest at sunrise, and the black flag over the city gaol.
