Tess of the d’Urbervilles · Thomas Hardy

Context

Dates

1840
Thomas Hardy born at Higher Bockhampton In a cottage on the edge of the heath, three miles from Dorchester, into a family of builders and masons.
1870
The Elementary Education Act The schooling that gives Tess her Sixth Standard English and opens the two-hundred-year gap Hardy describes between her and her mother.
1874
Far from the Madding Crowd The novel that made Hardy’s name and fixed Wessex as a place readers expected to revisit.
1886
The Mayor of Casterbridge Dorchester under its Wessex name, and the same interest in a man ruined by a single early act.
1891
Serialised in The Graphic Weekly from July to December, with the seduction, the baptism and other material cut or altered for a family illustrated paper.
1891
First book edition Three volumes from James R. Osgood, McIlvaine and Co., restoring the excised material and carrying the subtitle A Pure Woman Faithfully Presented.
1892
One-volume edition A cheaper single volume widens the readership, and Hardy goes on revising the text in later issues.
1895
Jude the Obscure The reception of Hardy’s last novel effectively ended his career as a novelist; he wrote poetry for the next thirty years.
1897
Lorimer Stoddard’s stage version opens in New York Minnie Maddern Fiske played Tess from 2 March, and made the part a major role in the American theatre.
1924
Silent film with Blanche Sweet Blanche Sweet as Tess, Stuart Holmes as Alec and Conrad Nagel as Angel; the film is now considered lost.
1928
Hardy dies His ashes were buried in Westminster Abbey and his heart at Stinsford, which is the most Hardyan fact about him.
1979
Roman Polanski’s Tess Nastassja Kinski in the title role; the film won Academy Awards for cinematography, art direction and costume design.
2008
BBC four-part adaptation Written by David Nicholls and directed by David Blair, with Gemma Arterton as Tess.

Places

Marnhull, Dorset
Hardy’s Marlott, in the Vale of Blackmoor: the club-walking, the cottage, and the night journey with the beehives.
Cranborne Chase
The Chase, one of the few woodlands in England of undoubted primeval date in Hardy’s description, and the place where the novel will not describe what happens.
Dorchester
Casterbridge, the market town Tess is driving towards when the mail-cart kills Prince.
Bournemouth
Sandbourne, a resort of villas and bath-chairs grown up in a generation, where Angel finds Tess in a boarding house called The Herons.
Stonehenge
The heathen temple where Tess sleeps on the altar stone and asks whether Liza-Lu may have her place.
Winchester
Wintoncester, the cathedral city whose gaol flies the black flag in the last paragraph.
