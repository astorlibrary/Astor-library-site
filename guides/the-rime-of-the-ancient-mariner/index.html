Samuel Taylor Coleridge

The Rime of the Ancient Mariner

A study guide

An old sailor stops a man on his way into a wedding and will not let him go. The story he tells has a crime in it that takes two lines and no explanation, a punishment that falls hardest on two hundred men who did not commit it, and a release that arrives while he is not trying for it. It ends with a neat moral about loving all creatures, spoken by a man who is still, decades later, compelled to seize strangers, and heard by one who goes home stunned and skips the party.

Written Planned on a walk in November 1797 and finished by March 1798; revised at intervals until 1834; first published 1798.
