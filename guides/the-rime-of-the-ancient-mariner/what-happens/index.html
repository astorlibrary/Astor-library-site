The Rime of the Ancient Mariner · Samuel Taylor Coleridge

What happens

Part I The guest is stopped, the ship goes south, the bird is shot

The Mariner picks one man out of three and holds him first by the hand and then, more effectively, by the eye. The voyage runs south past the equator into a storm and then into ice, where an albatross arrives, is fed, and seems to bring a wind. The part ends on the Wedding-Guest’s alarm at the teller’s face and the flat confession it draws out.

Part II The crew change their minds, and the wind stops

The shipmates first blame the Mariner for killing the bird of good luck and then, when the fog lifts, approve the killing, which makes them parties to it. The ship bursts into an unvisited sea and is becalmed under a small, bloody sun. Thirst sets in, the water seems to rot and burn, and the crew hang the dead bird round his neck.

Part III The spectre-bark and the game of dice

A sail appears, and the Mariner has to bite his own arm and drink the blood before he can call out. The ship that comes moves without wind, shows the sun through its ribs, and carries Death and a woman named Life-in-Death, who dice for the crew. She wins the Mariner; night falls at one stride; and two hundred men drop dead one by one, each cursing him with a look.

Part IV Seven days alone, and the water-snakes

The Wedding-Guest fears he is talking to a ghost and is told, not very comfortingly, that this body did not drop. Alone among the corpses, unable to pray or die, the Mariner watches the moon go up and then the water-snakes moving in the ship’s shadow. He blesses them without meaning to, finds he can pray, and the albatross falls from his neck into the sea.

Part V Rain, the dead men working, and two voices

Sleep and rain come. The ship moves without a breeze, worked by the bodies of the crew, which at dawn give out sweet sounds that fly to the sun and back. The spirit from the land of mist and snow carries the ship as far as the equator and lets go; the Mariner faints, and overhears two voices agreeing that he has done penance and will do more.

Part VI The trance, the curse again, and the home harbour

The voices explain how the ship is being driven while he lies in a trance. He wakes to find the dead men still staring, until the spell snaps and a private wind carries him home to the same lighthouse, hill and kirk he left. Seraphs stand on the corpses as signal lights, and a boat puts out with the Pilot, his boy and the Hermit.

Part VII The ship sinks, the Hermit asks, and the telling begins

The ship goes down like lead as the boat reaches it. The rescued Mariner terrifies all three rescuers, begs the Hermit to shrieve him, and is asked instead what kind of man he is, which wrenches the tale out of him for the first time. He explains that the agony comes back at uncertain hours, delivers his moral, and goes; the Wedding-Guest turns away from the feast.
