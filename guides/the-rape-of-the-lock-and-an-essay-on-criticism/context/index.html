The Rape of the Lock and An Essay on Criticism · Alexander Pope

Context

Dates

1688
Alexander Pope is born On 21 May, in Lombard Street, London, the son of a Catholic linen draper.
1700
Binfield By this year the family has moved to Binfield in Windsor Forest; as Catholics they are barred from many schools and offices, and Pope largely educates himself.
1705
William Walsh The critic William Walsh befriends the young poet and advises him to aim at correctness.
1709
The Pastorals Pope’s Pastorals appear in Tonson’s Poetical Miscellanies. In his collected works he later dated An Essay on Criticism to this year.
1711
An Essay on Criticism Published without Pope’s name on 15 May. John Dennis replies with an angry pamphlet, and on 20 December Addison praises the Essay in The Spectator.
1711
The lock is cut Robert, Lord Petre, cuts a lock of Arabella Fermor’s hair, and a friend of both families asks Pope for a poem to end the quarrel.
1712
The first Rape of the Lock A version in two cantos appears without Pope’s name in Lintot’s Miscellany in May.
1714
Five cantos The enlarged Rape of the Lock is published separately, with the sylphs and gnomes and a dedication to Arabella Fermor.
1715
The Iliad The first volume of Pope’s translation appears; by the last volume in 1720 the subscriptions have made him independent for life.
1717
Clarissa’s speech Pope adds Clarissa’s speech to the Rape in his collected Works. His father dies in October.
1719
Twickenham Pope takes the lease of a house with five acres beside the Thames at Twickenham, where he lives for the rest of his life.
1728
The Dunciad Pope’s mock-epic on dull writers appears in May, after the Bathos, his prose satire on bad poets.
1744
Pope dies On 30 May at Twickenham, where he is buried near his parents.
1751
Warburton’s edition William Warburton publishes Pope’s Works in nine volumes, with his last corrections and a long commentary on the Essay.

Places

Hampton Court Palace
The palace on the Thames, begun by Wolsey and enlarged by William III, where the Rape’s game of ombre and the cutting of the lock take place.
Lombard Street, London
Where Pope was born in 1688; his father had moved his linen business there.
Binfield, Windsor Forest
The village where Pope grew up and wrote his early poems, including An Essay on Criticism.
Twickenham
Pope’s home from 1719 until his death; he is buried in the parish church.
St James’s Park and the Mall
Where the beau monde strolled, and where the Rape says the new star will be hailed with music.

Critics

Samuel Johnson

“Pope”, in Lives of the Poets (1781)

Johnson thought The Rape of the Lock the most airy, ingenious and delightful of Pope’s poems, and praised the sylphs, a new kind of supernatural machinery that let a trivial quarrel carry the weight of epic.

Cleanth Brooks

“The Case of Miss Arabella Fermor”, in The Well Wrought Urn (1947)

Brooks argued that the poem’s attitude to Belinda is mixed: it mocks her world of cards, cosmetics and quarrels and admires its beauty at the same time, and its irony depends on holding both.
