Twelfth Night · William Shakespeare

Form and language

Song

Sung lyric used to carry mood, comment or structure.

The play is full of music, and Feste’s songs go in one direction: from ‘O mistress mine’ through ‘Come away, death’ to a closing ballad about wind and rain that darkens the happy ending.

If music be the food of love, play on; Give me excess of it, that, surfeiting, The appetite may sicken, and so die.

Orsino, 1.1.1

Speaking in disguise

A character telling the literal truth in a form the listener cannot decode.

Viola’s account of her father’s daughter who never told her love is autobiography delivered to the man it is about, and he takes it as a story.

Whoe'er I woo, myself would be his wife.

Viola, 1.4.40

The gulling scene

A trick staged in front of hidden observers who supply the running commentary.

The box-tree scene puts three men on stage interrupting a fourth as he reads, so that the audience laughs at the victim and at the watchers’ inability to keep quiet.

be not afraid of greatness: some are born great, some achieve greatness, and some have greatness thrust upon 'em.

Malvolio, 2.5.126

Identical twins

A plot device by which one actor’s part is doubled by another body.

The device is as old as Plautus, but these twins are brother and sister, so the resemblance depends on clothes more than nature, and that makes it a point about gender as well as a mechanical convenience.

I am all the daughters of my father's house, And all the brothers too: and yet I know not.

Viola, 2.4.116

The licensed fool

A professional entertainer permitted to say what others may not.

Feste’s wordplay on corruption and sentences is his job more than his temperament; Olivia defends him by saying there is no slander in an allowed fool, and he uses the permission to torment the one man who cannot laugh.

Nothing that is so is so.

Feste, 4.1.7

Prose and verse

Movement between unmetered and metered speech as a signal of register or state.

The kitchen plot is almost all prose and the court scenes almost all verse; Malvolio’s ruin is that he reads a forged letter as if it were a prophecy and then tries to talk like the people upstairs.

Oh, you are sick of self-love, Malvolio, and taste with a distempered appetite.

Olivia, 1.5.75
