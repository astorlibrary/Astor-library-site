Twelfth Night · William Shakespeare

Characters

Viola

A shipwrecked gentlewoman, disguised as Cesario; first appears 1.2

She invents Cesario as a practical measure and then finds the disguise doing things she did not intend: it makes her the confidant of the man she loves and the object of the woman she is sent to persuade. She is the only one who knows who Cesario is, and she can do nothing with the knowledge.

Olivia

A countess of Illyria; first appears 1.5

Her seven-year mourning is a position, and it lasts until somebody argues with her. Once she falls, she is the most decisive person in the play: she declares herself, sends after the messenger and marries a stranger within three days of meeting his face on somebody else.

Orsino

Duke of Illyria; first appears 1.1

He opens the play calling for music and stops it within eight lines, which is his whole character in one gesture. He has never spoken to Olivia; what he loves is the condition of loving, and the play transfers him to Viola with an ease that has troubled readers ever since.

Malvolio

Olivia’s steward; first appears 1.5

Humourless, ambitious and right about the drinking, which makes him unbearable. The trick works because the letter tells him what he already believes; the punishment goes far beyond the joke, and the play lets him leave without being forgiven or forgiving.

Feste

Olivia’s fool; first appears 1.5

He is paid by both households and belongs to neither, which lets him say things nobody else can. He sings the play’s best songs, performs the curate who torments Malvolio, and is left alone at the end to sing about a world where it rains every day.

Sir Toby Belch

Olivia’s kinsman; first appears 1.3

He lives in his niece’s house at her expense and funds his drinking by keeping a rich fool on the premises. He is the engine of the misrule and the first to tire of it: when the duel goes wrong and he is bloodied, he turns on Sir Andrew and marries Maria offstage.

Sir Andrew Aguecheek

A foolish knight, Olivia’s supposed suitor; first appears 1.3

He is being robbed in plain sight and is happy about it. Shakespeare gives him one line of real pathos (“I was adored once too”) and then has him beaten by a stranger and cast off by the man who has been living on his money.

Maria

Olivia’s waiting-gentlewoman; first appears 1.3

She writes the letter, and the letter is a piece of character analysis: she knows exactly what Malvolio wants to hear because she has watched him practise it. Her reward is marriage to Sir Toby, which happens offstage and is reported in a line.

Sebastian

Viola’s twin brother; first appears 2.1

He arrives late, is given money by a man who loves him, and spends his scenes being greeted by strangers who know his name. Handed a countess, he does not question it; he checks that he is not mad, and marries her within the hour.

Antonio

A sea captain, Sebastian’s friend; first appears 2.1

He risks arrest in a hostile city because he cannot stand to be parted from Sebastian, and says so in terms no other character in the play uses about anybody. He is arrested, believes himself betrayed, and at the end is neither punished nor paired: he simply stops speaking.
