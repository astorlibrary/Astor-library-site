Twelfth Night · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The shipwreck, and Viola becomes Cesario

Orsino is in love with the idea of being in love; Olivia has sworn off company for seven years to mourn a brother. Viola, believing her own brother drowned, takes service with the duke as a boy called Cesario and is sent within three days to court the woman her master wants, and does the job well enough to be loved for it herself.

1.1
Orsino calls for a strain, tires of it in eight lines, and hears that Olivia will see nobody.
1.2
Viola asks what country this is, is told her brother may have survived, and decides to serve disguised.
1.3
Sir Toby introduces Sir Andrew as a suitor and as a source of income.
1.4
Cesario is already the duke’s favourite and is sent to Olivia; Viola admits she wants the master.
1.5
Feste talks his way back into favour; Olivia unveils for the messenger and falls in love with him.

Act II A ring, a night of songs, and a forged letter

Olivia sends a ring after Cesario on a pretext, and Viola understands her own position exactly: she loves Orsino, Orsino loves Olivia, Olivia loves a disguise. In the same act Malvolio interrupts a drinking party, and Maria answers him with a piece of handwriting designed to fit the fantasy he already has.

2.1
Antonio, who has saved Sebastian from the sea, follows him into a city where he has enemies.
2.2
Viola works out what Olivia has done and leaves the knot to time.
2.3
A catch, a quarrel with the steward, and Maria’s plan for the letter.
2.4
Orsino discourses on men’s constancy; Viola answers with a sister who never spoke.
2.5
Malvolio reads the letter aloud while three men hide and provide the commentary.

Act III Yellow stockings, a duel and the wrong twin

Olivia abandons discretion and declares herself; Viola can say only that she is not what she is. Malvolio arrives smiling and cross-gartered in front of a woman in mourning and is taken for mad. Sir Toby arranges a duel between two people equally unwilling to fight it, and Antonio walks into the middle of it and is arrested.

3.1
Feste on the abuse of words, and Olivia’s open declaration to Cesario.
3.2
Sir Toby talks Sir Andrew into writing a challenge in a martial hand, with as many lies as the sheet will hold.
3.3
Antonio gives Sebastian his purse and arranges to meet him at an inn.
3.4
The stockings, the dark room, the duel, and Antonio mistaking Viola for her brother.

Act IV A beating, a betrothal and a dark room

Sebastian is attacked in the street by people who think they know him, is proposed to by a woman he has never met, and accepts. Malvolio, shut in the dark, is visited by Feste pretending to be a curate, who tells him the room is full of light. The comedy and the cruelty are running in the same scene.

4.1
Sebastian fights Sir Andrew and Sir Toby; Olivia stops it and takes him home.
4.2
Feste in a false beard argues that there is no darkness but ignorance.
4.3
Sebastian, sure he is not mad, agrees to be betrothed on the spot.

Act V The twins meet

Everything converges: Antonio’s accusation, Olivia’s claim to a husband, Orsino’s threat to kill the boy he loves, and then Sebastian walks on. The knot unties itself in a few dozen lines. Malvolio is released, produces the forged letter, learns the truth and leaves promising revenge, and Feste is left alone to sing about rain.

5.1
The twins meet, three marriages are arranged, Malvolio exits vowing revenge, and the clown sings.
