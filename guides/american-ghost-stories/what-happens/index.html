American Ghost Stories · F. Marion Crawford, Ambrose Bierce, Ellen Glasgow and others

What happens

Crawford, White and Bierce Travellers and strangers

Three stories about men away from home. Crawford’s is an after-dinner yarn about a state-room nobody should sleep in; White’s motorist crashes on a country road at dusk; Bierce’s stranger arrives by stage-coach, takes offence on a hotel porch and agrees to a duel with knives in the dark.

The Upper Berth (1886)
A passenger is warned that on the last three trips whoever slept in 105 went overboard; he wakes to find the porthole open and the cabin smelling of stagnant sea-water.
The House of the Nightmare
A motorist swerves at a stone that seems to have changed sides of the road, wrecks his car, and is met by a silent farm boy with a harelip.
The Middle Toe of the Right Foot (1890)
Three young men set up the duel as a trick on the stranger; next day the sheriff finds him dead of fright in a corner, with footprints in the dust.

Wynne, Peattie and Dawson Houses that come and go

Three stories in which the building itself is in question. In Wynne’s Vermont farmhouse some visitors find a little room with a blue chintz lounge and a pink shell, and others find a china-closet. Peattie’s young bride on a plains ranch can see a neighbour’s house that her husband says is not there. Dawson’s San Francisco is a city whose streets are blocked by houses being moved.

The Little Room (1895)
Two generations visit the same farmhouse and cannot agree whether the little room is there.
The House That Was Not
When the corn is cut, Flora sees a cottage to the west with smoke from its chimney. Bart tells her what happened there and that it burned.
An Itinerant House (1878)
The earliest story here, told among lodgers in early San Francisco, where houses were moved from street to street.

Glasgow and Freeman Women who see what the men deny

Glasgow’s nurse, hired by a surgeon she adores to sit with his wife, keeps seeing the wife’s small daughter, and is then told the child is dead. Freeman’s three sisters sit in the family house where their brother Edward lies dead and watch a shadow on the study wall that looks like him, while their other brother, the doctor who put the death down to gastric trouble, tries to explain it away.

The Shadowy Third (1916)
Miss Randolph sees a little girl with a ball in the Maradick house; the wife believes her husband killed the child.
The Shadows on the Wall (1903)
Whenever a lamp is set on the study table the shadow appears on the wall, and no moving of the furniture shifts it.

Dunbar and Wharton The dead who stay

The collection closes with its two strangest stories. Dunbar’s Shell of Sense is narrated by Frances, a dead wife who stays in her house, watches her husband and her sister fall in love, keeps them apart, and finally brings them together. In Wharton’s Bewitched, the latest story here, three neighbours are summoned through the snow by a farmer’s wife who says her husband is meeting a dead girl at a shack on Lamer’s pond.

The Shell of Sense (1908)
A ghost’s account of her own house, the small wrongs in it, and the jealousy she has to give up.
Bewitched (1925)
Orrin Bosworth, Deacon Hibben and Sylvester Brand hear Mrs Rutledge out; the girl she names is Brand’s dead daughter, Ora.
