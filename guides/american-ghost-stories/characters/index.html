American Ghost Stories · F. Marion Crawford, Ambrose Bierce, Ellen Glasgow and others

Characters

Frances

The narrator of The Shell of Sense, dead and still in her house; first appears The Shell of Sense

She comes back to find the room intolerably unchanged, then notices the small wrongs: windows shut too tight, her work-basket in disorder, her sister at her desk. Jealous, she keeps her husband and her sister apart for months, then makes one last great effort to bring them together.

Theresa

Her sister, living on in the house; first appears The Shell of Sense

She answers the condolence letters at her dead sister’s desk and has never minded disorder. She loves Allan, gives him up for her sister’s sake and prepares to leave the house; the story’s ending depends on her being decent.

Allan

The widower; first appears The Shell of Sense

A sceptic with, in his wife’s words, hard and fast denials of the invisible, who feels a chill in the room he cannot account for. In the night Frances comes to him with a message: don’t let Theresa leave, take her and keep her.

The Glynn sisters

Caroline, Rebecca Ann and Emma (Mrs Brigham), in The Shadows on the Wall; first appears The Shadows on the Wall

Three sisters in black in the family house where their brother Edward lies dead. They suspect their brother Henry and will not say so aloud. Freeman lets their conversation carry the story, and the shadow on the study wall arrives as something noticed over the sewing before it becomes a terror.

Henry Glynn

The surviving brother, a doctor; first appears The Shadows on the Wall

He had words with Edward the night before Edward died, called the death gastric, and saw no need for an examination. When the shadow appears he moves every piece of furniture, then goes at the empty air with his father’s old sword. He leaves for the city and does not come back.

Grossmith, who is Manton

The stranger in The Middle Toe of the Right Foot; first appears The Middle Toe of the Right Foot

He signs the hotel register as Robert Grossmith, takes offence on the porch and accepts a duel with knives in the dark in the old Manton house. The young men who arrange it know he is Manton, who cut the throats of his wife and two children in that house ten years before.

Miss Randolph

The night nurse in The Shadowy Third; first appears The Shadowy Third

Young, Southern, just out of training and half in love with the surgeon who hires her. She sees his wife’s dead daughter more than once and ends the story ready to swear on her deathbed to what she saw.
