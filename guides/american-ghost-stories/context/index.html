American Ghost Stories · F. Marion Crawford, Ambrose Bierce, Ellen Glasgow and others

Context

Dates

1878
Emma Frances Dawson’s ‘An Itinerant House’ published Emma Frances Dawson’s San Francisco story, the earliest in the collection.
1886
F. Marion Crawford’s ‘The Upper Berth’ published Crawford’s story appears in The Broken Shaft, a Christmas annual dated 1886.
1890
Ambrose Bierce’s ‘The Middle Toe of the Right Foot’ published Bierce’s story of the Manton house, later collected in Can Such Things Be? (1893).
1895
Madeline Yale Wynne’s ‘The Little Room’ in Harper’s Madeline Yale Wynne’s story in Harper’s Magazine for August.
1903
Mary Wilkins Freeman collects The Wind in the Rose-bush Freeman collects her supernatural stories, including The Shadows on the Wall, after their run in Everybody’s Magazine.
1905
Olivia Howard Dunbar’s essay on the ghost in American fiction Olivia Howard Dunbar argues that the ghost has declined in American magazine fiction.
1908
Olivia Howard Dunbar’s ‘The Shell of Sense’ in Harper’s Dunbar’s own ghost story, in Harper’s Magazine for December.
1916
Ellen Glasgow’s ‘The Shadowy Third’ in Scribner’s Ellen Glasgow’s nurse’s story, in Scribner’s Magazine.
1917
Dorothy Scarborough’s The Supernatural in Modern English Fiction Dorothy Scarborough publishes The Supernatural in Modern English Fiction.
1921
Dorothy Scarborough’s anthology Famous Modern Ghost Stories Scarborough’s anthology, the copy-text here for Bierce and Dunbar.
1925
Edith Wharton’s ‘Bewitched’ in Pictorial Review Edith Wharton’s story in Pictorial Review, the latest in the book.

Places

New York
Harper’s and Scribner’s, which printed Wynne, Dunbar and Glasgow.
San Francisco
Dawson’s roving houses, and the city where Bierce made his name as a journalist.
Randolph, Massachusetts
Freeman’s birthplace, and the New England of her stories.
Richmond, Virginia
Ellen Glasgow’s city.
Vermont
The farmhouse of Wynne’s Little Room.
The North Atlantic
The crossing on which Crawford’s state-room 105 does its work.
