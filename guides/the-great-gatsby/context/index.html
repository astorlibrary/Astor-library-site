The Great Gatsby · F. Scott Fitzgerald

Context

Dates

1896
F. Scott Fitzgerald born in Saint Paul, Minnesota Named for a distant relative who wrote the words of The Star-Spangled Banner.
1918
Fitzgerald meets Zelda Sayre while stationed in Alabama The courtship, the refusal and the eventual acceptance after his first novel sold all feed the Louisville chapters.
1919
The World Series is fixed The scandal that gives Meyer Wolfshiem his introduction in Chapter 4, and dates the world Gatsby’s money comes from.
1920
National prohibition takes effect, and This Side of Paradise is published The law that creates Gatsby’s business and the book that made his author famous arrive in the same year.
1922
The summer of 1922, in which the novel is set Fitzgerald began planning a third novel that summer in Minnesota, and in October moved to Great Neck on Long Island.
1924
The main draft is written in France A pencil manuscript of more than two hundred and fifty pages, then galleys revised so heavily in Rome that whole chapters changed shape.
1925
The Great Gatsby is published on 10 April Charles Scribner’s Sons, New York. The reviews were mixed and the sales well short of Fitzgerald’s hopes.
1940
Fitzgerald dies in Hollywood He was forty-four, at work on a novel about the film industry, and his reputation was at its lowest.
1945
The Armed Services Editions distribute 155,000 copies A free paperback for service personnel put the novel into more hands in a year than it had reached in twenty.
2021
Works published in 1925 enter the United States public domain The first-edition text became freely available for independent editions on 1 January.

Places

Great Neck, New York
The peninsula of new money that Fitzgerald was living on, and the model for West Egg.
Sands Point, New York
The older, wealthier neck across the bay, usually identified with East Egg and the Buchanans’ house.
The Corona ash dumps, Queens
The municipal ash tip beside the road and railway into Manhattan, generally taken as the valley of ashes.
The Plaza Hotel, Manhattan
Where a suite is taken on the hottest day of the summer and the argument is finally had out.
Louisville, Kentucky
Daisy’s home, the white roadster, and the officer who could not afford to stay.
Saint Paul, Minnesota
Fitzgerald’s birthplace, and the kind of Midwestern city Nick means when he says he is going home.

Critics

T. S. Eliot

Letter to F. Scott Fitzgerald (1925)

Thanking Fitzgerald for a copy, Eliot wrote that he had read it three times and that it seemed to him the first step American fiction had taken since Henry James.

Lionel Trilling

Introduction to The Great Gatsby (New Directions) (1945)

Trilling, writing when Fitzgerald’s reputation was being revived, argued that Gatsby, divided between power and dream, comes to stand for America itself. The essay was reprinted in The Liberal Imagination in 1950.
