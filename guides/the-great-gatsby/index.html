Study guides/The Great Gatsby

F. Scott Fitzgerald

The Great Gatsby

A study guide

A bond salesman rents a cottage next to a mansion whose owner gives parties for strangers, and gradually works out that the parties are addressed to one woman across the bay. Fitzgerald releases the facts about Gatsby in the wrong order on purpose, so that a rumour becomes a host, then a lover, then a poor farm boy called James Gatz. The summer ends with two people dead, a third shot in a swimming pool, and a narrator who has decided that the people who survive are the ones with enough money to leave.

Written 1922–24; first published 1925.
