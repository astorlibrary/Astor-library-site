The Great Gatsby · F. Scott Fitzgerald

What happens

Chapter 1 West Egg, East Egg and the green light

Nick Carraway introduces himself as a suspended judge, rents a small house in West Egg, and dines across the bay with his cousin Daisy and her husband Tom, who is reading about the decline of the white race and taking a telephone call from his mistress. On the way home he sees his neighbour on the lawn, reaching towards a green light.

Chapter 1
Daisy’s voice, Jordan’s boredom, Tom’s theories, and a telephone that will not stop ringing.

Chapter 2 The valley of ashes

The road from Long Island to New York runs through a landscape made of the city’s waste, watched over by a faded oculist’s hoarding. Tom takes Nick to a garage there, collects Myrtle Wilson from her husband’s business, and continues to a flat in Manhattan where the party ends towards midnight with a broken nose.

Chapter 2
A party of six, a dog bought on the street, and Tom breaking Myrtle’s nose for saying Daisy’s name.

Chapter 3 Gatsby’s party

Nick is one of the few guests who has been invited. He hears three contradictory biographies of Gatsby before meeting him, finds a drunk in the library marvelling that the books are real, and leaves at the end of a night that closes with a car in a ditch and a driver who does not understand that wheels are involved.

Chapter 3
A man in enormous spectacles establishes that the books have pages, which he treats as evidence of thoroughness.

Chapter 4 Gatsby’s story, Wolfshiem, and Jordan’s account

Driving into the city, Gatsby produces documents: a medal from Montenegro, a photograph from Oxford. Lunch introduces Meyer Wolfshiem, who fixed a World Series. Then Jordan tells Nick, in the Plaza’s tea garden, the story of a white roadster in Louisville in 1917, and what the parties have actually been for.

Chapter 4
Daisy in 1917, a letter the night before her wedding, and a request relayed from the man next door.

Chapter 5 Tea in the rain

Gatsby has Nick’s lawn cut and fills his house with flowers for a meeting arranged four chapters and five years in advance. He knocks over a clock, recovers, and takes Daisy through the mansion, throwing shirts onto a table until she cries into them. The rain stops on cue. The green light becomes a light on a dock.

Chapter 5
The house is exhibited as evidence; the tour ends with Daisy weeping over imported linen.

Chapter 6 James Gatz of North Dakota

The history the reader has been waiting for arrives at last: a boy who rowed out to a millionaire’s yacht and changed his name at seventeen. Daisy comes to a party and does not enjoy it. Gatsby explains to Nick that Daisy must tell Tom she never loved him, and Nick suggests that the past cannot be repeated.

Chapter 6
A name is invented on Lake Superior, and an inheritance of twenty-five thousand dollars is taken away by a lawyer.

Chapter 7 The Plaza, and Myrtle’s death

The parties stop and the servants are replaced. Five people drive to the Plaza in the heat and Gatsby asks Daisy to erase four years of her marriage; she cannot say the sentence. On the way back a yellow car kills Myrtle Wilson at the garage, and Nick learns who was driving. Gatsby stands in the shrubbery all night watching nothing.

Chapter 7
Tom takes the argument apart with the one fact Gatsby cannot dispose of: Daisy’s past is her own.
Chapter 7
The exchanged cars make the wrong person responsible, which is how responsibility works in this book.

Chapter 8 Gatsby’s death

Nick hears the Louisville story from Gatsby himself, tells him he is worth the whole lot of them, and goes to work. Gatsby waits for a call, swims for the first time all summer, and is shot in the pool by George Wilson, whom Tom has told whose car it was.

Chapter 8
A gardener’s plan to drain the pool is postponed by a day, and the day is the last one.

Chapter 9 The funeral, and Nick’s departure

Nobody comes. Wolfshiem sends regrets, Daisy and Tom have left no address, and apart from a few servants and the postman the mourners are Nick, Gatsby’s father with a boyhood schedule written in the back of a book, and the drunk from the library. Nick breaks with Jordan, meets Tom on Fifth Avenue, and goes back to the Midwest.

Chapter 9
Nick lies on the beach at night and turns one man’s backward-looking project into a history of the continent.
