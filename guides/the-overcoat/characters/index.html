The Overcoat · Nikolai Gogol

Characters

Akaki Akakievitch Bashmatchkin

Titular councillor and copying clerk; first appears The opening

A clerk in his fifties who has copied documents in the same chancellery for as long as anyone can remember. He loves the letters he forms, eats without tasting, cannot finish a sentence and asks nothing of anybody except to be left to work. The new cloak gives him, for the first time, something to want, and wanting changes him: he saves, plans, walks faster and even dreams of a marten collar. After the theft he speaks up once, is shouted down and dies. As a ghost he becomes the only version of himself that anyone fears.

Petrovitch

Tailor, once a serf, one-eyed and fond of brandy; first appears At Petrovitch’s

Once a serf called Gregor, he took a new name when he was freed and began to drink on every holy day in the calendar. He is sharp, proud of his trade and pleased to name a price that makes a customer flinch. He also makes the best thing that ever happens to Akaki: he spends a fortnight on the cloak, sews it all with silk, charges twelve roubles and follows it down the street to watch it being worn.

The important personage

A newly promoted official, whom Field also calls the Superintendent; first appears The important personage

Gogol never gives him a name or a post that can be pinned down. He has recently been promoted, has practised his stern voice in front of a looking-glass, and runs his ten subordinates on a single word, severity. Among men of his own rank he is pleasant; below it he freezes, and he knows he is missing something. He shouts at Akaki mainly to impress an old friend, feels a pang when he hears of the death, and changes his manner only after the ghost has taken his cloak.

The young clerk

A newcomer who joins the teasing once; first appears The office

He appears in one paragraph and matters out of all proportion. Something in Akaki’s voice stops him in the middle of a practical joke, and for years afterwards, in cheerful company, he hears the plea to be left alone and, behind it, a question about brotherhood. He is the only person whose view of Akaki changes while Akaki is alive, and the story’s few lines of open pity come to the reader through him.

The clerks of the chancellery

Akaki’s fellow officials; first appears The office

They drop paper snow on his head, invent stories about his landlady and demand a feast when he arrives in a new cloak. When he is robbed some of them laugh; most are sorry and take up a collection, which raises very little because they have just paid for their director’s portrait. They hear of his death four days after he is buried, and his desk is filled the next day.

The landlady

Akaki’s old landlady, aged seventy; first appears The office

The clerks joke that she beats him and ask when he will marry her. She lets him copy in her room so that he can save the fuel in his own, sends him to the district superintendent she knows through her former cook, sits by him in his fever and crosses herself at the titles he calls out in delirium.

The thieves

The men with long moustaches in the square; first appears The square

Several men whose faces Akaki cannot make out. One says the cloak is his and takes him by the collar, another holds a bony fist over his mouth, and by the time Akaki comes round in the snow they are gone. The story never finds them. At the very end a figure with an enormous moustache, taller than Akaki’s ghost, turns on a sentry and strides off into the dark, which leaves open whether the last ghost in the story is one of them.

The narrator

The talkative teller of the story; first appears The opening

He will not name the department, forgets where the party was, does not know who got Akaki’s belongings and admits he has never asked. He passes from comedy to sentiment and back inside a paragraph, and his asides about officials are funny partly because he shares their habits. Boris Eikhenbaum’s essay of 1919 put him at the centre of the story: in that reading the voice, with its jokes and lapses, decides how a reader feels about Akaki.
