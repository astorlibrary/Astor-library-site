Nikolai Gogol

The Overcoat

A study guide

A copying clerk in St Petersburg, mocked in his office and content with his desk, is told by his tailor that his old cloak cannot be patched again. He goes without tea and supper for months to pay for a new one, wears it for one day, and has it stolen on the way home from a party. The official he asks for help shouts at him, he dies of a fever within the week, and then a ghost starts pulling the cloaks off the backs of Petersburg’s officials. Gogol tells it through a talkative, forgetful narrator, so that the reader is never sure whether to laugh at Akaki or pity him, and readers have argued in those terms ever since.

Written 1839–1842; first published 1842.
