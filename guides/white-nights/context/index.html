White Nights · Fyodor Dostoevsky

Context

Dates

1703
Peter the Great founds St Petersburg on the Neva The new capital rose on marshland at about sixty degrees north, far enough north for the summer sky to stay light all night for several weeks around midsummer.
1821
Fyodor Dostoevsky is born in Moscow The son of a doctor at the Mariinsky Hospital for the poor.
1843
Turgenev’s poem The Flower Dostoevsky took the epigraph of White Nights from it, slightly altered and put as a question about staying even a moment near another’s heart.
1846
Poor Folk and The Double Dostoevsky’s first two books, one a novel in letters between a poor clerk and a young woman.
1847
The Petersburg Chronicle feuilletons Newspaper pieces in which Dostoevsky described the Petersburg dreamer, a strange creature of the neuter gender, in terms the story would repeat.
1848
White Nights appears in Otechestvennye zapiski In the December issue, signed F. Dostoevsky and dedicated to the poet Aleksei Pleshcheyev.
1849
Arrest and mock execution Dostoevsky and Pleshcheyev were arrested in April with other members of the Petrashevsky circle. In December Dostoevsky was led out to be shot, reprieved at the last moment, and sent to hard labour in Siberia.
1849
Druzhinin reviews the story In January’s Sovremennik the critic Aleksandr Druzhinin ranked it above several of Dostoevsky’s earlier stories, while wishing the dreamer had a clearer place, time and occupation.
1860
The revised text For his first collected works Dostoevsky revised the style and added the passage listing the dreamer’s romantic fantasies.
1861
Dobrolyubov’s Downtrodden People The radical critic saw the dreamer as an early version of the narrator of The Insulted and Injured, and mocked romantic lovers who run errands for their rivals.
1918
Constance Garnett’s translation White Nights and Other Stories, the tenth volume of her Novels of Fyodor Dostoevsky, which also contains Notes from Underground.
1957
Visconti’s Le notti bianche Marcello Mastroianni and Maria Schell in a modern Italian port town recreated on the sound stages of Cinecittà; the film won the Silver Lion at Venice.
1971
Bresson’s Four Nights of a Dreamer Robert Bresson moved the story to contemporary Paris and the embankments of the Seine.

Places

Catherine Canal (now the Griboyedov Canal), St Petersburg
Russian commentators take the canal embankment where the narrator finds Nastenka to be this one.
Fontanka, St Petersburg
Where he meets the old man with the gold-knobbed stick every day, and where peasants sleep in their barges.
Nevsky Prospect, St Petersburg
The main street, one of the walks on which he looks in vain for familiar faces.
Kamenny Island, St Petersburg
One of the islands of elegant summer villas to which the city departs, leaving him behind.
Kolomna, St Petersburg
The quarter of Pushkin’s The Little House in Kolomna, which appears among the dreamer’s fantasies as a little home of one’s own.
