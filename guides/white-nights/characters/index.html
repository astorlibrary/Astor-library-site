White Nights · Fyodor Dostoevsky

Characters

The dreamer

The narrator, twenty-six and a minor official; first appears First Night

He has lived in Petersburg for eight years and speaks to almost nobody, yet he nods to strangers he sees every day, greets the houses as friends and grieves when the town leaves for the summer. His real life happens in fantasies built out of Scott, Hoffmann and Pushkin, and he knows it; on the second night he describes his condition with painful accuracy, in the third person because he is ashamed. With Nastenka he is generous, funny and easily hurt. He delivers her letter to the man she loves, and when she chooses the lodger he blesses her.

Nastenka

Seventeen, living with her blind grandmother; first appears First Night

Quick, frank and much braver than the narrator. She has spent two years pinned to her grandmother’s dress, read her way out through Walter Scott and Pushkin, and once went up to the lodger’s room with her clothes in a bundle. She sets the terms of the friendship on the first night, teases the narrator’s grand manner, and knows more about his feelings than she lets on. When the lodger does not come she turns to the narrator honestly, and when the lodger does come she goes straight to him. Her letter the next morning is loving, sincere and entirely on her own side.

The lodger

The young man upstairs, gone to Moscow for a year; first appears Nastenka’s History

We see him only through Nastenka’s story and for a moment at the end. He is grave, poor and careful: he lends the family good novels, takes the grandmother and Nastenka to the opera, keeps his distance afterwards, and refuses to bind her with a promise he cannot keep. Whether he delays for three days out of doubt, business or indifference the story never says. He comes back, as he promised, and is to marry her within the week.

The grandmother

Nastenka’s blind grandmother; first appears Second Night

She owns the little wooden house, lives on her pension and the lodger’s rent, and remembers better days, when the sun was warmer and she sang Rosina in private theatricals. She pins Nastenka to her side and searches lent books for love letters, yet takes her to the opera and would like, it turns out, a young man upstairs. She is comic and a jailer, and Nastenka is fond of her.

Matrona

The dreamer’s old servant; first appears First Night

Matrona keeps his room, encourages the spider’s web on the ceiling and ignores his lectures about it. The dreamer says she has no imagination at all. On the last morning she brings Nastenka’s letter and announces that she has swept the cobwebs away, so he can have a wedding or a party; looking at her, he sees the fifteen solitary years ahead of him.

Fyokla

The grandmother’s deaf charwoman; first appears Nastenka’s History

Good-natured and very deaf, she once sat pinned to the grandmother in Nastenka’s place so that the girl could slip out to see a friend, and gave the game away when the old woman woke and asked her something she could not hear. Later it is through her that the lodger offers his books. Garnett calls her Fekla in that episode and Fyokla afterwards.
