Romeo and Juliet · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1554
Matteo Bandello publishes his Novelle His version of the Veronese lovers carries the story into the European collections that reach England.
1559
Pierre Boaistuau adapts Bandello into French The Histoires tragiques supply the immediate source for the English versions.
1562
Arthur Brooke’s poem Romeus and Juliet published Shakespeare’s chief English source: the feud, the Friar, the Nurse, the potion and the tomb, spread across months.
1567
William Painter prints a prose version The Palace of Pleasure shows how far the Italian novella tradition had entered English reading.
1597
First Quarto printed by John Danter An Excellent conceited Tragedie of Romeo and Iuliet; the title page claims frequent public performance by the Lord Hunsdon’s Servants.
1599
Second Quarto: ‘newly corrected, augmented, and amended’ Much fuller than Q1, and the basis of later quartos, the Folio text and modern editions.
1607
The play is entered in the Stationers’ Register A decade after the first quarto had already appeared in print.
1623
First Folio Printed as The Tragedie of Romeo and Juliet, following the later quarto line.
1748
David Garrick’s acting version Restores more of Shakespeare’s text than earlier adaptations while rewriting the ending for the eighteenth-century stage.
1867
Gounod’s Roméo et Juliette The opera makes the love plot the musical engine and gives the lovers extended duets.
1935
Prokofiev composes the ballet score It becomes the basis of the best-known dance treatments, including Kenneth MacMillan’s of 1965.
1957
West Side Story opens The feud is transferred to New York gang conflict, with music by Leonard Bernstein and lyrics by Stephen Sondheim.
1968
Zeffirelli’s film Casting two teenagers, Leonard Whiting and Olivia Hussey, as the leads changed what audiences expected the parts to look like.
1996
Baz Luhrmann’s Romeo + Juliet Shakespeare’s language kept intact inside a modern Verona Beach of guns, television and religious iconography.

Places

Verona
The whole play but one scene: the public square, the Capulet house, the orchard, the friar’s cell and the vault.
Mantua
Romeo’s place of exile, about twenty-five miles away, and the apothecary’s shop.
Vicenza
Home of Luigi da Porto, whose Italian novella stands early in the chain of sources behind Brooke.
London
Where the 1597 quarto was printed, its title page naming the Lord Hunsdon’s Servants as the company.
