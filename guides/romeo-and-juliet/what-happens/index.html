Romeo and Juliet · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The brawl, and the Capulet feast

Two servants start a street fight over nothing, the Prince threatens death for the next offence, and Romeo is in love with a woman who will not have him. At the Capulet feast he meets Juliet, and the two build a sonnet between them before either learns the other’s name.

1.0
The Chorus announces the feud, the lovers and their deaths in fourteen lines.
1.1
A servants’ quarrel becomes a civic riot; the Prince sets death as the penalty for the next one.
1.2
Capulet tells Paris his daughter is too young, and then invites him to the feast anyway.
1.3
Lady Capulet raises marriage; the Nurse answers with a long, fond, indecent memory.
1.4
Mercutio’s fantasy about the dreams of sleepers runs out of control on the way to the party.
1.5
Romeo sees Juliet, Tybalt is restrained by Capulet, and the two lovers learn each other’s surnames last.

Act II The balcony, and the secret wedding

Romeo climbs the orchard wall and hears Juliet say aloud what she would never have told him. She proposes marriage herself, and by the following afternoon Friar Laurence has performed it, hoping a secret wedding will do what the Prince’s threats cannot.

2.0
A second Chorus sonnet marks the change from old love to new.
2.1
Mercutio conjures for Romeo bawdily in the lane while Romeo hides on the other side.
2.2
Juliet, overheard, argues with a name; before the scene ends she has proposed marriage.
2.3
Friar Laurence, gathering herbs, agrees to marry them for political reasons of his own.
2.4
Mercutio finds Romeo restored to sociable joking; the Nurse arrives on her errand.
2.5
Juliet waits three hours for news and is teased with aching bones before she gets it.
2.6
The Friar warns against violent delights and leads them off to be married.

Act III Mercutio and Tybalt killed, and Romeo banished

Mercutio dies under Romeo’s arm, Romeo kills Tybalt within minutes, and the Prince commutes death to exile. Juliet spends her wedding night with a husband who must leave at dawn, and wakes to a father who will disown her if she does not marry Paris on Thursday.

3.1
Mercutio and Tybalt are killed in quick succession, and the Prince banishes Romeo, though he had threatened death.
3.2
The Nurse’s garbled news makes Juliet think Romeo dead, then curse him, then defend him.
3.3
Romeo will not hear the word banished; the Friar and the Nurse get him to Juliet.
3.4
Capulet fixes the wedding to Paris while his daughter is upstairs with her husband.
3.5
The lovers part; Capulet threatens to turn Juliet out, and the Nurse advises bigamy.

Act IV The Friar’s potion, and Juliet’s false death

Juliet takes the Friar’s vial without telling anyone, and the scene in which she swallows it is the longest she is ever left alone. The Capulets wake to find her cold, and in the space of one scene the household’s wedding preparations become a funeral.

4.1
Juliet meets Paris at the Friar’s cell and is given a potion and a forty-two-hour lie.
4.2
Juliet apologises to her father, who joyfully brings the wedding forward a day.
4.3
Alone, Juliet imagines waking among bones, and drinks anyway.
4.4
Capulet fusses over pastry and logs while his daughter lies drugged upstairs.
4.5
Formal lamentation from the family; the musicians stay behind to argue about their fee.

Act V The lost letter, and the tomb

Romeo in Mantua hears of Juliet’s death from a servant before the Friar’s letter can reach him, because the messenger is shut up in a plague house. He buys poison from a starving apothecary, kills Paris at the tomb door, and dies minutes before Juliet wakes.

5.1
Balthasar brings the wrong news; Romeo remembers an apothecary poor enough to break the law.
5.2
Friar John explains that plague searchers sealed the house and the letter never left.
5.3
Paris, Romeo and Juliet die; the Friar confesses, and the two fathers agree on statues.
