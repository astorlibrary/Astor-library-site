Romeo and Juliet · William Shakespeare

Characters

Romeo

Son of Montague; first appears 1.1

He arrives already in love, with a woman the audience never meets, and speaking a courtly idiom he has clearly borrowed. What Juliet does to him is mostly grammatical: he stops making balanced Petrarchan antitheses about himself and starts answering another person.

Juliet

Daughter of Capulet, thirteen; first appears 1.3

She is the practical one. She proposes the marriage, fixes the arrangements, questions the Friar’s plan for flaws before she takes it, and dies without a speech. Her soliloquies are the only places in the play where somebody thinks a decision through before making it.

Mercutio

Kinsman to the Prince, friend to Romeo; first appears 1.4

Related to neither house, which is why his death breaks the play open: the feud kills a man who had no stake in it. His Queen Mab speech is the longest flight of fancy Shakespeare had written to that point, and it collapses into bitterness before anyone can stop it.

Tybalt

Nephew to Lady Capulet; first appears 1.1

For him the feud is an identity more than a grievance, and he is the only character who says he prefers hatred to peace. Capulet’s refusal to let him act at the feast stores up the violence that arrives in the next act.

The Nurse

Juliet’s nurse since infancy; first appears 1.3

She has fed Juliet, buried her own daughter, and speaks the only unguarded prose in the Capulet house. Her advice after the banishment (marry Paris; the second match is better) is a servant’s realism more than treachery, and Juliet ends the friendship on the spot.

Friar Laurence

A Franciscan; first appears 2.3

A herbalist who knows that the same plant heals and kills, and who applies the principle to politics: a secret marriage, he hopes, will cure a public feud. Every one of his interventions is well meant, improvised, and worse than the situation it was designed to fix.

Capulet

Juliet’s father, head of the house; first appears 1.1

In Act I he tells Paris his daughter is too young and that her consent matters; in Act III he threatens to throw her into the street. Nothing has changed except that he has been contradicted, which is the most frightening thing about the scene.

Lady Capulet

Juliet’s mother; first appears 1.1

She cannot talk to her daughter without the Nurse in the room, and her one plan after Tybalt’s death is to have Romeo poisoned in Mantua. She married young herself, which the play mentions once and does not press.

Benvolio

Montague’s nephew, Romeo’s cousin; first appears 1.1

The play’s witness: he tries to stop the first fight, reports the second accurately to the Prince, and then disappears from the text without explanation. His function is to want peace and to be ineffective at it.

Paris

A young nobleman, kinsman to the Prince; first appears 1.2

He is not a villain, and that is the difficulty: he courts Juliet through her father because that is how it is done, speaks to her kindly when they meet, and comes to her tomb with flowers. He dies asking to be laid beside her.
