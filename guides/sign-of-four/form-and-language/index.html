The Sign of Four · Arthur Conan Doyle

Form and language

The chain of inference

A conclusion presented as a series of small observations, each of which the reader is invited to check.

The watch in chapter I is the model: conclusion first, protest from Watson, then the scratches round the keyhole and the pawnbroker’s numbers. The garret in chapter VI repeats it with a rope, a stump-mark and a footprint.

Detection is, or ought to be, an exact science, and should be treated in the same cold and unemotional manner.

Sherlock Holmes, Chapter I

Watson’s retrospective narration

An account given after the event by a narrator who already knows how it ends.

Watson writes after the event, and lets slip in the cab home from Norwood that Mary has talked to him about that night since, so the love plot is never in doubt, and his fear of her fortune is confessed from the safety of knowing it was lost.

“My mind,” he said, “rebels at stagnation. Give me problems, give me work, give me the most abstruse cryptogram or the most intricate analysis, and I am in my own proper atmosphere.

Sherlock Holmes, Chapter I

Embedded histories

A second, older story told by a character to explain the present one.

Three are nested in order of distance: Mary’s ten years, Thaddeus’s account of his father’s death, and Small’s thirty-year narrative, which takes over the last chapter and moves the book from Norwood to Agra.

The cursed greed which has been my besetting sin through life has withheld from her the treasure, half at least of which should have been hers.

Major John Sholto, Chapter IV

The material clue

A physical trace that carries the solution before anybody can read it.

A small bare footprint in creosote, a thorn, a stone-headed club and a scrap of paper with four names: each is on the page before it is understood, and the creosote is the best clue and also the one that leads to a barrel.

Physiognomy

Treating physical appearance as evidence of character, in the manner of Victorian physiognomy.

Watson reads refinement in Mary’s face, resolve in Small’s chin and bestiality in Tonga’s features, all with the same confidence. Holmes does it with handwriting. The habit passes as observation and works as prejudice.

In an experience of women which extends over many nations and three separate continents, I have never looked upon a face which gave a clearer promise of a refined and sensitive nature.

Chapter II

Gothic machinery

Isolated houses, locked rooms, moonlight, inherited guilt and faces at the window used to produce dread.

Pondicherry Lodge has a walled garden, a hidden garret, a curse-bearing treasure and a dead man grinning through a keyhole. Doyle then has Holmes measure the room with a tape.

Was ever such a dreary, dismal, unprofitable world? See how the yellow fog swirls down the street and drifts across the dun-coloured houses.

Sherlock Holmes, Chapter I

Foils

Characters placed side by side so that each shows up what the other lacks.

Jones’s instant theory against Holmes’s patience; Watson’s feeling against Holmes’s declared coldness; one twin generous and one grasping; and, in Small’s story, three Indian partners who keep an oath beside an English officer who does not.

“It is cocaine,” he said,—“a seven-per-cent. solution. Would you care to try it?”

Sherlock Holmes, Chapter I

Biased witnesses

Narrators with reasons to shape what they report.

Small expects to be tried for his life and tells his story to the men who will give evidence. He was forced at knife-point; he never touched Sholto; his heart softened before he tripped the merchant. It may all be so. He even says, maybe you gentlemen think that I am just making out a case for myself.

To him it brought murder, to Major Sholto it brought fear and guilt, to me it has meant slavery for life.

Jonathan Small, Chapter XI
