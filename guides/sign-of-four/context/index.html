The Sign of Four · Arthur Conan Doyle

Context

Dates

1857
The Indian Rebellion Beginning among sepoys of the Bengal army in May and spreading across northern India. Europeans from the surrounding country took refuge in the fort at Agra, as Small does, and an action was fought outside the city early in July.
1858
A penal settlement is founded at Port Blair The British opened the convict colony on the Andaman Islands in the aftermath of the rebellion, and many of its first prisoners were men sentenced for their part in it. Small and his three partners are sent there.
1859
Arthur Conan Doyle is born in Edinburgh He studied medicine at the university there and went into general practice at Southsea in 1882.
1868
Wilkie Collins publishes The Moonstone The earlier English novel of an Indian jewel looted in wartime and followed home. Collins sends the stone back; Doyle sends it to the bottom of the river.
1887
A Study in Scarlet appears in Beeton’s Christmas Annual Holmes and Watson’s first case, which Holmes criticises in the opening chapter of this one. It made little stir.
1889
Commissioned at a dinner at the Langham Hotel On 30 August Joseph Marshall Stoddart, over from Philadelphia for Lippincott’s, entertained Doyle and Oscar Wilde and came away with a promise of a story from each. Doyle wrote his that autumn. Wilde’s was The Picture of Dorian Gray. The Langham is where Captain Morstan vanishes.
1890
Lippincott’s Monthly Magazine prints the novel complete In the February number, as The Sign of the Four; or, The Problem of the Sholtos.
1890
Spencer Blackett publishes the first book edition In October, under the shorter title The Sign of Four, which many later editions keep.
1891
The Holmes short stories begin in the Strand Magazine A Scandal in Bohemia appeared in July. The monthly stories made Holmes a public figure, as the two novels had not, and sent readers back to this book.
1923
Maurice Elvey films the novel for Stoll Pictures A silent feature with Eille Norwood as Holmes, the last of his long run in the part.
1930
Doyle dies at Crowborough in Sussex Knighted in 1902, and by then far better known for Holmes than for the historical novels he valued more.
1932
Arthur Wontner plays Holmes in a sound film of the story Directed by Graham Cutts, with Ian Hunter as Watson; the plot is rearranged so that the Agra backstory comes first.
1987
Granada Television’s feature-length adaptation Jeremy Brett as Holmes and Edward Hardwicke as Watson, directed by Peter Hammond. It drops Watson’s engagement altogether.

Places

Baker Street, London
The sitting-room where the novel begins and ends, with the bottle on the corner of the mantelpiece.
Lyceum Theatre, Strand
The third pillar from the left, where Mary Morstan is told to wait at seven, and where a coachman collects the three of them in the fog.
Upper Norwood
Pondicherry Lodge: Major Sholto’s walled villa, its grounds dug over for six years in search of the chest that was in the roof.
Langham Hotel, Portland Place
Captain Morstan’s address on the night he disappeared in 1878, and the hotel where the novel was commissioned over dinner.
Plumstead Marshes
The melancholy south bank where the Aurora runs aground, Small’s wooden leg sinks in the mud, and the treasure and the Islander are both lost to the river.
Agra Fort
The Mughal fortress on the Yamuna where Small guards a side door in 1857, the merchant Achmet is murdered, and the jewels are first hidden in a wall.
Port Blair, Andaman Islands
The convict settlement in the Bay of Bengal where Small serves his sentence, the two officers play cards, and Tonga is found sick in the woods.
Southsea, Portsmouth
Where Doyle was in practice as a doctor when he wrote the book, some distance from the London it maps so confidently.
