The Sign of Four · Arthur Conan Doyle

Characters

Sherlock Holmes

The only unofficial consulting detective, by his own description; first appears Chapter I

This is the book that gives him his vices. He injects cocaine three times a day because there is no case, quotes Goethe and Richter, recommends a freethinking history of religion to Watson, boxes, disguises himself and cooks oysters for a policeman. He solves the who and the how within a night. He also loses the treasure, and the ending is careful to show that success leaves him exactly where the first page found him.

Dr John Watson

Narrator, army surgeon on half-pay, and the book’s lover; first appears Chapter I

He has a weak leg, a thin bank account and a published account of Holmes’s first case, which Holmes dislikes. He falls for the client at sight and spends the middle of the book hoping the investigation he is helping will fail, since success would make her an heiress and him a fortune-hunter. His judgements of faces (hers, the Islander’s, Small’s) are offered as plain observation and deserve more suspicion than he gives them.

Mary Morstan

Governess, client, and heir to a share of the treasure; first appears Chapter II

Sent home from India as a child and orphaned in effect at seventeen, she has kept her father’s papers in order for ten years and brings Holmes a properly documented case. Holmes calls her a model client. The novel then hands her to the love story: she faints, is taken home, and reappears to be shown an empty box. She is the one person with a claim on the jewels who never expresses a wish for them.

Thaddeus Sholto

The Major’s son, aesthete and hypochondriac; first appears Chapter IV

Bald at thirty, twitching, wrapped in astrakhan on a close night, he is drawn as a figure of fun, yet it was he who persuaded his reluctant brother to send Mary Morstan a pearl every year. His Corot and his hookah were bought with the same money. Doyle makes the decent heir ridiculous and leaves the reader to notice that he is decent.

Bartholomew Sholto

The other twin, who finds the treasure and dies beside it; first appears Chapter V

He never speaks. He is described by his brother as a little inclined to their father’s fault, works out by measurement that the house has four feet of unexplained height, and breaks through the ceiling to the chest. A reader first sees him through a keyhole, lit by the moon, wearing his brother’s face set in a grin.

Major John Sholto

Retired officer of the 34th Bombay Infantry; dead before the story opens; first appears Chapter II

He gambled himself into ruin while guarding convicts on the Andamans, bought the secret of the treasure from one of them, fetched it and kept it all. He then concealed his friend Morstan’s body, withheld the daughter’s share for years, and died of fright at a bearded face against the window. He retires to Norwood with native servants and a house named after a French port in India.

Jonathan Small

Worcestershire farm boy, soldier, overseer, convict, and the narrator of chapter XII; first appears Chapter X

He loses a leg to a crocodile, keeps coolies at work on an indigo plantation, helps murder a merchant for a rajah’s jewels and serves twenty years for it. His one fixed virtue is that he keeps faith with his three Indian partners, which the two English officers do not. His account is vivid, self-serving and the only one there is; nobody else alive was at Agra.

Tonga

An Andaman Islander; Small’s companion and the killer of Bartholomew Sholto; first appears Chapter X

He has no line of speech in the book. He is assembled out of a gazetteer entry, a footprint, Watson’s horror and Small’s patronage, and every one of those sources calls him hideous or a savage. What he actually does is recover from an illness, stay with the man who nursed him, sail an open canoe for ten days, get shown at fairs for pennies, and die in the river. Doyle took the portrait from colonial print and added to its errors; it is the part of the novel that most needs to be read against.

Mahomet Singh, Abdullah Khan and Dost Akbar

Small’s three partners in the sign of the four; first appears Chapter III

Two troopers on a fort gate and a foster-brother who lures the merchant in. Small calls them Sikhs, and Doyle has given them names that are mostly Muslim, which says how closely he was looking. They plan the murder, recruit Small at knife-point and then keep their word to him for thirty years, and he to them. At the end they are still in the Andaman convict-barracks, and nobody in London gives them another thought.

Athelney Jones

Scotland Yard detective; first appears Chapter VI

Stout, wheezing and quick to a theory, he arrests Thaddeus, the housekeeper, the gatekeeper and the Indian butler within an hour and has to release two of them the next day. He is not a fool so much as a man who decides first. By chapter IX he is asking for help, and he takes the official credit for an arrest he would have made at the wrong place.
