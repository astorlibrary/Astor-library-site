Coriolanus · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The corn riot, and the taking of Corioli

Citizens with clubs come out over the price of corn and are answered with a fable about a belly. Marcius insults them, the senate grants them tribunes anyway, and then a Volscian war conveniently arrives. He takes Corioli almost alone, is renamed for the city he captured, and refuses his share of the spoils.

1.1
The citizens rise over grain; Menenius tells the fable of the belly; Marcius arrives and calls them scabs.
1.2
Aufidius learns that Rome knows his plans and says he wants Marcius in the field.
1.3
Volumnia rejoices in her son’s wounds; Virgilia will not leave the house until he returns.
1.4
Marcius curses his own retreating soldiers and is shut inside the town alone.
1.5
Romans loot while Marcius, still bleeding, goes to find Cominius.
1.6
He asks for volunteers and is taken up in the arms of the whole army.
1.7
Lartius secures Corioli and follows to the field.
1.8
The two meet in single combat and are parted by Volscian soldiers.
1.9
He turns down his tenth of the spoils, is hailed Coriolanus, and forgets the name of the poor host he wanted pardoned.
1.10
Beaten five times, Aufidius says he will have Marcius by any means, honourable or not.

Act II The gown of humility

Rome welcomes him back with a noise that frightens the tribunes, and the senate offers the consulship. Custom requires him to stand in the Forum in a plain gown, show his wounds and ask each citizen for a voice. He gets the votes by sneering through the ceremony, and the tribunes go to work on the men who gave them.

2.1
Menenius baits the tribunes; Volumnia counts her son’s wounds before he arrives.
2.2
Cominius reports his deeds while Coriolanus walks out so as not to hear them praised.
2.3
He asks for voices, receives them, and the tribunes persuade the citizens to take them back.

Act III Banished

Told his election is revoked, he says in public what the patricians say in private: that giving the people a share in power will destroy the state. The tribunes call it treason and stage a trial. Volumnia persuades him to go back and apologise; he lasts a few lines before the word ‘traitor’ wrecks the performance, and Rome banishes him.

3.1
Coriolanus argues for abolishing the tribunate and is nearly seized on the spot.
3.2
Volumnia teaches him how to perform humility; he agrees to try.
3.3
Provoked by the charge of treason, he curses the city and banishes it from himself.

Act IV Exile, and the alliance with Aufidius

He parts from his family at the gate with unexpected gentleness, and walks to Antium in disguise to offer his throat to his oldest enemy. Aufidius embraces him, gives him half the army, and then watches his own officers begin to prefer the newcomer. The Volscian force moves on Rome and the tribunes discover what they have arranged.

4.1
He comforts his mother and wife at the city gate and leaves alone.
4.2
Volumnia meets the tribunes in the street and curses them with an appetite of her own.
4.3
A Roman spy and a Volscian exchange news of the banishment and the mobilisation.
4.4
Disguised outside Aufidius’s house, he reflects on how quickly friends turn enemies.
4.5
He names himself, offers his throat, and is given half the Volscian command.
4.6
The tribunes are congratulating themselves on a quiet city when the news arrives.
4.7
His lieutenant reports that the soldiers now follow Coriolanus; Aufidius begins to plan.

Act V Volumnia’s plea, and Coriolanus’s death

Cominius and Menenius are sent to plead and are turned away. Then his mother, wife and son come, and Volumnia argues, kneels and finally stops. He takes her hand and says nothing for as long as the scene will hold, then grants the peace and tells her what it will cost him. Aufidius kills him in Corioli, over a name.

5.1
Cominius reports that Coriolanus would not answer to his own name.
5.2
Menenius is refused at the guard post and sent back to Rome.
5.3
Volumnia, Virgilia and Young Marcius kneel; he holds her hand, silent, and yields.
5.4
Menenius describes a man grown into a dragon; the city hears the news and goes out to meet Volumnia.
5.5
Rome honours the women as its deliverers.
5.6
Aufidius calls him boy and traitor; the conspirators kill him, and then carry the body out with honour.
