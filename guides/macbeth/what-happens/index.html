Macbeth · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The witches, and the plan to kill Duncan

Macbeth and Banquo meet the witches on the heath and hear that Macbeth will be thane of Cawdor and king. When the first title arrives within minutes, the second stops being an absurdity. Lady Macbeth reads his letter, judges him too decent to take the nearest way, and has the murder arranged before he reaches the door.

1.1
Three witches agree to meet Macbeth on the heath.
1.2
A wounded sergeant describes Macbeth carving his way through a rebel army.
1.3
The witches hail Macbeth as Glamis, Cawdor and king hereafter, and tell Banquo he will father kings.
1.4
Duncan thanks Macbeth and then names his own son Prince of Cumberland, putting a step in Macbeth’s way.
1.5
Lady Macbeth reads Macbeth’s letter and calls on the spirits to strip her of pity.
1.6
Duncan arrives at Inverness and praises the sweetness of the air.
1.7
Macbeth decides against the murder; his wife changes his mind in under fifty lines.

Act II Duncan’s murder, and the knocking at the gate

Macbeth sees a dagger that is not there, kills Duncan offstage, and comes back unable to say ‘Amen’. The knocking at the gate, the Porter’s drunken joke about hell and the discovery of the body make a private act a public emergency. Malcolm and Donalbain run, which conveniently makes them suspects.

2.1
Banquo confesses uneasy dreams; alone, Macbeth follows a dagger he cannot grasp.
2.2
Macbeth returns with the grooms’ daggers, hearing a voice cry sleep no more.
2.3
The Porter plays hell’s gatekeeper; Macduff discovers the body and Macbeth kills the grooms.
2.4
Ross and an Old Man report horses eating each other; Macbeth goes to Scone to be crowned.

Act III Banquo’s murder, and the banquet

Banquo suspects and says nothing. Macbeth, who now fears the second half of the prophecy more than the first, hires murderers without telling his wife. Fleance escapes, the ghost sits in Macbeth’s chair at the banquet, and the marriage that planned Duncan’s murder starts to come apart over Banquo’s.

3.1
Banquo weighs what Macbeth has gained; Macbeth hires two murderers.
3.2
Macbeth keeps his wife ignorant of the plan for the first time.
3.3
Banquo is killed and Fleance escapes into the dark.
3.4
Banquo’s ghost takes Macbeth’s seat and the state dinner collapses.
3.5
Hecate rebukes the witches, in a scene most scholars think was added later.
3.6
Lennox speaks in careful irony; Macduff has gone to England.

Act IV The apparitions, and the murder of Macduff’s family

Macbeth demands more prophecy and is given three riddles that sound like safety. He orders the killing of Macduff’s wife and children, an act with no strategic purpose at all. In England, Malcolm tests Macduff by pretending to be worse than the tyrant, and the news from home arrives.

4.1
Beware Macduff; none of woman born shall harm Macbeth; he is safe till Birnam wood moves.
4.2
Lady Macduff’s son is murdered on stage, and she is chased off crying murder.
4.3
Malcolm tests Macduff’s loyalty; Ross brings the news from Fife.

Act V The sleepwalking scene, and Macbeth’s death

Lady Macbeth washes hands that are clean. Macbeth, told of her death, can find no more feeling in it than a line about tomorrow. Birnam wood moves because the army carries it; the man not of woman born was delivered by caesarean section. Every guarantee holds and every one betrays him.

5.1
A doctor and gentlewoman watch Lady Macbeth relive the murders in her sleep.
5.2
The Scottish lords join Malcolm’s army near Birnam.
5.3
Macbeth clings to the apparitions and asks the doctor to cure his wife’s mind.
5.4
Malcolm orders each soldier to cut a bough for cover.
5.5
The queen is dead; a messenger reports that the wood is moving.
5.6
The army throws down its screen of branches.
5.7
Macbeth kills a man he cannot fear and begins to look for Macduff.
5.8
Macduff reveals his birth, kills Macbeth, and Malcolm is hailed king.
