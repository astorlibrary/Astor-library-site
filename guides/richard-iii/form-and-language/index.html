Richard III · William Shakespeare

Form and language

Direct address

A character speaking straight to the audience, treating them as confidants.

Richard has the first word of the play and spends forty lines alone with us before the plot begins; the technique makes the audience an accessory before the fact.

Now is the winter of our discontent Made glorious summer by this son of York; And all the clouds that loured upon our house In the deep bosom of the ocean buried.

Gloucester, 1.1.1

Stichomythia

Rapid exchange of single lines, each answering and turning the last.

The wooing of Anne and the later wooing of Elizabeth are both line-by-line duels, and in the second one Richard’s trick stops working cleanly.

Never hung poison on a fouler toad. Out of my sight! Thou dost infect mine eyes.

Lady Anne, 1.2.148

Anaphora

Beginning successive clauses with the same word or phrase.

Margaret’s curses and the women’s laments rely on hammered repetition (“Thou slander… Thou loathed…”), which makes grief and hatred into something closer to ritual than conversation.

Thou elvish-mark'd, abortive, rooting hog! Thou that wast seal'd in thy nativity The slave of nature and the son of hell!

Queen Margaret, 1.3.228

The Vice

The joking, confiding tempter of the medieval morality plays, who explains his tricks to the audience as he performs them.

Richard names the inheritance himself in 3.1, comparing his own double meanings to “the formal Vice, Iniquity”: the play’s clearest signal about where its hero’s charm comes from.

And therefore, since I cannot prove a lover, To entertain these fair well-spoken days, I am determined to prove a villain And hate the idle pleasures of these days.

Gloucester, 1.1.28

Dramatic irony

The audience holding information a character on stage does not.

Because Richard briefs us in advance, almost every scene is watched from inside his plan: Clarence’s trust, Hastings’s confidence and the citizens’ silence are all things we can price and they cannot.

Off with his head! Now, by Saint Paul I swear, I will not dine until I see the same.

Gloucester, 3.4.75

Mirrored scenes

Two scenes built to the same shape so that the second comments on the first.

Two wooings, two tents on the night before Bosworth, and eleven ghosts who deliver the same words twice with opposite force: the play thinks in matched pairs.

Shine out, fair sun, till I have bought a glass, That I may see my shadow as I pass.

Gloucester, 1.2.256
