William Shakespeare

Richard III

A study guide

A man tells the audience in the first forty lines exactly what he intends to do, and then does it while everyone on stage fails to notice. Richard works by performance and hardly bothers with secrecy: he grieves for the brother he has imprisoned, refuses a crown he has arranged to be offered, and takes the audience into a confidence he offers nobody else. The trouble is that the confidence works, until Act IV, when the jokes dry up and the women take over the stage.

Written c. 1592–93; first printed 1597.
