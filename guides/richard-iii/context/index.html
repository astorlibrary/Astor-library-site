Richard III · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1471
Tewkesbury, and the death of Henry VI Two killings the play comes back to again and again: Prince Edward at the battle and his father in the Tower.
1483
Edward IV dies and Richard takes the throne The events of Acts II to IV are compressed from a few months of this year.
1485
Bosworth Field Richard is killed on 22 August and Henry Tudor is crowned; Bosworth is conventionally taken as the end of the Wars of the Roses.
1557
Thomas More’s history of Richard III printed Written decades earlier and left unfinished, it supplied the Tudor chroniclers with the calculating, theatrical Richard Shakespeare inherited.
1587
Holinshed’s Chronicles reissued Shakespeare’s immediate source, which carries More’s account through Hall into the material of the play.
1594
The True Tragedy of Richard the Third printed An anonymous play on the same story, evidence that Richard was already established stage material.
1597
First quarto Printed by Valentine Simmes for Andrew Wise, and reprinted repeatedly over the next four decades.
1598
Second quarto names Shakespeare The first of his plays to be widely reprinted in his lifetime carries his name from this edition onwards.
1623
First Folio A substantially different and longer text, printed among the histories.
1699
Colley Cibber’s adaptation A shortened rewriting that cut Margaret altogether and held the English stage for the best part of two centuries.
1741
Garrick’s debut as Richard The role made his name in London, and made the part an actor’s proving ground.
1814
Edmund Kean’s Richard at Drury Lane A fast, unheroic reading that became the standard against which nineteenth-century Richards were measured.
1955
Laurence Olivier’s film Still using much of Cibber’s shape, and some of his lines. Olivier’s delivery of the opening soliloquy became the one most people knew.
2012
Richard III’s remains found in Leicester The skeleton showed severe scoliosis, reopening old arguments about how much of the play’s body is Tudor invention.

Places

The Tower of London
Clarence’s cell, the princes’ lodging, and the council chamber where Hastings is condemned.
London
The streets, the palace and Baynard’s Castle, where Richard performs his refusal of the crown.
Pontefract Castle
Pomfret, where Rivers, Grey and Vaughan are executed remembering Margaret’s curse.
Salisbury
Where Buckingham is taken to execution on All-Souls’ day.
Milford Haven
Richmond’s landing place, reported to Richard as the invasion begins.
Bosworth Field
The two tents, the ghosts and the battle in which Richard is killed.

Critics

Jan Kott

“The Kings”, in Shakespeare Our Contemporary (1964)

Kott read the history plays as one repeated pattern, which he called the Grand Mechanism: each king climbs the great staircase of power by crime and is thrown down by the next. Richard is its most complete operator, and its last victim.
