Richard III · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The opening soliloquy, Clarence and Lady Anne

Richard sets out his intentions to the audience before anyone else appears, has Clarence sent to the Tower on a manufactured prophecy, and wins Lady Anne beside the corpse of the king he killed. Margaret arrives to curse everyone present, and the play files the curses for later use.

1.1
Richard explains the peace, his own body and his plan, then greets the brother he has just had arrested.
1.2
Interrupting Henry VI’s funeral procession, Richard talks the dead king’s daughter-in-law into accepting his ring.
1.3
The old Lancastrian queen walks into a Yorkist quarrel and names the deaths that will follow, in order.
1.4
Clarence describes a dream of drowning and judgement, then argues theology with the murderers his brother has sent.

Act II Edward IV’s death, and the princes brought to London

Edward IV has just reconciled his court when Richard tells him Clarence is dead, and the news kills him. The queen’s relatives are arrested on the road, the citizens in the street can see what is coming, and the young heir is brought to London under escort by the man who intends to take his place.

2.1
Edward makes his court swear friendship; Richard announces Clarence’s death and lets the guilt settle where it falls.
2.2
The Duchess of York, Queen Elizabeth and Clarence’s children compete in mourning while Richard proposes a quiet escort for the prince.
2.3
Three ordinary Londoners weigh a child king against ambitious kin and fear the worst.
2.4
News of the arrests at Pomfret sends the queen and the younger prince into sanctuary.

Act III Hastings executed, and Richard offered the crown

The princes are lodged in the Tower with courteous menace, Hastings is executed between one sentence and the next, and the crown is offered to a man who has rehearsed his reluctance with Buckingham beforehand. The theatre here is public and cynical, and the citizens’ silence is part of the record.

3.1
The young Duke of York jokes about his uncle’s shoulders; both boys are sent to the Tower for their safety.
3.2
Warned by Stanley’s nightmare, Hastings decides that his standing with Richard makes him safe.
3.3
Rivers, Grey and Vaughan are executed, and remember Margaret’s curse as they go.
3.4
Richard asks for strawberries, leaves the council, returns with a withered arm he blames on witchcraft, and has Hastings killed before dinner.
3.5
Richard and Buckingham stage an emergency in rusty armour and explain the execution to the city.
3.6
A clerk notes that the indictment of Hastings was written out before the man was accused.
3.7
Richard appears between two bishops and declines the crown twice before accepting it.

Act IV The princes murdered, and Buckingham dropped

Richard is king by the middle of the act and immediately less interesting to himself: the soliloquies stop, Buckingham hesitates over the princes and is dropped, and Tyrrel does the work. The long fourth scene gives the stage to three women who have nothing left to lose, and to a second wooing that looks like the first and is not.

4.1
The women are refused admission to the princes; Anne is summoned to be crowned and knows what it means.
4.2
Richard sounds Buckingham out about the princes, gets hesitation, and drops him mid-sentence.
4.3
The murder of the princes is described by a man who was not in the room, quoting men who were.
4.4
Margaret teaches the other queens how to curse; Richard then asks Elizabeth for her daughter and believes he has won.
4.5
Stanley sends secret word to Richmond that his son is held hostage.

Act V Bosworth

Buckingham goes to the block quoting the curse he brought on himself. On the eve of Bosworth the ghosts of everyone Richard has killed visit both tents in turn, cursing one sleeper and blessing the other. Richard wakes into the only soliloquy in which he cannot control who is speaking, and dies on foot.

5.1
Led to execution on All-Souls’ day, Buckingham recalls the oath he swore and the curse Margaret laid.
5.2
The invading earl describes the boar in the middle of England and counts his friends.
5.3
Two tents, two prayers, and eleven ghosts who speak the same words twice with opposite meanings.
5.4
Unhorsed and seeking Richmond in the field, Richard offers his kingdom for a mount.
5.5
Richmond takes the crown, pardons the soldiers and announces a marriage that ends the civil wars.
