Julius Caesar · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The holiday crowd, and Cassius works on Brutus

Two tribunes break up a celebration and strip the decorations off Caesar’s statues, which establishes before anything else that Rome’s loyalty is portable. Cassius then spends a long scene working on Brutus in the street, and the crown is offered offstage three times and reported afterwards by a man who found the whole thing distasteful.

1.1
Flavius and Marullus rebuke workmen for cheering the man who defeated Pompey.
1.2
The soothsayer’s warning, Cassius’s persuasion of Brutus, and Casca’s sour account of the crown.
1.3
A storm full of omens; Cassius recruits Casca and arranges forged letters for Brutus’s window.

Act II Brutus decides, and Calpurnia’s dream

Brutus reasons himself into the murder in an orchard at night, on the basis of what Caesar might do; Caesar has done nothing yet. The act is built from private scenes: a wife who asks to be told, another wife who has dreamt the assassination, and a group of men who will not swear an oath because Romans should not need one.

2.1
Brutus decides; the conspirators arrive muffled; Portia demands to share what is wrong.
2.2
Caesar agrees to stay at home, then is flattered out of it by Decius.
2.3
Artemidorus writes out the conspirators’ names and waits in the street.
2.4
Portia sends a boy to the Capitol and cannot say why, in the play’s most anxious scene.

Act III The assassination and Antony’s funeral speech

Caesar turns down a petition with a speech about being constant as the northern star, and is stabbed, Brutus last. Brutus then makes two mistakes in one scene: he lets Antony live and lets him speak. The crowd that applauded Brutus’s prose walks out of the Forum carrying torches, and kills a poet for having the wrong name.

3.1
The assassination, the hand-washing in blood, and Antony’s bargain with the conspirators.
3.2
Brutus explains in prose; Antony reads a will and shows a body.
3.3
A man is torn apart for sharing a conspirator’s name, and says so.

Act IV Proscription, and a quarrel in a tent

The new rulers of Rome sit down with a list and trade relatives. In the camp at Sardis, the two men who killed for principle argue about bribes and unpaid wages, and Brutus mentions his wife’s death twice, in two different registers. Caesar then walks into the tent as a ghost and says almost nothing.

4.1
Antony, Octavius and Lepidus mark names for death and discuss cutting the legacies.
4.2
Brutus and Cassius meet before their armies with forms of politeness that are already accusations.
4.3
The quarrel, the reconciliation, the news of Portia, and the decision to march to Philippi.

Act V Philippi, and the deaths of Cassius and Brutus

The battle is lost by misreading: Cassius sends a friend to check what a distant cheer means, assumes the worst, and kills himself on the strength of it. Brutus, who has spent the play trusting reason, runs on his own sword because the alternative is a triumph in Rome. Antony’s eulogy gives him the honesty the play has been testing all along.

5.1
The four commanders exchange insults; Brutus and Cassius say goodbye in case.
5.2
Brutus sends word to attack the wing that is giving way.
5.3
Titinius is mistaken for a prisoner; Cassius dies on the sword that killed Caesar.
5.4
Lucilius pretends to be Brutus to protect him, and is taken alive.
5.5
Brutus runs on his sword; Antony and Octavius arrange the funeral honours.
