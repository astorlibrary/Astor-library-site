Julius Caesar · William Shakespeare

Themes

Tyranny and the republic

The conspirators kill for what Caesar might do, and the play does not tell us whether they were right. Nothing on stage shows Caesar acting as a tyrant; nothing shows he would not have. The murder is committed on a forecast, and Acts IV and V are the bill.

Argued in 1.2, decided in the orchard in 2.1, and answered by 4.1, where three men divide the empire and mark relatives for death across a table.

Why, man, he doth bestride the narrow world Like a Colossus, and we petty men Walk under his huge legs and peep about To find ourselves dishonourable graves.

Cassius, 1.2.139

Men at some time are masters of their fates: The fault, dear Brutus, is not in our stars, But in ourselves, that we are underlings.

Cassius, 1.2.143

Rhetoric and the crowd

Two funeral speeches, delivered from the same pulpit within minutes of each other, produce opposite results, and the play writes the crowd’s replies into the scene so that the effect can be watched happening. Brutus argues in prose from principle; Antony works in verse, from objects, and never states his conclusion.

Rehearsed in Cassius’s persuasion of Brutus in 1.2, performed in 3.2, and shown at its worst in 3.3, where the mob kills a man for his name.

How many ages hence Shall this our lofty scene be acted over In states unborn and accents yet unknown!

Cassius, 3.1.115

Cry 'Havoc,' and let slip the dogs of war;

Antony, 3.1.282

Honour and self-image

Brutus does everything because of the kind of man he believes himself to be, and the belief is sincere and repeatedly catastrophic. Cassius appeals to it to recruit him; Antony makes the word ‘honourable’ a weapon by repeating it; and in the end Antony’s eulogy grants Brutus the title, sincerely.

Invoked in 1.2, acted on in 2.1, satirised in 3.2, tested against money in 4.3, and finally conceded in 5.5.

Why, man, he doth bestride the narrow world Like a Colossus, and we petty men Walk under his huge legs and peep about To find ourselves dishonourable graves.

Cassius, 1.2.139

It must be by his death: and for my part, I know no personal cause to spurn at him, But for the general. He would be crown'd: How that might change his nature, there's the question.

Brutus, 2.1.10

Omens and interpretation

The play is full of signs (a soothsayer, a storm, a lion in the Capitol, a dream of a statue running blood, birds of prey over an army), and every one proves accurate, though most are dismissed or explained away by somebody with an interest. The trouble in this play lies in reading the signs.

Ignored in 1.2, argued over in 1.3, reinterpreted in 2.2 by Decius, and confirmed too late in 3.1 and 5.3.

Beware the ides of March.

Soothsayer, 1.2.21

Men at some time are masters of their fates: The fault, dear Brutus, is not in our stars, But in ourselves, that we are underlings.

Cassius, 1.2.143

Friendship and betrayal

Caesar’s last words are about a friend. The play sets political necessity against personal loyalty again and again (Brutus and Caesar, Brutus and Cassius, Antony and his own nephew), and the quarrel in the tent is more about a broken friendship than about the money that starts it.

Exploited in 1.2, broken in 3.1, repaired briefly in 4.3, and mourned on the battlefield in 5.3 and 5.5.

Et tu, Brute! Then fall, Caesar.

Caesar, 3.1.79

Cry 'Havoc,' and let slip the dogs of war;

Antony, 3.1.282

Public role and private self

Almost every important decision in the play is taken at home and announced in the street. Portia and Calpurnia both try to enter the political scene through the household and both are refused, one by kindness and one by flattery, and both are dead or silent before the battle.

Set up in 2.1 and 2.2 as matching domestic scenes, and closed off in 4.3, when Portia’s death is reported as a piece of camp news.

I grant I am a woman; but withal A woman well-reputed, Cato's daughter. Think you I am no stronger than my sex, Being so father'd and so husbanded?

Portia, 2.1.297

I am Cinna the poet, I am Cinna the poet.

Cinna the Poet, 3.3.27
