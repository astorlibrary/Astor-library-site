Doctor Faustus · Christopher Marlowe

Context

Dates

1517
Luther’s ninety-five theses at Wittenberg The university where Marlowe sets his play became the centre of the Reformation, and of its arguments about grace, faith and the freedom of the will.
1540
A German magician called Faust dies, c. 1540 A wandering astrologer and conjuror of that name is mentioned in several sixteenth-century German records. Almost nothing about him is certain, and the legend grew after his death.
1564
Christopher Marlowe born in Canterbury The son of a shoemaker, baptised in February, two months before Shakespeare.
1585
The fire-ship at Antwerp During the Prince of Parma’s siege, an explosive ship wrecked his bridge across the Scheldt. Faustus wants ‘stranger engines’ than ‘the fiery keel at Antwerp’s bridge’, which dates the speech after this event.
1587
The German Faust book printed at Frankfurt The Historia von D. Johann Fausten, published by Johann Spies, gathered the legends into one cautionary life. In the same year Marlowe took his Cambridge MA and Tamburlaine reached the London stage.
1592
Earliest surviving edition of the English Faust book The translation by ‘P. F.’ is Marlowe’s direct source, down to the grapes and the horse-dealer. An earlier printing may have existed, which is one reason the play cannot be dated firmly.
1593
Marlowe killed at Deptford Stabbed on 30 May, aged twenty-nine, days after being called before the Privy Council. None of his plays except Tamburlaine had yet been printed.
1594
First recorded performances, at the Rose Philip Henslowe’s diary lists the play in the Admiral’s Men’s repertory at the Rose from the autumn of this year. It was already an old play by then.
1602
Henslowe pays for additions William Birde and Samuel Rowley received four pounds for ‘adicyones in doctor fostes’. Whether their work survives in the 1616 text is still debated.
1604
First quarto printed (the shorter A-text) The first quarto, the shorter version, and the one followed here.
1616
Second, longer version printed (the B-text) A longer version with new comic and papal scenes, a softened vocabulary of oaths, and a title page showing Faustus in his conjuring circle.
1808
Goethe’s Faust, Part One Goethe’s scholar is saved in the end. The contrast with Marlowe’s has shaped how both are read.
1850
Alexander Dyce’s edition of Marlowe Dyce printed the 1604 and 1616 texts separately instead of blending them. His text of the 1604 quarto is the one used for every quotation here.
1947
Thomas Mann’s Doktor Faustus The pact retold as the life of a German composer, written in exile during and after the Second World War.

Places

Wittenberg
Faustus’s university and Luther’s. The 1604 text spells it ‘Wertenberg’, and Faustus offers to have it sacked for Helen.
Stadtroda, Thuringia
The ‘town call’d Rhodes’ of the Prologue is Roda, as this town was then known, where the Faust book says he was born to parents ‘base of stock’.
Rome
Mephistopheles gives a guidebook tour of the seven hills and the Ponte Angelo before the visit shrinks to stealing the Pope’s dinner.
Trier
The first stop on the aerial tour of Scene 7, ‘environ’d round with airy mountain-tops’.
Naples
Where Faustus sees ‘learned Maro’s golden tomb’. Medieval legend made Virgil a magician, so one conjuror visits another.
Anhalt
The play’s ‘Vanholt’ is usually identified with this small German principality, a day’s ride from Wittenberg. The Duchess gets her grapes here.
Canterbury
Marlowe’s birthplace. He went from the King’s School to Cambridge on a scholarship intended for future clergymen.
The Rose, Bankside
Henslowe’s playhouse, where the Admiral’s Men played Faustus with Edward Alleyn in the part.
