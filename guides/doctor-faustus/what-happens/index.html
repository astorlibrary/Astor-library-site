Prologue, Scenes 1–2 Faustus turns from his studies to magic

The Chorus promises neither war nor love, only the form of one scholar’s fortunes. Faustus sits in his study and dismisses each university discipline in turn because none of them can make him more than a man, then sends for two magicians. His colleagues already fear the worst.

Prologue
The Chorus gives Faustus’s birth, his doctorate and his fall in advance, as Icarus.Key line “His waxen wings did mount above his reach…”
Scene 1
Faustus rejects logic, medicine, law and divinity, hears the two Angels, and is promised the world by Valdes and Cornelius.Key lines “Yet art thou still but Faustus, and a man.” · “The reward of sin is death: that’s hard.” · “But his dominion that exceeds in this…” · “I’ll have them fly to India for gold…” · “O, Faustus, lay that damned book aside…”
Scene 2
Wagner chops logic with two scholars, who learn whom Faustus is dining with and go to tell the Rector.

Scenes 3–5 Mephistopheles, and the contract signed in blood

Faustus conjures and Mephistopheles comes, though not, he explains, because he was summoned. The devil describes hell truthfully and begs Faustus to stop asking; Faustus lectures him on manly fortitude and offers his soul. After a comic scene in which Wagner hires a servant on the same terms, the deed is written in blood that tries not to flow.

Scene 3
Mephistopheles appears, is sent back to change into a friar, and tells Faustus that this is hell.Key lines “For, when we hear one rack the name of God…” · “Why, this is hell, nor am I out of it” · “O, Faustus, leave these frivolous demands…” · “Had I as many souls as there be stars…”
Scene 4
Wagner binds a hungry clown to seven years’ service with two devils and a handful of coins.Key line “he would give his soul to the devil…”
Scene 5
The blood congeals, ‘Homo, fuge’ appears on Faustus’s arm, the contract is read aloud, and his first request, a wife, is refused.Key lines “The god thou serv’st is thine own appetite” · “My blood congeals, and I can write no more.” · “Consummatum est; this bill is ended…” · “Hell hath no limits, nor is circumscrib’d In…”

Scene 6, Chorus 2, Scene 7 Faustus wavers, and the Seven Deadly Sins

Faustus wavers, and each time he wavers he is given something to look at. When he asks who made the world, Mephistopheles will not answer; when he calls on Christ, Lucifer arrives in person with the Seven Deadly Sins as a pastime. The Chorus sends him round the heavens in a dragon-drawn chariot and sets him down in Rome to snatch the Pope’s dinner.

Scene 6
An astronomy lesson ends at the question of creation; Lucifer answers Faustus’s prayer with a parade.Key lines “My heart’s so harden’d, I cannot repent…” · “Never too late, if Faustus can repent.” · “Christ cannot save thy soul…” · “I cannot read, and therefore wish all books were burnt.”
Chorus 2
A chorus speech reports the journey through the sky and announces Rome.
Scene 7
Invisible, Faustus steals dishes, boxes the Pope’s ear and is cursed with bell, book and candle.Key line “How! bell, book, and candle,—candle…”

Chorus 3, Scenes 8–12 The Emperor’s court, the ostlers and the horse-courser

The man who was going to wall Germany with brass becomes a court entertainer with a famous name. Ostlers steal one of his books and conjure with it well enough to fetch Mephistopheles from Constantinople. Faustus shows the Emperor a likeness of Alexander, cheats a horse-dealer of forty dollars and fetches grapes for a pregnant duchess, while the years run out between scenes.

Chorus 3
The Chorus reports Faustus’s return home and his invitation to the court of Charles V.
Scene 8
Robin the ostler plans to use a stolen conjuring book on the maids of the parish.
Scene 9
Robin and Ralph gull a vintner, summon Mephistopheles out of the stolen book and are turned into an ape and a dog.
Scene 10
Faustus raises spirits in the shapes of Alexander and his paramour and plants horns on a sceptical knight.Key line “But such spirits as can lively resemble Alexander…”
Scene 11
A horse sold for forty dollars turns to hay in the water; Faustus, asleep, loses a false leg to the buyer.Key line “Tush, Christ did call the thief upon the Cross…”
Scene 12
At the Duke of Vanholt’s, Faustus sends a spirit to the other side of the world for a dish of grapes.

Scenes 13–14, Epilogue The Old Man, Helen, and the last hour

An Old Man makes the last human appeal and very nearly succeeds. Mephistopheles answers with a threat, Faustus renews the bond in blood and asks for Helen to put repentance out of his mind. The scholars leave him to pray next door, the clock strikes eleven, and twenty-four years are paid for in fifty-odd lines.

Scene 13
Helen crosses the stage for the scholars, the Old Man pleads, the bond is renewed, and Faustus takes Helen as his paramour.Key lines “I see an angel hovers o’er thy head…” · “I do repent; and yet I do despair…” · “Was this the face that launch’d a thousand ships.” · “Her lips suck forth my soul…” · “I will be Paris, and for love of thee…” · “His faith is great; I cannot touch his soul…”
Scene 14
Faustus confesses to the scholars, then faces eleven, half past and midnight alone.Key lines “the serpent that tempted Eve may be saved, but not Faustus.” · “Stand still, you ever-moving spheres of heaven…” · “O, I’ll leap up to my God!—Who pulls…” · “Ugly hell, gape not! come not, Lucifer! I’ll burn my books!—Ah, Mephistophilis!”
Epilogue
The Chorus draws the moral: wonder at unlawful things, and do not practise them.Key line “Cut is the branch that might have grown full straight…”
