As You Like It · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1590
Lodge’s Rosalynde published The prose romance that supplies almost all of the plot, including the wrestling, the disguise and the shepherds.
1593
Christopher Marlowe killed at Deptford Phebe’s ‘Dead Shepherd’ in 3.5 is the play’s direct salute to him, and the line she quotes is his.
1598
Marlowe’s Hero and Leander published The poem Phebe quotes, and the source of the play’s one open tribute to another writer.
1599
The Globe opens on Bankside The theatre for which the play was almost certainly written, and the setting of the joke behind ‘All the world’s a stage’.
1599
As You Like It most likely written Usually dated to 1599 or early 1600, close to Henry V and Julius Caesar.
1600
Entered in the Stationers’ Register The entry orders publication to be stayed, and no quarto ever appeared.
1623
First Folio The only early text, printed among the comedies.
1740
Restored to the London stage After long neglect the play returned to the repertory at Drury Lane, and Rosalind became one of the great parts for an actress.
1936
Paul Czinner’s film With Elisabeth Bergner as Rosalind and Laurence Olivier as Orlando; the first sound film of the play.
1967
All-male production at the National Theatre Directed by Clifford Williams, it revived the Elizabethan casting convention and reopened the argument about what the disguise means.

Places

Forest of Arden, Warwickshire
The wooded country north of Stratford, and the English name behind the play’s forest.
The Ardennes
Lodge sets his romance in France; Shakespeare keeps the French court and lets the two Ardens overlap.
Stratford-upon-Avon
Shakespeare’s mother was Mary Arden, from a family of the Warwickshire woodland.
The Globe, Bankside
Opened in 1599, and the likely first home of the play and of the seven-ages speech.
Deptford
Where Marlowe, the play’s dead shepherd, was killed in 1593.
