As You Like It · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The wrestling match, and the flight to Arden

Orlando has been kept as a servant by the brother who was told to educate him, and wins a wrestling match he was meant to lose. Duke Frederick, who has already usurped his own brother, turns on Rosalind for being her father’s daughter, and the two cousins leave together with the court fool and a plan involving a doublet and hose.

1.1
Orlando complains that his brother keeps him worse than the horses, and Oliver arranges for the wrestler to break his neck.
1.2
Rosalind and Celia try to talk the challenger out of it, Orlando wins, and Rosalind gives him a chain and no explanation.
1.3
Frederick exiles Rosalind on ten days’ notice; Celia decides to go too, and Rosalind decides to go as a man.

Act II Arriving in Arden

The forest is introduced twice over: once by the old duke, who finds sermons in stones, and once by a cold, hungry fool who observes that he was better off at home. Orlando arrives starving with an old servant on his back, draws his sword on a banquet, and is fed. Jaques delivers the seven ages to a stage that immediately contradicts him.

2.1
Duke Senior praises the woods and then proposes to go and kill deer, which Jaques has already objected to on their behalf.
2.2
Frederick discovers his daughter gone and blames Orlando’s brother for it.
2.3
The old servant warns Orlando that Oliver means to burn his lodging, and offers him his own life’s wages.
2.4
The travellers reach Arden exhausted, overhear a shepherd in love, and buy a cottage and a flock.
2.5
Amiens sings; Jaques supplies a verse of his own calling everyone who came here an ass.
2.6
Orlando carries the old man to shelter and goes to find food.
2.7
Jaques reports his meeting with a motley fool and delivers the great set piece, just as Orlando carries Adam in.

Act III Poems on trees, and a cure for love

Orlando covers the forest with bad verses, Touchstone improves them by parody, and Rosalind, safely disguised as a man, offers to cure him by making him woo a Ganymede who will play Rosalind. Meanwhile Touchstone courts a goatherd, Corin explains what a shepherd actually does all day, and Phebe falls for the wrong person.

3.1
Oliver is stripped of his lands and sent to find his brother or lose everything.
3.2
Touchstone parodies Orlando’s verses, Corin and Touchstone debate court and country, and Ganymede proposes the cure.
3.3
The fool arranges a marriage under a bush with a hedge-priest and is talked out of it by Jaques.
3.4
Rosalind is furious that a lover can be late; Corin offers a pageant of scorn to watch.
3.5
Ganymede tells the shepherdess to take the offer she has, and she falls in love with him on the spot.

Act IV The lessons

The centre of the play is two long conversations in which a woman pretending to be a man pretending to be herself teaches the man who loves her what love is likely to be like once it is domestic. Then a bloodied handkerchief arrives, the brother who came to kill Orlando has been rescued by him, and the disguise starts to cost something.

4.1
Rosalind disposes of dying for love, marries Orlando in a mock ceremony and promises to be jealous as a Barbary cock-pigeon.
4.2
A hunting song, sung by the men who were criticised for hunting in 2.1.
4.3
Oliver brings the handkerchief and the story of the lioness; Ganymede faints and then claims it was counterfeited.

Act V Four weddings

Four couples, one god of marriage and a conversion offstage: the play gathers every thread together and knows how implausible it is being. Jaques declines the dance, Duke Frederick meets a religious man on the edge of the wood and gives back a dukedom, and Rosalind steps out of the play to ask for applause in a woman’s clothes and a boy actor’s body.

5.1
Touchstone frightens off a rival suitor with a list of ways to kill him.
5.2
Oliver and Celia fall in love at sight; four voices build a formal round on the same refrain.
5.3
Two pages sing to Touchstone and Audrey, and Touchstone thinks little of the tune.
5.4
Rosalind returns as herself, four marriages are made, news arrives of Frederick’s conversion, and Jaques leaves for a cave.
