The Importance of Being Earnest · Oscar Wilde

What happens

Act I A cigarette case and an interview in Half-Moon Street

Algernon produces an inscribed cigarette case and forces Jack to confess that he is Jack in the country and Ernest in town. Gwendolen accepts a proposal on the strength of a name, and her mother interviews the candidate, finds his origins to be a railway cloakroom, and refuses.

Act I, the cigarette case
Lane and the cucumber sandwiches; Algernon extracts the truth about Cecily and the double life.
Act I, Bunbury explained
Jack’s imaginary brother is matched against Algernon’s imaginary invalid.
Act I, the proposal
Gwendolen announces that her ideal has always been to love a man called Ernest, and Jack proposes on his knees.
Act I, the interview
Lady Bracknell’s examination of income, politics, property and parentage ends at the hand-bag.

Act II Algernon as Ernest in the garden at Woolton

Algernon arrives in the country as the wicked brother and is engaged to Cecily inside half an hour, having been engaged to her in her diary for months already. Jack comes home in mourning for a brother who is standing on the lawn, and the two women meet over tea and discover they are engaged to the same name.

Act II, the German lesson
Miss Prism, Dr Chasuble and a chapter on the Fall of the Rupee that may be omitted.
Act II, Ernest arrives
Algernon presents himself as Jack’s brother and Cecily produces the record of their engagement.
Act II, mourning
Jack returns in black to announce a death that contradicts the guest in the garden.
Act II, tea
Gwendolen and Cecily compare diaries, sugar and cake, and conclude they have both been deceived.
Act II, christenings
Both men book the same clergyman for the same afternoon and the same name.

Act III A hand-bag, and a christening cancelled

Lady Bracknell arrives and approves of Cecily once she hears the size of her fortune, Jack withholds his consent as Cecily’s guardian until his own engagement is allowed, and then Miss Prism is recognised, the hand-bag is produced and Jack turns out to be Algernon’s elder brother. His father’s name is looked up in an Army List and is, inevitably, Ernest.

Act III, the fortune
Lady Bracknell’s objections to Cecily evaporate on hearing the figure in the Funds.
Act III, deadlock
Jack refuses consent for Cecily until his own engagement is allowed.
Act III, Miss Prism
A governess, a perambulator, a three-volume novel and a hand-bag left at Victoria Station.
Act III, the Army Lists
Jack’s real name is found in print, and the title of the play is spoken as its last line.
