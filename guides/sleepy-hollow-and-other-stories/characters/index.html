Sleepy Hollow and Other American Halloween Stories · Washington Irving, Edgar Allan Poe and others

Characters

Ichabod Crane

The Connecticut schoolmaster of Sleepy Hollow; first appears The Legend of Sleepy Hollow

Lank, greedy, superstitious and calculating, he courts an heiress for her father’s barns and reads Cotton Mather for pleasure after dark. Irving gives him no dignity and a good deal of sympathy, and then removes him from the story without saying what happened.

Brom Bones

Abraham Van Brunt, the local champion; first appears The Legend of Sleepy Hollow

Broad, good-humoured and fond of practical jokes, he is the obvious husband and the obvious suspect. Irving’s last paragraphs let him look knowing whenever the pumpkin is mentioned, which is as close to a solution as the story offers.

Katrina Van Tassel

The farmer’s daughter, and his only heir; first appears The Legend of Sleepy Hollow

Eighteen, plump, and perfectly aware of what she is worth. She is described in the same sentences as her father’s land and livestock, which is Irving’s joke about the courtship and his most pointed observation about it.

The Headless Horseman

The Hessian trooper of the local stories; first appears The Legend of Sleepy Hollow

A cavalryman whose head was carried off by a cannon-ball in the Revolution, said to ride nightly in search of it and to be obliged to return to the churchyard before daybreak. He exists in the village’s talk long before he appears on the road, which is how Irving has it both ways.

Young Goodman Brown

Hawthorne’s Salem husband, out for one night; first appears Young Goodman Brown

He leaves his wife Faith at sunset on an errand he will not describe and meets, in the forest, everybody he has been taught to respect. Whether the meeting happened is left open; what it does to the rest of his life is not.

Poe's narrators

The men explaining themselves in three of the stories; first appears The Tell-Tale Heart

Each opens by disputing an accusation nobody in the text has made (that he is mad, or nervous, or fanciful), and then supplies the account that convicts him. Poe’s method is to make the reader the jury and the confession the evidence.

The narrator of The Yellow Wall-Paper

A woman under a rest cure, writing in secret; first appears The Yellow Wall-Paper

Forbidden to work, she gives the pattern on the wall the attention she would have given a book, and finds a woman behind it. The story is the sharpest thing in the volume about who is believed, and it was written by someone who had been through the treatment.

The Outsider

Lovecraft’s narrator, climbing out; first appears The Outsider

He has lived in a castle among books and cannot remember anyone speaking to him. The story is a single movement upward towards light and company, and its last paragraph rearranges everything that came before it.
