Village superstition

The collection’s hundred years runs from a community’s shared ghost to a single mind’s private one. Irving’s horseman belongs to everyone in the Hollow; Poe’s heart is audible to one man; Gilman’s woman is behind one room’s paper.

Through the book The fear moves steadily indoors and inward, and the later stories have no village left to consult.

In the text

A drowsy, dreamy influence seems to hang over the land, and to pervade the very atmosphere.

The Legend of Sleepy Hollow

Two adjectives mean nearly the same thing, and the verb will not commit: ‘seems’. Irving builds the supernatural out of climate and hearsay, so that nothing in the story ever has to be true.

the apparition of a figure on horseback without a head

The Legend of Sleepy Hollow

The most famous image in American folklore is introduced as a piece of village reputation, at second hand, several thousand words before anything rides. Irving lets the community invent the ghost, and then sends Ichabod out to meet what it invented.

Narrators

Nearly every story arrives through a teller with an interest: a chronicler quoting a village, a murderer insisting on his sanity, a coroner’s court reading from a dead man’s diary, a woman writing where she has been told not to.

Through the book Irving’s narrator is amused and detached; by the 1890s the tellers are inside the events, and by The Outsider the teller does not know what he is.

In the text

this sequestered glen has long been known by the name of SLEEPY HOLLOW, and its rustic lads are called the Sleepy Hollow Boys throughout all the neighboring country

The Legend of Sleepy Hollow

The place is named by its reputation, and the capitals make the name sound quoted from local speech. Irving establishes from the start that everything in the story is what people say about the place.

was found the hat of the unfortunate Ichabod, and close beside it a shattered pumpkin

The Legend of Sleepy Hollow

The evidence is laid out and not interpreted: a hat, a pumpkin, hoof-prints at furious speed. Irving gives the reader everything needed to solve it and then spends his last pages letting the village prefer the ghost.

Concealment

Hawthorne’s veil, Poe’s floorboards and plastered wall, Bierce’s boarded window: again and again the volume hides something and then listens to it. Concealment is the plot of at least half these stories.

Through the book In Hawthorne the guilt is general and unnameable; in Poe it is specific and buried; in Bierce it is a fact the narrator withholds until the final line.

Puritan inheritance

Salem, Mather, the meeting-house and the devil in the woods sit under the American stories the way old churches sit under English ones. Irving’s schoolmaster reads witchcraft history for entertainment; Hawthorne’s characters live in its consequences.

Through the book What begins as a comic superstition in Irving becomes, in Hawthorne, a religion that can ruin a man in one night.

In the text

Spare the rod and spoil the child

The Legend of Sleepy Hollow

A proverb from Proverbs, used to license a schoolmaster’s caning and reported with a perfectly straight face. It is the first sign that this narrator will tell you what people believe without ever telling you what he believes.

his appetite for the marvellous, and his powers of digesting it, were equally extraordinary

The Legend of Sleepy Hollow

The eating metaphor runs through the whole portrait: the man who can out-eat anybody also swallows every story he is told. Irving makes credulity a form of greed, which sets up the ride home exactly.

American settings

A Dutch valley, a swamp, a forest, a tarn, a frontier clearing. The settings are local, named and recent, and the supernatural in them belongs to a particular soil, a long way from old castles.

Through the book Usher’s tarn and Lovecraft’s castle are the exceptions, unplaced and old, and even the castle turns out to be somewhere else.

Money and land

Irving’s ghost story is also a story about a poor schoolmaster’s designs on a rich man’s barns, and The Devil and Tom Walker is about usury. The supernatural keeps arriving where property is at stake.

Through the book The theme fades as the stories move indoors: by Gilman and Freeman the question is authority inside a household, no longer property.

In the text

He was tall, but exceedingly lank, with narrow shoulders, long arms and legs, hands that dangled a mile out of his sleeves

The Legend of Sleepy Hollow

The comic exaggeration is done with exact parts (shoulders, arms, sleeves), so the reader can see him before the sentence ends. Irving is teaching American prose how to do caricature without cruelty, and the technique passes straight into Twain.

a huge feeder, and, though lank, had the dilating powers of an anaconda

The Legend of Sleepy Hollow

A schoolmaster compared to a snake swallowing something whole, in a sentence that is entirely affectionate. The joke also tells you what he wants from the farm, which is everything on it.
