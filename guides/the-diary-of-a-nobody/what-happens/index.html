The Diary of a Nobody · George and Weedon Grossmith

What happens

Chapters I–IV The scraper, the enamel paint and the Mansion House

Pooter takes The Laurels, resolves to keep a diary, and begins the record with tradesmen, a broken bell and a boot-scraper that throws every caller down the side steps. He falls out with Gowing and Cummings over a walk to a public-house, makes two jokes nobody wants, paints most of the house red and is astonished by an invitation to the Lord Mayor’s reception, at which he falls over in front of the room.

Chapter I
The six-roomed residence, two pounds off the rent for the railway, the butterman who doubts that a City clerk can be a gentleman, and the curate’s torn trousers.
Chapter II
Pitt tells him to keep his hair on, Farmerson bores through the gas-pipe, the dry rot joke convulses him for a whole night, and Stillbrook gets into the Cow and Hedge while Pooter waits outside.
Chapter III
Merton’s free passes turn out to be worthless, the tie falls into the pit, the enamel paint reaches the bath, and the pun on his friends’ names empties the room.
Chapter IV
A week of preparation, a fall on the back-door flags, and an evening in which Perkupp turns out to have arranged the invitation and Farmerson turns out to know a sheriff.

Chapters V–VII A quarrel, a seaside holiday and a son out of work

Carrie states the case against her husband’s conduct at the ball in one unbroken speech, the local paper prints his name as Pewter, and Gowing’s stick has been enamelled black without permission. The Pooters take their holiday at Broadstairs with Lupin, who has been dismissed from the bank at Oldham. Back at home the diary itself is torn up for firewood, and Lupin announces an engagement.

Chapter V
The headache, the lobster, Carrie’s indictment, the blackened stick, two failed corrections in the Blackfriars Bi-weekly News, and the joke about frayed shirts that the office enjoys and the shirtmaker does not.
Chapter VI
Willie has become Lupin, has resigned from the bank, and has in fact been dismissed; the family holiday follows, with billiards, music halls and a game called Cutlets.
Chapter VII
Mrs. James redecorates Carrie’s taste, the neighbours throw a cracker at Pooter’s hat, five or six weeks of the diary are burnt, and Perkupp finds Lupin a place.

Chapters VIII–XIII Daisy Mutlar, the Irving imitations and an insulting card

Lupin’s engagement fills the house. The Pooters give the grandest party of their lives, at which the champagne runs out just as Perkupp arrives. The engagement breaks off and is repaired, Burwin-Fosselton brings his Henry Irving make-up night after night, Padge takes the best chair and says nothing else, and Christmas brings a card that Pooter is certain was meant to insult him.

Chapter VIII
A fiancée older than Lupin, a brother who does imitations for an hour without stopping, a firework evening that goes wrong, and a row between servant and charwoman over the missing pages.
Chapter IX
One evening, one chapter. Gowing greets the host by criticising his trousers, the Blondin Donkey is performed, and Mr. Perkupp arrives to find nothing left on the table.
Chapter X
The morning after, a pun about side dishes, a blanc-mange that returns to the table at every meal for five days, and Lupin forbidding her name to be mentioned.
Chapter XI
Burwin-Fosselton insists on the double name and the make-up, Padge laughs at other people’s injuries, the odd sock joke lands with everyone but Pooter, and a long impertinent letter arrives which Lupin has already read and approved.
Chapter XII
Carrie walks out during a reading from it, the household laughs at the idea of publication, Pooter invokes Evelyn and Pepys, and the engagement is on again.
Chapter XIII
Ten shillings offered for the sender’s name, a good Christmas at his mother-in-law’s, a punch in the dark, and the discovery that the card was addressed to L. Pooter all along.

Chapters XIV–XVIII A hundred a year, a collapse of chlorates and a ball at East Acton

Perkupp promotes Pooter to senior clerk and raises his salary by a hundred pounds, and Lupin answers the announcement with a speech that copies its wording and doubles its figure. The Parachikka Chlorates collapse, Daisy marries Murray Posh, and Lupin is taken into Perkupp’s office. A stylographic pen, a Volunteer ball and a cabman follow.

Chapter XIV
Four days of suspense outside Perkupp’s door, a hundred pounds a year, Lupin’s two hundred in one day, a pony-trap, and an invitation from Gowing to a house where Gowing is not at home.
Chapter XV
Gowing’s letter that went astray, a Sunday drive in which Lupin chaffs a cartload of roughs while his father rides backwards, and the first sight of the three-shilling hat millionaire.
Chapter XVI
Twenty pounds sold for two, Cummings left with the whole loss, Job Cleanands absconded, and a letter announcing Daisy’s marriage to Murray Posh.
Chapter XVII
Lupin smashes a hat marked Posh’s Patent, is taken into the firm, and Pooter lies awake planning the nails they will hammer in together.
Chapter XVIII
The pen that oozes, an evening of civility that ends in a bill for three pounds and sixpence, and a cabman who pulls the diarist’s beard while a policeman watches.

Chapters XIX–XXIII and Chapter the Last Huttle, the séances and the freehold

An old schoolfellow’s uncle shows Pooter his family portraits, two of which turn out to be of the dead. At Franching’s dinner the American Hardfur Huttle defines respectability as insipidness and asks Pooter to agree. Lupin is dismissed for handing the firm’s best client to a rival, is rewarded by the rival, and moves to Bayswater; Mrs. James brings table-turning into the house; and Perkupp closes the book by buying the Pooters their home.

Chapter XIX
Watney Lodge, a collie that licks the blacking off his boots, two portraits of the dead admired in the wrong terms, and a dream that nobody present will listen to.
Chapter XX
The ladies decline to leave the table, respectability is defined and demolished, Pooter checks his made-up tie in the glass, and Carrie says on the way home that Huttle is exactly like Lupin.
Chapter XXI
Crowbillon takes his custom elsewhere on Lupin’s advice, Pooter writes sixteen pages to win him back, and Gylterson’s engage Lupin at two hundred a year.
Chapter XXII
A kicked shin at Sutton, a table that spells NIPUL and POSH, and a sealed question whose answer, when the seal is broken, turns out to be a joke about Pooter’s age.
Chapter XXIII
Rooms at two guineas a week, a dinner at which everyone but the Pooters knew it was full dress, and one sentence of political doubt on the drive home.
Chapter the Last
Perkupp buys the freehold of The Laurels and presents it to him, telegrams go out to Gowing and Cummings, and the post brings Lupin’s news of another engagement.
