George and Weedon Grossmith

The Diary of a Nobody

A study guide

Charles Pooter, a City clerk of twenty-one years’ standing, moves into a rented house in Holloway and begins to write down what happens to him. Almost nothing does. A boot-scraper trips his visitors, he paints the bath red and thinks he is bleeding to death, he makes a pun about Gowing coming and Cummings going and nobody laughs. His son Lupin loses one post, gains another, loses a fiancée, gains a fortune and despises his father throughout. Pooter records all of it in the same careful, aggrieved, pleased-with-himself voice, and the joke is that he keeps the score of a game he does not know he is losing. At the end his employer buys him the freehold of the house, and the last word in the book is Lupin’s.

Written 1888–89; first published 1892.
