The Chimes · Charles Dickens

Context

Dates

1798
Malthus’s Essay on the Principle of Population The argument that population outruns food, and that the poor should put off marriage, stands behind Filer’s claim that Meg and Richard have no business to marry.
1812
Dickens born in Portsmouth His family’s later debts, and his own months of factory work as a boy, gave him first-hand knowledge of the poverty the gentlemen in The Chimes discuss from a distance.
1834
Poor Law Amendment Act Parishes were grouped into Unions with workhouses. Will Fern keeps Lilian out of the Union twenty-eight miles from his home, where his own father was taken when he could no longer work.
1843
A Christmas Carol published Dickens’s first Christmas book appeared on 19 December and set the pattern The Chimes follows: a single night, supernatural visitors and a waking change.
1844
Dickens moves his family to Genoa He wrote The Chimes there in the autumn, and his letters from the city mention the bells he heard.
1844
Readings to friends before publication Dickens came back to London to correct proofs and read the new book aloud to friends. Daniel Maclise drew one of these readings, in John Forster’s rooms, in pencil.
1844
The Chimes published Issued on 16 December as the second of Dickens’s Christmas books.
1845
The Cricket on the Hearth The third Christmas book, a domestic story with far less politics in it.
1846
The Battle of Life The fourth Christmas book, a love story set on the site of an old battlefield.
1848
The Haunted Man and the Ghost’s Bargain The fifth and last of the Christmas books, published on 19 December.
1870
Dickens dies at Gad’s Hill He died at his house in Kent on 9 June.
1876
Forster’s bequest John Forster’s library and papers, including the manuscript of The Chimes, passed to the South Kensington Museum, now the Victoria and Albert Museum.

Places

City of London
Trotty’s church is never named. Cute expects to speak of Sir Joseph’s son in the Common Council, and the banker Deedles is high in office in the Goldsmiths’ Company, which puts the gentlemen’s world in the City.
The Thames
The book calls it only ‘the River’, the place in the vision where Meg goes with her baby on the last night of the year.
Dorset
Lilian’s mother died in Dorsetshire, the detail that lets Mrs Chickenstalker recognise the child in the last pages.
Genoa
Dickens was living here in 1844 and wrote The Chimes in the city.
Lincoln's Inn Fields
John Forster’s rooms, where Dickens read the story to friends before publication and Maclise drew the scene.
Victoria and Albert Museum
The manuscript of The Chimes is kept here in the Forster Collection.
