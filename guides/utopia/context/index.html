Utopia · Thomas More

Context

Dates

1478
Thomas More born in London Son of John More, a lawyer who later became a judge of the King’s Bench. The year usually given is 1478.
1490
A page in Cardinal Morton’s household, c. 1490 As a boy More waited at the table of John Morton, Archbishop of Canterbury and Chancellor, whom Hythloday describes in Book I.
1497
The Cornish rising crushed Hythloday says he was in England ‘not long after the rebellion in the West was suppressed’, which places Morton’s dinner in about this year.
1507
Vespucci’s voyages in print An account of Amerigo Vespucci’s four voyages was printed at Saint-Dié with a new map of the world. Hythloday claims to have sailed on three of them.
1511
The Praise of Folly printed Erasmus wrote it in More’s London house and titled it Moriae Encomium, a pun on his friend’s name. Utopia comes from the same circle and the same habit of serious joking.
1513
Machiavelli writes The Prince Another out-of-office official’s advice to rulers, taking the opposite view of how far a counsellor should accommodate power. It was not printed until 1532.
1515
Begun during the embassy to Flanders In May More went with Cuthbert Tunstall to negotiate with the commissioners of Charles, Prince of Castile, and in Antwerp became friends with Peter Giles. The island of Book II was probably written later in the year.
1516
First edition, Louvain Printed in Latin by Dirk Martens late in the year, with a woodcut map of the island, a Utopian alphabet and letters and verses by More’s friends.
1518
Revised editions printed at Basel Johann Froben printed revised editions at Basel, with woodcuts by Ambrosius Holbein.
1529
More becomes Lord Chancellor He succeeded Wolsey in October, in the office Morton had held, and resigned in 1532 as Henry VIII moved against the Church.
1535
More executed Beheaded on Tower Hill on 6 July, having refused to accept Henry VIII as supreme head of the church in England.
1551
The first English translation Ralph Robinson’s version was printed in the reign of Edward VI, sixteen years after More’s death.
1684
Burnet’s translation Gilbert Burnet, later Bishop of Salisbury, translated the book soon after the execution of his friend Lord William Russell. His English is the text quoted here.
1935
More declared a saint Pope Pius XI canonised More, with John Fisher, four hundred years after their deaths.

Places

Antwerp, Cathedral of Our Lady
More first sees Hythloday after mass at ‘St. Mary’s, which is the chief church’ of the city; the talk moves to the garden of More’s lodging.
Bruges
Where Henry VIII’s envoys met the commissioners of Charles, Prince of Castile, and failed to agree.
Brussels
Where the Prince’s commissioners went for instructions, leaving More free to visit Antwerp.
Lambeth Palace, London
The London residence of the Archbishops of Canterbury, and so of Morton, in whose household More served as a boy. The book does not say where the dinner took place.
Leuven (Louvain)
Where Dirk Martens printed the first edition in 1516.
Basel
Where Johann Froben printed the revised editions of 1518.
Calicut (Kozhikode)
Where Hythloday, having travelled by way of Ceylon, found Portuguese ships to carry him home.
Tower Hill, London
Where More was beheaded in 1535.
