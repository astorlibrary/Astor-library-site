Daniel Defoe

Robinson Crusoe

A study guide

A young man who will not stay in the middle station of life is enslaved at Salé, prospers in Brazil, joins a voyage to buy Africans and is wrecked alone for twenty-eight years. Most of the book is work: salvage, fencing, baking, pottery, a canoe he cannot move to the water. Defoe makes an account-book out of a life, and the same habit that counts the goatskins also counts the mercies and calls the total Providence.

Written 1719; first published 1719.
