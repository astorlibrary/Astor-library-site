Robinson Crusoe · Daniel Defoe

Context

Dates

1632
The novel’s date for Crusoe’s birth: 1632, at York The first sentence of the novel gives the year, the city and his father’s origins in Bremen.
1659
The novel’s shipwreck: 30 September 1659 30 September 1659, the date the journal opens on and the day notched at the head of the post.
1660
Daniel Defoe born in London The son of a tallow chandler, educated for the Dissenting ministry and afterwards a merchant, bankrupt and pamphleteer.
1686
The novel’s rescue: Crusoe leaves the island, 19 December 1686 19 December 1686, after eight-and-twenty years, two months and nineteen days.
1703
Defoe pilloried for The Shortest Way with the Dissenters A satire taken for the thing it imitated: an early sign of how completely he could disappear into a voice.
1704
Alexander Selkirk is put ashore on Juan Fernández He was taken off in 1709; his case was widely reported and is the nearest thing the novel has to a documented source.
1719
Robinson Crusoe published 25 April, by William Taylor, with no author’s name and a title page promising a life written by Crusoe himself.
1719
The Farther Adventures of Robinson Crusoe published Published in August of the same year, taking Crusoe back to the island and on to China and Siberia.
1720
Serious Reflections During the Life of Robinson Crusoe published A third volume of essays, in which the story is defended as an allegory of Defoe’s own life.
1731
Defoe dies in Ropemaker’s Alley, London In lodgings, evading creditors, having written more than most of his contemporaries had read.
1762
Rousseau makes it the one book in Émile’s library In Émile, Rousseau recommends Robinson Crusoe as the whole of a boy’s early reading, and its career as an educational text begins.

Places

York
Crusoe’s birthplace, and the middle station of life he refuses.
Hull
Where he goes on board in September 1651 without his parents’ knowledge.
Yarmouth Roads
The anchorage where his first ship founders, and where he ignores the first warning.
Salé
The Moroccan port whose rovers take him; he is a slave there for two years.
Bahia, Brazil
Where he lands after his escape and builds the plantation that makes him rich in his absence.
Mouth of the Orinoco
The stretch of coast the island is placed off, which is why the canoes and the Spaniards come from the mainland.
Lisbon
Where the old Portuguese captain gives an honest account of the Brazilian plantation and pays what he can of what he owes.

Critics

James Joyce

Lecture on Daniel Defoe, Università Popolare, Trieste (1912)

Joyce called Crusoe the true symbol of the British conquest and found the whole Anglo-Saxon spirit in him: manly independence, unconscious cruelty, persistence, slow but effective intelligence and sexual apathy.

Ian Watt

The Rise of the Novel (1957)

Watt made Crusoe the type of economic individualism. He keeps accounts of his life as of a business, counts the gains of his island against the losses, and turns even Providence into a kind of bookkeeping; for Watt this is why the novel begins with him.
