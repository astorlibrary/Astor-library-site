The Haunted Man and the Ghost’s Bargain · Charles Dickens

What happens

Chapter I The Gift Bestowed

Redlaw sits alone at twilight in his room, part library and part laboratory, in an old college squeezed among the streets of London. William Swidger, the keeper, brings his dinner; William’s wife Milly decorates the room with holly; William’s father Philip, who is eighty-seven, remembers Christmases back to his childhood and repeats the prayer under the founder’s portrait, Lord, keep my memory green. Milly mentions a sick, poor student in Jerusalem Buildings who will not let Redlaw help him. When they have gone a phantom in Redlaw’s likeness recites his history and offers to cancel his memory of sorrow, wrong and trouble. He accepts, and is told that he will destroy the same memory in everyone he approaches. A ragged boy Milly has taken in runs into the room, and Redlaw finds he feels nothing for him.

Chapter II The Gift Diffused

In the Tetterbys’ crowded shop in Jerusalem Buildings, Mrs Tetterby confesses to her husband that she had wished she had never married, then remembered what their hard years have given them. Redlaw arrives looking for the student and touches Mr Tetterby, and when he looks back from the stairs the family has turned sullen and cold. The student, Edmund Denham, tells him that his real name is Longford: he is the son of the friend who wronged Redlaw. Under Redlaw’s influence he turns on Milly. Redlaw then follows the boy, who alone is unchanged, to a lodging-house among the arches of a viaduct. There a young woman with a bruised face insists that nobody hurt her, and Philip and William are watching over William’s elder brother George, who is dying. George begins to repent and warns that a ruined man who was in the room a moment ago means to kill himself; at Redlaw’s approach George hardens, Philip disowns him, and William turns on his father. Back in his room, with Milly at the door begging for help, Redlaw calls on the Phantom to take the gift back.

Chapter III The Gift Reversed

Towards dawn Christmas music reaches Redlaw and moves him to tears, and the Phantom returns, less cruel, holding the hand of Milly’s shadow. It tells him he cannot repair the harm himself and must go to her, and explains the boy: a child who has never had a humanising memory, the image of what Redlaw chose to become. In the morning the Tetterbys, still quarrelling, recover the moment Milly comes down the street, and Edmund begs her pardon. Milly has found the ruined man in time: he is Longford, Edmund’s father and Redlaw’s old friend, and Redlaw, who cannot remember the wrong, pays for him to go away and says he would forgive him if he knew for what. When Milly speaks of the baby she lost, Redlaw’s memory returns. He vows to protect and teach the boy and gives a Christmas dinner for the Swidgers in the old hall, under the portrait.
