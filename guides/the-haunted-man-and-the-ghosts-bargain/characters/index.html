The Haunted Man and the Ghost’s Bargain · Charles Dickens

Characters

Redlaw

A learned chemist and lecturer at an old London college; first appears Chapter I

Famous for his chemistry and loved by his students, he lives alone with the memory of a neglected childhood, a friend who took the woman he loved, and a sister who died. The bargain leaves his learning intact and takes everything that made it humane. He grows cold, then frightened of himself, and spends the rest of the night trying to reverse what he chose. By the end the learned man walks beside the keeper’s wife as though their positions were reversed and she were the teacher.

The Phantom

Redlaw’s double; first appears Chapter I

An awful likeness of Redlaw that gathers out of the shadow behind his chair, with his features, his bright eyes and his grizzled hair. It tells his story in the first person and admits that its words are an echo of his own mind. It returns in the last chapter less cruel, holding the hand of Milly’s shadow, and tells him to seek her out.

Milly Swidger

The keeper’s wife, called Mrs William; first appears Chapter I

Small, quiet and neat, she mothers the students, nurses the sick student in secret, brings a starving boy home from a doorstep, and turns down Redlaw’s purse. She lost a baby who lived only a few days, and says the memory has made her kinder to every child she sees. Wherever she goes the gift loses its hold, though she has no idea she is the cause; when she tells Redlaw about her child, his memory comes back.

William Swidger

Keeper of the college; first appears Chapter I

Fresh-coloured and always busy, he agrees with everyone before they have finished and prefaces most of what he says with That’s what I always say. He is devoted to his wife and his father until the gift reaches him at his brother’s deathbed, when he looks at his father and sees only a calendar of meals.

Philip Swidger

William’s father, custodian of the college for more than fifty years; first appears Chapter I

He tells everyone that he is eighty-seven and remembers every Christmas since he was a small boy, including the one when his mother died. He knows the founder’s prayer by heart. At his eldest son’s deathbed he loses his memories and his love together, disowns George and nibbles at a sprig of holly, and he gets both back when Milly comes home.

Edmund Denham

A poor student in Redlaw’s class; first appears Chapter II

Ill with a fever and too proud to go home or ask for help, he lodges above the Tetterbys’ shop and is nursed by Milly. He has studied under an assumed name: he is the son of Longford, the friend who wronged Redlaw, and has kept away from his teacher out of shame and respect. Touched by the gift he treats Milly as a servant who wants paying; restored, he begs her forgiveness, and the young woman he is to marry arrives at the college to find him.

Longford

Redlaw’s old friend; first appears Chapter II

The friend who won the woman Redlaw loved, and whom Redlaw’s sister also loved. He married her, left her and their son, gambled and sank, and is found in the lodging-house, penniless and meaning to kill himself. Milly traces him in time, and Redlaw, who can no longer remember the wrong, pays for him to go somewhere he can do no more harm.

George Swidger

Philip’s eldest son; first appears Chapter II

His mother’s favourite, he has wasted his life in vice and is dying on a truckle-bed in a lodging-house for travellers. His father’s memories of him as a child begin to soften him, and he uses his last strength to warn that the man who brought his family will kill himself. Redlaw’s presence hardens him; Milly’s visit at sunrise brings him to repentance.

The boy

A child with no name; first appears Chapter I

About six years old, barefoot and in rags, found by Milly shivering on a doorstep. He has no name and does not know what it means to live anywhere. Redlaw’s gift has no effect on him, and the Phantom explains why: nothing kind has ever happened to him that could be taken away. By the end Redlaw vows to protect and teach him, and he will stay only by Milly.

Mr and Mrs Tetterby

A newsman and his wife in Jerusalem Buildings; first appears Chapter II

Adolphus Tetterby, who trades as A. Tetterby and Co., Newsmen, has tried and failed at every line from sweets to tobacco. His wife Sophia is twice his size. They have seven sons and a baby girl, Sally, called Moloch, and they are loving and generous with very little. Touched by Redlaw’s gift they find each other plain and their marriage a sacrifice; Milly’s arrival in the street restores them.

Johnny Tetterby

The Tetterbys’ son and nurse to the baby; first appears Chapter II

The small boy who carries the baby Moloch everywhere, staggering under her weight and missing everything worth seeing. Under the gift he slaps her and says he will go for a soldier because there are no babies in the Army; the moment he sees Milly coming he picks the baby up as tenderly as an old nurse.
