Measure for Measure · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1565
Cinthio’s Hecatommithi The collection containing the Epitia story, in which the sister yields to the magistrate and the brother is executed anyway.
1578
George Whetstone’s Promos and Cassandra A two-part English play on the same material, and Shakespeare’s nearest model in his own language.
1599
James VI’s Basilikon Doron The king’s own book of advice on rule, reprinted in London in 1603; its arguments about how a prince should hold himself were fresh when this play was written.
1603
James VI of Scotland becomes James I of England A new ruler with known views on justice, clemency and the private conduct of magistrates.
1603
Plague closes the London playhouses A long closure that pushed the King’s Men out to the provinces and back to court performance.
1604
The play is written Usually dated to this year, alongside Othello and the other so-called problem plays.
1604
First recorded performance at court Played on 26 December during the Christmas season, before James I.
1606
The Act to Restrain Abuses of Players Oaths and holy names were legislated off the public stage, one reason Folio texts of the period can differ from what was first spoken.
1623
The First Folio The only early text of the play, printed among the comedies.
1662
Davenant’s The Law Against Lovers A Restoration adaptation that grafted Beatrice and Benedick onto this plot and gave the ending the romance the original withholds.

Places

Vienna
The play’s setting: palace, nunnery, prison and city gate, imagined and not observed.
Whitehall, London
The royal palace where the first recorded performance was given at Christmas 1604.
Bankside, Southwark
The Globe’s own neighbourhood of theatres, brothels and prisons, which the play’s Vienna resembles more than any Austrian city.
Ferrara
The city of Cinthio, the Italian writer whose story of a corrupt magistrate lies behind the plot.
Warsaw
Poland is where the Duke announces he is going and where he never goes.
