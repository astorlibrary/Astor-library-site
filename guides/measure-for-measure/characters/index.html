Measure for Measure · William Shakespeare

Characters

Duke Vincentio

Duke of Vienna, disguised for most of the play as Friar Lodowick; first appears 1.1

He owns the problem and delegates the correction, which he admits privately is a way of avoiding the odium of enforcing laws he let sleep. As a friar he is everywhere, hearing confessions he has no right to hear and arranging the outcome scene by scene, and the play makes him charming enough that his manipulations take a while to look like manipulations.

Angelo

The Duke’s deputy; first appears 1.1

When the play begins he is no hypocrite, only a man with no experience of his own appetite, and he says so with something close to wonder the first time it appears. He becomes dangerous when he discovers that his reputation will carry any denial he chooses to make.

Isabella

A novice of the order of Saint Clare, Claudio’s sister; first appears 1.4

She enters asking for a stricter rule than the convent offers and is immediately handed the most worldly argument in the play. Her rhetoric is the best in it (she wins every exchange with Angelo on the merits), and the same absolutism that makes her unanswerable on mercy makes her merciless to her brother twenty minutes later.

Claudio

A young gentleman under sentence of death; first appears 1.2

His offence is getting his own betrothed with child before the contract was made public, which is why the sentence looks less like justice than like a demonstration. He accepts death for about forty lines, and the speech in which he stops accepting it is the most frightened writing in Shakespeare about what being dead might be.

Lucio

A gentleman, and Claudio’s friend; first appears 1.2

He fetches Isabella, coaches her through the first interview and keeps the plot moving whenever it stalls, which makes his punishment at the end feel wildly out of proportion. He is also the only character who tells the disguised Duke what Vienna actually thinks of him, and the Duke never forgives it.

Escalus

An ancient lord, second in the commission; first appears 1.1

He is the play’s picture of what ordinary good government looks like: he argues for Claudio, loses, and then spends a whole scene patiently failing to get a coherent account of a case out of a constable and a bawd. His reward for consistency is to be the last man in Vienna to learn what has been going on.

Mariana

Betrothed to Angelo, living at the moated grange; first appears 4.1

She was abandoned when her dowry was lost at sea and has been waiting ever since, which the play presents without comment as a normal consequence of a marriage market. Her plea for the man who wronged her is the moral turn of the last scene, and she makes it by asking Isabella, of all people, to kneel with her.

Pompey Bum

A tapster and bawd, later assistant to the executioner; first appears 1.2

He wonders aloud whether the state means to geld and splay all the youth of the city, the sharpest question anyone asks about the new policy. Moving him from brothel to scaffold is the play’s bluntest joke: the trade is the same, and the second one is legal.

Barnardine

A prisoner of nine years, condemned for murder; first appears 4.3

He is guilty, he is due to die, and he simply declines, which stops the whole machinery of the plot until a corpse can be found elsewhere. He is the one person in Vienna over whom neither the law nor the Duke has any purchase at all, and at the end he is pardoned without asking.
