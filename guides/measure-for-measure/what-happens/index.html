Measure for Measure · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I Angelo in charge, and Claudio condemned

The Duke gives Angelo his authority in a scene of strange, clotted syntax, and announces a journey to Poland he does not take. Within hours a dormant statute has a man under sentence of death for fornication, and his sister is fetched out of a convent before she has taken her vows.

1.1
The Duke deputes Angelo, with Escalus as his second, and leaves without explanation.
1.2
Lucio and the bawds learn that the brothels in the suburbs are to be pulled down; Claudio is led to prison.
1.3
The Duke admits he let the laws sleep for fourteen years and explains why he wants someone else to wake them.
1.4
Isabella asks for a stricter rule; Lucio arrives and sends her to plead for her brother’s life.

Act II Isabella pleads, and Angelo names his price

Escalus argues for mercy and gets nowhere. Isabella argues better, and gets somewhere worse: Angelo discovers desire at exactly the moment he is being praised for having none. The second interview drops the metaphors and states the price.

2.1
Angelo refuses Escalus’s appeal, then leaves him to a bawdy case that no court could untangle.
2.2
Isabella pleads for Claudio; Angelo refuses, tells her to come again, and is left horrified by his own appetite.
2.3
The disguised Duke hears Juliet’s confession and wants to know whether she repents the act or the shame.
2.4
Angelo names his terms, threatens to outweigh her word with his reputation, and gives her until tomorrow.

Act III Claudio and Isabella, and the Duke’s plan

The Duke, in his friar’s habit, prepares Claudio for death with a long argument that life is not worth keeping, and Claudio agrees until the alternative is named. Isabella’s refusal is absolute and her brother’s collapse is immediate. Out of that wreck the Duke improvises the bed trick, and the play’s verse gives way to plotting.

3.1
The Duke’s consolation, Claudio’s terror, Isabella’s fury, and the first mention of Mariana.
3.2
Pompey is arrested, Lucio slanders the absent Duke to his face, and the Duke is left alone with a rhymed verdict on Angelo.

Act IV The bed-trick, and the substituted head

A woman takes another woman’s place in the dark and the law is satisfied by a technicality it was never told about. Angelo breaks his side of the bargain anyway and orders the execution brought forward, so a second substitution is needed; a condemned man refuses to be hanged, and a prisoner who has died of fever supplies the head.

4.1
A song for Mariana, and Isabella’s account of the garden gate, the keys and the appointed hour.
4.2
Angelo’s message demands Claudio’s execution by four and his head by five; the Duke persuades the Provost to disobey.
4.3
A drunk prisoner declines to be executed, and Ragozine’s death of a fever provides a head instead.
4.4
Angelo cannot make sense of the Duke’s letters; alone, he trusts Isabella’s shame to keep her quiet, and wishes Claudio had lived.
4.5
The Duke, out of disguise, sets his return in motion.
4.6
Isabella is told to accuse herself of what never happened, and does not know why.

Act V The hearing at the city gate

The Duke returns in his own person and judges a case in which he has already been every witness. He lets Isabella be called mad, lets Mariana be dismissed, and reveals himself only after Angelo has condemned himself twice. Four marriages are ordered, two of them as punishments, and the last question of the play is put to a woman who does not reply.

5.1
Accusation, disbelief, the friar unhooded, Angelo’s sentence, Mariana’s plea, Isabella’s kneeling, Claudio alive, and a proposal.
