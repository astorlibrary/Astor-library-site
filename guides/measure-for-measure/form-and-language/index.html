Measure for Measure · William Shakespeare

Form and language

The disguised ruler

A governor who moves among his own subjects unrecognised.

The habit lets the Duke hear confessions, coach witnesses and edit the evidence, so the device that usually produces comic knowledge here produces a judge who has tampered with his own case.

Be absolute for death; either death or life Shall thereby be the sweeter. Reason thus with life: If I do lose thee, I do lose a thing That none but fools would keep:

Duke Vincentio, 3.1.5

The bed trick

A substitution in the dark, in which one woman takes another’s assignation.

Because Mariana was already contracted to Angelo, the trick makes his sin technically lawful and hers technically a marriage, which is the play at its most legally exact and least comfortable.

Shifts of register

Moving between verse and prose to change the standing of a scene.

The great moral arguments are in verse and the cases that actually come to court (Elbow, Froth, Pompey) are in prose, so the law is heard twice, once as philosophy and once as a shambles.

Some rise by sin, and some by virtue fall: Some run from brakes of ice, and answer none: And some condemned for a fault alone.

Escalus, 2.1.38

Imagery of coin and seal

Metaphors drawn from minting, weighing, stamping and testing metal.

Angelo is a name stamped on metal, virtues are coins that must go forth to be worth anything, and the bargain in 2.4 is transacted in the language of false coining.

Spirits are not finely touch'd But to fine issues, nor Nature never lends The smallest scruple of her excellence But, like a thrifty goddess, she determines Herself the glory of a creditor,

Duke Vincentio, 1.1.35

Soliloquy

A character alone on stage, thinking aloud to the audience.

Angelo gets the play’s best ones and uses them to discover himself in real time; Isabella is given one short one, at the end of 2.4, and it closes on a couplet, which is one reason her decisions are so fiercely argued over.

so our decrees, Dead to infliction, to themselves are dead; And liberty plucks justice by the nose; The baby beats the nurse, and quite athwart Goes all decorum.

Duke Vincentio, 1.3.27

Dramatic irony

The audience knows something a character on stage does not.

For three acts the audience watches people confess to, plead with and slander a friar they do not know is the duke, and in 5.1 that accumulated knowledge is used to make the audience complicit in a public humiliation.

But man, proud man, Dressed in a little brief authority, Most ignorant of what he’s most assured, His glassy essence, like an angry ape Plays such fantastic tricks before high heaven As makes the angels weep;

Isabella, 2.2.128
