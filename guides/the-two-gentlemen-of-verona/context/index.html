The Two Gentlemen of Verona · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1559
Montemayor’s Diana The Spanish pastoral romance that contains the story of Felismena, who follows her lover to court disguised as a page. Its undated first edition is usually placed about 1559.
1585
Felix and Philiomena at court A lost play of this title was performed at court by the Queen’s Men in January 1585, probably drawn from the same story.
1590
Probable date of writing Often counted among Shakespeare’s first plays; estimates run from the late 1580s to about 1593.
1598
Named by Francis Meres His Palladis Tamia lists Gentlemen of Verona first among Shakespeare’s comedies.
1598
Diana in English Bartholomew Yong’s translation of Montemayor was printed, though he said he had finished it many years before.
1623
First Folio The first printing, second among the comedies after The Tempest. No earlier edition survives.
1762
Benjamin Victor’s adaptation Staged at Drury Lane; the earliest recorded performance of any version of the play.
1826
Schubert’s An Silvia Schubert set Who is Silvia? in a German translation by Eduard von Bauernfeld.
1971
The musical A rock musical version with music by Galt MacDermot and lyrics by John Guare, first staged by the New York Shakespeare Festival. It won the Tony Award for Best Musical in 1972.

Places

Verona
Home of Valentine, Proteus and Julia, where the play begins.
Milan
The Duke’s court, where Valentine meets Silvia and Proteus betrays him.
Mantua
Silvia hears Valentine is living here; the last scenes are set in a forest on its borders.
