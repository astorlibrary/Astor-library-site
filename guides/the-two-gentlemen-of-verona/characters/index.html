The Two Gentlemen of Verona · William Shakespeare

Characters

Valentine

A gentleman of Verona; first appears 1.1

He leaves Verona scorning love, and in Milan falls for the Duke’s daughter Silvia, who loves him in return. They plan to elope by a rope ladder, but Proteus betrays the plan and the Duke tricks Valentine into revealing it. Banished, he becomes captain of a band of outlaws in the forest. There he stops Proteus attacking Silvia, forgives him on the spot, and wins Silvia and a pardon for the outlaws from the Duke.

Proteus

A gentleman of Verona, Valentine’s friend; first appears 1.1

He stays in Verona for love of Julia until his father sends him to Milan, where he falls for Silvia on sight. In three soliloquies he talks himself out of his vows to Julia and his friendship with Valentine. He betrays Valentine’s elopement to the Duke, and courts Silvia while pretending to plead for Thurio. In the forest he tries to force her, is caught by Valentine, repents, and returns to Julia when she reveals herself.

Julia

A lady of Verona, loved by Proteus; first appears 1.2

She pretends indifference to Proteus’s letter, then pieces it back together, and exchanges rings with him when he leaves. Following him to Milan dressed as the page Sebastian, she overhears him court Silvia, is hired as his servant, and is sent to give Silvia the ring she gave him. In the forest she reveals herself by producing the rings, and wins him back.

Silvia

The Duke of Milan’s daughter; first appears 2.1

She is secretly betrothed to Valentine, refuses Thurio, whom her father favours, and rejects Proteus with scorn, reminding him of Julia and of his friend. After Valentine’s banishment she escapes to find him with Sir Eglamour’s help. She is captured by outlaws, rescued by Proteus and then attacked by him, and she does not speak again after Valentine intervenes.

Speed

Valentine’s page; first appears 1.1

A quick-tongued page who argues in puns and mock logic. He diagnoses his master’s love, sees through Silvia’s letter trick when Valentine cannot, reads out Lance’s list of his sweetheart’s qualities, and goes into exile with Valentine.

Lance

Proteus’s servant, owner of the dog Crab; first appears 2.3

He acts out his family’s tearful farewell with his shoes, staff and hat, judges his master a knave, and keeps a written list of the virtues and vices of the milkmaid he loves. He has sat in the stocks and stood in the pillory for his dog’s offences. Sent to give Silvia a little dog from Proteus, he loses it and offers her Crab instead.

Crab

Lance’s dog; first appears 2.3

He watches Lance’s whole family weep at his master’s departure without shedding a tear. Presented to Silvia as a gift, he steals a capon’s leg from her plate and disgraces himself under the Duke’s table, and Lance takes the whipping for him.

Lucetta

Julia’s waiting-woman; first appears 1.2

She brings Proteus’s letter to her mistress, rates the suitors frankly, advises Julia on the page’s costume, and warns her that oaths and tears are servants to deceitful men.

The Duke of Milan

Silvia’s father, also called the Emperor; first appears 2.4

He wants his daughter to marry the wealthy Thurio. Warned by Proteus, he traps Valentine by asking his advice on wooing a lady and borrowing his cloak, under which he finds the rope ladder and a letter to Silvia. He banishes Valentine and recruits Proteus to slander him. At the end, brought in by the outlaws, he accepts Valentine as a gentleman worthy of his daughter.

Thurio

A rich, foolish suitor to Silvia; first appears 2.4

Favoured by Silvia’s father and no match for Valentine’s wit, he follows Proteus’s advice to serenade her. When Valentine threatens him in the forest he immediately gives up his claim, saying only a fool would risk his body for a girl who does not love him.

Sir Eglamour

A gentleman who helps Silvia escape; first appears 4.3

A gentleman who vowed chastity on the grave of the woman he loved. Silvia trusts him to escort her to Valentine and he agrees without hesitation, but when outlaws attack in the forest he outruns them and leaves her behind.

Antonio

Proteus’s father; first appears 1.3

Told by his servant Pantino that his son should see the world, he orders Proteus to the Emperor’s court in Milan the next day and refuses him even a day or two’s delay, which sets the plot moving.

The Outlaws

Banished men living in the forest; first appears 4.1

Some are gentlemen, banished for crimes of their own, among them plotting to carry off a lady and stabbing a man. They capture Valentine, and, impressed by his looks and his languages, make him their captain on condition that they do no outrages on women or poor travellers. At the end the Duke pardons them at Valentine’s request.
