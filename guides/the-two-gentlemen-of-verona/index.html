William Shakespeare

The Two Gentlemen of Verona

A study guide

Valentine leaves Verona for the Duke’s court at Milan, teasing his friend Proteus for staying at home for love of Julia. Proteus is soon sent after him, and exchanges rings with Julia before he goes. In Milan, Valentine has fallen in love with the Duke’s daughter Silvia and plans to elope with her by a rope ladder. Proteus falls for Silvia on sight, betrays the plan to the Duke, and Valentine is banished; in the forest he is made captain of a band of outlaws. Julia follows Proteus disguised as a page, and he hires her to carry his gifts to Silvia, who refuses them. When Silvia runs away to find Valentine, Proteus rescues her from the outlaws and then tries to force her. Valentine stops him and forgives him, Julia reveals herself, and the play ends with two marriages planned. Beside the lovers, the servants Speed and Lance, and Lance’s dog Crab, keep up a running commentary on love and loyalty.

Written c. 1589–1593; first printed 1623.
