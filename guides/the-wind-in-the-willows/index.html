Kenneth Grahame

The Wind in the Willows

A study guide

A mole abandons his spring-cleaning, finds a river, and acquires three friends and a way of life within a single afternoon. Around that quiet centre Grahame builds two very different books: a farce in which a rich amphibian steals a motorcar and goes to prison, and a set of chapters about homesickness, wandering and awe that have no jokes in them at all. The animals are the size of their own houses and of human courtrooms by turns, and the novel never explains the discrepancy because it does not need to.

Written Out of bedtime stories and letters sent to the author’s son in 1907; first published 1908.
