The Wind in the Willows · Kenneth Grahame

Themes

Home

Every animal in the book is defined by a dwelling (a hole in a bank, a house under a wood, a hall with a banqueting room), and the plot repeatedly takes one away. Grahame’s most serious writing is about the moment a house you abandoned calls you back, and about how difficult it is to admit that the call has been heard.

Mole leaves his house in Chapter 1 without a backward look, is ambushed by the smell of it in Chapter 5, and by Chapter 12 is planning the recovery of somebody else’s.

Wandering

Against the riverbank sits everything outside it: the Wide World Rat refuses to discuss, the open road Toad takes up as a fashion, and the southern ports the Sea Rat describes until Rat starts packing. The book is honest enough to make the case for leaving eloquent, and then to treat it as an illness to be nursed.

Refused as a topic in Chapter 1, played as farce by Toad in Chapter 2, and taken seriously enough in Chapter 9 to require physical restraint and a sheet of paper.

Friendship

Loyalty here looks like supervision: Rat feeds and teaches Mole, Badger and Rat put Toad under house arrest, and Mole holds a struggling friend down on the floor until the fit passes. Affection in this book is rarely declared and almost always expressed as taking charge of somebody.

Hospitality in Chapter 1, rescue in Chapter 3, house arrest in Chapter 6, physical restraint in Chapter 9, and a military operation in Chapter 12.

I want to show you the world! I’m going to make an animal of you, my boy!

Class

Toad has a hall, a boathouse and a solicitor; the Wild Wooders have holes and no names. The novel’s comedy of a gentleman in a washerwoman’s dress depends on the assumption that women and stoats alike are types more than people, and its happy ending restores a landowner to his property by force.

Signalled in the ‘Private. No landing allowed’ notice-board in Chapter 2, tested in the barge-woman’s kitchen, and settled at the point of a cudgel in Chapter 12.

The motorcar

The car arrives first as a noise, then as a cloud of dust, then as a court case, and the book records what it does to everyone else on the road: a horse in the ditch, a cart on its side, a canary in a cage. Grahame published in the years when speed limits, licences and registration plates had just become law.

A hum like a distant bee in Chapter 2, an obsession by the end of it, twenty years’ imprisonment in Chapter 6, and in Chapter 11 a claim to be less keen on motorcars, followed at once by a brilliant idea about motor-boats.

They found him in a sort of a trance, a happy smile on his face, his eyes still fixed on the dusty wake of their destroyer. At intervals he was still heard to murmur “Poop-poop!”

Wonder

One chapter steps outside the comedy altogether: two animals hear piping at dawn, see something they can only call the Friend and Helper, and are then given oblivion as a kindness. The book will not let awe be kept, catalogued or used later, which is why the experience leaves no mark on the plot.

Announced by music on an island in Chapter 7, granted and immediately withdrawn, and echoed faintly in the Sea Rat’s spell two chapters later.
