The Wind in the Willows · Kenneth Grahame

Form and language

Shifting registers

Moving between comic, domestic and exalted styles within one book, without harmonising them.

Chapter 7 is written in the cadences of the King James Bible and Chapter 10 in those of a music-hall sketch; the same four animals appear in both, and neither chapter apologises for the other.

Spring was moving in the air above and in the earth below and around him, penetrating even his dark and lowly little house with its spirit of divine discontent and longing.

Chapter 1

Animal and human scale

Keeping creatures simultaneously animal and human in size, station and behaviour, and never resolving the contradiction.

Toad is small enough to be picked up by a barge-woman and large enough to drive a motorcar and stand trial; Mole is the size of his own front door and of a punt seat by turns.

I want to show you the world! I’m going to make an animal of you, my boy!

Toad, Chapter 2

Catalogue

A long list of objects or names used in place of narrative.

The luncheon basket is emptied in a single unpunctuated word (“coldtonguecoldham” and the rest), so abundance arrives as a sound before it is an inventory.

“There’s cold chicken inside it,” replied the Rat briefly: “coldtonguecoldhamcoldbeefpickledgherkinssaladfrenchrollscresssandwichespottedmeatgingerbeerlemonadesodawater⁠—”

Water Rat, Chapter 1

Song

Sung lyric used to carry information a speech cannot.

The field-mice carol at Mole’s door, the Rat hears verse in the reeds, and Toad composes a hymn to himself in a bedroom and sings it twice, the surest sign that his reform is skin-deep.

They found him in a sort of a trance, a happy smile on his face, his eyes still fixed on the dusty wake of their destroyer. At intervals he was still heard to murmur “Poop-poop!”

Chapter 2

Idiolect

Giving each character a distinct and consistent way of speaking.

Rat’s sentences drift and trail off, Badger’s are short and final, and Toad speaks almost entirely in exclamations about himself; the four voices can be told apart without attribution.

there is nothing⁠—absolute nothing⁠—half so much worth doing as simply messing about in boats.

Water Rat, Chapter 1

Doors and thresholds

Physical barriers used to carry an idea about what is concealed behind them.

Badger’s door in the snow, ‘Mole End’ in Gothic lettering over a bellpull, a cell door, and finally a forgotten tunnel that opens inside the butler’s pantry.

directly facing them was Mole’s little front door, with “Mole End” painted, in Gothic lettering, over the bellpull at the side.

Chapter 5

Mock-heroic

Epic apparatus (arms, councils, homecomings) applied to small domestic events.

The last chapter is called ‘The Return of Ulysses’, the weapons are laid out in rows on the table, and the battle for a dining room is fought by four animals with sticks.

“Ho, ho!” he said to himself, as he marched along with his chin in the air, “what a clever Toad I am! There is surely no animal equal to me for cleverness in the whole world!

Toad, Chapter 10
