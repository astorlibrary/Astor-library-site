The Wind in the Willows · Kenneth Grahame

What happens

Chapters 1–2 The River Bank and the Open Road

Mole throws down his whitewash brush, comes up into the sunlight and meets a Rat who owns a boat and a picnic hamper. Within a chapter he has a new life; within two, Toad has abandoned a caravan for the motorcar that ran it into the ditch, and the book’s two rhythms are both running.

Chapters 3–4 The Wild Wood and Mr Badger

Mole goes into the Wild Wood alone to call on Badger, is frightened by faces and whistling, and is found asleep in a hollow tree by a Rat carrying pistols. Badger’s underground house turns out to be built in the cellars of a vanished city, and the terror of the wood becomes a lecture on who lasts.

Chapters 5–6 Dulce Domum and Mr Toad

On a winter walk Mole catches the smell of his own abandoned house and breaks down in the road; Rat takes him back, and the field-mice sing carols at the door. Then Badger, Rat and Mole put Toad under house arrest, and he escapes out of a bedroom window by knotted sheets before lunch.

Chapters 7–8 The Piper at the Gates of Dawn and Toad’s Adventures

Searching at night for Otter’s lost son, Rat and Mole hear piping on an island and see something they are then mercifully allowed to forget. Alongside that chapter sits Toad in a dungeon, weeping over bubble-and-squeak, and the gaoler’s daughter proposing a washerwoman’s dress.

Chapters 9–10 Wayfarers All and the Further Adventures of Toad

A Sea Rat talks the Water Rat into a trance of harbours and southern ports, and Mole has to hold him down and put a pencil in his paw. Toad meanwhile escapes by train, barge and stolen horse, sells the horse to a Gypsy, and boasts his way into a river.

Chapters 11–12 The tears of Toad and the return to Toad Hall

Toad comes home to find his house occupied by stoats and weasels and, for once, listens while Badger explains a plan. The four of them retake the hall through a tunnel, and Toad’s banquet is remarkable chiefly for the speeches and songs he does not give.
