Great Expectations · Charles Dickens

What happens

Chapters 1–7 The churchyard and the file

Pip is turned upside down among the graves of his parents and told to bring food and a file to a man with an iron on his leg. He steals from his own kitchen, is not found out, and the convict protects him by confessing to the theft, a debt that Dickens takes fifty chapters to call in.

Chapter 1
The convict rises from among the graves; Pip learns the identity of things by being held over them.
Chapter 2
The pork pie, the brandy and the file, taken from a house where everything is counted.
Chapter 5
The soldiers retake both convicts on the marsh; Magwitch takes the blame for the stolen food.
Chapter 7
Joe tells Pip about his own father, and about why he lets Mrs Joe rule the forge.

Chapters 8–19 Satis House, and the end of the first stage

Pip is sent to play at a house where the clocks stopped at twenty to nine and the wedding breakfast is still on the table. Estella calls his hands coarse and his boots thick, and he comes home ashamed of a life he had been perfectly happy in. Then Jaggers arrives with money from a source Pip is forbidden to ask about.

Chapter 8
Cards with Estella; Pip looks at his own hands afterwards and does not like them.
Chapter 9
He lies about the visit to his sister and Pumblechook, and Joe tells him plainly that lies are lies.
Chapter 13
Miss Havisham pays for the apprenticeship, and Pip is bound to the forge he now despises.
Chapter 18
Jaggers announces the great expectations, the condition of secrecy and the removal to London.
Chapter 19
Pip leaves for London; the mists rise, and the first stage of his expectations ends.

Chapters 20–28 Little Britain, Barnard's Inn and the Castle

London turns out to be uglier, narrower and dirtier than Pip expected. Pip meets Herbert Pocket, who renames him Handel and teaches him how to hold a fork; Wemmick, who keeps a drawbridge between his office and his feelings; and Jaggers, who washes his hands of his clients with scented soap. Joe visits and is made miserable by good manners.

Chapter 20
Smithfield, Newgate and the crowd of people waiting outside Jaggers’s office.
Chapter 22
Herbert gives Pip the history of Miss Havisham and the bridegroom who did not come.
Chapter 25
Walworth, the Aged P, the drawbridge and the gun: Wemmick’s second life.
Chapter 27
Joe comes to London, calls him sir, and leaves sooner than go on being out of place.

Chapters 29–39 Debts, Estella, and the end of the second stage

Pip believes Miss Havisham has designed Estella for him; Miss Havisham lets him. He runs up debts, half-ruins Herbert and secretly rescues him, buries his sister and neglects Joe. On a wild night in his rooms above the river, a man climbs the stairs and holds out both hands.

Chapter 29
Estella returns as a woman, and Miss Havisham tells Pip to love her whatever it costs him.
Chapter 35
Mrs Joe’s funeral, larks over the grave, and Pip’s promise to visit that he does not keep.
Chapter 38
Estella warns Pip against herself, tells Miss Havisham she is only what she was made, and admits she entraps every man but him.
Chapter 39
Magwitch returns, names himself the source of the money, and the second stage ends.

Chapters 40–52 Magwitch’s return

Everything Pip has built rests on a man who will be hanged if he is found in England. Herbert learns Magwitch’s history; Compeyson turns out to be Miss Havisham’s bridegroom; Estella marries Drummle; and Miss Havisham, asking what she has done, catches fire in her own wedding dress.

Chapter 42
Magwitch’s account of his own childhood, and of the gentleman who took the lighter sentence.
Chapter 44
Pip tells Estella she is part of his existence; she tells him she is to marry Bentley Drummle.
Chapter 49
Miss Havisham asks Pip’s forgiveness, repeats one question until the dress catches.
Chapter 51
Pip works out Estella’s parentage and makes Jaggers admit it without admitting anything.

Chapters 53–59 The river, the fever and the garden

Orlick nearly kills Pip in the sluice-house on the marshes; the escape down the Thames fails within sight of the steamer; Magwitch dies in the prison infirmary believing his daughter is alive and a lady. Pip falls ill, is nursed by Joe, works eleven years in the East, and comes back to a demolished house.

Chapter 53
Orlick, the lime-kiln, and an accounting of every injury Pip never noticed he was causing.
Chapter 54
The rowing boat, the four-oared galley and Compeyson under the paddle-wheel.
Chapter 56
Magwitch’s death, after Pip tells him that his daughter is alive, a lady, and loved.
Chapter 59
The ruined garden at Satis House eleven years later, and the evening mists rising.
