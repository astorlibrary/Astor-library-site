Great Expectations · Charles Dickens

Context

Dates

1812
Dickens born in Portsmouth Pip’s childhood belongs roughly to the same decade as Dickens’s own.
1824
Dickens sent to work in a blacking warehouse Dickens’s father was imprisoned for debt and the boy was sent out to work, an episode behind the novel’s shame about hands, clothes and company.
1840
Transportation to New South Wales ends The system that sent Magwitch to Australia, and the sentence he risks hanging by breaking.
1850
David Copperfield completed Dickens re-read it before starting Great Expectations, to be sure he was not repeating himself in the first person.
1857
Dickens moves to Gad’s Hill Place In the Kent country the novel’s marshes and estuary are drawn from.
1860
Serialisation begins The first weekly instalment appeared in All the Year Round in December; Dickens took the novel on partly to lift the journal’s sales.
1861
The ending is changed Bulwer-Lytton read the manuscript ending and objected; Dickens wrote the replacement that has been printed ever since.
1861
Serial ends and the book appears The last instalment ran in August, and Chapman and Hall published the novel in three volumes.
1870
Dickens dies at Gad’s Hill Great Expectations was his penultimate completed novel, followed by Our Mutual Friend.
1946
David Lean’s film Its graveyard opening and Satis House interiors have shaped how the novel is imagined ever since.
1998
Alfonso Cuarón’s film Moved to Florida and New York, with Pip as a painter, which shows how much of the novel is plot and how much the class system.
2012
Mike Newell’s film A return to period adaptation, with Ralph Fiennes as Magwitch and Helena Bonham Carter as Miss Havisham.

Places

Cooling, Kent
The marsh churchyard with its row of small graves, usually taken as the model for the opening chapter.
Rochester
The market town behind Pip’s, and the brewery and old houses behind Satis House.
Gad's Hill Place, Higham
Dickens’s house, a few miles from the marshes he was writing about.
Smithfield and Little Britain, London
Jaggers’s office, in sight of Newgate and the meat market Pip arrives at, smeared with filth and fat.
Walworth
Wemmick’s castle, drawbridge and nine o’clock gun.
Hammersmith
Matthew Pocket’s house on the river, where Pip is educated alongside Drummle and Startop.
