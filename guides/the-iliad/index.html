Homer

The Iliad

A study guide

The most famous war poem ever made leaves out the war’s beginning and its end. It covers some fifty days in the tenth year of the siege of Troy, and its subject, named in its first line, is one man’s anger. Achilles, publicly shamed by his commander over a captive woman, withdraws from the fighting and asks the gods to let his own side be beaten until they beg for him. They are beaten, and the price is his closest friend. His anger then turns on Hector, the Trojan prince who killed Patroclus, and the poem follows it through a river choked with bodies to the point where it burns out. The last book ends with a supper, and no victory: the old king of Troy kisses the hands that killed his son, and the man who killed him weeps with him.

Written Composed in Greek, probably in the second half of the eighth century BCE; Samuel Butler’s English prose translation appeared in 1898; first printed 1488.
