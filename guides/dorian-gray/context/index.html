The Picture of Dorian Gray · Oscar Wilde

Context

Dates

1854
Oscar Wilde is born in Dublin Son of a surgeon and of the poet who wrote as Speranza.
1873
Walter Pater’s Studies in the History of the Renaissance Its conclusion, that one should burn always with a hard, gem-like flame, is the argument Lord Henry repeats in a garden with the ethics removed.
1884
Huysmans publishes A rebours The decadent French novel usually identified with the yellow-backed book Lord Henry sends; Wilde declined to confirm it.
1890
The novel appears in Lippincott’s Monthly Magazine Thirteen chapters, printed in the July issue, and denounced in several London papers as a book for outlawed noblemen.
1891
The revised book edition Published by Ward, Lock and Co. with six new chapters, a preface of aphorisms, and the James Vane plot added to give the second half a pursuer.
1891
Wilde publishes Intentions and Lord Arthur Savile’s Crime Wilde’s most productive year as a prose writer, immediately before he turned to the stage.
1895
The Importance of Being Earnest opens at the St James’s Theatre In February, with Wilde at the height of his fame and three months from the first trial.
1895
The novel is used as evidence in court Passages were read out and Wilde was cross-examined on them during the libel action he brought against the Marquess of Queensberry.
1897
De Profundis is written in Reading Gaol A long letter to Lord Alfred Douglas, and Wilde’s own account of a life lived for sensation.
1900
Wilde dies in Paris At the Hôtel d’Alsace, aged forty-six, three years after his release.
1945
Albert Lewin’s film for MGM Shot in black and white, with the portrait revealed in colour: the best-known of many attempts to show what Wilde only describes.

Places

Grosvenor Square, Mayfair
Dorian’s house, with the locked schoolroom at the top of it.
Curzon Street
Lord Henry’s address, where he is nearly always at home at five o’clock.
Euston Road
The Vanes’ shabby lodgings, where Sibyl’s brother says goodbye before he sails for Australia.
Covent Garden
Where Dorian walks at dawn after leaving Sibyl, among carts of lilies.
Blue Gate Fields, Shadwell
The docks district of the opium dens, where a woman calls him Prince Charming and Sibyl’s brother hears it.
Paris
Basil’s destination on the night he is killed, and the city of the yellow book and of Wilde’s last years.

Critics

Walter Pater

“A Novel by Mr. Oscar Wilde”, in The Bookman (1891)

Pater praised the book’s skill but objected to its version of Epicureanism. A true Epicureanism, he wrote, aims at the harmonious development of the whole person, and to lose the moral sense, as Dorian does, is to fall short of it.

Ellen Moers

The Dandy: Brummell to Beerbohm (1960)

Moers placed Dorian and Lord Henry in the history of the dandy from Beau Brummell onwards, a figure who makes a public art of dress, manner and indifference, and read the novel as Wilde’s most complete statement of that ideal and its cost.
