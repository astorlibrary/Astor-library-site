Niccolò Machiavelli

The Prince

A study guide

A dismissed Florentine official sets down what fourteen years of embassies taught him about how states are won and lost, and sends it to the family that dismissed him. Machiavelli sorts principalities into kinds, measures each kind against a ruler who succeeded or failed, and comes back again and again to one man, Cesare Borgia, who did everything right and lost anyway. Its notorious chapters on cruelty, fear and broken promises take up five of the twenty-six; most of the rest is about armies, and the last is a plea for somebody to drive the foreigners out of Italy.

Written 1513; first printed 1532.
