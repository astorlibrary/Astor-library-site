The Prince · Niccolò Machiavelli

Context

Dates

1469
Niccolò Machiavelli is born in Florence On 3 May, in the year Lorenzo the Magnificent took charge of the city.
1494
Charles VIII of France invades Italy The Medici are driven from Florence and the republic is restored; chapter 12 dates Italy’s ruin from this unopposed march.
1498
Savonarola is executed; Machiavelli enters the chancery The friar was hanged and burned in May, and within weeks Machiavelli was secretary to the Second Chancery of the republic.
1502
Machiavelli is sent as envoy to Cesare Borgia He was with the duke’s court during the pacification of the Romagna and the killings at Sinigalia on the last day of the year.
1503
Alexander VI dies and Julius II is elected Machiavelli was in Rome for the conclave; the conversation with Borgia reported in chapter 7 belongs to these weeks.
1512
Machiavelli dismissed as the Medici return to Florence A Spanish army restored them, the republic fell, and Machiavelli was dismissed from every office he held.
1513
Written on his farm after prison and release Arrested in February on suspicion of conspiracy, tortured and freed in an amnesty, he withdrew to his farm at Sant’Andrea in Percussina. A letter of 10 December to Francesco Vettori says he has written a small work on principalities.
1516
Giuliano de’ Medici, the intended dedicatee, dies The letter to Vettori names Giuliano as the intended recipient; the dedication as it stands is to his nephew Lorenzo di Piero.
1527
Machiavelli dies in Florence On 21 June, weeks after the Sack of Rome and the second expulsion of the Medici; the restored republic had not given him back his post.
1532
First printed editions Antonio Blado printed the book in Rome, with papal privilege, and Bernardo Giunta in Florence in the same year.
1559
Placed on the Roman Index of Prohibited Books All of Machiavelli’s works were banned, which did more to spread the name than the reading of him.
1576
Innocent Gentillet publishes his attack on Machiavelli The French treatise commonly called the Contre-Machiavel gave northern Europe a Machiavelli made of extracted maxims, and the stage villain followed.
1640
Edward Dacres’s English translation is printed The first English version to reach print, a century after English writers had begun using the name as an insult; Marriott quotes Dacres’s rendering of Petrarch at the end of chapter 26.
1740
Frederick of Prussia’s Anti-Machiavel appears A refutation by a crown prince who, as king, invaded Silesia within the year.

Places

Florence
The republic Machiavelli served from the Palazzo Vecchio, and the city whose mercy to Pistoia and hold on Pisa supply some of his sharpest examples.
Sant’Andrea in Percussina
The family farm south of Florence, near San Casciano, where the book was written in 1513.
Cesena
The Romagna town where Ramiro d’Orco was left in the piazza one morning with the block and a bloody knife.
Senigallia
Marriott’s Sinigalia, where Borgia received his reconciled captains on 31 December 1502 and had them strangled.
Rome
Seat of the ecclesiastical principality of chapter 11, and the place where Borgia lost everything at the conclave of 1503.
Milan
Won by Francesco Sforza, lost by his sons, taken and lost twice by Louis XII: the book’s first example and its most frequent.
Syracuse
City of Agathocles, who seized it by massacre, and of Hiero, who rose in it by merit; the two appear two chapters apart.

Critics

Jean-Jacques Rousseau

The Social Contract (1762)

Rousseau suggested that Machiavelli, while pretending to give lessons to princes, gave great ones to the people, and called The Prince the book of republicans.

Isaiah Berlin

“The Originality of Machiavelli”, in Studies on Machiavelli (1972)

Berlin argued that Machiavelli did not separate politics from morality. He set two moralities against each other, the Christian virtues and the civic virtues of ancient Rome, and showed that a ruler could not live by both.
