Moby-Dick · Herman Melville

Characters

Ishmael

Narrator, foremast hand, sole survivor; first appears Chapter 1

He signs on as an ordinary sailor and then takes over the book, interrupting the voyage to explain whale anatomy, quote Sir Thomas Browne and stage a scene as drama. His curiosity saves him from Ahab’s kind of attention: he wants to know what things are, and Ahab wants to know what they mean about him.

Captain Ahab

Master of the Pequod; first appears Chapter 28

A Nantucket whaling captain of forty years’ experience who has decided that one animal is responsible for everything wrong with the universe. He is a brilliant manager of men and a competent navigator, and he uses both skills to convert thirty crew members and a hundred thousand dollars of other people’s property into an instrument of revenge.

Starbuck

First mate, a Nantucket Quaker; first appears Chapter 26

The only officer who states plainly that hunting one whale for revenge is blasphemy and bad business, and the only one with the standing to stop it. He is brave in a measured, professional way, thinks of his wife and child at Cape Cod, and once stands outside Ahab’s cabin with a loaded musket and cannot fire it.

Queequeg

Harpooneer, a prince of Kokovoko; first appears Chapter 3

A tattooed South Sea islander who left home to learn Christian civilisation and concluded that it was not worth the trouble. He is the best workman aboard, rescues a man who has just insulted him, and has his coffin built while he is still able to climb into it and try the fit.

Stubb

Second mate; first appears Chapter 27

He kills whales with a pipe in his mouth and a joke running, and treats the whole voyage as an amusing arrangement that somebody else is responsible for. His good humour is the book’s most disturbing moral position, because it cannot be told apart from not caring what happens to anyone.

Pip

Ship’s boy; first appears Chapter 27

A small Black cabin boy from Tolland County, Connecticut, who is put into a whaleboat, jumps twice, and is left alone on the surface of the Pacific for a time no one bothers to measure. What comes back is treated by the crew as an idiot and by the narrator as a witness, and he is the one person Ahab treats tenderly.

Fedallah

Ahab’s own harpooneer, kept below until the first lowering; first appears Chapter 47

A Parsee smuggled aboard with a boat’s crew of his own, who gives Ahab a prophecy built like the witches’ riddles in a tragedy: two hearses, a hemp rope, and a promise that he will go before his captain. Every clause comes true in a way Ahab never considered.

Moby Dick

An albino sperm whale; first appears Chapter 133

Rumoured to be in several oceans at the same time and impossible to kill, he is also a large animal doing what large animals do when harpooned. The book will not decide between those two descriptions: Ahab needs the first, and Ishmael keeps producing evidence for the second.
