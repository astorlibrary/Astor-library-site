Moby-Dick · Herman Melville

Form and language

Encyclopaedic digression

Interrupting a narrative with technical, historical or taxonomic material presented straight.

Whole chapters on the whale’s skin, its head, its tail and the pictures painted of it delay the plot for hundreds of pages, and the delay is the book’s argument about how long it takes to know anything.

Dramatic form

Passages set out as a play, with stage directions, soliloquies and asides.

From Chapter 36 onwards Ahab’s speeches are printed as theatre, and Chapter 40 abandons the narrator entirely for a scene of song and quarrel on the forecastle.

“Vengeance on a dumb brute!” cried Starbuck, “that simply smote thee from blindest instinct! Madness! To be enraged with a dumb thing, Captain Ahab, seems blasphemous.”

Starbuck, Chapter 36

Ambiguous symbols

An object placed at the centre of a work whose meaning is never settled by it.

The doubloon is nailed to the mast and then studied by eight of the crew in turn, from Ahab to Pip, each of whom finds something of himself in the same coin.

All visible objects, man, are but as pasteboard masks. But in each event⁠—in the living act, the undoubted deed⁠—there, some unknown but still reasoning thing puts forth the mouldings of its features from behind the unreasoning mask. If man will strike, strike through the mask!

Ahab, Chapter 36

Allusion

Borrowing names, phrases and situations from earlier texts to stand behind the present one.

The narrator is named for an outcast in Genesis, the captain for a king of Israel condemned by a prophet, and the rescuing ship for a mother weeping for her children.

Call me Ishmael. Some years ago⁠—never mind how long precisely⁠—having little or no money in my purse, and nothing particular to interest me on shore, I thought I would sail about a little and see the watery part of the world.

Chapter 1

Prophecy and foreshadowing

Early signs and predictions that the reader can only read correctly in retrospect.

Elijah on the wharf, Fedallah’s two hearses, and a coffin built for a sick harpooneer that becomes the ship’s life-buoy and then the last page’s raft.

Catalogue

Accumulating items in a list until the list itself produces an effect.

Ishmael’s list in Chapter 41 of all that most maddens and torments Ahab piles clause on clause until the whale’s white hump carries the rage of the whole race from Adam down.

All that most maddens and torments; all that stirs up the lees of things; all truth with malice in it; all that cracks the sinews and cakes the brain; all the subtle demonisms of life and thought; all evil, to crazy Ahab, were visibly personified, and made practically assailable in Moby Dick.

Chapter 41
