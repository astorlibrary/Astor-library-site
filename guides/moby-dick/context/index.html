Moby-Dick · Herman Melville

Context

Dates

1819
Herman Melville born in New York His father’s business failed in 1830 and his father died two years later, in debt. Melville’s formal schooling ended early.
1820
The whaleship Essex is sunk by a sperm whale Owen Chase’s narrative of the wreck and its aftermath became one of the novel’s central sources.
1839
J. N. Reynolds publishes an account of Mocha Dick A white sperm whale of the Pacific, already famous in maritime print before Melville named one.
1841
Melville sails from Fairhaven on the whaler Acushnet He deserted in the Marquesas eighteen months later, which gave him the material for his first book.
1846
Typee makes him briefly famous A South Seas narrative sold as fact; the reading public wanted more of the same from him for the rest of his life.
1850
Melville moves to Arrowhead and meets Hawthorne The book he was already writing grew considerably longer and stranger during the friendship.
1851
Published as The Whale in London and Moby-Dick in New York The London issue was expurgated, ran to three volumes, and omitted the epilogue, so its reviewers met a narrator who appeared not to have survived.
1852
Pierre is published and the reviews turn hostile Melville’s reputation as a writer of readable sea adventures did not survive the two books together.
1891
Melville dies in New York He had spent nearly twenty years as a customs inspector on the docks, and left Billy Budd unpublished.
1921
Raymond Weaver’s biography opens the Melville revival The first full life of the writer, published thirty years after his death, made the case that the neglected books were the important ones.
1930
Rockwell Kent’s illustrated edition His black-and-white designs shaped how a generation of readers pictured the ship and the whale.
1956
John Huston’s film, written with Ray Bradbury Gregory Peck as Ahab fixed the popular version of the story as a chase with the cetology cut out.

Places

New Bedford, Massachusetts
The whaling capital where Ishmael arrives too late for the packet, and meets Queequeg at the Spouter-Inn.
Nantucket
Where the Pequod is fitted out, and the island the novel credits with inventing the whale fishery.
Pittsfield, Massachusetts
Arrowhead, the farmhouse where Melville finished the book within sight of Mount Greylock.
Cape Horn
The passage into the Pacific, and the route Ahab took the day after his wedding.
Strait of Sunda
The narrow water between Java and Sumatra where the Pequod meets the Grand Armada.
The Japanese whaling ground
The Pacific cruising ground off Japan where the typhoon strikes, before the Pequod runs south to the equator for the final chase.
