The Tempest · William Shakespeare

What happens

Act I The storm, and Prospero’s story

The shipwreck is staged first and explained second. Prospero then tells Miranda the history she has never been told, wakes and dispatches Ariel, insults Caliban, and steers Ferdinand into meeting his daughter: four interviews in one scene, each conducted by a man who already knows how it ends.

1.1
Courtiers get in the way of the crew; the Boatswain tells them rank means nothing to the weather.
1.2
Prospero recounts the usurpation to Miranda, sets Ariel back to work, curses Caliban and arranges Ferdinand’s arrival.

Act II Antonio’s plot, and Caliban’s new master

On one part of the island Gonzalo imagines a commonwealth with no trade, law or labour and is mocked for it; within a hundred lines Antonio has talked Sebastian into murdering his brother. On another, Caliban meets a butler with a bottle and decides that a new master is the same thing as freedom.

2.1
Gonzalo’s utopia is laughed down; Antonio persuades Sebastian to kill the sleeping Alonso, and Ariel wakes them.
2.2
Trinculo shelters under Caliban’s cloak, Stephano arrives with wine, and Caliban swears allegiance to a drunkard.

Act III A betrothal, a plot and a banquet that vanishes

Miranda proposes marriage to Ferdinand while her father watches unseen. Caliban lays out a workable murder (take the books first) and is repeatedly interrupted by Ariel’s mimicry. The act ends with the courtiers accused of their old crime by a spirit dressed as a harpy.

3.1
Ferdinand carries wood; Miranda offers herself as wife, and Prospero approves from a distance.
3.2
Caliban explains how to kill Prospero; Ariel sows confusion by throwing his voice.
3.3
A banquet appears and disappears; Ariel indicts Alonso, Antonio and Sebastian to their faces.

Act IV A masque, broken off

Prospero gives the young couple a wedding entertainment performed by spirits, and then stops it mid-dance because he has remembered Caliban. The interruption is the hinge of the play: the most beautiful thing he makes is abandoned because of the one creature he cannot govern by art.

4.1
Iris, Ceres and Juno bless the betrothal; Prospero breaks off the show, then traps the conspirators with a rack of glittering clothes.

Act V Forgiveness, and the end of the magic

Ariel describes the suffering of the charmed courtiers and Prospero chooses mercy before he has heard a word of apology from any of them. He abjures his art, restores Ferdinand to his father, claims Caliban as his own, frees Ariel, and steps forward to ask the audience for release.

5.1
Prospero promises to break his staff, forgives his brother, discovers the lovers at chess, frees Ariel and asks for applause as absolution.
