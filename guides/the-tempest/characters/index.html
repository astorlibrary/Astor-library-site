The Tempest · William Shakespeare

Characters

Prospero

The exiled Duke of Milan, and the island’s magician; first appears 1.2

He lost a dukedom by preferring his library to government and has spent twelve years acquiring a power no government could match. Almost every event in the play is something he has arranged, which makes the one decision he has not rehearsed (to forgive, when he could punish) the only true action in it.

Miranda

Prospero’s daughter, aged about fifteen; first appears 1.2

She has met three people in her life and remembers none of her own history until her father decides she should. Her instincts are consistently better than his: she pities the shipwrecked before she knows who they are, and she proposes marriage herself before anyone can arrange it for her.

Ariel

A spirit of the air, bound to Prospero’s service; first appears 1.2

Everything the audience calls Prospero’s magic is in fact Ariel’s labour: the storm, the songs, the harpy, the masque, the invisible mischief. The relationship runs on a promise of freedom that is repeatedly deferred, and the play’s most surprising moment is Ariel telling a human what pity would look like.

Caliban

Son of the witch Sycorax; the island’s earlier inhabitant; first appears 1.2

He welcomed Prospero, showed him the fresh water and the fertile ground, and was enslaved for it after attempting to rape Miranda. The play gives him the case against his master and the best verse about the island, and then does not let either cancel the other out.

Ferdinand

Prince of Naples, Alonso’s son; first appears 1.2

He is separated from the ship, told his father has drowned, and set to carrying logs, a trial with no purpose except to make Miranda seem hard-won. He accepts the labour without complaint, which is the only test Prospero can devise and the only one he passes.

Alonso

King of Naples; first appears 1.1

He put Antonio into Prospero’s dukedom and spends the play believing he has lost his son as a consequence. Grief breaks him where argument could not: by Act V he is the only one of the three guilty men who says anything resembling an apology.

Antonio

Prospero’s brother, the usurping Duke of Milan; first appears 1.1

He took a dukedom once and, within an hour of washing ashore, is arranging a second murder for someone else’s benefit. He is forgiven in the last scene and does not reply, a silence that most productions have to make a decision about.

Sebastian

Alonso’s brother; first appears 1.1

A mocker of other men’s speeches who turns out to need only one conversation to be talked into killing his sleeping brother. He is the proof that Antonio’s crime was a piece of ordinary political reasoning, and no single aberration.

Gonzalo

An old counsellor of Naples; first appears 1.1

He is the reason Prospero survived: he stocked the leaking boat with food, water and the books. His imagined commonwealth without money or magistrates is mocked on stage as nonsense, and it is also the only vision in the play of an island run by nobody.

Stephano

Alonso’s butler, washed ashore with a butt of wine; first appears 2.2

Given a bottle and a worshipper, he declares himself king of the island within a scene. His plot against Prospero is the same plot as Antonio’s, played by a drunk, and the parallel is the play’s sharpest joke about how little ambition requires.
