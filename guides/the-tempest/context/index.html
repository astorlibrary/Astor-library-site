The Tempest · William Shakespeare

Context

Dates

1603
Florio’s translation of Montaigne published The English ‘Of the Cannibals’ behind Gonzalo’s commonwealth speech becomes available.
1609
The Sea Venture wrecked on Bermuda Bound for Virginia, the ship broke up on the reefs; the survivors reached Jamestown the following year.
1610
Jourdain’s A Discovery of the Barmudas printed One of the pamphlets through which the wreck became a London story of storm, survival and abundant islands.
1611
Performed before James I at Whitehall on 1 November The Revels record is the earliest firm evidence that the play existed and was being acted.
1613
Played at court during the festivities for Princess Elizabeth’s marriage One of a group of plays given by the King’s Men that winter for the betrothal and wedding celebrations.
1623
First Folio The Folio is the only early text, and it opens with The Tempest, unusually well printed, with full stage directions and a cast list.
1667
Dryden and Davenant’s The Enchanted Island A Restoration rewriting that gave Miranda a sister and Caliban a mate, and held the stage for well over a century.
1957
Peter Brook directs John Gielgud as Prospero At the Shakespeare Memorial Theatre; remembered for a severe Prospero.
1970
Jonathan Miller’s colonial production Casting made the island’s politics explicit, and helped establish a reading British theatre has not since put down.
1991
Peter Greenaway’s Prospero’s Books A film that puts Prospero’s library at the centre of the play.
2010
Julie Taymor’s film, with Helen Mirren as Prospera Changing the magician’s sex reorganises the play’s questions about parenthood, exile and authority.

Places

Milan
The dukedom Prospero lost to his brother while he studied.
Naples
Alonso’s kingdom, and the wedding party the shipwrecked court was returning from.
Tunis
Where Claribel was married, and the voyage home from which the storm interrupts.
Algiers
Argier in the text: the city from which Sycorax was banished to the island.
Bermuda
The still-vex’d Bermoothes of Ariel’s speech, and the site of the 1609 wreck behind the play’s storm.
Whitehall Palace, London
Where the first recorded performance was given before James I on 1 November 1611.

Critics

Octave Mannoni

Prospero and Caliban: The Psychology of Colonization (1950)

Writing after the uprising in Madagascar of 1947, Mannoni used Prospero and Caliban as figures for the colonist and the colonised and the dependence between them. His book began the colonial readings of the play. The French original appeared in 1950 and the English translation in 1956.

Frank Kermode

Introduction to the Arden edition of The Tempest (1954)

Kermode read the play as a pastoral drama about nature and nurture. Caliban, a creature of nature without nurture, is set against the courtiers, who have every advantage of civilisation and behave worse.

Paul Brown

““This thing of darkness I acknowledge mine”: The Tempest and the Discourse of Colonialism”, in Political Shakespeare (1985)

Brown argues that the play takes part in the colonial thinking of its time and also shows its strains: Prospero’s power depends on the Caliban he condemns, as the line of the title suggests.
