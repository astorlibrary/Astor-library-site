The Tempest · William Shakespeare

Form and language

Retrospective exposition

Putting the backstory into a single long scene of telling.

Act I Scene 2 is about a quarter of the play and consists largely of Prospero explaining the past to people who interrupt him. Because he controls what is told and when, the exposition is also a demonstration of his character.

If by your art, my dearest father, you have Put the wild waters in this roar, allay them.

Miranda, 1.2.1

The court masque

A courtly entertainment of gods, song, dance and blessing, staged for an occasion, with little plot.

The betrothal masque of 4.1 imports an entire Jacobean art form into the play and then breaks it off in the middle, which no masque at court would ever have done.

Our revels now are ended. These our actors, As I foretold you, were all spirits and Are melted into air, into thin air:

Prospero, 4.1.162

Metatheatre

A play drawing attention to the fact that it is a play.

Prospero’s actors are spirits, his revels end, the great globe dissolves, and the last speech asks the audience to release him with their hands. The illusion is dismantled in front of the people it was made for.

And by that destiny to perform an act Whereof what's past is prologue, what to come In yours and my discharge.

Antonio, 2.1.275

Song

Sung lyric used to carry information a speech cannot.

Ariel’s songs lead Ferdinand ashore, tell him his father is dead in language too beautiful to be grief, and wake Gonzalo in time to prevent a murder. The music does the work the plot would otherwise need scenes for.

Full fathom five thy father lies; Of his bones are coral made; Those are pearls that were his eyes: Nothing of him that doth fade But doth suffer a sea-change Into something rich and strange.

Ariel, 1.2.463

Parody subplot

A low plot that repeats the main plot’s action in a debased form.

Stephano, Trinculo and Caliban plan exactly the crime Antonio and Sebastian plan, a few scenes later, with a bottle in place of a sword, and they are played as farce while the courtiers are played as a threat.

The classical unities

Keeping a play to one place, one day and one action.

Alone among the late plays, this one observes them: the characters repeatedly state the time, and the three hours of the story run at roughly the speed of the performance.
