Joseph Conrad

Heart of Darkness

A study guide

Waiting for the tide on a yawl in the Thames estuary, the seaman Marlow tells four friends how he once got command of a river steamer for a Continental trading company in Africa. He finds chained labourers, a grove where sick workers are left to die, agents who talk of nothing but ivory, and a steamer sunk at the bottom of the river. Everyone speaks of Mr Kurtz, the chief of the Inner Station, who sends down more ivory than all the rest together. After months of repairs and a slow journey upstream, an attack from the bank and the death of his helmsman, Marlow finds Kurtz dying in a station ringed with heads on stakes. He brings him away, hears his last words, and, back in Europe, lies about them to the woman Kurtz was to marry.

Written 1898–1899; first published 1899.
