About Astor Library

Astor Library publishes annotated editions of classic novels, plays and poems, in paperback, hardback and Kindle. This site has a free page for every book in the catalogue and free study guides to many of them.

The editions

Every Astor edition prints the complete text. Alongside it there is a summary of each chapter or scene, notes on the words and references a modern reader is likely to need, and, in many editions, an account of the book’s background and publication. The plays have line numbers, and the notes sit on the same page as the lines they explain.

The Study Editions are written for students at school and university. As well as the complete text and its summaries, they include closer analysis of the writing, key quotations with commentary, work on characters and themes, the arguments critics have made, and guidance on writing about the book. They are not tied to any one exam board. A few Study Editions cover two books commonly studied together.

Some titles are also in hardback, with the same contents as the paperback.

How the facts are checked

Each book page lists the sources used for its dates, publication history and performance history. Every quotation in a study guide has been compared with a named text of the book, and the guide says which text that is. The editorial standards set this out in more detail.

If you find a mistake, please write to support@astorlibrary.com.

Haydn Wood

Founder and editor

Astor began as an idea while I was studying for my Master’s degree. My research was on Langston Hughes and the Harlem Renaissance, which meant spending as much time on the history around a text as on the writing itself. I was also reading books covered in highlighter, Post-it notes and pencil, and I started to think that more of what a reader needs could be built into the edition.

When I began teaching, I annotated the set texts I was teaching, starting with Macbeth and Jekyll and Hyde. Those notes became the first Astor editions. The list has since grown to cover nine collections, from Homer to Orwell.

I still teach, and the editions still come out of that work: the questions students ask, the words and references that need explaining, and the background that makes a difficult book easier to read. The aim is to give you the complete book with that material already in it.

What needs an account

Very little. The book pages, study guides, close readings, quizzes and Today’s questions are free to read without signing in. Quiz scores, reading progress and saved books are kept in your own browser.

The illustrated guides show their first three slides to everyone and ask for a free account to read the rest. An account also keeps a list of the guides you have saved.

The name

The name comes from the Astor Library in New York, a free reference library paid for by a bequest from John Jacob Astor and opened in 1854. In 1895 it joined the Lenox Library and the Tilden Trust to form the New York Public Library.
