About

Astor Library

Astor Library publishes classic novels, plays and poems in annotated paperback and hardback editions, and keeps a free page on this site for every book.

Founder and editor

Haydn Wood

Hi, I’m Haydn, founder of Astor Library.

Astor began as an idea during my Master’s degree. My research focused on Langston Hughes and the Harlem Renaissance, which meant spending a lot of time thinking about the history around a text as well as the writing itself. I was also constantly reading books covered in highlighter, Post-it notes and scribbled annotations, and I started thinking that classic texts could do with something more built into the edition itself.

As a teacher, I began by annotating set texts such as Macbeth and Jekyll and Hyde. Over time, those editions grew, and so did Astor Library, expanding across different periods, writers, genres and ideas.

Each Astor edition is designed to give readers more than the text alone. Depending on the book, that can include its publication history, historical background, information about the writer, summaries, explanatory notes, analysis and other material that helps make the work easier to understand and study.

The aim is a simple one: to give you the complete book, with the useful material already there, so you do not need quite so many Post-it notes, pencil marks and scribbles in the margins. Although, of course, there is nothing wrong with those either.

Alongside Astor Library, I work as a teacher, and much of what goes into the editions grows directly out of that work: the questions students ask, the things that need explaining and the details that make a difficult text easier to approach.

The books

The editions

A main Astor edition prints the complete novel, play or poem, with a summary of every chapter or scene and notes on the words and references a modern reader is likely to need.

The plays have line numbers and scene summaries, with the notes on the same page as the text. Many editions also have chapters on where the text comes from, how it was first published and how it has been performed.

Study editions are for school and university work. They add key quotations with analysis, the arguments critics have made, and model paragraphs, and are not tied to one exam board. Some titles are also in hardback, with the same contents as the paperback.

Checking

Sources

Every book page lists the sources used for its dates, publication history and performance history, and names the text its quotations were taken from.

Each quotation in a study guide has been matched word for word against that text. Summaries are written from the book itself. Where a point is a matter of opinion, the page says so.

The editorial standards page sets out the rules the editions follow.

Accounts

What needs an account

Almost nothing. The book pages, study guides, close readings and quizzes are free to read without signing in.

The illustrated guides show their first three slides to everyone and ask for a free account to read the rest. An account also keeps a list of the guides you have saved.

Quiz scores, reading progress and the books in My library are kept in your own browser.

The name

Why Astor?

The name comes from the Astor Library in New York, a free reference library paid for by a bequest from John Jacob Astor and opened in 1854.

In 1895 it joined the Lenox Library and the Tilden Trust to form the New York Public Library.
