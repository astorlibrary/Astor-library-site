About Astor Library

What Astor Library publishes.

Astor Library is an independent publisher of classic editions, study editions and free literature resources.

The name

Why Astor Library?

Astor Library is a modern independent publisher. It is named after the nineteenth-century Astor Library in New York.

The original Astor Library in New York was founded through the bequest of John Jacob Astor. In 1895, it was combined with the Lenox Library and the Tilden Trust to form The New York Public Library.

The original Astor Library was a reference library rather than a circulating library. The modern publisher uses the name for editions intended for reading, reference and study.

The main editions

Complete texts with reading support.

Main Astor Editions reproduce the complete novel, play or poem. The book page for each title lists the material included in that edition.

Prose editions may include chapter summaries, short explanatory notes, historical context and longer sections on structure, language and publication history.

Shakespeare editions include scene divisions, line numbers, scene summaries and explanatory notes. Many also include material on sources, early printed texts and performance history.

Astor Study

Study editions and teaching resources.

Study editions are intended for lessons, independent study, essays and exams.

They include summaries, context, selected quotations, critical interpretations, model paragraphs and reading notes. The material is not tied to one exam board.

The website also provides free guides, booklets, presentation material, annotated passages and lesson plans. These resources are separate from the main reading editions.

Editorial process

Research, checking and revision.

Dates, publication details and historical claims are checked against the sources listed on each page. Plot, character, structure and language are explained in direct prose.

Interpretation is identified as interpretation. Pages are revised when clearer explanations, better sources or corrections become available.

Purpose of the notes

What the additional material explains.

Summaries identify the action and its sequence. Notes define difficult words and references. Context sections explain relevant historical or literary information. Critical sections analyse form, language and interpretation.

Complete texts remain available alongside the explanatory material.
