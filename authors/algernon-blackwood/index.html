Biography

Blackwood’s life.

Algernon Blackwood was born in Kent in 1869 into a strict evangelical family, and was educated at Wellington College and at a Moravian school in the Black Forest. In his twenties he went to Canada and then to New York, where he tried farming and business before working as a newspaper reporter, and he spent his holidays in the forests of Ontario and Quebec. He came back to England in 1899 and began publishing ghost stories in his late thirties.

“The Willows” (1907) came out of a canoe trip down the Danube, and “The Wendigo” (1910) out of hunting trips in the Canadian woods. He belonged for a time to the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, and late in life he became well known on BBC radio and television as a teller of ghost stories. He died in 1951.

The Wendigo

A moose-hunting party goes into the backwoods north of Rat Portage, in Canada, and the guide, Défago, is called away in the night by something that shouts his name. Modern Classics

The Willows

Two men canoeing down the Danube in flood tie up for the night on a sandy island among the willows below Pressburg, and cannot leave. Algernon Blackwood had made the same journey by canoe himself, a few years earlier. This is the original British text of 1907, printed without footnotes.Open book page →
