Biography

Weedon Grossmith’s life.

Weedon Grossmith was born in London in 1854, seven years after his brother George. He trained as a painter at the Slade and the Royal Academy Schools and exhibited at the Academy, but in the 1880s he went on the stage and became a popular comic actor, often of nervous, put-upon little men not unlike Mr Pooter. He later managed the Vaudeville Theatre and wrote plays of his own.

The Diary of a Nobody was a joint book. The brothers wrote it together for Punch, and Weedon drew the illustrations for the book edition of 1892. His memoir, From Studio to Stage, came out in 1913, and he died in 1919.

The Diary of a Nobody

‘Why should I not publish my diary?’ asks Charles Pooter, a City clerk who has just moved into The Laurels, Brickfield Terrace, Holloway. For fifteen months he records trouble with tradesmen, a humiliating ball at the Mansion House and his son Lupin’s schemes. It began as a series in Punch in 1888–89.Open book page →
