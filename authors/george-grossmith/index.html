Biography

George Grossmith’s life.

George Grossmith was born in London in 1847, the son of a journalist and lecturer. He followed his father as a reporter at Bow Street police court before making his name as a comic entertainer at the piano. In 1877 he was cast in Gilbert and Sullivan’s The Sorcerer, and for the next twelve years he created the leading comic parts in their operas, among them Sir Joseph Porter, the Major-General, Bunthorne, the Lord Chancellor and Ko-Ko.

He wrote The Diary of a Nobody with his brother Weedon. It ran in Punch in 1888 and 1889 and came out as a book, enlarged and illustrated by Weedon, in 1892. George went back to touring as a solo entertainer and died in Folkestone in 1912.

The Diary of a Nobody

‘Why should I not publish my diary?’ asks Charles Pooter, a City clerk who has just moved into The Laurels, Brickfield Terrace, Holloway. For fifteen months he records trouble with tradesmen, a humiliating ball at the Mansion House and his son Lupin’s schemes. It began as a series in Punch in 1888–89.Open book page →
