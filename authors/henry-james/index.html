Biography

Henry James’s life.

Henry James was born in New York in 1843 and spent much of his childhood moving between America and Europe with his family. He settled in London in 1876, made his name with Daisy Miller (1878) and The Portrait of a Lady (1881), and wrote for the rest of his life about Americans and Europeans misreading one another. He became a British subject in 1915 and died in London the following year.

The Turn of the Screw ran as a serial in Collier’s Weekly in 1898 and came out as a book later that year. Readers have argued ever since about whether the governess sees the ghosts or imagines them, an argument that took its modern shape after Edmund Wilson’s essay on the story in 1934.

Victorian

The Turn of the Screw

A young governess takes charge of two orphaned children, Miles and Flora, at a country house called Bly, on condition that she never troubles their uncle in London. She begins to see a man and a woman about the grounds, and learns that the last valet and the last governess are both dead. Henry James published the story in 1898, and readers still disagree about whether the ghosts are there.Open book page →
