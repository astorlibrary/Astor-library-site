L. M. Montgomery

L. M. Montgomery (1874–1942) grew up in Cavendish, Prince Edward Island, the model for Avonlea in Anne of Green Gables (1908); The Blue Castle followed in 1926.

Biography

Montgomery’s life.

Lucy Maud Montgomery was born in Prince Edward Island in 1874. Her mother died before she was two, and she was brought up by her grandparents in Cavendish, the farming village that became Avonlea. She trained as a teacher, worked for a year on a Halifax newspaper and wrote in the evenings. Anne of Green Gables was turned down by several publishers and put away in a hatbox before L. C. Page of Boston took it; it came out in 1908 and was an immediate success.

In 1911 she married a Presbyterian minister, Ewan Macdonald, and spent the rest of her life in Ontario, where she wrote seven more Anne books among many others. The Blue Castle (1926), one of her few novels for adults, is set in the Muskoka lake country of Ontario. She died in Toronto in 1942.

The Blue Castle

Valancy Stirling is twenty-nine, unmarried and ordered about by her family in the Ontario town of Deerwood. Then a doctor’s letter tells her she has a year to live, and she stops doing what she is told. Unusually for L. M. Montgomery, the novel is set in Ontario, not Prince Edward Island.
