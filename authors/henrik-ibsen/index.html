Biography

Ibsen’s life.

Henrik Ibsen was born in Skien, Norway, in 1828. His father’s business failed when he was a boy, and at fifteen he was apprenticed to a pharmacist in Grimstad. He learned the theatre as a writer and director in Bergen and Christiania, then left Norway in 1864 and lived in Italy and Germany for twenty-seven years. There he wrote Brand, Peer Gynt and the prose plays of modern life that made him famous across Europe. He returned to Norway in 1891 and died in Christiania in 1906.

A Doll’s House was published in December 1879 and first staged in Copenhagen that month. For the German premiere the leading actress refused to play a mother who leaves her children, and Ibsen, to prevent a worse version by someone else, wrote an alternative ending in which Nora stays. He called it a barbaric outrage on the play.
