Biography

Paine’s life.

Thomas Paine was born in Thetford, Norfolk, in 1737, the son of a Quaker staymaker. He followed his father’s trade, then became an excise officer and was dismissed twice. In London in 1774 he met Benjamin Franklin, who gave him letters of introduction, and he sailed for Philadelphia. Common Sense, published in January 1776, argued plainly for independence and sold in enormous numbers; the first number of The American Crisis followed that December, and Washington had it read to his troops.

After the war Paine went back to Europe. Rights of Man (1791–92), his defence of the French Revolution, led to his trial in England for seditious libel; by then he was in France, where he was elected to the National Convention and later imprisoned for ten months. He returned to America in 1802 and died in New York in 1809.

Romantic & Regency

Common Sense and The American Crisis

Thomas Paine had been in America barely a year when Common Sense told the colonists, in January 1776, that an island could not govern a continent and that the time to part had come. This edition prints the enlarged text of February 1776 with all sixteen papers of The American Crisis, which followed the war from the retreat of 1776 to the peace.Open book page →
