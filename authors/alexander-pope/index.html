Biography

Pope’s life.

Alexander Pope was born in London in 1688, the son of a Catholic linen merchant. As a Catholic he could not go to university, vote or hold office, and the family moved out to Binfield in Windsor Forest, where he largely taught himself. A tubercular infection of the spine in childhood stunted his growth, and he was in pain and poor health all his life.

An Essay on Criticism (1711) made his name at twenty-three. The Rape of the Lock appeared in two cantos in 1712 and in the five-canto version, with its sylphs, in 1714. His translations of Homer’s Iliad and Odyssey made him rich enough to live independently, at a villa at Twickenham with a famous grotto, and he spent his later years on satire and philosophical verse, from The Dunciad to An Essay on Man. He died at Twickenham in 1744.

Restoration & Enlightenment

The Rape of the Lock and An Essay on Criticism

A young lord snips a curl from a beauty’s head at Hampton Court, and Alexander Pope tells the story as an epic, with sylphs for guardian spirits, a card game for a battle and a new star at the end. This edition pairs The Rape of the Lock with An Essay on Criticism, the verse argument about judging poetry that made Pope’s name in 1711, and prints the two poems in their later forms.Open book page →
