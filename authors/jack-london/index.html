Biography

Jack London’s life.

Jack London was born in San Francisco in 1876 and grew up poor across the bay in Oakland. Before he was twenty-one he had worked in a cannery, raided oyster beds, sailed on a sealing schooner to Japan and tramped across America. In 1897 he joined the Klondike gold rush, came home the next year with scurvy and no gold, and started writing about the North.

The Call of the Wild (1903) made him famous, and White Fang (1906) told the same story in reverse: a wolf dog that comes in from the wild to live with people. London became one of the best-paid writers in America, a lecturer for socialism and the owner of a large ranch in Sonoma County, where he died in 1916, aged forty.

American Classics

White Fang

White Fang, three-quarters wolf, is born in the Yukon wild and passes from a Native owner, Gray Beaver, to a dog-fighter called Beauty Smith before a mining engineer, Weedon Scott, takes him home to California. Jack London wrote it in 1906 as a companion to The Call of the Wild, with the journey running the other way.Open book page →
