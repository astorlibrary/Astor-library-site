Biography

Chopin’s life.

Kate Chopin was born Katherine O’Flaherty in St Louis in 1850. At twenty she married Oscar Chopin, a cotton factor, and lived in New Orleans and then in rural Natchitoches Parish, raising six children. Oscar died in 1882, and two years later she went home to St Louis, where she began to write at the end of her thirties.

Her stories of Creole and Cajun Louisiana, collected in Bayou Folk (1894) and A Night in Acadie (1897), made her reputation. The Awakening (1899) damaged it: reviewers condemned Edna Pontellier’s desires, and Chopin published little afterwards. She died in 1904, and the novel was largely forgotten until scholars took it up again in the 1950s and 1960s.

The Awakening

Edna Pontellier, twenty-eight, married to a New Orleans businessman and the mother of two boys, spends a summer at Grand Isle and learns to swim. By the autumn she has moved out of her husband’s house. Reviewers in 1899 disliked the book so much that Kate Chopin published very little afterwards.
