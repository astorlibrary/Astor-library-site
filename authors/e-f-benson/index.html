Biography

Benson’s life.

Edward Frederic Benson was born in 1867 at Wellington College, where his father was headmaster; his father later became Archbishop of Canterbury, and his brothers A. C. and R. H. Benson were writers too. After Marlborough and King’s College, Cambridge, he worked for some years at the British School at Athens, and his first novel, Dodo (1893), a society comedy, was a best-seller.

He wrote nearly a hundred books and is best known now for the Mapp and Lucia comedies and for his ghost stories, which appeared in collections from The Room in the Tower (1912) to More Spook Stories (1934). He lived for many years at Lamb House in Rye, which had been Henry James’s house, served three terms as the town’s mayor, and died in 1940.

E. F. Benson Ghost Stories

For fifteen years a man dreams of a silent tea-party and a hostess who tells him that Jack will show him the room in the tower, until he goes to stay with friends and finds himself in that house. This volume brings together two of E. F. Benson’s major supernatural collections in full, The Room in the Tower and Other Stories (1912) and Visible and Invisible (1923): twenty-nine stories in all, seventeen from the first book and twelve from the second.Open book page →
