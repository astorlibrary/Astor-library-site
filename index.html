Inside an Astor edition

How an Astor page is arranged

Notes should send you back to the sentence, not save you the trouble of reading it.

Jane AustenPride and Prejudice · Chapter I

Original text

“It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife.”

However little known the feelings or views of such a man may be on his first entering a neighbourhood, this truth is so well fixed in the minds of the surrounding families, that he is considered the rightful property of some one or other of their daughters.

Complete textChapter 1Page 1

Material around the text

01 · Orientation

What has happened?

Netherfield Park has been let to Mr Bingley, a wealthy unmarried man. Mrs Bennet wants her husband to visit him because she hopes Bingley may marry one of their daughters.

02 · Context

Marriage and property

Marriage could determine a woman’s financial security and social position. Austen begins with that real pressure, then makes the neighbourhood’s certainty sound absurd.

03 · Language

Who believes the “truth”?

The opening looks like an impartial rule. By the end of the next sentence, it belongs to the surrounding families. “Must be in want” quietly gives their wish the authority of universal law.

The Astor name

Where the name comes from

The name is a nod to one of the great library names of the nineteenth century. The original Astor Library in New York was founded through the bequest of John Jacob Astor. In 1895 it was combined with the Lenox Library and the Tilden Trust to form The New York Public Library.

It was not a circulating library. Its books were there to be consulted and studied. Astor Editions take that reference-library idea into the text itself.
