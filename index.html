Inside the editions

The work stays at the centre.

The main editions are made for any reader. A prose chapter is followed by a clear summary and factual context; a play keeps its scenes, line numbers and notes close to the words.

The study editions are made for classroom and exam work. They add sustained work on character, theme, quotation, criticism and writing, while the main editions remain books for any reader.

Why Astor?

A library name with a history.

The name is a nod to one of the great library names of the nineteenth century. The original Astor Library in New York was founded through the bequest of John Jacob Astor. In 1895, it was combined with the Lenox Library and the Tilden Trust to form The New York Public Library.

It was not a circulating library. Its books were there to be consulted and studied. Astor Editions take that reference-library idea into the text itself.
